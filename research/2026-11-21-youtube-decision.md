# Should Project Rival have a YouTube channel for the SEMA year, and what shape

Research run 2026-11-21 for @elcoyotediesel (577 Instagram followers, Creator account, one man, 1959 Ford F600 on a 2005-2007 Super Duty frame, built 7.3 Powerstroke, 10R140, Road to SEMA Nov 2 to 5, 2027; partners Driven Diesel, ISSPRO, Power Hungry Performance). Builds on `2026-11-21-crossposting-decision.md` section 2 (Shorts mirrored via Buffer Free), `playbook/00-content-plan.md` ("no cross-posting project yet, long-form is a second production pipeline"), `2026-11-21-build-series-structure.md` section 1 (Westen's "welcome sign to the hotel", Hard Up Garage's DRIVEN-then-YouTube release) and `2026-11-21-diesel-growth-case-studies.md` 1.7 to 1.9 (DriveHub's 16-episode OBS 7.3 curve, TurboRich831 stuck at 2K).

## 0. Decision in one paragraph

Yes, but not the channel the September plan was worried about. The YouTube channel already has to exist for the Buffer Shorts mirror, so the real question is whether long-form goes on it, and when. The data says a weekly long-form build channel from a standing start averages 180 views on video one and 272 on video ten (vidIQ, 448,413 new channels), the one-man F600 frame-swap channel most like Rival sits at 2,040 subscribers after five years, and the middle episodes of every build series in the record run at 25 to 50 percent of episode one. So long-form will not grow the account and will not earn ad money in the SEMA year (a channel created now is a post-Feb-2027 applicant and needs 8,000 watch hours). What it does do is give three partners and every prospect a linkable, time-stamped record that the parts went on this truck, and give SEMA week a tentpole. That is worth about two hours a week, not a second production pipeline. Recommendation: milestone-keyed long-form, not calendar-keyed. Episode 1 goes up the week the long block is assembled (early 2027, inside the window brands pick booth vehicles), one episode per build milestone through first fire, then a biweekly 10 to 15 minute build log from first fire to paint, the drive to Vegas as an episode, the reveal as the tentpole. Shorts keep flowing from Buffer the whole time with a Related Videos link back to the latest episode. Podcasts cover the talking part cheaper than a channel ever will; pitch The Diesel Podcast and Ford Era Radio after first fire.

### 1b. What the table says, in five lines

1. **Nobody under 100K grew on build episodes.** TurboRich (build only) stopped at 2K after 24 months. Smiths (weekly, real shop, viral TikTok) is at 3.1K after 19 months with build and shop videos at 190 to 1,300. Binns Boys got to 11.7K on tests and trips; their OBS build episodes run 4 to 24K while their trip videos run 127 to 130K. Dentside's 92K came from takes and history; his partner installs sit at the channel floor.
2. **Series decay is universal and steep.** Hard Up 512K, 267K, 123K, 95K across four weeks with 21 partners and a Paul Walker car. DriveHub 197K to 49K across 14 episodes with two milestone bumps. Travis 235K to 45K. JH Diesel holds median only because each episode is a bet or a stunt.
3. **The reveal is not reliably a pop.** DriveHub's finale did (279K, the second-best of the series); Hard Up's did a fifth of episode one; Travis's drag-race finale did not; Smiths' finale did 1.4K against a 20K episode one. The permanent-record value of the reveal is real; the view value is not something to plan on.
4. **The drive and the failure out-draw the build on every channel that has both.** Travis's drive-home titles 3 to 1 over the full build; Binns Boys' trips 10 to 30 to 1; DriveHub's won't-start Short 20 to 1 over the finale; JH's free truck that "Instantly Blew Up" 285K.
5. **Partner channels are tiny.** Power Hungry Performance 3,690 subs, 36 to 1,300 views on its own long-form; Dirty Diesel Customs' Axiom walkthrough 1,064. A clean install episode is something Rival gives a partner, not something a partner gives Rival.

### 1c. Time to 1K subscribers and to monetization

- **No study measures time to 1K.** vidIQ's 32.9M-channel study (Sep 11, 2026; channels at 0 to 999 subs, 360-day window) measures uploads instead: of channels that added 52 or more videos in the year, 30.77 percent reached 1,000 subscribers; 12 to 51 videos, 8.65 percent; 1 to 11 videos, 1.86 percent; zero, 0.20 percent. Not format-specific, not a random sample. (https://vidiq.com/research/youtube-uploads-1000-subscribers/)
- **What the first ten long-form videos do** (vidIQ, Jul 28, 2026, 137,355 long-form channels created Jan to Jun 2025): median day-30 views 180 on the first upload, 211 on the fifth, 272 on the tenth; best of the first ten, median 1,948. 57.8 percent get one video past 1,000 views inside the first ten; 30.8 percent get one past 10,000. Shorts on the same channels: 1,362 median on the first, flat through the tenth, best-of-ten 8,832. (https://vidiq.com/research/youtube-first-10-videos-study/)
- **In this niche today, three dated starts:** Binns Boys went 0 to 11.7K in 18 months at weekly long-form plus Shorts, with the first two months already at 1K to 30K views a video because the format was "cheap vs expensive" tests, not a build. Smiths Diesel Performance went 0 to 3,130 in 19 months of weekly long-form from a working diesel shop with a TikTok doing 300K-plus on its best clips. TurboRich831 went 0 to about 2K in 24 months of build-only fab footage and then stopped. Nobody in the set grew on build episodes; everybody who grew did it on a question, a test, a take, a drive, or the thing breaking. For Rival, 1,000 subscribers inside the SEMA year is plausible only because the Shorts shelf is being fed daily from the reel pipeline; the long-form will not do it alone.
- **Blog consensus** (third-party, unverified): 6 to 15 months and 50-plus uploads to 1K; the 4,000 watch hours usually arrive after the 1,000 subscribers.

### 1d. YPP thresholds as of Nov 2026, and what they mean for a channel created now

- **Today** (YouTube Help, answer 72851, read 2026-11-21): 1,000 subscribers plus either 4,000 qualified public watch hours in 12 months or 10M qualified Shorts views in 90 days. Fan-funding tier: 500 subscribers, 3 public uploads in 90 days, 3,000 hours or 3M Shorts views. The Help page says updates land "starting February 1, 2027" and that existing partners must accept new terms by Jan 31, 2027.
- **From Feb 1, 2027** (Tubefilter, Aug 10, 2026, Amjad Hanif): new applicants need 8,000 hours in 365 days or 20M Shorts views in 90 days; Shorts ad revenue requires 10M Shorts views in 90 days for everyone; current members are grandfathered. (https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/08/10/youtube-partner-program-ad-eligibility-requirements-shorts/)
- **The arithmetic for Rival:** a channel that posts its first long-form video in early 2027 applies under the 8,000-hour rule. At a 12-minute episode watched to 40 percent (about 4.8 minutes a view), 8,000 hours is 100,000 long-form views in a year. At the vidIQ medians (180 to 272) or TurboRich's 598, that is not happening; at Binns Boys' build-episode band (4K to 24K) it still is not. Ad revenue is not a 2027 outcome. The 500-subscriber fan-funding tier (3,000 hours or 3M Shorts views) is reachable mostly through Shorts and unlocks Shopping and memberships; that stays the side goal named in the crossposting decision.

### 1f. Shorts to long-form: what YouTube itself has said (2025 to 2026) and what the niche shows

- **"Shorts truths," YouTube Team, May 6, 2025:** "not seen evidence of Shorts negatively impacting long-form performance"; separate recommendation systems per format; Shorts "help drive traffic to your long-form content too." No numbers. (https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/debunking-common-myths-about-youtube-shorts/)
- **YouTube Help, Shorts discovery tips:** Shorts performance does not negatively impact long-form recommendations; Shorts help discovery, cannot hurt. (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/11914225)
- **"How to convert YouTube Shorts views into long-form channel growth," Julia Lee Harter, Jul 14, 2026:** the Related Videos link attaches a clickable link on a Short to any long-form video; verbal call to action in the last five seconds; the long video's first 5 to 10 seconds must pay off the Short's promise; no channel graphics or logos up front. No conversion rate published. (https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-related-videos-traffic-guide/)
- **Mohan's 2026 letter, Jan 21, 2026:** Shorts "now averages 200 billion daily views"; YouTube number one in US streaming watch time for nearly three years (Nielsen); brand links in Shorts coming. Nothing on conversion. (https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/the-future-of-youtube-2026/)
- **Made on YouTube, Sep 23, 2026:** Shorts series (seasons, episodes, custom thumbnails, sequential playback, TV) for YPP creators; microdramas 6.5B views H1 2026; 40 percent of microdrama views arrive after day 30. Covered in the crossposting decision, section 2. A new channel is not in YPP, so Shorts series is not available to Rival at launch.
- **Metricool 2026 (via Tubefilter, Aug 3, 2026; 799,718 videos, 71,177 accounts):** 61 percent of all measured views come from the Shorts feed; long-form views up 76 percent year over year, long-form watch time up 11 percent, long-form engagement down 45 percent; recommended cadence 2 to 4 videos a week for maximum long-form views. (https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/08/03/youtube-shorts-long-form-study-metricool/)
- **Third-party "41 percent faster" and "3x faster" mixed-format claims** could not be traced to any YouTube source and are not used here.
- **What the niche shows about direction:** on every build channel pulled, the Short outran the long-form and the long-form did not inherit the Short's audience: DriveHub's "Rare 7.3 Won't Start" 5.76M against a 279K finale; Dentside's 3.5M Short against 1K to 14K installs; Binns Boys' 21K Short against 2K to 8K long-form; Dirty Diesel's 367-view Shorts. Conclusion for Rival: Shorts fed by Buffer will be the door; the long-form is the room a few hundred people walk into. That is the right size for a sponsor archive and the wrong size for a growth plan.

## 2. What sponsors actually want from a YouTube presence for a SEMA build

### 2a. What the builders and brands say (primary quotes)

- **SEMA News builder panel** (sema.org, 2023/22, read today): Hunt (2M-plus YouTube) got his first deal by offering install videos for store credit; "You're not going to get sponsored because you're cool," companies expect "more sales"; "You need to know the sponsor's demographic better than your own." Gabby Downing: "I shifted to promoting myself as a business and not a person." Parts come first, money follows once value is proven (Downing's first cash sponsor "saw what I could do if I had financial assistance"; Kuhnhausen got Radium four years into a five-year build).
- **Dillon Houck, SEMA 2026 giveaway Subaru** (The Shop): NIC Industries (Cerakote, Prismatic Powders) on why they back him: "the time, quality and attention to detail he puts into every video" and "he always does a great job telling the story." LIQUI MOLY: "always willing to bring fresh ideas." Entry mechanic: follow on Instagram or YouTube, both for two entries; reveal on Instagram Live, not YouTube. 40 named entities (28 partners, 5 helpers, 7 local shops). The follow is the deliverable; video quality is the reason they stay.
- **Hard Up Garage, 2 Fast 2 Broken** (The Shop, Oct 19, 2026): four episodes, each premiering on DRIVEN seven days before YouTube, 21 partners, reveal at the Aces Fuel Injection booth Nov 3, 11:30 am. The partner-first window is the pattern: the partner's platform gets the episode before the public one. For Rival the equivalent is the partner kit sent 24 hours before the episode (partner-repostability doc), not a streaming deal.
- **Westen Champlin** (SEMA News 2026, via the series bible): shows unboxing and install in one video; pitches brands himself and "90 percent of the time they're like, yeah, that's awesome."
- **Roadkill Customs, "Get Sponsored and Make it to SEMA"** (OEM-program oriented but the calendar transfers): proposals posted February to March, final proposals often due by May, planning starts in January; bring media partners on board before submitting; keep a log of every piece of coverage and price it against equivalent advertising to show ROI; send photos to every partner so they can track exposure; partnerships run 18 months or more when they work. (https://m.roadkillcustoms.com/how-to-get-sponsors-for-sema/)
- **SEMA show mechanics** (semashow.com, read via search summary): a vehicle gets on the floor as a booth vehicle or a feature vehicle, and a feature-vehicle space has to be sponsored by an exhibiting company first. BUILD.md already notes brands pick booth vehicles six to nine months ahead, so the truck must be pitchable with photos by early 2027.

### 2b. What that means a YouTube presence must contain

1. **A linkable archive, episode by milestone, title carrying the part and the truck.** A brand's marketing manager wants one URL to drop in a dealer email, a product page, or an internal deck that proves the part went on the truck, with the install visible. The Instagram grid cannot be linked that way; the elcoyotediesel.com portal can, but it is text. Episode titles should be searchable nouns: "Driven Diesel regulated return on a 7.3 Powerstroke, 1959 F600 build, Ep 5" beats "RIVAL EP 5."
2. **Install minutes, not logo minutes.** 1of10's baseline sponsor unit is a 60 to 90 second integration; a dedicated video is 2 to 4x that; Westen's rule is unboxing plus install in one piece. In a 12-minute build log the partner's part gets a 3 to 5 minute install segment with the part name said out loud and shown on the bench, and one Short cut from it. That is the "dedicated build episode" Launchpoint prices at $15K to $40K for 500K-plus channels; at Rival's size it is paid in product, but it is the same artifact.
3. **The reveal video as the tentpole, and the live reveal somewhere else.** Houck revealed on Instagram Live; Hard Up revealed at a partner booth and the YouTube reveal did 95K against a 512K episode one; DriveHub's finale was the second-highest long-form of the series (279K). The YouTube reveal is the permanent record the partner links for the next 18 months (Roadkill's "18 months or more when it works"); the live moment belongs to Instagram and the booth, and the view count is not the point.
3a. **The partner-side reality.** Power Hungry Performance's own channel has 3,690 subscribers and does 36 to 1,300 views on long-form; Dirty Diesel Customs' walkthrough with Axiom did 1,064. A 12-minute episode with their part going on a SEMA truck, cut clean, titled with the part name, is content these partners cannot produce for themselves. That is the pitch line: "you get an episode," not "you get exposure."
4. **Numbers brands quote.** Brands and the rate-card sites price on median views of the last 10 videos, not subscribers (1of10: floor = median views x niche CPM / 1,000 + production cost, quote 20 to 30 percent above); average view duration above 50 percent on long-form is the figure cited as "premium" (InfluenceFlow, attributed to YouTube Creator Academy, unverified); engagement 4 to 8 percent is "strong"; perpetual usage rights or 90-day whitelisting add 50 to 100 percent to a rate (Launchpoint, updated Mar 2026). None of that applies until the channel has ten videos; what applies now is the log Roadkill describes, kept from episode one: views, AVD, comments, every repost by a partner.
5. **A coverage log with ad-equivalent pricing.** Keep the spreadsheet from day one; it is what turns 600 views into "a $25 to $50 CPM automotive placement your competitor paid for" in the 2028 renewal conversation.

## 3. The two options, costed in hours

Shared facts for both: the YouTube channel exists either way (Buffer mirrors every reel as a Short, with a searchable noun title, about 1 minute each, per the crossposting decision). All footage already exists because it is shot for reels (Pocket 3 plus phone, mostly vertical; the Pocket 3 shoots 16:9 when rotated). build_reel3.py is hard-coded 1080x1920 (W, H constants at line 43; ASS captions at PlayRes 1080x1920; a landscape-to-portrait crop step), so a 16:9 cut is a fork, not a flag.

### Option A: minimum viable channel from now

Format: a 10 to 15 minute build log, one per two weeks, cut from the fortnight's raw clips with Jacob's voice over it; the SEMA reveal as the tentpole; the Shorts shelf from Buffer; nothing else (no vlogs, no reaction long-form, no podcast of his own).

One-time cost:
- Fork build_reel3 into build_log.py: W, H = 1920, 1080; drop the portrait crop; keep xfade, punch-ins, vidstab, captions (lower third, SemiBold, thin outline, per the reel look rules), audio bed and ducking; add chapter markers from the EDL and a 1280x720 thumbnail export. 4 to 8 hours including a test render.
- Channel dressing (banner, about, playlist "Project Rival, Road to SEMA 2027", end screen template): 1 hour.

Per episode (26 a year):
| Step | Tool | Hours |
|---|---|---|
| Voice: narrate while working during the fortnight (phone voice memos, 2 to 3 min a session) plus one 3-minute wrap-up to camera | phone, Pocket 3 | 0.3 |
| Log and select clips for two weeks (40 to 80 clips) | watch.py | 0.75 to 1.0 |
| Transcribe voice memos for the script order and the caption file | Whisper (existing) | 0.2 (mostly unattended) |
| Write the EDL: order, trims, which clip carries which voice line, partner install segment marked | text | 0.75 |
| Render, review, one fix pass | build_log.py, ffmpeg | 0.5 attended, 0.3 to 0.7 unattended |
| Title (noun first, part and truck named), description with timestamps and partner links, thumbnail from a frame, Related Videos link set on that fortnight's Shorts | YouTube Studio | 0.5 |
| **Total attended** | | **3.0 to 3.5 hours per episode** (6 to 8 for the first) |

Year: 26 x 3.25 = about 85 hours, plus the one-time 5 to 9, plus the reveal video (a dedicated shoot day, licensed music, several cuts: 20 to 40 hours). Roughly 2 hours a week on top of the reels, and a 30-hour block in October 2027.

What it buys: a 26-episode archive by SEMA, every partner part time-stamped on install, a channel with 10 videos before the Feb to March 2027 pitch window closes, a growing long-form watch-time base toward the 500-subscriber fan-funding tier.

What it costs beyond hours: 12 of those 26 episodes land in the block-prep, machining-wait and parts-wait stretch (now through the long block), where the record says the middle of a build decays to 25 to 50 percent of episode one even for 130K channels, and a new channel's median is 180 to 600 views. Calendar cadence forces episodes with no news, which is the exact thing Mat Armstrong's milestone rule and Paddy Galloway's "heavy context is a retention danger zone" warn about.

### Option B: no long-form until the truck rolls

Format: Shorts only via Buffer until the rolling chassis (BUILD.md: first quarter 2027) or first fire; then launch long-form with a backfilled "how we got here" cut from the reel archive and run the build log through body, paint and the reveal.

Cost now: zero beyond Buffer. Cost later: the backfill episode is 10 to 15 hours of archaeology through eight months of 9:16 clips with no voice recorded at the time, and it lands in spring 2027, the same weeks as bodywork, the interior shop hand-off and the booth-vehicle paperwork. The channel is 0 long-form videos old when brands evaluate booth vehicles (Feb to March 2027), so the pitch link is the portal and the Instagram grid only. No Related Videos targets exist for the Shorts in the months when the Shorts are the only thing posting, so the door opens onto an empty room.

What it buys: nothing is published in the dead months, so the channel's first long-form impression is engine-in or first fire, the two moments the record says pop (DriveHub PT7 at a milestone, 269K; Travis's "will it run" at video 6, 111K; Rival's own rule 24, milestones go out raw within the hour).

### The hybrid that the data actually supports (recommended): milestone-keyed, then biweekly

Long-form is posted when the build has news, not when the calendar says so, until first fire; then biweekly. That keeps Option A's archive and pitch-window benefit and Option B's refusal to publish episodes with no news.

Kill rule: if after six long-form episodes the median is under 300 views, average view duration under 35 percent, and no partner has linked or reposted an episode, drop back to milestone-only and keep the reveal.

Start date: pipeline work in a slow week before the end of 2026; Ep 1 the week the long block is assembled (early 2027); do not post a trailer, an introduction or a lockup video first (none of the first-30 studies found one that worked; DriveHub's trailer did 1.2K on a 130K channel).

## 5. Recommendation

Create the channel now because Buffer needs it, dress it once, and build the 16:9 fork in a slow week before January. Do not post long-form until there is news. Ep 1 is the assembled long block, early 2027, inside the booth-vehicle window; Eps 2 to 4 are frame rolls, engine in, first fire; biweekly 10 to 15 minute build logs from first fire through paint; the drive to Vegas and the reveal are the two tentpoles. Shorts carry the reach and point back with Related Videos links. Budget two hours a week after first fire, under one before. Treat views as irrelevant and the archive as the product: every partner gets a URL with their part going on, titled with the part and the truck. Keep the coverage log from episode one. Pitch The Diesel Podcast and Ford Era Radio the week after first fire. Revisit the whole thing at six episodes with the kill rule.
