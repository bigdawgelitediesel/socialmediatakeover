# AI-era editing tools and local equivalents for the Rival reels
Research date 2026-11-20. Lane: tools that raise production value without faking anything (clean, track, reframe, caption, smooth, sharpen). No generated b-roll, no synthetic voice.

Machine this was judged against: i5-14500 (20 threads), 32 GB, Intel UHD 770 iGPU, no discrete GPU, Windows 11. Stack already present: ffmpeg 9.0.2 full (arnndn, afftdn, minterpolate, vidstab, QuickSync all compiled in), Python 3.12 with faster-whisper 1.2.1, ctranslate2 4.9, onnxruntime 1.32, opencv 5.1, scenedetect 0.7.3, auto-editor 29.3.1. NOT present: torch, ultralytics, open_clip, mediapipe, demucs, deepfilternet.

Footage reality check (probed the Drive inbox today): 26 iPhone HEVC 1920x1080 30 fps landscape clips, 4 s to 9 min; one iPhone 1080x1920 vertical; three DJI Pocket 3 clips that are ALREADY vertical: 1728x3072 at 59.94 fps (two) and 1080x1920 at 29.97. So the "16:9 4K60 to 9:16" problem is mostly an iPhone problem, and the DJI 3K60 vertical files are a gift for slow-mo and punch-ins (1.6x of pixel headroom over 1080x1920).

Numbers marked MEASURED were timed on this machine today on a 4 s 1080p30 segment of IMG_4191.MOV and 30 s of IMG_4196.MOV audio. Everything else is from the cited sources and should be treated as indicative.

## 1. Dialogue cleanup (pulling voice out of impact guns and compressors)

Verdict order for this shop: DeepFilterNet3 first, ffmpeg arnndn as the always-on cheap pass, Adobe Enhance Speech free tier as the occasional "hero clip" tool, Demucs/UVR only as an experiment.

DeepFilterNet3 (Rikorose). Best quality of the open options. On VoiceBank-DEMAND, PESQ 3.16 offline vs 2.56 for Demucs dns64 and 2.11 for RNNoise; it also led STOI and DNSMOS (https://github.com/ImAvgErix/ExoLauncher/pull/5). A 2025 study on conferencing speech agreed: best intelligibility and quality, RNNoise faster (https://publikasi.mercubuana.ac.id/index.php/sinergi/article/download/21917/9619). CPU real-time factor around 0.19 per one write-up, so a 60 s clip is roughly 12 s (https://noisereducerai.com/blogs/deepfilternet-vs-rnnoise/). Latest upstream release v0.5.6, supports Windows, 48 kHz mono wav in, pre-built deep-filter binary on the release page (https://github.com/Rikorose/DeepFilterNet/releases). Install on Windows: the deep-filter-*-windows.exe from the releases page (no Python), or `pip install deepfilternet-rs` (cp312 win-amd64 wheels around the official Rust runtime, converts input to 48 kHz mono itself, https://pypi.org/project/deepfilternet-rs/). MIT/Apache dual licence. Caveat for shop audio: it is a speech enhancer, so under a running impact gun it will suppress the gun and may pump; keep a 20 to 40 percent dry mix of the original under it so the room still sounds like a shop.

ffmpeg arnndn (RNNoise). Already compiled in. Models from GregorR/rnnoise-models: somnolent-hogwash (speech signal, recording noise) is the standard speech pick; beguiling-drafter is "voice incl. laughter, recording noise"; the README says the names are not meaningful and the model files are not copyrightable (https://github.com/GregorR/rnnoise-models). MEASURED: 0.25 s for 30 s of audio with sh.rnnn, 0.25 s (both about 120x real time) with bd.rnnn, and afftdn 0.1 s. Quality: much weaker than DFN3 on broadband noise; a 2026 ffmpeg-vs-DFN test found arnndn helped "a little" on keyboard and dog noise (https://www.creativeainews.com/articles/deepfilternet-vs-rnnoise-vs-ffmpeg-noise-removal-2026/). Use `arnndn=m=sh.rnnn:mix=0.7` as the free default inside build_reel2 and escalate to DFN3 when there is real dialogue. Model files are cached at the scratchpad path dl/rnnoise/sh.rnnn and bd.rnnn; copy them into VIDEO/tools/models.

Adobe Podcast Enhance Speech. Still the best-sounding single button for voice. Free tier is audio only, 30 min per file, 1 hour per day; Premium stopped being sold standalone on Aug 30 2026 and moved into Firefly Pro (https://podcast.adobe.com/en/plans, https://helpx.adobe.com/il_en/podcast/adobe-podcast-premium-plan-change-faq.html). Workflow cost is manual upload and re-sync, so reserve it for talking segments that carry a reel. Local look-alike resemble-enhance (MIT, denoise + enhance modules) exists but needs torch and nobody publishes CPU timings (https://github.com/resemble-ai/resemble-enhance); skip for now.

Demucs / UVR. Music separators, not speech enhancers. htdemucs on CPU is anywhere from 0.3 to 4 minutes per minute of audio depending on who you believe (https://stemsplit.io/blog/demucs-local-setup-guide.md). One experiment worth running: impact-gun hits land in the "drums" stem, so vocals plus reduced "other" minus drums might beat a denoiser on the worst clips. UVR on CPU is "much slower" and undocumented for dialogue (https://github.com/anjok07/ultimatevocalremovergui). Cloud: Replicate cjwbw/demucs, T4, about $0.014 per run (https://replicate.com/cjwbw/demucs).

## 2. Frame interpolation and slow motion

Verdict: shoot slow-mo on the DJI at 60 fps and conform; for iPhone 30 fps use RIFE through the ncnn-Vulkan build on the iGPU for 2x (measured: same speed class as minterpolate, much cleaner picture); keep ffmpeg minterpolate as the zero-setup fallback already wired into build_reel2. Budget about 30 s of render per second of 1080p slow-mo either way, so slow only the shot, never the clip.

ffmpeg minterpolate. MEASURED: 54.5 s (4.4 output fps, about 14x slower than real time) to turn 4 s of 1080p30 into 60 fps (240 output frames), and 127.6 s for 16 s of output (3.8 fps; a 10 s hero shot slowed 4x is a 5 minute render) for a 4x slow-mo conform (480 synthesized frames). It is effectively single-threaded in mci mode and users report multi-day renders on long files (https://lists.ffmpeg.org/pipermail/ffmpeg-user/2021-January/051610.html). Artifacts: block ghosting at object edges and smearing on fast or complex motion; `scd=fdiff` protects cuts (https://redeyeautomations.com/notes/rife-vs-minterpolate).

RIFE via rife-ncnn-vulkan (nihui). Runs on any Vulkan GPU including Intel iGPUs; Windows zip, no install, ships models up to rife-v4.6 (https://github.com/nihui/rife-ncnn-vulkan/releases). MEASURED on the UHD 770: 28.3 s including PNG read/write for 2x on 60 frames of 1080p (so about 2.1 new frames per second, 4.2 output fps, roughly the same throughput as minterpolate but with far cleaner edges), and 68.8 s, so the iGPU is 2.4x faster than the CPU path and should always be used with `-g -1` on CPU. Practical-RIFE (hzwer) is the PyTorch line with newer models (4.25 recommended, 4.26 latest, MIT) but has no Windows CPU story worth the install (https://github.com/hzwer/Practical-RIFE). Video2X 6.4.0 wraps Real-ESRGAN plus RIFE 4.25/4.26 ncnn-Vulkan in a Qt GUI and Windows installer if a GUI is ever wanted (https://github.com/k4yt3x/video2x). Artifact profile: RIFE is cleaner than minterpolate at edges but still tears on very fast motion; nobody has published a smoke or steam test, and both tools will smear semi-transparent volumes, so do not interpolate exhaust, weld smoke or steam shots; cut those at native speed. Practical recipe used in the test: `ffmpeg -i in.mp4 fr/%04d.png`, `rife-ncnn-vulkan -i fr -o out -m rife-v4.6 -g 0 -j 2:2:2`, `ffmpeg -framerate 60 -i out/%08d.png -c:v libx264 -crf 16 out.mp4`; use 30 fps on the re-mux for 2x slow motion. The ncnn build reports fp16 and int8 support on the UHD 770. Binary extracted at the scratchpad path dl/rife/x/rife-ncnn-vulkan-20221029-windows; copy to VIDEO/tools/bin.

Cloud: Replicate pollinations/rife-video-interpolation is listed at about $0.037 per run but is currently unrunnable (deprecated Cog); Google FILM runs on a T4 for about $0.015 per run (https://replicate.com/pollinations/rife-video-interpolation, https://replicate.com/google-research/frame-interpolation). Not needed if the local Vulkan path works.

## 3. Upscaling and sharpening

Verdict: not worth it for a native 1080x1920 deliverable. It IS worth it for the specific case of a 9:16 crop out of iPhone 16:9 1080p, because that crop is only 608 px wide and gets stretched 1.78x to 1080.

Real-ESRGAN ncnn-Vulkan. Windows portable zip, runs on the iGPU; models realesr-animevideov3 (fast, soft) and realesrgan-x4plus (slow, sharp) (https://github.com/xinntao/Real-ESRGAN/releases). MEASURED on the UHD 770: 22.2 s for animevideov3 x2 on 5 frames of 1080p (4.4 s per frame), 268.6 s, 134 s per frame, unusable for x4plus on 2 frames. At 4.4 s per 1080p frame a 10 s shot is 22 minutes and a 608x1080 crop (one third of the pixels) is still about 7 minutes per 10 s, so this is a hero-shot-only tool run while doing something else, never a pipeline stage. Per-frame SR flickers on motion unless followed by a temporal pass (https://www.forasoft.com/learn/ai-for-video-engineering/articles-ai/real-esrgan-basicvsr-ott-archive-upscaling). CPU-only PyTorch builds take "hours per minute" (https://backendside.com/blog/2026/10/18/ai-video-upscaling-enhance-low-resolution-videos-with-real-esrgan-4x-on-windows/). Instagram re-encodes everything to roughly 1080x1920 at a few Mbps, so added detail above 1080 is thrown away; what survives is edge contrast. Cheap substitute that survives the compressor: `scale=1080:1920:flags=lanczos,unsharp=5:5:0.6:5:5:0.0` or `cas=0.4` in ffmpeg, plus exporting at 15 to 20 Mbps so IG's encoder has clean input.

Topaz Video is now a $299 per year subscription (perpetual Video AI discontinued) and a GPU app with a history of Intel iGPU problems (https://novoads.ai/en/blog/topaz-video-ai-pricing, https://community.topazlabs.com/t/very-slow-performance-on-topaz-photo-ai-and-video-enhance-ai-on-windows-with-intel-gpu/40973/4). If an upscale is ever needed, fal.ai's Topaz endpoint is $0.02 per output second at 1080p (about $0.60 for a 30 s reel) and Replicate lucataco/real-esrgan-video is about $0.20 per run on an A100 (https://fal.ai/models/fal-ai/topaz/upscale/video, https://replicate.com/lucataco/real-esrgan-video). Only for the one 720p clip (IMG_9679) or old phone footage.

## 4. Subject tracking and auto reframe (16:9 to 9:16)

Verdict: build it; this is the biggest visible upgrade for the iPhone landscape clips. Use YOLO11n person/hand detection on sampled frames, a Gaussian-smoothed crop path, and let ffmpeg do the crop via sendcmd so frames never round-trip through Python.

Reference implementation: idopinto/ai-video-reframing (MIT). YOLO11m plus BoT-SORT ReID every third frame, YuNet face detector, picks one track per clip by confidence x area, frames on body centre offset toward the face, Gaussian smoothing with sigma of one third of a second, eases back to centre over 2 s when the subject leaves, holds at the median when the subject barely moves, and renders with a single ffmpeg crop driven by a sendcmd script (https://github.com/idopinto/ai-video-reframing). Simpler CLIs: kamilstanuch/Autocrop-vertical and KazKozDev/auto-vertical-reframe (https://github.com/topics/auto-reframe-video). Install: `pip install ultralytics openvino` (Ultralytics is AGPL-3.0, fine for our own renders, not for a redistributed product). Speed: YOLO11n at 640 px on this CPU is roughly 30 to 60 ms per frame; export to OpenVINO (`model.export(format="openvino")`) and run `device="intel:gpu"` for the iGPU, which Ultralytics documents at up to 3x on CPU and faster again on Intel GPUs (https://docs.ultralytics.com/integrations/openvino, https://www.ultralytics.com/blog/seamlessly-deploy-ultralytics-yolo11-using-openvino-tm). Sampling every 3rd frame on a 10 s clip is about 100 inferences, well under 10 s. Smart punch-ins that follow hands: detect `person` box plus hand keypoints from the yolo11n-pose model, and when the hand box is stable for more than 1 s, key a 1.2x crop centred on it; build_reel2 already supports `zoom`, so the tracker only needs to emit the crop expression. Shoot-side rule that removes the problem: keep filming the DJI vertical (it already is) and hold the iPhone vertical for talking shots; reframe only b-roll.

CapCut Auto Reframe is a Pro feature in 2026 per the third-party guides (https://fluxnote.io/guides/capcut-ai-features-free-vs-paid-2026); the local path is free and scriptable.

## 5. Auto captions with word timing

Verdict: keep faster-whisper as the engine, add `hotwords` and a post-pass dictionary now, add WhisperX alignment only if word-highlight karaoke timing looks off.

faster-whisper. `transcribe()` has `hotwords` ("hint phrases to provide the model with"), `initial_prompt` (first window only), `word_timestamps`, `vad_filter`; `BatchedInferencePipeline.transcribe` also takes `hotwords` and defaults VAD on (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SYSTRAN/faster-whisper/master/faster_whisper/transcribe.py). The project's CPU table on an i7-12700K: small int8 1m42s for the 13 min test file, 51 s with batch_size=8 (https://github.com/SYSTRAN/faster-whisper). MEASURED here on 30 s of shop audio (IMG_4196, Jacob talking over a parts washer), small int8, beam 5, VAD on: 6.8 s without hotwords and 8.9 s with hotwords "7.3 Powerstroke, F600, Project Rival, rocker arms, El Coyote, Cheyenne, turbo, injectors" (about 4x real time either way, model already warm). Both transcripts were word-for-word correct on the speech; the hotword run only changed casing and dropped punctuation (and it kept "pallet" vs "palette", a reminder that hotwords do not fix homophones). Word times matched across runs to within 0.1 s. So the hotword cost is about 30 percent more time and it is only worth paying on clips where part names are actually spoken. Hotwords are a soft bias and can collide with similar-sounding words, so keep the list short and domain-specific (https://discuss.huggingface.co/t/adding-custom-vocabularies-on-whisper/29311). Add a deterministic fixer after transcription: regex map for "seven three|7 3|seven point three" to "7.3", "F six hundred|F 600" to "F600", "rocker arm" variants, "Rival", "El Coyote", "Cummins", "Powerstroke"; the earlier Harley mishear shows the medium model on a 16 kHz mono cut is the escalation path. Word timing from faster-whisper is usually inside 200 ms and drifts after silence (https://whipscribe.com/blog/word-level-timestamps-whisper-2026), which is visible on karaoke highlight.

WhisperX. Same decoder (faster-whisper) plus wav2vec2 forced alignment to tens of milliseconds, plus pyannote diarization with an HF token for speaker-aware captions (https://raw.githubusercontent.com/m-bain/whisperX/main/README.md). Install on CPU: `pip install torch --index-url https://download.pytorch.org/whl/cpu` first, then `pip install whisperx` (v3.8.x, 2026 release added `condition_on_previous_context`), run with `--compute_type int8 --device cpu`; `pip install "whisperx[fast]"` speeds alignment. BSD-2. Alignment on CPU is fast relative to decoding. Speaker-aware captions (two colours for Jacob vs a tech) need the pyannote model agreement; the cheaper first step is manual speaker tags in the EDL.

## 6. Stabilization beyond vidstab

Verdict: nothing to add locally. Gyroflow needs gyro logs recorded with the video; the iPhone Camera app does not write them and Gyroflow will not work when in-camera stabilization was on (https://docs.gyroflow.xyz/app/getting-started/supported-cameras/mobile-phones). The Pocket 3 is a mechanical gimbal and is not on Gyroflow's DJI list (Avata, O3/O4, Action 2 to 6, Neo); v1.6.x releases added Insta360 Go Ultra and ProRes RAW, nothing for Pocket 3 (https://docs.gyroflow.xyz/app/readme/features, https://github.com/gyroflow/gyroflow/releases). MEASURED: two-pass vidstab on 4 s took 1.9 s (about half real time, two passes), cheap enough to leave on for handheld iPhone b-roll. CapCut desktop Stabilize is free (Recommended / Minimum cut / Most stable) and is the right tool when Jacob is already in CapCut (https://www.capcut.com/resource/capcut-stabilizer). For the "low level editing" complaint: stabilised plus a slight punch-in reads as deliberate; raw handheld reads as phone footage.

## 7. Scene understanding for auto-editing

Verdict: assemble from parts we have (scenedetect, auto-editor, ffmpeg signal stats, YOLO from section 4) and add an open_clip ranker as a Python module; nothing off the shelf fits a shop-floor build log.

auto-editor already supports motion-based editing and boolean combos: `--edit motion:threshold=0.02`, `--edit "(or audio:0.03 motion:0.06)"`, `--margin 0.3s,1.5sec`, and `--export premiere` or `resolve` for timelines (https://github.com/WyattBlue/auto-editor). Use it to pre-trim dead air and idle shots before shot picking.

Best-moment scoring per shot: audio RMS and spectral flux from ffmpeg `astats`/`ebur128`, motion energy from frame differencing (opencv already present), person/hand presence from YOLO, and a CLIP prompt score. The DEV tutorial pattern is the right shape: open_clip ViT-B-32, score = mean similarity to positive prompts ("mechanic working on a diesel engine, hands on a wrench, turbo close-up, sparks, truck frame") minus mean similarity to negatives ("blurry, floor, empty bay, back of head"), which normalises lighting (https://dev.to/masonwritescode/pick-a-better-video-thumbnail-automatically-with-ffmpeg-pyscenedetect-and-clip-4gpk). Videogenic shows the same CLIP prior sliding over frame scores to pick highlight intervals (https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.12493v1). ViT-B-32 on CPU is about 50 to 100 ms per frame; one frame per shot is nothing. Install `pip install open_clip_torch` after the CPU torch wheel.

Open-source Opus-style clippers (supoclip, hi-lite, autoclip, VideoHighlighter) are transcript-plus-LLM rankers for podcasts and streams; none do visual ranking in a free tier (https://github.com/FujiwaraChoki/supoclip, https://github.com/Aseiel/VideoHighlighter). Their one reusable idea, a per-clip hook/engagement score, we get by having Claude score candidate segments from the transcript plus the watch.py frame sheets.

## 8. CapCut desktop, late 2026 (the fallback Jacob already uses)

Prices: Free, Standard about $9.99/mo (mostly mobile), Pro $19.99/mo or $179.99/yr; region dependent (https://bigvu.tv/blog/capcut-free-vs-pro-what-2026s-restructure-actually-gives-you/, https://capcut.com/resource/capcut-standard-vs-pro). Free: full timeline, keyframes, chroma key, speed ramp, Stabilize, basic auto-cut, 1080p export; Pro-tagged templates and effects watermark on free exports. Pro: Vocal Isolation, Camera Tracking, Remove Flickers, speaker-ID captions, Auto Reframe, 4K export, 100 GB cloud (https://vidpros.com/capcut-pro-vs-free/, https://fluxnote.io/guides/capcut-ai-features-free-vs-paid-2026). Auto captions: sources conflict (one says free with monthly limits, CapCut's own article says Pro); treat as metered. EditPilot is a PC-only conversational assistant that does filler-word cleanup, rough trims, captions, voice enhancement and noise reduction on the timeline; CapCut does not say whether it is metered (https://www.capcut.com/tools/edit-pilot). Smart Cutout is "Auto Cutout" in current builds; basic background removal is free, advanced is Pro. Color Match tier is unconfirmed. Cloud editors from the YouTube transcript lane (Opus Clip $15 to $29/mo, Submagic $19 plus $19 add-on, Veed $20 to $50, Captions $10 to $25) sell face-tracked reframe, speech enhancement, word captions and "magic zooms" (sections 1, 4, 5, 7 here, free) bundled with AI b-roll, AI hooks and eye-contact fixes that are off the table for this audience. ## IMPLEMENT IN OUR STACK (order of operations per clip)

0. Ingest: watch.py as today (shots, sheets, transcript). Add `auto-editor --edit "(or audio:-22dB motion:0.04)" --margin 0.3s,0.8s --export json` to mark dead air and idle shots as candidates to skip.
1. Audio first, from the ORIGINAL file (never after video processing): extract 48 kHz mono, run DeepFilterNet3 (deep-filter.exe) for talking clips or `arnndn=m=sh.rnnn:mix=0.7` for b-roll, then blend 70 percent cleaned with 30 percent dry, then the existing loudnorm to -14 LUFS. Keep the dry track for the GUIDE render.
2. Stabilize (optional, per-slot `stab` as today) on the source resolution before any crop, so vidstab has the full frame to borrow from.
3. Reframe to 9:16: run the YOLO/OpenVINO tracker on every 3rd frame, Gaussian-smooth the x path (sigma about 10 frames), write a sendcmd crop script, crop at source resolution. For the DJI 1728x3072 files just scale; for iPhone 16:9 crop 608x1080 then scale 1080x1920 with lanczos and `cas=0.35` (Real-ESRGAN animevideov3 x2 on the crop only for a 3 to 5 s hero shot, about 2 to 3 minutes of iGPU time, and only if the lanczos+CAS version looks soft).
4. Speed changes: 60 fps DJI slow-mo is a straight `setpts`; iPhone 30 fps slow-mo under 2x goes through rife-ncnn-vulkan (png in, png out, re-mux), minterpolate only if Vulkan fails. Never interpolate smoke, steam, sparks, or whip pans.
5. Punch-ins and transitions as build_reel2 does now (`zoom`, `xfade`), after reframing so the crop expressions are in 1080x1920 space.
6. Captions: faster-whisper small int8 with hotwords on the cleaned audio, dictionary fixer, medium model re-run on any line that matters, optional WhisperX align for karaoke; burn ASS as today.
7. Grade and export: existing warm/punch grade, then `-c:v h264_qsv` or libx264 at 16 to 20 Mbps, 1080x1920, 30 fps (or 60 if the whole reel is 60), AAC 192k. Upscaling to 4K for IG is wasted.

## TOP 5 that would most change how the reels look and sound

1. DeepFilterNet3 dialogue pass with a dry blend. Shop noise under speech is the single biggest "phone video" tell; this is a 12 s render per minute and free. Pair with arnndn as the default on everything.
2. Tracked 9:16 reframe with smoothed crop path and hand-following punch-ins. Turns the 26 landscape iPhone clips into usable vertical footage without letterbox or static centre crops, and gives the deliberate camera moves that read as "edited."
3. Word-accurate captions with a part-name dictionary and hotwords (then WhisperX if karaoke timing drifts). "7.3", "F600", "rocker arms" spelled right every time is the cheapest credibility win.
4. RIFE on the iGPU for clean 2x slow-mo of iPhone 30 fps (same render time as minterpolate, measured, but without the block ghosting), and shooting the DJI at 60 for everything that might be slowed.
5. Sharpen-not-upscale: lanczos plus CAS on crops, 16 to 20 Mbps exports, and Real-ESRGAN only for sub-1080 sources. Keeps edges crisp through Instagram's encoder without the Topaz tax.

Everything in the top 5 runs on this machine with no GPU purchase; the only paid items in this report are optional (Adobe free tier, under $1.50 per reel in cloud fallbacks, CapCut Pro $19.99 if Jacob wants Vocal Isolation inside CapCut instead of our pass).
