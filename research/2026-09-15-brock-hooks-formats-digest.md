# 1. SHARES — the whole ballgame for Jake

**Why this section is first:** shares/reach 0.045% is his worst metric, and this material's entire causal model is *shares → disconnected reach → new followers*. His reach is fine (45,641/30d); his conversion is not. Shares are the mechanism Brock claims fixes conversion, because a shared post is pre-qualified — it arrives from a human who thought of a specific other human.

## 1.1 The engagement→views ladder (`pWNDkFuV0RY`) — and why the number is unreliable

> "One additional like ≈ **15** additional views. One save ≈ **45**. One comment ≈ **75**. One share ≈ **150**. And some studies indicate one share ≈ **400**." — attributed to "a recent InstaClub study" **[E-ish, unpublished]**

⚠ **This number is unstable across his own videos**: 150 (`pWNDkFuV0RY`), "over 250" (`WIgXSoQSZMs`), 400 (cited in both, and 400 already flagged as suspect in §2.6 of the existing digest). Three different values, one alleged study. **Treat the ordering (share > comment > save > like) as the usable claim; treat the multipliers as marketing copy.** Do not build a forecast on any of them.

The ordering claim *is* corroborated by something real: Mosseri publicly uses **"sends per reach"** as a ranking signal, which Brock cites correctly **[E]**. Note the shape of that signal — it's a *ratio*, which is exactly why a fixed "one share = N views" multiplier can't be literally true.

## 1.2 The five kinds of shareworthy content (`pWNDkFuV0RY`) — the core of the set

All five must be **feed posts** (reel/photo/carousel — "rarely do stories get shared") and all five must be **on-niche**.

**1. Motivational / inspirational.** "Motivation is like food. We need it daily." Highest-frequency, lowest-production type. He insists on zero production value: "white background, black text… the focus is on the value that they provide, not the vanity." Requires empathy for the *specific* audience — "the way you motivate a 23-year-old guy in sales is very different than a 67-year-old grandmother."
→ **Jake:** this is the cheapest weekly post he has and he almost certainly isn't making it. Black screen, white text, aimed at one person: the guy who works, tows, and can't afford downtime. *"Your truck doesn't care that you're tired. Neither does Monday."* / *"Nobody's coming to fix it. That's why you learned."* Shot on a phone at the counter in 90 seconds.

**2. Call-to-arms / community.** Hardest to make, rarest, highest ceiling. ALS Ice Bucket Challenge as the archetype — and his read on why it worked is the useful part: **"tag three friends" is a share instruction wearing a costume.** His own repeatable example: a photo of a flattened receipt itemizing that supporting small businesses costs $0 — "every single time I post it, it goes viral." Note the craft detail: it *looks like a real-world object*, not text on a screen.
The cheat version he gives: **the phrase "I need your help."** His experiment reel opened with "I'm doing an Instagram experiment and I need your help" — went viral on shares that were almost all sent back to him.
→ **Jake:** a "receipt" prop is trivially on-brand for a parts guy — an actual parts-counter receipt, itemized, as the visual. And "I need your help" is a hook he can use honestly: *"Help me settle this. I need 100 of you to tell me what you actually run."*

**3. Relatable humor.** Highest-frequency, lowest burnout, easiest to conceive. **"Relatability comes from specificity"** — the most-repeated line in the entire set (appears in 4 of the 18). The vanilla-ice-cream argument: almost nobody loves or hates vanilla; 95% feel nothing. "Stop making your content vanilla."
Worked contrast he gives: "me trying to grow a business on Instagram" (vanilla) vs "me having to do 17 different things at once to grow my small business and keep up with all the algorithm updates, meanwhile my reel only got seven likes" (shares).
→ **Jake:** not "diesels are expensive." Instead the exact, dated, numbered specific: the 5:40am −14° no-start; the customer who wanted a part for a truck he described only as "the white one"; the $9 clip that's on national backorder. **This directly matches his best-ever post (15.46%, first-person, personal).**

**4. Controversial.** "Say the thing that everyone else in your niche has been thinking, but no one has been bold enough to proclaim." Explicitly **not** attacking people or businesses — attacking *ideas, strategies, schools of thought*. The share mechanism: "it's a way that they can say 'I believe this' without being brave enough to say it themselves."
His guardrail **[O, and a good one]**: only post opinions that are **hills you'd die on**, because "you might post it and then really change your mind and then you've made half your audience angry for no reason." He also reframes the unfollows as a filter: "rarely is someone about to swipe their credit card and then they press unfollow."
→ **Jake:** this is where the repo's §2.1 candidate list belongs (dealership upsell, emissions-as-a-tax-on-working-people, "most truck guys online have never worked"). Pick only the ones he actually believes.

**5. Timely.** Three sub-types: (a) **breaking news** — "arguably the most shareworthy content on all of social media," and you can either break it *or* be first to relate it to your niche; (b) **pop culture / NAP — Niche Adjacent Post**; (c) **trending audio** (he's lukewarm — see contradiction §8.3).
The delay play he gives is important for a guy with a full-time job: if you miss the first few hours, don't skip it — **go a layer deeper.** His example: everyone covered the Taylor/Travis engagement in hour one; the florist covers the *floral arrangements* on day three, the jeweler covers *the ring*.
→ **Jake:** a Ford TSB or recall, an emissions rule, a fuel-price move, a new truck launch, a viral towing fail. His day-three angle is the differentiator: *"Everyone's talking about the recall. Here's the actual part number and what it costs at my counter."* Green-screen reaction is ~3 minutes of work.

## 1.3 What's explicitly OFF the list: education

> "What was left off this list was educational tips… Think about your local library. There are thousands of textbooks that are super valuable but have been sitting on the shelf for a decade. No one reading them, and definitely no one recommending that textbook to a friend."

Mechanism given: **education gets saved, not sent** — and saves are the weakest signal. "Instagram has learned over 5–10 years that saves are just not a good predictor of how long someone's going to spend on the platform." He caps pure education at **"20% max — one out of every five feed posts"** **[O]**.

→ **This is the load-bearing strategic claim for Jake and it cuts against instinct.** A parts manager's natural content is education. The material says education builds trust and credibility but does not get shared, and shares are the growth engine. Practical resolution: keep the expertise, change the *wrapper* — the tip rides inside a relatable/controversial/timely frame rather than being served as a tip. (See §8.2 — he contradicts himself on this.)

## 1.4 Ask for the share — templates matched to content type

"The most basic tip… it's funny how many people don't do this." But **never just "share this"** — always give the reason. His five, one per content type:

| Content type | CTA, verbatim |
|---|---|
| Controversial | "Share this post **if you agree**" |
| Relatable | "Share this post **if you can relate**" |
| Timely | "Share this post **if you learned something new**" |
| Call-to-arms | "Share this post **help spread the message**" |
| Motivational | "Share this post **if you're feeling inspired**" |

**Placement, ranked:** (1) in the reel itself — spoken or on-screen text; (2) caption, only if it doesn't fit naturally in the reel. He is emphatic elsewhere (`SxQVNxw5gwY`) that **burying the CTA at the bottom of a long caption is one of the most common mistakes** — "we can't hope people discover it like some secret treasure."

This adds a layer to the existing digest §2.2: the **who + why** formula is already captured there; what's new here is the **type-matched why**, which is a lookup table Jake can use without thinking.

## 1.5 The five craft tips for shareworthy posts

1. **Hook first** — spend the majority of your effort there.
2. **Tell them to share + why** (above).
3. **Success leaves clues — but look OUTSIDE your niche.** "Don't look at what other competitors are creating. Look at different verticals. Find the content that you're sending to your spouse, that your friend sent you, that your colleague dropped in the work group chat. That content is shareworthy — it's proven, because it's already been shared." Then port the *structure* into your niche. **[O, but the single most useful research instruction in the set.]** → Jake should keep a folder of everything he personally DMs to his buddies, regardless of topic, and reverse-engineer the format.
4. **The miniskirt rule.** "Long enough to cover what you need to cover, still short enough to hold attention." No universal length. Anti-padding rule: don't say the same thing three ways.
5. **The 2-second rule.** From TV/cinema: "there should be some edit, change, transition, or something happening every 2 seconds." → This is the most likely single fix for Jake's **6.8s vs 8.5s watch time**. It doesn't require more filming, only more cuts and more visual change per second. ⚠ but see §8.4 — it contradicts his "don't edit at all" advice.

## 1.6 Other share levers scattered through the set

- **Reposts** (`XPdfSb1umG4`): a repost tab/button now exists. "The posts that will be most repostable are also the posts that are most shareworthy." Adds a second surface for the same content type; no new work required.
- **Collabs** (`Qr2rHFP4-G4`): "Collaborations represent **less than 3%** of all posts on Instagram, and yet collab posts are consistently the best performing kind of post. The best kind of post is not a reel, not a carousel, not a story — it's a collab." **[E-ish]** Criteria: similar niche + **similar average engagement** (follower count irrelevant). → **Highest-ROI underused tactic on this list for a 215-follower account.** He should be looking for other small diesel/truck creators with ~200–2,000 followers and similar view counts, not sponsors. This is also the one tactic that doesn't require him to out-produce anyone.
- **In-group language drives shares but must stay out of the hook** — already resolved in existing digest §2.5.

# 2. The 7 types of reels the algorithm loves (`XPdfSb1umG4`)

Framing he gives up front: five of the seven are share mechanisms, two are retention mechanisms. Summary line at the end: "you need **shares, likes, and high watch time**."

**1. Controversial reels.** Twin engine: agreers share ("finally someone said it"), disagreers comment. Format he demonstrates is a 3-shot reel, ~10 seconds: clip 1 "unpopular opinion," clip 2 the opinion, clip 3 "share this if you agree." Guardrail: on-niche + hill-you'd-die-on.
→ *Diesel version:* "Unpopular opinion: your 6.7 doesn't need a tune, it needs a fuel filter on schedule." / "Unpopular opinion: the guy with the loudest truck at Cars & Coffee has never pulled a trailer." Counter (~8 min): film once, three takes, no edit.

**2. Relatable humor.** Memes, movie clips, trending sound bites bent to the niche. Specificity is the whole trick (§1.2 above).
→ *Diesel version:* the counter is a goldmine — "POV: he says it's for a Ford and he's holding a Cummins filter." Uses a location he's in 40 hours a week. **Most viable format he has.**

**3. Plot-twist reels.** Surprise ending / contradicted expectation. Why it works: retention *and* shares ("we want our friend to have the same oh-my-gosh reaction").
Two sub-formats: (a) **"Put a finger down if…"** — evolved from one-liners into long specific stories with a twist, put a finger down at the end to confirm it happened to you; (b) **hook swaps / transitional hooks** ⚠ see §7.
→ *Diesel version:* "Put a finger down if a customer has ever described his truck by its color." Long, specific, true. Very high viability.

**4. Timely content.** Same three sub-types as §1.2.5 above; "breaking news is arguably the most shareworthy content on all of social media." NAP explained at length.
→ *Diesel version:* TSBs, recalls, EPA rulings, fuel prices, a new Super Duty launch, a viral towing fail. **NAP for diesel:** a Yellowstone/football/Super Bowl moment bent to trucks; any big-truck moment in the news bent to pickups. Semi-parts knowledge is the natural NAP bridge — he can borrow attention from Class 8 topics into pickup content and back.

**5. Series.** ⚠ His advice here **directly contradicts** `p5XTBTLeM7c` and `ElEhpmx5lnY` — see §8.1. Here: *never* plan a series in advance. "Most people plan out the whole series before ever creating the first video… by video three it's obvious no one cares, and then do you stop mid-series?" Instead: post normally, and when one post over-performs, retroactively declare it part two. Then use **Link a Reel** (three dots → link a reel) to chain them into a bingeable playlist; he claims Instagram gives preferential treatment to new features **[O, unevidenced]**.
→ *Diesel version:* this is the right version of series advice for a one-man operation. He already has a natural series in the Chief build; the discipline is to let the audience pick which thread becomes the series.

**6. Street interviews.** Go somewhere with variables, bring a mic, ask strangers on-niche questions. Why it works: "the viewer knows there was a lot of unpredictability… they assume you don't know what's going to happen, so they keep watching." Corroborated by the outdoor test in §5.
→ *Diesel version:* **the parts counter IS the street.** He doesn't need a downtown — he needs permission and a mic, and then "what's the dumbest thing you've done to your truck?" asked of whoever walks in. Also: truck stop, fuel island, a local show. ⚠ Viability caveat: needs employer sign-off and customer consent; plausible but not free. Single highest-upside format in the set for his specific situation.

**7. Inspirational reels.** "Last and easiest to make." Daily-cadence capable, zero production value, "sometimes white background, black text is good enough to go viral." Requires empathy, not equipment.
→ *Diesel version:* same as §1.2.1. **Start here — it's the only one of the seven with a zero-minute production cost.**

**Bonus (the anti-list):** purely educational reels are explicitly named as the thing that *feels* like it should work and doesn't, because it earns saves.

# 3. Hooks (`2WHJv3Xo28Y`) — what's genuinely NEW vs the existing digest

The existing digest already covers, from the same 1,300-hook study: 5–8 words (7.7 avg), <5th-grade reading level, negative words, drunk-grandma test, curiosity-gap Goldilocks, the 15 hook categories, and visual-beats-words. **Do not re-litigate those.** Genuinely new material below.

## 3.1 New and immediately actionable

- ⭐ **`view rate past first 3 seconds`** — a real Instagram insight. Open a reel → View Insights → scroll to the bottom. A pink bar against a tick mark: above the mark = average viewer watches past 3s; below = the hook is the problem. There's a percentage under it. **His instruction: write today's baseline down, re-check in 30 days.** → This is the one measurable in the whole set that tells Jake whether his hook or his body is broken. Given 6.8s avg watch time, he needs this number before changing anything.
- ⭐ **The caption's first line is a second hook.** "Everyone is always talking about the hooks on your reels, but then when it comes to captions, you're just winging it… otherwise people aren't going to read it and you're wasting your time typing it." His own practice (from `WIgXSoQSZMs`): first caption line restates the video's topic or repeats the reel's hook verbatim — which doubles as SEO.
- **Brighter hooks ≈ 60% more engagement** **[E-ish, InstaClub]**. Not overexposed — just not dimly lit; nudge exposure/saturation so the frame pops against the reels above and below it. → **Direct hit on Jake's shooting environment.** A parts counter and a shop bay are fluorescent, grey, and dim. This is a free fix in-camera and in edit.
- **Face and eyes visible in the first second improves retention.** No sunglasses, no hat brim shadow. (Quantified in `WIgXSoQSZMs`: **+10% retention** when a human face appears in the first 3s; **+5.6% engagement and +25% retention** for speech-based vs music-only video. **[E-ish, "a recent study," unnamed]**)
- **The prop rule.** Hooks perform better with a visual object *related to the topic* — and worse with random attention-grab junk. "Don't put an emoji of a monkey or a GIF of a dancing clown… it's going to confuse them and make it feel like you're trying to trick them." If the video is about rock climbing, be at the climbing gym. → Jake's props are free and authentic: the part in his hand, the truck, the racking.
- **Audio is half of video**, and it's the tip he says he'd never ignore again. Three sub-rules: (a) **balance levels** — music not louder than voice; (b) **buy a cheap external mic**, and he specifically recommends **wired plug-in over wireless at the low end** — "$10, $15, $20… the ones that plug right in are actually usually better and cheaper until you get to a certain point, and you don't have to worry about charging them"; (c) **music chosen for emotion**, sound effects only when they relate.
  → ⭐ **Highest-priority gear note for Jake.** He shoots in a **loud shop** on a **DJI Osmo Pocket 3**. The Pocket 3's onboard mic will pick up compressors, impact guns and shop radio. A cheap wired lav is the single cheapest quality upgrade available to him — and audio quality is precisely the thing that makes a "just talking" reel usable without editing.
- **The negative-hook ceiling: don't exceed ~80% of posts.** "Over time people stop paying attention to your negative hooks because everything is negative… they get bored and either unfollow or become disengaged." The existing digest has the negative-word rule but not the ceiling.
- **The fifth-grade check, mechanically:** paste the hook into ChatGPT and ask "is this at or below a fifth grade reading level?" His own failure example: he said "if you **loathe** talking head videos" and should have said *hate* or *dread*. "Who was I trying to impress?"
- **The jargon carve-out, stated cleanly.** The Pokémon example: creators in that niche don't have to explain what Pokémon is. "You don't need to dumb it down so far that literally anyone could understand, but you do need to simplify it enough that your average *target audience member* will." → **This resolves the tension flagged in existing digest §2.5.** For Jake: "6.7" and "turbo" are fine — his target audience owns one. "CP4," "EGT," "DPF delete" and "Class 8" are not hook words — they're body words.

## 4.2 From "11 Viral Reel Ideas" (`p5XTBTLeM7c`)

1. **Series / challenge** — "day [N] of [hard thing] every day until [goal]." Must be genuinely hard; his examples are extreme (a mile per follower; a year in isolation). "If your challenge is trying a new salad recipe every day for 30 days, it's not going to be very engaging." Must be on-niche. → **B.** ⚠ Presupposes capacity Jake doesn't have. 2. **Hook swaps** — ⚠ §7. But his *upgrade* is TOS-clean and worth stealing: **create your own viral hooks** rather than borrowing others'. → **B** for the self-made version.
3. **NAP (niche adjacent post)** — as above. → **A**.
4. **This-or-That split screen** — screen split top/bottom, two near-identical clips (or the *same* clip with different labels). Why it works: people watch twice (once per half), and the spot-the-difference instinct. → **A** — easy in CapCut/Edits/Canva, scales by swapping text. "Prepared morning / unprepared morning" → "guy who services his truck / guy who doesn't."
5. **"Tasteful rage bait"** — ⚠ §7. Includes the deliberate-typo tactic. **Do not relay.** The one clean sub-tactic: hide a small odd detail in the background for people to spot.
6. **Silent audio trend** — mute the reel, text says "Instagram tips only really ugly business owners can hear." Joke is at no one's expense. → **A** — costs nothing, works in a loud shop *because* it's muted. Genuinely clever fit.
7. **2026 ins vs outs** — two columns, on-niche predictions/hopes. Shareworthy because agreers amplify. → **A** — pure text, 10 minutes. "In: gauges. Out: deleting your truck and calling it a build."
8. **The award** — same as 4.1 #8. **A+**.
9. **Partner's POV** — "Wait, tell them what you just told me," filmed from the other person's POV, often with a fake-Snapchat text bar for perceived authenticity. → **C/B** — needs a second person; ⚠ the fake-Snapchat overlay is mild manufactured authenticity, flag it.
10. **Delayed-gratification trends** — trending formats where the payoff is at the *end* ("oh no" song). Audience already knows to wait, so retention is hacked by the format itself. His instruction is a standing brief: watch for these and grab them early. → **B**.

# 5. Filming and editing (`EDxTxachl0U`, plus `WIgXSoQSZMs`)

## 5.1 "Easy editing comes from intentional filming" — the five filming rules

The most directly copyable material in the set, and all five are free.

1. **Never record without at least an outline** — but **not a full script**: "I end up trying to be too perfect and second-guess myself if I veer off." Bullet outline, talk extemporaneously. The outline also serves as the edit map afterwards.
2. ⭐ **Stop between sentences** — or even between independent clauses. Payoff: a flubbed sentence costs you one sentence, not the take. "If I make it to the end of that sentence without a mistake, that was a good take and I can move on."
3. ⭐ **Pause, hold eye contact with the lens, and smile for a beat before and after each take.** Prevents the clipped-word starts that make edits look amateur.
4. **Use visual markers** — hold a hand up in front of the lens to mark "everything before this is garbage." Instantly visible when scrubbing the timeline.
5. ⭐ **Last take = best take.** "When you say the good take, stop right there. Good enough is good enough." Otherwise you shoot four worse versions and then have to hunt the good one out of the pile. With this rule you always scroll to the end of the clip.

→ For Jake these five convert into: one bullet list on his phone, then film in sentence-sized chunks with hand-slaps between bad takes and a beat of eye contact around good ones. It is the difference between a 40-minute edit and a 10-minute edit, and it costs nothing.

## 5.2 The editing stack

- **CapCut Desktop** (paid Premium) — his main tool. Syncs to CapCut mobile mid-project. Used for three job types: medium edits (5–30 min, faster than briefing someone); short-but-fiddly edits (e.g. a 7-second reel needing an effect Instagram/Edits can't do); and long/complex edits needed same-day.
- **Gling AI** — rough-cut automation: feed it raw footage, it strips filler, ums and bad takes. He uses it *only* for the rough pass, then adds taste by hand. Works on a pile of short clips as well as long-form.
- **Edits** (Instagram's app) — for quick/mobile edits, and **specifically when the trend requires Instagram-native audio** (you can import saved IG audio and cut to the beat, then post directly).
- ⭐ **Myth dispelled:** "You will **not** get preferential treatment for using the Edits app, nor diminished views for using CapCut. **It does not matter where you edit your reels.**" **[O, but he's consistent and it contradicts a common belief.]**
- Human editors — irrelevant to Jake, but note the boundary he draws: he still does anything quick or urgent himself.

## 5.3 The four editing-process rules

1. **Edit on desktop, not phone** — faster, more features.
2. ⭐ **Edit off the audio waveform, not the video.** "Don't look at the preview window at all. Just look at the audio file at the bottom — you'll be able to tell when you were talking and when you weren't." Combined with last-take-best-take: jump to the end, cut where the waveform starts, cut where it ends, done in seconds.
3. **Keyboard shortcuts** — Cmd-C/V, **Cmd-B = blade**, and **`A` alone cycles cursor modes** (select → drag → blade).
4. ⭐ **One task per pass.** Don't finish clip 1 then clip 2. Pass 1: remove bad takes. Pass 2: framing/cropping. Pass 3: colour. Pass 4: audio levelling. Pass 5: captions. Pass 6 (last): sound effects and filters. "It allows you to stay focused and be more efficient with each step."
5. **Batch.** "The day I filmed this podcast I recorded about 15 reels on my phone and I haven't touched any of them yet. Film a bunch at once, edit a bunch later." → This is the operating model that fits a full-time job: one filming session per week, one editing session per week.

## 5.4 The "all or nothing editing" curve (`WIgXSoQSZMs`)

A U-shaped curve, views on Y, editing effort on X. Both ends win; **the middle loses.** Left end = yapping/FaceTime reels with no editing. Right end = full cinematic production. "Either go all in… or go to the opposite end and don't do any editing at all."
⚠ **Important honesty in his own caveat:** "The trick of these yapping reels is that they often ARE very scripted, very thought out, and take **dozens of takes** to speak eloquently for 60–90 seconds without ums or filler." So "no editing" means *no post-production*, not *no work*. → For Jake, the left end is the only viable end, and the real cost is retakes, not timeline time.

## 5.5 Framing and camera (the shorts, plus `WIgXSoQSZMs`)

- ⭐ **The eye line** (`hOi_v4hioxo`): "There's an imaginary line that divides the middle and upper third of your video. By lining up your eyes with the eye line, your followers will stay more engaged." → **Direct conflict with the Osmo Pocket 3's face tracking, which centres the face.** Jake should either disable tracking and frame manually with his eyes on the upper-third line, or shoot wider and crop down in the edit. This is a two-minute change with a claimed retention payoff.
- **Get outside** (`WIgXSoQSZMs`): InstaClubHub ran identical reels, identical scripts, only variable indoor vs outdoor. Outdoor performed **~34% better** on views and engagement **[E-ish, internal, n unstated]**. "Commit to the bit. If you need a prop, buy it. If you have an idea to go downtown and film, go downtown." → Jake has Wyoming outside his door and a shop yard full of Class 8 iron. Worth testing against his own numbers rather than taking on faith.
- **Face + voice**: +5.6% engagement, +25% retention for speech-based over music-only; +10% retention for a face in the first 3s **[E-ish]**.
