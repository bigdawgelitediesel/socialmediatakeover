## 1. CONVERSION — viewer → follower

This is Jake's whole problem (45,641 reach → 17 followers = 0.037%). **The corpus barely addresses it.** 15 of 17 videos assume reach is the bottleneck and optimize for reach. Only four items in the entire 900KB are genuinely about the follow decision. Here they are.

### 1a. The profile — "ABCs of the optimized profile" [`1OOw7yd5beE`, `UpSlFTVdiP8`]

His only explicit model of conversion. Opinion, no data behind it, but it's cheap and specific:

- **A — attention-grabbing profile picture.** Personal brand → close-up, well-lit photo of the face, solid background, no props. Business → icon-only logo, never the full logo with tagline shrunk down.
- **B — bio beginning.** First sentence is the only one most people read. Plug the niche into "I help [who] with [what]."
- **C — clear, concise, no clichés.** Kill non-niche fun facts. His line: *"If you wouldn't say it to someone in an elevator, don't put it in your bio."* Don't say the same thing five ways — pick the phrasing your audience actually uses.
- **S — searchable.** See §4.

**Bio length:** four lines or less, or Instagram truncates it. The four lines should say what the topic is and why someone should follow. [`UpSlFTVdiP8`]

**Direct quote on what the bio is for:** *"the optimized Instagram profile, which is actually going to convert people from just a viewer into an actual follower."* That is the single sentence in 17 videos that names Jake's metric.

### 1b. Three pinned posts — a specific formula [`1OOw7yd5beE`]

The most directly actionable conversion item in the corpus. Pin exactly three:

1. **About me / start here** — what the account is and what to expect from it.
2. **A quick win** — immediate value with no email, no comment, no action required. They watch, they learn something, done.
3. **A business post** — the one with the actual call to action.

For Jake: #1 and #2 are the conversion pair. He has no #3 worth pinning yet, and shouldn't fake one.

### 1c. Why people follow, and why they unfollow [`1OOw7yd5beE`, `UgkNKDezyiU`]

Both cited to a **ManyChat report** (his partner — treat as vendor marketing, not a study):
- **#1 reason people follow:** "they post content I find **consistently useful or entertaining**." The operative word is *consistently* — meaning a viewer must be able to predict what the next post will be.
- **#2 reason people follow:** relatability.
- **#1 reason people unfollow:** political content.

His inference, which is the useful part regardless of the source's quality: **a viewer converts on the promise of the next post, not the quality of this one.** A great one-off reel earns a view; a legible, repeatable pattern earns a follow. This is the mechanism that explains Jake's numbers — 3.8× follower-count reach with 0.037% conversion is exactly what a profile with no predictable promise looks like.

**Relevance to Jake's 260,008-view outlier:** one reel carrying 90% of lifetime views and converting almost nobody is the textbook case here. The reel was entertaining to strangers; the profile did not tell them what they'd get tomorrow.

### 1d. Instant follow-DM [`ooCESNnwqso`, `SanUx9tKrMo`]

New ManyChat feature (late 2025), officially Meta-approved: auto-DM the moment someone follows.

His framing, which is good: *"the moment that someone follows you is usually the peak of when they are most engaged, most attentive to, and most interested in your account."* Use it to **thank them, nothing else**. Set a ~5-minute delay. No link, no email capture, no freebie, no sale. His analogy: don't propose on the first date.

This is retention, not conversion — it keeps a new follower from going dormant. Jake, whose single best post worked *because he replied to nearly every comment*, is the exact profile this suits: it automates the first beat of the relationship he's already good at.

### 1e. Follow incentives that came up

- **"Early access reels"** [`TwrUvnFwcfc`] — a setting that shows non-followers a blurred reel labelled followers-only. Literally a follow gate built by Instagram. He notes it works better the bigger your audience, so it's weak at 215, but it is free and it is the only native follow-conversion surface mentioned anywhere in the corpus.
- **Daily challenge / series** [`1OOw7yd5beE`, `UgkNKDezyiU`, `twZUZCoazjw`] — his loudest 2026 recommendation. "Day X of [thing] until [follower goal]." His own: reviewing one follower's reel daily until 1M. Claimed benefits: incentivizes the follow, builds recognition ("you're the guy who…"), and removes the daily what-do-I-post decision. Rules he gives: it must relate to the niche, and it must actually be *challenging* (a constraint, a stake, a countdown) or there's no reason to watch. **Evidence:** one anecdote, a student who went 48 → 4,800 followers in 5 days sending personalized voice-note prayers to every new follower. Single unverified anecdote — treat as a hypothesis, not a result.
- **Announce with a filter** [`1OOw7yd5beE`] — when driving people to the account, say *"only follow me if you're interested in X"* and explicitly tell friends/family not to follow out of obligation. Rationale: off-niche followers never engage, which drags the ratios and teaches the algorithm the wrong audience. Directly relevant to Jake, whose 215 almost certainly contains Wyoming acquaintances who don't care about the build.

### 1f. What the corpus does NOT have

No data on reach→follow rates, no benchmark for what a normal conversion rate is, no A/B evidence that any profile change moves it. **Every conversion claim above is assertion.** Nobody in 17 videos measures the thing Jake needs measured.

## 2. The "39K in 90 days" case study — the title is false

**`4GMo8FNPpZ0` does not contain a 39K-in-90-days result.** Read the actual numbers.

**Who:** Jamie Brindle, freelancing coach, LA. **~850,000 followers across social before the experiment.** Professional video editor by trade ("I came up in the video production world"). Runs a content business full-time with his wife Courtney; they shut down their agency because the content business outgrew it. Has a custom GPT trained on *thousands* of his own pieces of content.

**What he did:** went from **3 posts/day to 12 posts/day** for 90 days. Solo — no editors — but full-time.

**The actual 90-day results, in his words:**

| Metric | Change vs. prior 90 days |
|---|---|
| Reach | **+30%** |
| Profile visits | **+30%** |
| External link taps | +75% |
| New messages | +77% |
| Returning messages | +32% |
| **New followers** | **+9%** |

He 4×'d output and got **+9% on followers** while reach and profile visits rose 30%. That is *the same conversion collapse Jake has*, at 850K followers, and neither host notices. Brock's on-air reaction is to hunt for a reason it's secretly good.

**Was it repeatable?** No, for Jake:
- Subject is a professional editor; that's the entire reason 12/day is possible. He says so: *"it's very easy for me to cut something together… as I'm shooting it, I'm editing it."*
- He has a trained language model on thousands of his own posts.
- He works on this full-time. Jake has a full-time job at a semi shop.
- Per-post engagement, views and reach all **went DOWN** and stayed down. He explicitly reset his baseline: *"we've just kind of had to become accustomed to a new baseline."* At 215 followers, Jake cannot afford a lower per-post baseline.
- The one result he's most excited about is being able to **hide promotions** in volume — "it's one of 12." Irrelevant at Jake's scale.

**What IS transferable from it, and it's the genuinely valuable part:**

1. **Content formats (repeatable templates), not content ideas.** He listed formats first, then scripted **31 posts per format** — a month of one format at a time. Examples of his formats: two-person conversation, tweet-screenshot, quote-over-photo, client text-message thread, notes-app journal, masterclass clips (one masterclass → ~62 clips).
2. **Fully scripted, word-for-word, before shoot day.** Rationale: *"on the day I might be exhausted… I'd rather have that work done preemptively."*
3. **Never take one post from idea to posted in one sitting.** Batch by stage: script all → shoot all → edit all → schedule all. He does hardest-first; Brock does easiest-first; they agree the point is batching by stage, not the order.
4. **He cut his conversation format from two exchanges to one** to halve shooting time — *"and wouldn't you know it, it made retention a lot better because suddenly these videos just get to the point."* Accidental finding, directly relevant to a 6.8s watch time.
5. **Scheduling was the biggest time sink** — a full day to schedule a month. First thing he'd hire out.
6. **Unfollow rate did not move.** People who didn't want the content just stopped being shown it. Meaningful counter-evidence against "posting more annoys your followers."
7. **The first ~14 days spiked hard** (thousands of followers, program signups) then flattened. Brock's hypothesis: the algorithm briefly over-served existing followers before recalibrating. Unresolved — Jamie was re-running that week to test it. **Treat as an open question, not a finding.**

**Bottom line for Jake:** the case study is a batching-and-templating manual wearing a growth-result headline. Take the production system. The volume result does not apply and the follower result is a warning, not a promise.

### 3b. CONTRADICTIONS with `research/2026-09-15-algorithm-reference.md` — flag these

**(i) The three signals are re-ranked into a hierarchy Mosseri never stated.** [`jzO3T2AiX_I`]

## 4. Instagram SEO and keywords

Now that hashtags are capped, this is where discovery moved. [`L7jDiYGf4bc` (July 2025), `Imu3qUdifZg`]

### What is indexed

Per Mosseri's ordering (§3a): **caption > video > audio > comments.**

⚠️ **Brock misreports this ordering in his own SEO episode.** In `L7jDiYGf4bc` he presents it as *"caption > spoken audio > visuals > comments"* — he swaps video and audio, then builds advice on the swapped version. Mosseri's actual words put the **video** second. Practically this means the *frames* matter more than the *voiceover* for topic classification. Trust the quote, not the recap.

### The name field — the highest-leverage unused asset

- **Username** and **Name field** are the only word-for-word searchable lines on a profile, on Instagram *and* on Google.
- **The username must be unique; the Name field does not.** Anyone can put any keywords there. **56 characters.**
- Most people waste it on their own name or business name. *"Most people don't know our name or our business's name."*
- His example: username `brock11johnson`, name field `Instagram Growth Coach`.
- Real-world proof he offers: he found his videographer for the Mosseri interview by searching Instagram for **"San Francisco videographer"** — the guy who won the job had that exact phrase in his name field; better videographers with cute taglines ("Wave Wind Media," "your videography bestie") were invisible.

**For Jake this is the cheapest fix available.** `@elcoyotediesel` / "Jake Dalton" currently spends both searchable lines on things nobody searches. 56 characters could carry "6.7 Powerstroke Build + Diesel Parts" or similar. His semi-parts differentiator is a keyword nobody else in pickup-diesel owns.

### ⚠️ Is the bio searchable? The videos contradict each other

- `L7jDiYGf4bc` (July 2025): *"Unfortunately, as it currently stands, our Instagram bio is **not** searchable… the words you put in your bio are not likely to influence what results you show up as."* He adds Instagram plans to make the full bio searchable "by end of 2025 or at some point in 2026."
- `Imu3qUdifZg` (later): *"by strategically including quality keywords in your captions, in your nameline, **in your bio**, and in your posts, you will get discovered more."*

Unresolved. **Safe play: treat the name field as load-bearing and the bio as a human-conversion surface that may also be indexed.** Writing the bio well serves both.

### Keywords — do and don't

- **DO use them organically, in sentences.** Long-tail (3–5 words) beats generic. "Save this easy **gluten-free Thanksgiving mac and cheese** recipe" ≫ "Save this easy recipe."
- **The first sentence of the caption is weighted most heavily** [`Imu3qUdifZg`].
- **DON'T keyword-stuff.** Lists of comma-separated keywords at the bottom of the caption → **Instagram has explicitly said this is spammy and downranks the post.**
- **DON'T hide color-matched keyword text on the video.** Same treatment. ⚠️ ToS-adjacent; do not do this.
- **Watch the visuals** — the algorithm samples key frames. His own example: a carousel titled "37 reel ideas for July" got classified as **rare fish investing** because he wore a "rare fish investor" meme t-shirt in slide one. For Jake: shop backgrounds, brand-logo shirts and parts packaging in frame all feed classification. Could work for him or against him.
- **Check what Instagram thinks your post is about** via the **Recommended Search** hyperlink at the top of your own comments section. Free diagnostic, not widely known. **Jake should run this on the 260K reel and on his three worst-performing build posts** — it will show directly whether the account is being classified as "diesel trucks" or as something useless.
- **First comment on your own post** to nudge classification. One comment, not five.

### Hashtag evidence

`Imu3qUdifZg` cites a **SocialPilot** study of "millions of posts": posts with **zero hashtags performed 23% better** than posts with one or more, and an **inverse relationship** between hashtag count and reach. Unlinked, unverified, and correlational — accounts that still use 30 hashtags are probably lower-quality accounts generally. **Do not treat as causal.** The directional conclusion (hashtags are not a growth lever) is well supported by Mosseri's own words regardless.

Also removed: **hashtag following** and the **"Recent" tab** under hashtags.

## 5. The shadowban video [`UgkNKDezyiU`] — claims vs. reality

Largely accurate and lines up with the reference doc. Worth keeping.

**What he gets right:**
- *"I believe that 99% of people will never be shadowbanned… but 100% of people feel like they've been shadowbanned."* 70% of his own poll respondents said they'd felt it.
- **The check is real and he names it:** Settings → **Account Status**. Green checks = not restricted. Red = tappable, tells you what happened, and there's an **appeal process** in-app.
- Definitional point that does real work: *"the key word in this definition is **secret**"* — and Account Status makes it not secret, so it isn't a shadowban.
- **Strike escalation** (his numbers, unsourced): 1st ≈ 3 hours, 2nd ≈ 7 hours, 3rd ≈ 24 hours, 4th ≈ 3 days, 5th ≈ 1 week, 6th ≈ 3 weeks to a month+.
- **Political content:** confirms Mosseri's early-2024 removal of political content from non-follower reach, the user-facing control added months later, and the **2025 reversal**. Then the honest part: he gets identical "I'm being suppressed" complaints from **both ends** of the spectrum, which is itself the disproof. Real mechanism is negative engagement — disagreement, "not interested," unfollows — not ideology.
- **Correctly condemns**: buying followers/likes/engagement; engagement pods (*"the same exact engagers at the same time every time you post,"* so the engagement is weighted down); and — importantly — **mass-removing "inactive" or "spam" followers**. His argument: the algorithm sees a follower-count drop, not a cleanup, and suppresses subsequent posts. Consistent with the reference doc's ratio logic.

**Where to discount him:**
- **"One Instagram share usually equates to 400 additional views" — per "an Insta Club study from 2026."** His own membership, no methodology, no sample size. This is his most-quoted number and it is unverifiable house data. **Do not carry it into the repo as fact.** It is also implausibly clean.
- He lists **gray-area topics** he'd avoid: THC/marijuana, alcohol, guns, adult content, and the make-money-online niche. Plausible, unsourced.
- **The "break in case of emergency" post** — a carousel saying "I think I'm shadowbanned, comment if you see this." He caveats it heavily (once a year max, may cause unfollows). It is engagement bait with extra steps. **Don't.**

**Applying it to Jake:** he has no evidence of restriction. The first action is 10 seconds — open Account Status. If it's green, the entire shadowban frame is off the table and the conversation returns to conversion, where it belongs.

⚠️ **Diesel-specific risk flag, mine not theirs:** the corpus flags guns/alcohol/THC as recommendation-gray-area niches. It does not flag vehicle-modification content, but **emissions-delete content is a real exposure for a diesel creator** — both as potential recommendation-ineligible material and, independently of Instagram, because removing emissions equipment violates the US Clean Air Act and Jake works at a commercial truck shop. Worth a deliberate decision rather than a drift.

## 6. Starting over / dead account [`275pX26Kols`] — and the answer for Jake

He does make a case for it, but a gated one. **Eight yes/no questions; five or more "yes" = start over.** Score Jake:

| # | Question | Jake |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Account older than 3 years? | Unknown — likely **yes** |
| 2 | Posted **5×/week to feed, every week, for 12 months**? | **NO.** 53 posts total, ever |
| 3 | Is your content above average for your niche? | Unknown |
| 4 | Used inorganic growth tactics? | **No** |
| 5 | **3+** community-guideline violations? | Check Account Status; presumed **no** |
| 6 | Critically low engagement for 4–6 months? | **NO** — see below |
| 7 | Viral post brought a flood of off-niche followers? | **Worth checking** (the 260K reel) |
| 8 | Major rebrand / niche change? | **No** — truck content to truck content |

**His own engagement benchmarks** (engagements ÷ views, sustained over months):
- **under 5K followers: critically low is below 2%**
- 5K–20K: below 0.85% · 20K–100K: below 0.6% · 100K+: below 0.5%

**Jake's best post is 15.46%.** He is not remotely near critically low.

**Verdict: Jake scores at most 1–2 of 8. By the video's own criteria, do not start over.** Question 2 is decisive and he answers it himself: *"if you haven't been that level of consistent, five per week for a year, then it's too early to start over. You don't need a new account. You need to get more consistent."*

**On the 15 pre-Creator posts:** starting over is the wrong tool. The video supplies the right one — **archive**. He describes archiving a post that brought in the wrong followers (high-schoolers) because Instagram kept serving his next posts to them and tanking performance for 2–3 weeks. Archiving removes a post from the profile and from distribution without touching the account. **Archive the off-niche pre-Creator posts; keep the account, the handle, and the history.**

⚠️ One claim to discount: *"newer accounts oftentimes get preferential treatment from the algorithm."* His own reasoning undercuts it — he says what's really happening is the algorithm hasn't yet accumulated evidence that nobody wants your content. That's not preferential treatment, that's an uninformed prior. The reference doc's position (recommendation architecture is follower-agnostic at seed stage) is better grounded. ## 9. Where these videos contradict each other

Several months of "NEW algorithm" videos, and they disagree. Ranked by how much it matters to Jake.

**(1) Reels vs. carousels — the sharpest one, and it collides with Jake's own data.**
- `a34p7BHRryQ` (State of Social Media Engagement 2026, **52M posts**): reels get **36% more reach** than carousels; carousels get **12% more engagement**. Median engagement rate: **carousels 6.9%, single images 4.4%, reels 3.3%.**
- `UgkNKDezyiU`: *"carousels actually get more engagement than reels, and they get significantly more views from your followers than reels."* → so post a **carousel, not a reel**, to revive a stalled account.
- `twZUZCoazjw`: carousels are a "power play" for 2026 — *"carousels. Period."*
- `ooCESNnwqso`: but then — *"reels have a significantly higher amount of views and reach from non-followers… if you want to reach new people, reels are probably your best bet."*

**Resolution, and it matters for the CLAUDE.md line "static posts average 116 views — stop making them":** these are not in conflict once you separate the metrics. **Carousels win on engagement RATE among people who already follow you. Reels win on REACH to people who don't.** At 215 followers, a carousel's ceiling is roughly 215 people — 6.9% of which is ~15 engagements. That is why Jake's static posts show 116 views. **The audit's "stop making static posts" is correct for reach and correct for now.** But the carousel case is not "more views," it's "more engagement from existing followers" — i.e. a **retention** tool, not a growth tool. Given Jake's problem is conversion, not reach, there is a narrow argument for one carousel a week aimed at the 215 he has. One tactic makes it cheap: **`SanUx9tKrMo` — add music to a carousel and it becomes eligible for the Reels tab.** That is a carousel with reel-like distribution, and it's the only way the carousel advice survives contact with Jake's follower count.

**(2) Upcycling — how soon can you repost the same content? Three different answers.**
- `-Qi9-ZT1_GE`: *"I spoke with Instagram staff directly… once it's been **a week or so**, you are free to re-upload."*
- `a34p7BHRryQ` / `twZUZCoazjw` / `Imu3qUdifZg` / `ooCESNnwqso`: **90 days.**
- `a34p7BHRryQ`, quoting Mosseri: you could re-upload **every single day** without algorithmic punishment; he just advises against it for audience reasons.
- `ooCESNnwqso` states it flatly wrong: *"Instagram no longer gives views to content that is a direct duplicate of something you've posted before."* **That rule applies only to trial reels**, which he clarifies correctly in three other videos.

**Resolution:** no algorithmic penalty for re-uploading to your normal feed at any interval; 90 days is a taste convention, not a rule. **Trial reels are the exception** — duplicates there are explicitly a spam vector per Mosseri, and variation must be substantial in the **first 6–7 seconds of visual content**; new audio, new caption or a reworded hook is NOT enough.

The upcycling rule he lands on across four videos: **post every evergreen post three times before judging it. Retire only after three consecutive flops.** Rationale is reasonable — the same post can flop then go viral — but note it's also a content-volume hack dressed as an insight. He says 60–75% of his own feed is re-uploads or recreations.

**(3) The three ranking signals** — see §3b(i). "Shares are unequivocally #1" (older) vs. "it's not one clear answer, shares for non-follower reach and likes for follower reach" (April 2026). **The newer one is correct.**

**(4) Is the bio searchable?** See §4. Direct July-2025 "no" vs. later implied "yes."

**(5) Does engaging with your audience help?** See §7. "Zero algorithmic benefit" vs. comments being a named algorithm input vs. DM-automation posts performing best *because* they generate comments.

**(6) Caption/video/audio/comments ordering** — Mosseri's actual quote vs. Brock's recap with audio and video swapped. See §4.

**(7) Reach is down vs. reach is "a blend."** Mosseri says blend; Brock disagrees and cites **Metricool, Dec 2024, 400,000 accounts, 32M posts**: reels reach **−44%**, carousels **−58%**, feed images **−65%**, stories **−47%** year over year. Separately, `a34p7BHRryQ` cites a 52M-post study: **engagement down 26% in two years.** These are the two best-sourced numbers in the corpus. Both support "the baseline got worse for everyone," which contextualizes Jake's numbers charitably.

**(8) The Edits app.** `TwrUvnFwcfc` catches Mosseri contradicting himself: first "we will not give preferential treatment to reels made with Edits," then months later on stories, "we have noticed that people who use the Edits app are getting slightly more reach." Brock's explanation — the "Made with Edits" badge added a fraction of a second of watch time because people looked at it — is a nice piece of reasoning and probably right. **Practical upshot: use whatever editor you like; CapCut is not penalized.** ⚠️ But **watermarks and floating logos DO downrank** — so export from CapCut without the watermark.

## 11. ToS and risk flags

**Named and correctly condemned** (matches the reference doc): buying followers/likes/engagement · engagement pods · follow/unfollow · follow trains · paid giveaway follow-loops · bought shoutouts.

**Additional, from these videos:**
- ⚠️ **Keyword stuffing** — comma-separated keyword lists at the bottom of a caption. Instagram has stated this is detected and downranks the post.
- ⚠️ **Hidden color-matched keyword text** on video. Same. Do not.
- ⚠️ **Duplicate trial reels** — Mosseri calls it *"a spam vector that we are addressing."* Direct from the CEO.
- ⚠️ **Exceeding the trial-reel daily cap** — he reports people getting 30-day restrictions.
- ⚠️ **Mass-removing followers** — not a ToS violation, but he says it reliably tanks subsequent reach.
- ⚠️ **Reposting others' content** — the aggregator crackdown. Credit does not make it original. Compilations count. Low-effort edits (border, captions, watermark) count. **Relevant to Jake:** reposting other people's truck clips is a common growth move in this niche and it is now suppressed.
- ⚠️ **Watermarks/logos on video** — long-standing downrank.

**Grey areas, flagged not endorsed:**
- **DM automation via ManyChat** is a Meta-approved partner integration; Brock confirms it with Mosseri on camera, though he notes Mosseri got visibly thrown by the "comment X and I'll send you Y" example and pivoted to talking about account hacking. The 2024 @creators post that cited "comment blank and you'll receive blank" as an **engagement-bait example** was, per Brock, walked back as a bad example. **Status: the tool is approved; the specific comment-to-DM pattern has a documented history of being called engagement bait.** Not a clean green light.
- **"Follow first, then freebie" gating** [`ooCESNnwqso`] — withholding a deliverable until someone follows. Nobody in the corpus asks whether incentivized follows are engagement bait. **Treat as unresolved.** It is also strategically wrong for Jake: a follow bought with a PDF is a follower who doesn't want truck content, which is exactly the off-niche-follower problem `275pX26Kols` warns about.
- ⚠️ **Emissions-delete content** — my flag, not theirs. See §5.
