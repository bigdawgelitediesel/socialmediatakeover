# Trial Reels and posting on Instagram, November 2026

Research agent, run late on 2026-11-22 for the 2026-11-23 masterclass, part 2. Jacob's brief: "Research trial reels and posting on Instagram." This file extends section 28 (Trial Reels) and section 31 (cadence) of `research/2026-11-22-masterclass/05-instagram-2026.md` and the funnel numbers in `research/2026-11-21-profile-conversion.md`. It repeats a source from those files only where it is used for a new fact.

Method. 30 WebSearch calls (extended mode for 2026 items, standard for older ones), one schema pull from the Composio Instagram toolkit, and the account's own Insights pulls in `data/instagram/README.md` and `analysis/2026-11-12-refresh.md`. WebFetch returned ENOTFOUND on every host tried (about.fb.com, socialmediatoday.com, planoly.com, kapwing.com, creatoros.ca) and curl to help.instagram.com was refused by the proxy (403), so Instagram's own pages are quoted the way search snippets and the vendors that cite them render the text. Grades: **official** (Instagram, Meta, Mosseri, Help Center, developer docs), **measured** (a dataset with a stated sample), **creator** (one account's experience, an educator, or a vendor blog).

The short version. The Trial Reels mechanics have not changed since launch except for scheduling (April 2, 2026), desktop publishing from Edits for Mac (November 5, 2026) and an API parameter. Nothing Instagram has published gives a view count for the auto-share test, a distribution duration, a per-day cap, or a follower floor other than the Help Center's 200 for professional accounts. The measured creator data on Trials is three single-account tests and they say the same thing: more strangers, lower engagement per view, a small follower gain that comes from one or two reels. On posting, every 2026 dataset is brands with teams; none reports follower growth by format for accounts under 1,000; the only follower-conversion numbers that apply to this account are its own, and they say the profile converts at 30 to 38 percent while 0.14 to 0.93 percent of reach ever opens it.

## 1. Trial Reels as of November 2026

**Eligibility.** Public account, eligible for recommendations (Settings, Account Status), at least 200 followers for a professional account (Business or Creator) and 1,000 for a personal account. That is the Help Center text (article 1013292530224018) as quoted by six guides, one of them dated November 6, 2026. The dissent is Brock Johnson (November 19, 2026, "1,000 followers and a professional account") and one of the ten guides returned, which says both help pages still read 1,000 for professional accounts; another guide explains the gap as history: Mosseri confirmed 1,000 in July 2025 and the professional-versus-personal split came later. No region restriction is stated since the July 14, 2025 wide rollout. Losing recommendation eligibility removes the Trial option until Account Status clears. Read: at 580 followers on a Creator account the toggle should be there; if it is not, Account Status is the first place to look, not the follower count.

**Where it lives.** The Reels composer, final share screen, a "Trial" toggle above the Share button, with "Share automatically" as a sub-setting. Since April 2, 2026 a Trial can be scheduled from the same screen (Mosseri, answering a creator who had asked the previous November; rollout uneven, public account required; one vendor dates it February). Since November 5, 2026 Edits for Mac publishes directly as a Trial; there is no Windows build. The API: Meta's Content Publishing changelog added an optional `trial_params` field on the media container for both the Instagram Login and Facebook Login paths ("applies to all versions", date not captured). The Composio tool this account publishes with, `INSTAGRAM_POST_IG_USER_MEDIA`, was schema-checked today and has no such field (caption, media_type, video_url, cover_url, thumb_offset, share_to_feed, user_tags, audio_name, location_id, graph_api_version defaulting to v21.0). So Trials are an in-app step for this account unless someone tests a raw Graph call with `trial_params` through the Composio proxy.

**Distribution.** Shown to non-followers only; not on the profile grid, not on the Reels tab, not in followers' feeds or Reels. A follower can still come across it if a non-follower sends it to them (launch FAQ, not re-read this run). Instagram publishes no duration and no count. The evidence on size is indirect: insights populate at roughly 24 hours, the auto-share decision uses the first 72 hours, and the Kapwing trial kept being distributed to 1 million views, so there is no hard stop. Mosseri (June 2026 Q&A via Kontentino): Trials and regular Reels are ranked by the same system and a Trial gets no reach advantage; one guide in the results attributes to him the line that Trials "almost always get less reach than regular Reels." He never uses them himself and posts directly, while saying they make sense for creators who want to protect a curated grid or not over-post to followers.

**Metrics and timing.** Insights appear about 24 hours after posting and are shown against the account's previous Trials. Standard Reel metrics apply (views, reach, watch time, retention, skip rate, likes, comments, shares, saves). Whether follows per Trial is shown is not confirmed; the Insights redesign in test since November 20 sorts content by follows and adds a Trial reels shortcut, which would make it visible in-app even though the API has no media-level follows for Reels.

**Graduation.** Three routes. Manual: "Share with everyone" at any time, after which the reel sits on the grid and is eligible to followers on all surfaces. Automatic: the toggle text is "share automatically with followers if we determine it's performing well based on the views it receives within the first 72 hours"; Instagram publishes no number and Metricool's help text says the judgement is relative to other reels. Re-shoot: the common creator route, the winning opening re-filmed as a normal Reel. Mosseri, July 2026: an old Trial was never on the profile, so leaving it alone does nothing to the account or the grid; no need to delete or archive.

**Counting.** Trials do not appear on the profile. They have their own section in Insights; Planoly's advice, compare Trials with Trials and regular with regular over the same weeks, is the practical reading of that. Whether Trial views roll into the account-level reach and views totals is not stated anywhere I could find.

**Collab.** One guide states that Collab partners cannot be added to a Trial. No second source, but it is consistent with the design: a Collab lands on both grids and a Trial is off-grid. Treat as probable.

**Same video twice.** The Help Center warning, quoted by Planoly: "your trial reel may get limited reach if we detect that you've previously shared the same content." Mosseri, October 16, 2026: reposting the same Trial repeatedly, "we're going to crack down on that." A vendor table of re-trial attempts says text-only changes on the same footage were usually flagged and a new spoken line plus a new first shot had the best odds, with no guarantee. A "30-day duplicate penalty" appears on one real-estate blog with no Instagram source.

**Reposts tab and originality.** The Reposts button is for other people's posts and files them in the Reposts tab; it is a different action from a Trial and cannot be combined with one. A Trial of your own old reel re-uploaded as a new file is the near-duplicate case above. Recommendation eligibility, which the originality rules can remove, is a precondition for Trials at all.

**New in 2026, by date.** April 2: scheduling (Mosseri). June Q&A: ten reels a day "a lot, but not banned or penalized"; sustainability is the limit. August 28 Q&A: scheduled and manual posts enter ranking the same way. September 3 to 24: Mosseri's four-part ranking series names "auditioning", a post shown to some non-followers and shown to more if it does better than expected, and (in bazzaal's summary) names Trials as the tool for testing with non-followers first. October 16 and 30 AMAs: Trials do not hurt the account; several different Trials a day is fine; the same Trial again will be cracked down on. November 3: Tessa Lyons says Feature clips can run as Trials. November 5: Edits for Mac direct Trial publish. November 20: Insights redesign test with a Trial reels shortcut. Trials for photos or carousels: asked at a 2026 Q&A (Kontentino has the question); nothing launched; Trials are Reels-only as of this week. Caps: Instagram says it limits Trials per period and does not publish the number (SocialBee, April 2026, a 30-day window); the creator figures are 5 a day (Millie Adrian) and 20 a day (Brock Johnson, November 19), neither confirmed.

## 2. What creators measured with Trials, 2025 to 2026

**Answer.** Three tests with a control exist, all single accounts. They agree: a Trial reaches about two to two and a half times the non-followers of the same account's regular Reel, engagement per view falls by two thirds or more, and the follower difference is small and comes from a few reels. The big totals (2,600 in 15 days, 4,000 in 50 days, 65,000 in 12 weeks) are high-volume creators posting five or six Trials a day, usually re-trialling talking heads that had already worked, and two of the three are sales pages. Nobody has published a Trial test on an account under 1,000 followers, and nobody has published hook-variant results with numbers; the hook protocols below are vendor method, not data.

**Every case with numbers.**
1. Creatorhouse, May 2026. 4,200-follower productivity account, 14 days, 10 Trials against 10 regular Reels. Non-follower reach 2.3x; total engagement 0.6x, so engagement per view about a quarter; net new followers 12.4 per Trial against about 9 per regular Reel (1.4x). Measured, n = 20, one account. https://creatorhouse.app/blog/trial-reels-14-day-test-2026
2. Kapwing, about October 2025. 10-day test, a Trial against a regular Reel of comparable content. The Trial reached 1 million views, 2.3x the regular Reel, with the same number of likes, and gained one more follower than the regular Reel; the author then saw "months of a lull, the familiar pattern after mega-virality." Measured, n = 2 posts. https://www.kapwing.com/resources/instagram-trial-reels-study-what-we-learned-after-a-trial-reel-hit-1-million-views/
3. Millie Adrian (Modern Millie), 2026. About 2,600 followers from about 75 Trials in 15 days, five a day; 90 percent of the followers came from 2 of the 75, both re-posts of talking heads that had already done well with followers. Says most creators are limited to about 5 Trials a day. Creator claim. https://itsmodernmillie.com/blog/instagram-trial-reels-everything-you-need-to-know/ and via https://www.aisocialmedianow.com/blog/instagram-trial-reels
4. TrialReel (vendor) case, 2026. 300 Trials in 50 days (six a day), 4.5 million views, 4,000 followers: 13 followers per Trial, 0.09 percent of views. Creator (vendor case). https://trialreel.com/blog/instagram-trial-reels-guide
5. niredonahue, 2025 to 2026. 9 Trials, 2.3 million impressions; the follower figure was not in the snippet and the page did not load. Creator claim. https://niredonahue.com/instagram-trial-reels-case-study/
6. "65,000 followers in 12 weeks" (Substack, paid post) https://theartofsuccess.substack.com/p/how-i-used-trial-reels-to-gain-65000 and "15,000 followers" (Gumroad guide, 27.99 GBP) https://georgiasocial.gumroad.com/l/trialreelsguide. Self-reported, unverified, selling a product.
7. Instagram's own figure, undated, quoted by Soundstripe and brandID (June 2026): 40 percent of creators who tried Trials posted more often and 80 percent of those saw higher non-follower reach. Official (secondhand, no sample given). It is a statement about posting more, not about Trials converting.
8. The "Marcus Rivera / EcoHome" case on socialecho.net reads as a hypothetical; excluded.

**Trials per day.** Millie five; the TrialReel case six; Robert Benjamin (November 4, 2026) recommends one to two feed Reels plus five Trials, which is team volume; Mosseri says several different Trials a day is fine and that ten Reels a day is a lot but not penalized. The one-man constraint, not Instagram, sets the number.

**Hook-testing protocols (method, no published results).** Buffer: two versions, one variable changed, judge at about 24 hours. Hook Studio and FavStash: the "3-hook method", the same take with three different first lines, posted as three Trials. Viral Slice: run it as a split test, same time of day and weekday, 24 to 48 hours apart, change only the opening premise, keep audio, length, caption and body identical. Reloop: judge each Trial against the average of the last four Trials; high views with low shares means the body, not the hook, needs work. Notnoise: switch auto-share off before a hook test so that two strong versions do not both reach followers. Lazyscaler: "test every reel first," every post goes out as a Trial and only winners are shared. Planoly: compare Trials with Trials. Sources: https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-trial-reels/, https://www.hookstudio.ai/guides/instagram-trial-reels, https://www.favstash.app/blog/how-to-post-trial-reels, https://viralsliceco.com/blog/instagram-trial-reels-strategy, https://reloop.so/blog/article/instagram-trial-reels/, https://notnoise.co/blog/instagram-trial-reels-test-hook-before-release, https://lazyscaler.substack.com/p/the-trial-reel-framework. All creator (vendor).

**What graduates and what does not.** In the only account that reported it, the graduates were talking heads with a track record among followers (Millie's 2 of 75). The 0-view Trial has a short diagnostic list: too new (under 24 h), a near-duplicate of something already posted, over 3:00, a foreign watermark, or an account that is not recommendation-eligible (vidova, in the 05 file). The "sits at 150 to 200 views" pattern is reported widely and explained by nobody official.

**Failure modes.** (a) Views without engagement: Kapwing's Trial had 2.3x the views and the same likes; strangers watched and did nothing. (b) Views without follows: one extra follower at 1 million views. (c) The post-viral lull. (d) Auto-share sending two near-identical winners to followers. (e) The near-duplicate flag when the same footage is re-trialled with only new text. (f) "Performs as a Trial, flops when shared": no Instagram statement and no measured case. The mechanics make it plausible: the share puts the reel in front of followers, where likes per reach weigh more than sends, and a meme that strangers sent around may already have reached some followers by DM. Planoly's argument runs the other way: a Trial that did not win with strangers rarely wins with followers either. The practical reading is that the share step is for followers and the grid, not for a second wave of strangers.

**Confidence:** medium on the direction (more strangers, less engagement per view, small follower gain); low on every magnitude, because n is one account each time and the two biggest totals are sales pages.

## 3. Posting in late 2026 for a small creator account

**Cadence, with samples.**
- Buffer frequency study, August 13, 2025: about 2.1 million posts, 100,000+ accounts. Reach per post +12 percent at 3 to 5 posts a week, +18 at 6 to 9, +24 at 10 or more, against 1 to 2; follower growth rate about 2x at 3 to 5 a week versus 1 to 2. Measured. https://buffer.com/resources/how-often-to-post-on-instagram/
- Metricool 2026 Instagram study: 24.3 million posts, 375,000 accounts, January to February 2025 against the same months of 2026. Average posting up 8.79 percent, 1.19 to 1.30 posts a week; only 21 percent of accounts under 10,000 followers grew; secondary write-ups put peak interactions for small accounts at 7 to 14 posts a week (the brackets conflict: "2K to 10K" in one, "under 10K" in another). Measured. https://metricool.com/press-release-instagram-study-2026/
- Socialinsider Q3 2026: 67.5 million posts, 579,000 brand accounts. Engagement by followers: carousels 0.50 percent, Reels 0.48, images 0.35, overall 0.45 (0.48 in 2025); image engagement down 18 percent year over year; 1k to 5k accounts post about 8 Reels a month. Measured. https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/instagram
- Dash Social (the company formerly named Dash Hudson) 2026 Instagram Benchmark Report, press release November 17, 2026: 3,313 accounts with 1,000+ followers, organic plus boosted. Carousels 156,000 views per post against 126,000 for Reels (24 percent more); Reels lead interactions, shares (711 per post) and saves; Reels engagement 3.6 percent overall, 2.7 percent among top brands against 1.4 for carousels and 1.3 for static. Monthly follower growth rate by format is in the paid download only. Measured, brands. https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/11/17/3404579/0/en/instagram-carousels-average-24-more-views-than-reels-dash-social-benchmark-report-finds.html
- Mosseri, June 2026: ten Reels a day is a lot but not penalized; the real limit is what you can sustain. Official (secondhand, Kontentino). September 2026 series: recency is a stated ranking goal, measured as the share of daily impressions going to posts under 24 hours old (Reelyze's reading, https://getreelyze.com/guides/instagram-reels-algorithm). Official (secondhand).

**Time of day.**
- Buffer State of Social Engagement 2026: 52 million posts, 10 platforms, 200,000+ accounts, January 2024 to December 2025; Instagram 9.6 million (Buffer's own page says "nearly 10 million"). Best slot Thursday 9 a.m., then Wednesday 12 p.m. and 6 p.m.; Friday worst, weekends weakest. Measured. https://buffer.com/resources/when-is-the-best-time-to-post-on-instagram/
- Later, 6 million+ posts (2024 data): 5 a.m. best, Monday best. Hootsuite, 1 million+ posts: Monday, Tuesday, Thursday. Sprout Social: midweek afternoons and early evenings. Dash Social: 750,000 posts from 1,549 brands for its timing table. The hour disagrees across all five; midweek agrees; time zone conventions differ. Measured, brands. Roundups: https://socialk.it/en/best-time-to-post/instagram, https://kompozy.io/guides/best-time-to-post-on-instagram-2026-data, https://www.dashsocial.com/blog/best-time-to-post-on-instagram
- Mosseri: posting time "doesn't matter much"; post right before your followers' activity peaks; first-hour performance correlates with final performance but is not a guarantee either way; scheduled and manually posted Reels enter ranking the same way (August 28, 2026). Official (secondhand: Shopify, Kontentino). https://www.shopify.com/ca/blog/time-post-instagram, https://www.kontentino.com/blog/does-scheduling-instagram-posts-hurt-reach/
- "First 30 minutes": no Instagram source for a 30-minute window. Hootsuite's early-engagement experiment, one account, two weeks, carousels: week two reached 3x week one and had 4.5x the engagement rate at one hour, with more non-follower reach. Measured, n = 1 account. https://blog.hootsuite.com/experiment-instagram-early-engagement/. The anonymous "ReachLab" regression (timing explains under 4 percent of reach variance) has no published method; ignore.
- For this account: the audience peak is 16:00 to 19:00 MT (Insights), the 11-03 note preferred 8 to 10 and 13 to 15 and avoided 16 to 20, and the 11-12 refresh logged a good 1:22 p.m. post and a weak 4:30 p.m. post. The datasets cannot settle this for 580 followers; the posted-time log can, by day 15.

**Format and follower conversion.** No 2026 dataset reports follower growth by format for accounts under 1,000; Dash Social sells it by brand size. What is published: Reels carry non-follower reach and shares, carousels carry views and saves in brand samples, images lose on every measure (Metricool: carousels 9x the saves of a single image, carousel views 1.69 percent above Reels, Reels 4x the interactions of a single image; one write-up has Reels at 29 percent more reach than carousels, which conflicts with the rest). The account's own data is sharper than any of it: static posts reach 94 to 136, a carousel reached 125 under a 389,000-reach reel, Reels are 99.6 percent of views. Rescroll's "Reels 3 to 5x the reach of carousels for accounts under 5K" is unsourced; the direction matches the account.

**Stories and the next Reel.** No official statement that posting Stories lifts a Reel. Stories are ranked separately; a Reel shared to a Story reaches followers on a second surface; Mosseri is reported as saying that re-sharing feed posts to Stories does not boost their reach. His standing advice is a couple of Stories a day for the connected audience. Official (secondhand) on the no-boost line, nothing measured. The Kontentino question "do story shares actually help?" exists at https://www.kontentino.com/q-and-a/instagram-reel-recommendations-lifespan/; the answer was not in the snippet.

**Pinned posts, grid, bio, highlights.** Mosseri's profile advice (German trade press, undated in the snippets): a recognisable profile photo with no text in it, pinned posts to control the top line, keywords in the name and bio for search, not too staged. Three pins maximum. Vendors add that pinned Reels autoplay muted and that most visitors never scroll past the first row; the "15 to 25 percent conversion lift from pinning" and the "0.8 to 9.4 percent" case have no source. Official (secondhand) for Mosseri; creator for the rest. https://onlinemarketing.de/social-media-marketing/instagram-impressions-profiloptimierung-mit-plattformchef, https://socialz.ai/blog/how-to-pin-a-post-on-instagram. The 18-account study in `research/2026-11-21-profile-conversion.md` stands.

**Deleting or archiving.** Mosseri: deleting an early underperformer is generally the wrong move, "there's no real downside in leaving it up"; hide it from the grid if it bothers you; old Trials were never on the profile, leave them. Official (secondhand, Kontentino). https://www.kontentino.com/q-and-a/should-i-delete-instagram-posts-that-do-not-perform-well-right-away/. Jake deleted three weak posts on November 3; no effect was measurable either way.

**Reposting your own old Reels.** The originality rules (April 30, 2024, extended April 30, 2026) target other people's content and aggregators. Your own old file re-uploaded is not that, but the duplicate matcher does see your own history (the Trial warning text proves it), so the identical file can be limited. The Reposts button is for others' posts and files them in the Reposts tab. Safe routes for your own old material: re-cut with a new opening, or share the original to a Story. Official on the rules; creator on the practice. https://www.lilachbullock.com/reposting-reels-on-instagram-allowed/, https://growcreator.pro/blog/does-reposting-hurt-reach-on-instagram. The "10 reposts in 30 days" count is unsourced (see the 05 file).

**Collab posts.** Emplifi, H1 2026, about 520,000 posts from about 3,800 brand profiles: creator-published Collabs outperform both ordinary brand posts and brand-published Collabs; "more than 5x the amplification" in ecommerce and retail; the metric is interactions and shares, not follows. Measured, brands. https://emplifi.io/resources/instagram-collaborations-boost-organic-engagement/. A 100-post academic study: Collab reach tracks a weighted average of the two accounts' individual reach potential, and timing matters. Measured, small. https://hstalks.com/article/11245/the-impact-of-collaborative-posts-on-digital-reach/. Vendor figures (2.2x reach, 47 percent more impressions in 24 h, 30 to 50 percent) are unverified. No source measures follows from Collabs. A Collab puts the post on both grids and in both follower feeds with both names on it, so the partner's audience is the gain and the partner's size sets it.

**Broadcast channels and Notes.** No verified benchmark for accounts under 50,000 (likesreal rejects the "40 percent open rate" and "Notes double profile visits" claims). Eligibility reports conflict (no minimum versus 1,000). Mosseri calls channels "a direct line to the people who are the most excited about what you do." They retain; they do not reach. Creator. https://likesreal.com/blog/instagram-notes-broadcast-channel-analytics-benchmarks-2026

**Confidence:** medium on cadence (large samples, but brands); low on hour of day (five datasets disagree, and Mosseri says it matters little); medium on format direction and nil on format-to-follow numbers for small accounts; medium on the Mosseri quotes, all secondhand; low on Collab and channel effects for an account this size.

## 4. Follower conversion: what turns a viewer into a follower

**What is known from outside.** Almost nothing with a number. No public benchmark exists for profile visits to follows; the vendor figures (13.5 percent average, 20 to 40 percent "with strong proof assets", 2 to 5 percent "profile conversion", 15 to 20 percent "strong fit") are unsourced and define the ratio differently. One vendor says Meta deprecated the profile views API metric on January 8, 2025; this account's own API pull on November 3, 2026 returned account-level profile views (657), so the account-level metric still exists; what does not exist is media-level follows or profile visits for Reels (tested, in CLAUDE.md). Instagram's in-app answer is coming: the Insights redesign in test since November 20 sorts content by follows. Mosseri, week of November 13: "Following counts still matter on Instagram more than they do on platforms like TikTok." Non-followers can follow from the reel itself (the follow button next to the username) without opening the profile, so follows can exceed profile visits.

**The account's own funnel is the only measured data that applies.**
- October 4 to November 3, 2026 (the viral month): reach 472,097; profile views 657 (0.14 percent of reach, 1.4 per 1,000); follows 249 (38 percent of profile views, an upper bound because of in-reel follows); follows per reach 0.053 percent. September baseline 0.037 percent.
- The two windows before that: September 4 to October 4, reach 66,469, profile views 122 (1.8 per 1,000), follows 16 (13 percent); August 5 to September 4, reach 46,916, profile views 130 (2.8 per 1,000), follows 25 (19 percent).
- November 3 to 12 (back to normal posting): reach 16,170; profile visits 151 (0.93 percent of reach, 9.3 per 1,000); follows 46 (30 percent); follows per reach 0.28 percent.
- Read: the profile converts three in ten who arrive. Normal content in November sent 6.6x more of its viewers to the profile than the viral meme did (0.93 against 0.14 percent) and 3 to 5x more than the pre-viral windows. The meme travelled as a send with no reason to tap the name; the talking heads give one. The lever is the tap rate, and the tap rate is a property of the reel, not the profile.
- Grid: Jake pinned the F600 intro and the October 23 reel on October 28; the third slot is reserved for a 20-second "start here" reel not yet filmed. Profile views converted at 13 and 19 percent before the rebuild and at 30 to 38 percent after it. That part is done.

**What the sources agree moves the tap, without numbers.** (1) A reason to come back: a series or a named next episode. Rachel Karten called series "a goldmine" in the Tessa Lyons call and Instagram is testing a Series format (October 28); the one vendor number, 0.5 to 1.2 percent view-to-follow for a series pointer against about 0.1 percent for "follow for more", is unsourced. (2) The profile matching the reel: the first row (three pins) and bio line 1 saying the same thing the reel just said. (3) A person in frame, which the 11-21 account study showed separates the growing single-truck pages from the stalled ones. (4) Trials, which by construction only reach non-followers, so every follow from one is new; Creatorhouse's 12.4 per Trial at 4,200 followers and Kapwing's +1 at a million views bracket how little views alone do.

## What it means for a 580-follower diesel creator posting two a day

Each rule says how to tell whether it is working.

1. Open the Reels composer on the phone and look for the Trial toggle before anything else. Test: the toggle is there (the 200 floor is confirmed for this account) or it is not; if not, open Account Status before blaming the follower count.
2. Trials are an in-app step for now. The Composio publish tool has no trial field. Test: one raw Graph call with `trial_params` through the proxy on a throwaway clip; until it works, the Trial is posted from the phone and the API posts only the shared Reels.
3. The second post of the day is a Trial of the first post's talking head with a different first sentence, not a second subject. Two first lines on the same take, auto-share off, 24 hours, judged on skip rate and sends per reach. Test: the loser's skip rate is at least 5 points worse; if the two are within 5 points, the hook was not the variable.
4. Never post the same file twice as a Trial. Change the spoken first line and the first shot or do not re-trial it. Test: no "limited reach" notice on any Trial this month.
5. Expect a Trial to show roughly double the strangers, a quarter of the engagement per view of the same reel posted normally, and under a dozen followers each. Test: after ten Trials, more than 5 followers per Trial means the format converts; under 2 means the hook test is working but the body is not.
6. Share a Trial to followers for the grid and for the connected audience, not for a second wave of reach. Test: the shared reel's likes per reach in the 48 hours after sharing against a normal post; if it is lower, the meme already reached them by DM and future winners get re-shot rather than shared.
7. Keep two Reels a day, zero static posts, one Story set, until the posted-time log fills in. Mosseri says ten a day is fine and timing matters little; the account's own data says static is dead. Test: by day 15 the log has at least ten posts in each of the two windows and the better window is chosen on skip rate and reach, not on a vendor table.
8. Count the tap, not the view. Track profile visits per 1,000 reach weekly by hand from Insights (the API has no per-reel follows). Test: the November 3 to 12 rate was 9.3 per 1,000; a talking head that beats 10 keeps its opening structure, a reel under 3 is a meme and is judged on sends only.
9. Treat Collabs as a borrowed audience, not a growth hack. One Collab a week with the shop account or a parts brand whose followers are the pickup audience. Test: followers gained in the 48 hours after a Collab against the same reel type posted alone.
10. Do not delete, archive or re-upload. Underperformers stay up (no downside), old Trials stay invisible, old Reels get re-cut with a new opening if they deserve a second run. Test: zero deletions in the 30-day plan; any "second run" has a new first three seconds and goes out as a Trial first.
