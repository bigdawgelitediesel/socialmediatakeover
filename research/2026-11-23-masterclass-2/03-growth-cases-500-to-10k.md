# 03. Growth cases, 500 to 10,000 followers: what the measured ones did, what "quality" means, and what multiplies reach without breaking the rules

Written 2026-11-22 for @elcoyotediesel (580 followers; 215 on Sep 15, 470 on Nov 3, 577 on Nov 21). Brief from Jacob: a plan to 10K that could work in as little as 30 days, with good followers, not just a number. This file extends `2026-11-21-diesel-growth-case-studies.md` (the twelve diesel accounts, their first 30 posts and breakout posts) and `2026-11-22-masterclass/07-the-masters.md` (long-hold formats). It does not repeat them; where a diesel case matters it is cited by section.

Method: 31 WebSearch calls (extended for 2025 to 2026 material, standard otherwise). Every WebFetch failed (rachelpedersen.com, creatorhouse.app, viralfy.ai, virev.ai all `getaddrinfo ENOTFOUND`), so no web page was read in full; every web number below comes from a search snippet and is graded accordingly. Grades: **measured** (creator-published counts with dates, a platform statement, a dataset with a stated sample, or this repo's own pulls), **claim** (a creator, coach or vendor says so, no sample or no dates), **folklore** (repeated with no source behind it). House rule applies throughout: no pods, no follow/unfollow, no bought followers, no bots, no scrapers, no loop giveaways.

The honest headline first: **no account with a verifiable number went from under 1,000 to 10,000 in 30 days without either a viral post or an audience carried in from elsewhere.** The fastest cases that publish their own numbers took 42 to 90 days and were a staircase: two or three posts did most of it. The plan this file supports is 10K by day 90 with a day-30 checkpoint, run as if the 30-day version were possible, because the posts that make the 90-day case are the same posts that would make the 30-day one.

## 1. The cases, and what each one says about the mechanism

**Rachel Pedersen, +13,200 in 30 days (claim).** Posted reels several times a day for two weeks, then eased off. One reel reached 2.4 million views. Her framing is four reel types, but the arithmetic is one post. She does not publish post-growth engagement. The transferable part is the front-loading: volume in the first two weeks to find the post that travels, then protect the floor.

**Kim Peretz, +10,000 in 8 days (claim with mechanics).** The step came from a format pivot, from video to saveable quote carousels, plus keyword-comment automation that moves commenters into DMs, plus Trial Reels. She credits more than 1,000 follows to several Trial Reels that passed 100K views. The mechanism is saves and sends on strangers, with the profile doing the close. Comment automation is a third-party tool and belongs to a product funnel, not to a diesel creator; the Trial Reels and the "saveable" criterion transfer.

**cosycreator, 3,000 to 10,000 in about 90 days, 20,000 by month 11 (claim with dates).** Sat under 2,000 for two years. The move was dropping forced daily video for carousels that travel, one of which reached 800K. Her stated lesson is that daily reels were not required. For this account the lesson is narrower: carousels are a stranger format in a visual niche; on @elcoyotediesel static posts average 116 views and reels are 99.6 percent of views (CLAUDE.md), so the format that travels here is already known.

**Pawan Bisnoi, 0 to 10,800 in four months (press, undated).** An electrician-mason posting ordinary jobs as 15 to 60 second reels, each at 100K plus views. The closest trade analog found. The press piece notes brands were slow to treat him as a channel, which is the "popularity without monetization" pattern. He is the proof that a trade shown plainly, at daily cadence, can clear 10K in a season without a persona.

**Transformation Junction, 1,228 to 2,346 in 90 days of daily posting (measured).** The most useful honest case in the set because it is small and complete: 90 posts, three reels did most of the 1,118 gain, the rest trickled. The author's conclusion is that reel quality beat frequency. The repo's reading is slightly different: the frequency bought the 90 at-bats that produced the three hits, and the hits were the posts a stranger could use. Expect this shape.

**VeganTechNomad, 70,000 in two weeks (vendor case, Aug 2024).** Daily posting for one to two months that "didn't take off," then three viral posts in a December did 70K Instagram followers and 14K newsletter subscribers in two weeks. Her operational change was scheduling TikTok content to every platform at once. Start count not given, so the multiple is unknown. Same staircase.

**Collab anecdotes, 400 to 2,100 in three weeks and +420 in a week at 12K (claims).** Both say the same thing: a Collab with an account slightly larger than yours, weekly, after a plateau. Neither has a control. They line up with the one dataset on Collabs (section 3).

**The in-repo cases.** Robert hit 45K Instagram followers 14 days into a six-a-day TikTok run of reaction content (2022; a different platform era, but the mechanics in section 5 of the case studies file still hold). Jessica Carter's 40 talking heads in six weeks are the only measured curve of a daily one-seat series, and they show views falling while likes per view rose 2.4x before the account compounded to 250K. Mariah's 114K to 436K came from one utility reel, not from the series she is known for. Smiths and DDC took 19 and 29 months to reach 12.6K and about 10K on Instagram while growing on TikTok, which says a diesel page that is not working Instagram directly does not get Instagram followers quickly.

**What the fast cases publish about follower quality afterwards: almost nothing.** Pedersen, Peretz, cosycreator and the vendor cases give no post-growth engagement rate or comments per post. The only measured after-numbers in the whole set are the repo's own: Carter's likes per view rising as views fell; Mariah's 49,968 likes and 941 comments on a craft reel to a 436K audience (11.5 percent of her followers liked it, which is a strong audience); the Diesel Queen and Cummins Cowgirl sponsor feeds at 1.5K to 7.5K views on 433K to 521K followers (0.3 to 1.7 percent of followers viewing, which is a dead audience).

**Confidence:** low on the 30-day cases (all claims, none with start counts and after-numbers together), medium on the 90-day shape (one small measured case, three cohorts and the repo's own curves agree), high that the growth is a staircase carried by two or three posts.

## 2. What "quality followers" means operationally, and the 2026 benchmarks

Quality is not a feeling. For this account it is five ratios measured weekly plus two checks done by hand.

**The ratios (all available from Insights or the API at 580 followers):**

1. **Likes per reach and comments per reach on the last 10 posts against the 10 before.** These are two of Mosseri's three named ranking signals (watch time, likes per reach, sends per reach, repeated unchanged since January 2025). If followers are the right people, these hold or rise as reach rises. Carter's measured curve is the model: views down, likes per view up 2.4x.
2. **Sends per reach.** Mosseri: sends "matter a ton" for unconnected reach; likes count slightly more for connected reach. No published benchmark exists for sends per reach at this size; the only per-reach figures found were saves per reach 1.2 percent average and 3.8 percent top quartile, shares per reach 2.5 percent top quartile (SearchLab and Upgrow, both vendor tables, unverified). The account's own baseline is the number to beat.
3. **Stories reach as a share of followers.** Socialinsider 2025 data via Upgrow: accounts with 1K to 5K followers reach 9.55 to 10.40 percent of followers per Story; 100K to 1M reach 0.50 to 0.65 percent. Other vendors put small accounts at 5 to 15 percent. A follower who never opens a Story is a follower in name. Target: at or above 10 percent through the growth, measured on the Monday Story each week.
4. **Follower geography and age against the brief.** The audience is 87 percent US, 76 percent male, 51 percent aged 25 to 44 (CLAUDE.md). Pull demographics every two weeks (`period=lifetime` plus `timeframe`, one breakdown per call). If the US share falls below 80 percent or the male share below 65 percent during a growth week, the post that drove it brought the wrong room. HypeAuditor's "Quality Audience" is the same idea for brands: the share of followers who are real people and not mass-followers or suspicious accounts, scored into an Audience Quality Score where 40 to 59 is average and under 25 is low. Brands pitching this account will look at that number.
5. **Follows minus unfollows per week, by hand from Insights.** No published churn benchmark exists for small accounts; the only formula offered is unfollows divided by starting followers. The one measured churn figure anywhere is for giveaway followers: a 52-account study put a 71 percent probability that more than half of giveaway followers leave within two weeks (Social Media Examiner). Organic followers should churn at a small fraction of that; if weekly unfollows exceed 2 percent of the base during growth, look at what the growth post promised.

**The hand checks:**

6. **Profile visits to follows, weekly.** No vendor benchmark survives scrutiny (Meta deprecated the profile views API metric on Jan 8, 2025; vendor claims run from 2 to 5 percent up to 13.5 percent and "20 to 40 percent with strong proof assets"). This account measured 38 percent in the October wave (`2026-11-21-profile-conversion.md`). That number is the asset; the hole is reach-to-visit, not visit-to-follow.
7. **Twenty new followers, opened.** Once a week open 20 of the newest followers. Count how many have a truck, a shop, a field or a job in their own grid, how many are US, how many follow more than 3,000 accounts (the follow-churner profile from the $10 experiment). Write the three counts in the measurement log. This is the only quality check that sees what the API cannot.

How to read it for this account: the brand-page benchmarks are the floor, not the target. @elcoyotediesel's PERSONAL posts ran 14.80 percent engagement on about 1,127 views (CLAUDE.md), which is far above every brand and creator tier figure above, though the denominators differ (that figure is per view, the benchmarks are per follower). ## 3. Legitimate reach multipliers for a small account in 2026

**Collabs (co-authored posts).** The one dataset: virev measured 4.5 million Collabs in 2026 and found Collab reels got about 1.52x the views of the same creator's other reels after controlling for length, caption, hashtags and posting time (measured, vendor, primary not read). Emplifi's H1 2026 Instagram analysis found Collab posts, especially creator-published reels, outperform standard brand posts, with the smallest profiles seeing the largest relative uplift (measured, association only). Follower effects are anecdotes: +420 in a week at 12K, 400 to 2,100 in three weeks on weekly Collabs with slightly larger accounts (claims). How the post distributes: it sits on both profiles and goes to both followings, with one caption, the inviter's. The repo's own measurement is the strongest piece here: across 19 diesel brand grids, 26 percent of tiles (60 of 228) are co-authored with a creator; Derale 75 percent, Power Hungry 67, BD and @dieseltrucking 42, FASS, Industrial Injection and Axiom 33 (`2026-11-21-partner-repostability.md`). Brands feature creators by accepting Collab invites, not by reposting. A Collab accepted by FASS (290K) or @dieseltrucking (1.4M) is a feature with Jacob's line 1 as the brand's post. Peer Collabs with accounts 1x to 3x his size (the 7.3 OBS tribe, Binns Boys runs its Instagram this way with @obs.solutions) are the repeatable weekly version. Grade: measured for views, claim for follows.

**Aggregator features and the native Repost.** The live diesel aggregator is @dieseltrucking (1.4M, near daily, 5 of 12 recent tiles creator-authored, DieselTrucking@yahoo.com); @dieselsellerz is dormant since Sep 2024, @dieseltrucks is a dead handle, @dieselnation_ is a pay-to-play reupload page that Instagram's April 30, 2026 originality policy demotes (partner-repostability file). What they take: the part doing something, a clean roller, a record, a first fire, with the creator's caption underneath; nobody in that set reposts talking heads. Credit: the Collab tag or an "Owner: @" line, as DDC does for every member clip. Instagram's own Repost feature (launched Aug 2025 per about.fb.com) credits the original poster, can be recommended to the reposter's followers who do not follow the creator, and per two secondary reports attributes views and engagement to the original; Later adds that heavy reposters may lose reach under the originality rule. No published number anywhere on follows from a feature. The repo's own count of what happened when brands co-authored (Derale's grid at 75 percent creator tiles) is the best available evidence that it is a real channel. Grade: measured for mechanics and for who does it, no data on conversion.

**Duets and stitches on TikTok as a feeder.** No 2025 study isolates the effect; "+20 percent duet and stitch boost" and "5,000 to 20,000 followers from one duet" are folklore. The measured evidence is in the case studies file: gearman's stitch at post 15 did 360K (prior posts 10K to 60K), Robert's duet 1.0M, the Diesel Queen's stitch 24M. A stitch is a breakout format because the other creator's audience is delivered to you. Whether it feeds Instagram is a different question, below.

**Cross-posting the same reel.** viral.app, 3.3M videos, October 2025 to August 2026: among about 1,900 accounts active on both TikTok and Instagram, the share of TikToks with an identical Reel within 72 hours rose from 27 to 34 percent; the Reel got more views in 57 percent of pairs; when the creator's Instagram had no more followers than their TikTok, the TikTok did better (measured, vendor). Creafico's 2026 TikTok benchmark: accounts under 1K followers sit at a median 327 views per video, 60 to 119 second videos do best (`2026-11-21-crossposting-decision.md`). Visible third-party logos get less distribution on Instagram (Mosseri, Oct 30, 2026, same file). **No source measures what share of TikTok, Shorts or Facebook followers come back to Instagram**; the in-repo decision stands: yes to TikTok, Shorts and the Facebook toggle at 5 to 10 minutes a day, measure 30 days, cut per the kill rules, and do not expect it to move the Instagram count inside the window. Grade: measured for views, nothing for follow-back.

**Commenting with substance on large accounts.** No controlled test exists. One magazine claims 20 to 30 percent reach increases within two weeks for accounts that comment before posting (claim, no method). An arXiv preprint on Instagram comments notes a comment on a large account is buried among tens of thousands. The measured number in this family is on the creator's own posts: Buffer's dataset shows replying to comments lifts engagement 21 percent, and Metricool says reels pull most comments in the first hour (profile-conversion file). Verdict: the reply habit is measured and cheap; commenting on strangers' big posts is unmeasured and should be done only where Jacob has something to say as a parts guy.

**Going Live with a peer.** Requirements: host and every guest at 1,000 followers, public account, up to three guests; each guest's followers are notified when the guest joins (mechanics, consistent across sources). Guides suggest partners within two to three times each other's size (claim). No published follower data for small accounts. Locked for this account until 1,000 in any case.

**Giveaways.** The one measured piece: across 52 accounts and six giveaways, a 71 percent probability that more than half of the followers gained leave within two weeks, effective cost per follower rising from 6 to 9 cents to 16 to 23 cents, engagement lower two weeks after (Social Media Examiner, medical niche, small sample, measured). Loop giveaways add demographic pollution on top. In the diesel set, DDC ran a 10K giveaway and Smiths runs them with a local smoke show, as rewards to an audience already there. Not a growth engine, and the house rule already says no.

**Threads as a feeder.** Vendor claims only: accounts with 10K plus Instagram followers grow 4.1x faster on Threads than accounts from zero (127-creator analysis, method loose); 20 to 100 Threads followers from an existing Instagram audience; a "10 to 25 percent conversion" with no source. The only concrete mechanism is one-time: joining Threads notifies your Instagram followers. Nothing measures Threads sending followers to Instagram. The in-repo decision is no to Threads video; this pass does not change it.

**Confidence:** medium on Collabs (one large views dataset, the repo's own brand-grid count, consistent anecdotes), medium on the mechanics of features and reposts, low on everything else in this section because nobody has measured follow-through.

## 4. The series and the hook test as growth engines

**What the web adds (thin).** A 100K creator's own account: recurring series "give your audience something to look forward to" and give the creator a framework, and some of the best videos were spontaneous (claim). The daily-challenge literature is mixed: one creator posted daily reels for 30 days and gained 20 to 30 followers; another ended a 30-day course at 12 followers; the PT studio gained 1,118 in 90 days with three posts doing most of it. The Outfame cohort (981 accounts) shows the typical growing account had 62 percent of its 90-day gain by day 30, which is front-loading, not compounding. No "day N of" case with a published follow curve surfaced.

**What the repo already measured, which is stronger.** Carter: 40 talking heads in six weeks from one seat, views down then likes per view up 2.4x, then the series lifted the floor and a launch set the ceiling (250K by month five). Kearney: 150 sofa videos in 74 days, median 130 s, median 18,100 views. Cothran: one cab video a day for three years. Zac's rating series moved his quarterly median from 6.5K to 71.5K to 279K in two quarters. Robert's roast series carried the audience loop in the caption from post 1. Nate's Rant is a named segment with a named character. Dentside Depot's "simple reason why Ford did X" ran six times a week for three years.

How the creators describe the compounding, in their own words where the repo has them: the format exists to make the cadence possible (the masters file, finding 11); the set does the branding so the edit does not have to; the audience supplies the next subject ("send in yours", "what should I yap about next"). The compounding is not the series going viral; it is the series producing enough at-bats, in a shape strangers recognize in under a second, for the two or three hits to arrive and for the profile to show a stranger what tomorrow's post will be.

**Trial Reels in those cases.** Meta's June 2025 analysis of more than 400,000 active Reels creators: 40 percent posted more often after using trials, and 80 percent of that group saw more non-follower reach (measured, platform statement, secondhand). One 14-day single-account test, 10 trials against 10 normal reels: 2.3x non-follower reach, 0.6x engagement, 12.4 net new followers per trial reel, no normal-reel comparator (creatorhouse.app, measured, tiny). Kim Peretz credits 1,000 plus follows to several trials over 100K views (claim). A brand reports 5 to 7K followers from reposting old content as trials (claim). justyn.ai auto-posts hook variants as trials and keeps the winner (PLAYBOOK-ADDENDUM item 40). Eligibility is contested: 1,000 followers in most guides, 200 for professional accounts in four late-2026 guides; the composer is the only answer. The hook test the house already runs (one variable per reel, two line-1 variants, winner to followers after 24 h) is exactly what Trial Reels make cheap.

**Confidence:** high that a fixed-set daily series is the delivery mechanism (five measured curves in the repo agree), medium on Trial Reels (one platform statement, one tiny test), low on any claim that a series by itself produced a follow curve.

## 5. What stalls accounts between 1K and 10K

1. **Cadence collapses to the median.** The 1K to 10K median is 0.45 percent a month (2,073 creators) and 0.9 to 1.7 percent (Socialinsider brands); accounts posting seven or more reels a week ran 16.2 percent a month (about 7,900 accounts). Both are vendor datasets, and the gap is too wide to be sampling. Grade: measured.
2. **Posting for followers instead of strangers.** Reels reach only 9.78 percent of followers at 1K to 5K (Socialinsider), so nine tenths of every post's reach is strangers by construction. Sends per reach is the signal for unconnected reach (Mosseri). A post written for the 580 who already follow has no sender. Grade: measured for reach shares, platform statement for the signal.
3. **Topic drift.** Instagram's "Your Algorithm" topic controls launched on Reels in December 2025 and Explore in April 2026, and viewers who remove a topic become hard to reach regardless of engagement (Buffer and SocialPilot, vendor summaries of a platform feature). Content that jumps between unrelated topics is harder to classify (Meet Edgar, claim). No Mosseri statement says off-topic posting hurts; the topic controls make it so mechanically. For this account the drift risk is the opposite of the usual: not too many topics but the 7.3 crowding out the relatable talking heads that Jacob said should breathe.
4. **Sponsor content too early.** This account: SPONSOR 1.36 percent engagement against PERSONAL 14.80 on the same reach, sponsor skip rate 72.5 against 48.2 (CLAUDE.md, measured). Diesel Queen, Cummins Cowgirl and gearman kept view spikes on paid posts and lost the organic median (case studies file, measured by quarterly medians). Favikon, 100 creators: sponsored posts average 56.5 percent less engagement than the creator's organic posts, smallest gap for micro creators (LinkedIn, measured). Digiday 2026: sponsored posts' view advantage is paid boost, not audience (measured). Emplifi 2026: Collab posts outperform brand posts (measured). The pattern: a sponsor inside a job, an event or a Collab holds; a sponsor as the post does not.
5. **Incentivized followers.** Giveaway followers leave within two weeks (section 3); bought followers are "follow-churners" (the $10 experiment, claim); one 0-to-10K blog admits buying 1,000 early, which is what makes its numbers useless. Grade: measured for giveaways.
6. **Inconsistent set, no series.** The 30-day daily-posting failures (20 to 30 followers, 12 followers) posted daily without a repeatable shape; the measured risers (Carter, Kearney, Cothran, Fisher) never moved the camera. Grade: measured curves on one side, anecdotes on the other.
7. **No reply habit.** Replying lifts engagement 21 percent (Buffer) and comments arrive in the first hour (Metricool). An account that posts at 17:00 and checks at 22:00 has missed the window every day. Grade: measured.
8. **Volume without a floor.** Creafico's TikTok data: three or more posts a day cut per-video views 89 percent with the same weekly total; a vendor claim that Instagram tracks an "average score" is unconfirmed. Grade: measured for TikTok, claim for Instagram.
9. **Third-party logos and reposted feeds.** A visible app logo gets less distribution (Mosseri, Oct 30, 2026); accounts that repost heavily may lose reach (Later). Grade: platform statement.
10. **Mistaking the staircase for a slope.** The PT studio, Pedersen, VeganTechNomad and the Outfame cohort all show two or three posts and a long trickle. Creators who read the trickle as failure change format at day 20 and reset the experiment. Grade: measured.

**Confidence:** high on items 1, 2, 4, 7 and 10 (measured, two sources each), medium on 3 and 9 (platform features reported secondhand), low on 6 and 8 for Instagram specifically.

## The mechanics that recur

1. **Growth is a staircase.** In every case with its own numbers, two or three posts carried most of the gain and the rest trickled (PT studio 3 of 90, VeganTechNomad 3, Pedersen 1, Outfame's 62 percent by day 30, every breakout in the diesel set).
2. **Cadence buys the at-bats, it does not produce the hits.** Seven plus reels a week ran 16.2 percent a month against 0.45 for the median account; daily posting with nothing a stranger can use produced 12 to 30 followers in 30 days.
3. **The posts that moved counts were made for strangers.** A side taken, a question, a number, a thing failing, a reaction to someone else's truck. The caption line a follower would enjoy is not the one a stranger sends.
4. **The step came with a format pivot, not a schedule change.** Carter to one-seat talking heads, Peretz to saveable posts, cosycreator to carousels, Caylee to repair sagas, Zac to ratings. Each found a shape and kept it.
5. **The profile closes at 38 percent once a stranger arrives.** The measured hole on this account is reach to profile tap, not tap to follow. The pinned comment and the first-hour reply are the two measured conversion tools.
6. **Borrowed audiences are the only multiplier with data behind it.** Collab reels at 1.52x views; stitches as the breakout post in three diesel accounts; brands featuring creators through accepted Collab invites on a quarter of their tiles.
7. **Quality is a ratio per reach, measured weekly, plus twenty opened profiles.** Totals lie during growth; likes per reach, sends per reach and Stories view share do not.
8. **Incentivized followers leave.** More than half of giveaway followers gone in two weeks; bought followers never engage; both pollute the targeting that is this account's only asset.
9. **A sponsor as the post kills the median; a sponsor inside a job holds it.** Measured on this account (1.36 against 14.80) and on three large diesel feeds.
10. **Topic controls make drift expensive and make the lane valuable.** A viewer who removes a topic is gone regardless of engagement; a tight lane is how the right viewers keep arriving.
11. **Cross-platform spillover is unmeasured and slow.** A new TikTok sits at 327 median views; nobody has published a follow-back share; mirror at zero cost and expect nothing inside 30 days.
12. **The fast tier is 60 to 90 days, not 30.** 42 days is the fastest cohort case to 10K, 163 the average; every 30-day claim rests on one viral post or an audience brought from elsewhere.

## What it means for El Coyote at 580 followers

1. **Write the target as 10,000 by day 90 with a day-30 checkpoint of 1,500 to 2,500, and run the 30 days as if 10K were possible.** The arithmetic: 9,420 follows in 30 days is 314 a day; at the September reach-to-follow rate of 0.037 percent that needs 25 million reach, at 1 percent about a million. The posts that would do it in 30 are the same posts that do it in 90.
2. **Seven or more reels a week, every one with a line 1 a stranger can send.** Jacob's four-a-day plan already clears the cadence band; the test for each reel is whether someone with any truck, or no truck, sees himself in it (Jacob's 11-22 rule), which is the stranger test in other words.
3. **Two of every seven are reactions: a submitted truck rated, a stitch of a claim, a hater read deadpan.** The measured breakout family in the diesel set, and the only format that reaches Cummins and Duramax viewers without changing the truck.
4. **The cab talking head is the series that supplies the at-bats.** Same seat, same mount, same open, 40 to 60 s, graded on watch share and likes per reach. Expect views down and likes per view up for the first month and do not touch the format when it happens; that is Carter's measured curve.
5. **Trial Reels on every post if the composer shows the toggle.** Two line-1 variants, winner to followers after 24 hours, net new follows per trial logged against the one published figure (12.4). If the toggle is not there at 580, this is the first concrete reason to want 1,000.
6. **One Collab invite a week to an account between one and three times his size, and one a month to a brand that already co-authors (Derale, FASS, Power Hungry, @dieseltrucking's creators).** The Collab is the feature; the caption is his. No reposts of other people's clips on his own grid.
7. **Measure quality every Monday:** likes per reach, comments per reach, sends per reach on the last ten posts; Stories reach as a share of followers (hold 10 percent); follows minus unfollows; profile visits to follows by hand (38 percent baseline); demographics every two weeks against 87 percent US, 76 percent male, 51 percent 25 to 44; twenty new followers opened and counted.
8. **No giveaways, no loops, no shoutout buys, no engagement groups, in any week, at any count.** DDC's 10K giveaway was a thank-you to a room already full. The house rule and the measured churn agree.
9. **Partners appear inside an install or a job, tagged, as a Collab invite; no thank-you reels until the median reel passes 5,000 views.** Sponsor-as-post ran 195 to 347 views at 66 to 75 percent skip on this account.
10. **Reply to every comment inside the first hour and pin one comment that names the series and tomorrow's post.** It is the only "commenting strategy" with a measured lift, it costs ten minutes at 17:00 and 18:00, and it is the part of the funnel that turns a stranger's visit into the 38 percent.
