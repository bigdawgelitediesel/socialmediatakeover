# Hooks and retention: the editor's playbook for @elcoyotediesel

Date: 2026-11-20. Lane: what the editor does in frames 1 to 90, how to re-hook, how to pay off, how to end for loops and sends. Sources: six hook transcripts in the scratchpad (Kallaway, heyDominik, Jade Beason, Will Carmack, HimanshuG, Chriss Boustedt) plus web research. Account context: 577 followers, meme reels share at 2 to 4 percent of reach, build reels hold 10 to 22 s but do not share, skip rate averages 48 percent (best posts near 32). Renderer: `Desktop/VIDEO/tools/build_reel2.py` driven by an EDL JSON (slots, audio beds, ASS captions).

## 1. Findings

**A hook has one job and two outputs.** Kallaway (1.2M views, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2byPP_9F0-Q): the hook only has to deliver topic clarity and on-target curiosity. Four failure modes, in order of how easy they are to fix: delay (topic not stated in the first 1 to 2 s; move line three to line one), confusion (sixth-grade words, active voice, only one way to read it), irrelevance (say "you/your" not "I/my", agitate a pain the viewer already has), disinterest (no contrast between what they believe, A, and what you offer, B). Hooks are two to three lines: clarity in line one, contrast in lines two to three. Contrast can be stated (A vs B spoken) or implied (B only, A is obvious). Note for us: vague "you won't believe this" openers only work when the face, the emotion or the text hook carries them; the words add nothing.

**The hook is a moment, not a sentence.** Jade Beason (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dNT7gd3ulAg): words plus visuals plus pacing. Words must be specific, surprising, a little dramatic ("drama test"). Visuals: movement toward camera, word-by-word captions that appear as spoken, a quick snapshot of what is coming, one unusual angle. Pacing: no dead air, no millennial pause, but a deliberate pause used once for effect lands. Repurposed footage usually needs a separately shot hook. The foreshadowing trend (open on the fall, then the events that led to it) worked because the template forced the most exciting frame to the front.

**First frame is a composition problem.** heyDominik (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xqy9L-88wcw): swipers are on autopilot and often swipe past then swipe back, which he treats as a ranking trigger. Context must sit on the first frame; keep text inside the safe zone and smaller than you think; declutter the frame so the subject reads; blur or hide the part of the frame that would answer the question so the viewer cannot close the loop themselves; the text hook should challenge a belief or plant one question ("this coffee should not taste good..."). Open loops, close one, open another.

**Four curiosity engines and the pattern break.** HimanshuG (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fPfPOQYl924): information gap (Loewenstein 1994) built four ways: tease the insight, challenge a common belief, withhold the payoff (the yo-yo: pull them toward the result and back), and the reward claim (works, borders on clickbait). Second block is biology: the orienting response to a pattern break, delivered through video, text, sound or voice. His retention-edit cadence: a frame change, pause or pattern break every 3 to 10 s. Mantra: say it, show it, write it.

**Editing moves that read as effort.** Chriss Boustedt (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ucJLVa6PGdI), CTV workflow: Camera movement (keyframed zoom out or whip in on an adjustment layer, bezier eased, 360 shutter for motion blur), Typography (stacked word-by-word text that slides up 4 frames with opacity 30 to 100), VFX (a 2-frame black-and-white flash on the cut, a riser into the transition and a click on it, kept around -10 dB). A desaturated first 6 frames that bloom to full color is a subtle open. Will Carmack (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rwsLUKw1n54): MrBeast devices are all about pointing at the thing that matters: animated dashed path lines (trim paths, ease out, glow), a circle splash on impact that expands fast and thins to nothing, a light sweep over the hero object, typewriter-on text tracked to a head with a slight wiggle, and a name/label tag tracked above a person. Every one is a label-the-frame move an editor can do after the fact.

**Jenny Hoyos.** Hook no longer than 3 s and often 1 s, rewrite the whole idea if the hook is weak, power words (free, cheap, banned, $1), foreshadow right after the hook (what is coming, what the conflict is, what you get), "but/then" turns to build tension, the ending judged by the intensity of emotion at the close, aim for 90 percent retention (https://www.marketingexamined.com/blog/jenny-hoyos-short-form-video-playbook). vidIQ's summary of her: visual shock, clear setup, promised payoff, and no satisfaction without a proper payoff (https://x.com/vidIQ/status/1867932188991221911).

**Paddy Galloway.** 5,400 shorts across 33 channels: the metric is viewed vs swiped away; the best keep 70 to 90 percent past the first beat, under 60 percent rarely performs; "make your first second really punchy" (https://twitter.com/PaddyG96/status/1646898359808974851). Most shorts in that study ran 20 to 40 s.

**MrBeast production doc, applied.** Zero-second hook ("the first sentence earns the right to explain"), promise stack of 3 to 6 previews, stakes locked, a scheduled re-engagement beat rather than one found in the graph later, every loop opened must close, end bigger than the title implied (https://influencernotes.com/blog/how-mrbeast-keeps-viewers-watching-retention-analysis, https://passionfru.it/mrbeast-leaked-document-81151/). Compressed to a reel: hook by 1 s, promise by 3 s, one scheduled reset, biggest payoff last.

**Hormozi.** Hook, Retain, Reward is the content unit; retain via a list, steps or a story; the reward must settle the promise the hook made, and a unit's reward can open the next unit's hook (https://www.shortform.com/summary/100m-leads-summary-alex-hormozi). The visual/verbal/text three-layer split is general practice, not his (https://www.whaaat.ai/blog/social-media-hooks-verbal-visual-subliminal-hook-examples, https://vidiq.com/blog/post/viral-video-hooks-youtube-shorts/).

**Hook pattern libraries.** Mustafa Alfredji's ten (open loop, callout, contrarian claim, correction, expert confession, result first, numbered promise, story cold-open, stakes, pattern interrupt) with the rule that the payoff must deliver more than the hook promised or it becomes clickbait (https://mustafaalfredji.com/hooks/). vidIQ's 18 for shorts, with 3 to 7 words on screen, 5 to 10 spoken, text in the top third, and "mirror the final shot back to the opening shot" for loops (https://vidiq.com/blog/post/viral-video-hooks-youtube-shorts/).

**Pattern interrupt cadence.** Nobody has platform data; the practitioner range is a visual change every 1.5 to 5 s, with a warning that cuts faster than every 2 s feel exhausting and that unmotivated glitches cost trust. CapCut's own guide: for a 15 to 30 s video use two or three interrupts (opening, middle reset, final takeaway); rhythm is interrupt, explain, prove, reset; audio interrupts go at decision points (first frame, problem reveal, before/after, drop to silence before a reveal); "if every cut has a sound effect, nothing feels surprising" (https://www.capcut.com/create/pattern-interrupt-technique-short-form-video, https://www.opus.pro/blog/tiktok-length-format-retention-data, https://blitzcutai.com/blog/tiktok-hooks-stop-scroll). Opus's unsourced numbers: mid-video re-hooks at about 15 and 30 s, no static shot over 5 s, text on screen at least 2 s, hold the final frame 1 to 2 s.

**Curiosity gap backfire.** Consistent across sources, none with hard data: a vague gap reads as bait and produces a drop near 10 to 12 s; a gap resolved in the first 10 s "gives permission to leave"; the fix is specificity and a payoff that matches or beats the tension (https://highstyle.ai/insights/viral-hook-frameworks, https://www.merra.ai/blog/curiosity-gap-examples). Retention-curve reading: cliff = hook failed, slow bleed = pacing, mid crater at 30 to 60 percent = content gap or B-roll not matching audio, spike then collapse = payoff did not match promise, flat line above 50 percent at the end = replays (https://instavar.com/research/production-systems/youtube-shorts-retention-curve-guide-2026).

**Loops.** Reels replay automatically and replays fold into views, no separate count (https://socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-updates-metrics-to-focus-creators-on-views/723645/). An Emplifi figure cited secondhand puts sub-7 s seamless loops at +18.7 percent replay rate (https://virvid.ai/blog/looping-structure-shorts-retention-2026, unverified). Loops suit entertainment, not tutorials, because the value is extracted by the end (https://wreltik.com/en/learn/reels/psychology-of-looping-video).

**What 2026 ranking rewards.** Watch time, likes per reach, sends per reach, Mosseri January 2025 and restated through 2026; sends matter more for non-followers; comments are "not the most important part of value by a long shot" (repo doc 2026-11-20-instagram-ranking-and-capcut-recipe.md; https://www.pepperagency.com/blog/every-instagram-update-adam-mosseri-has-shared-in-2026---updated-october-2026). Share-rate bands from a vendor (treat as rough): under 0.5 percent forgotten, 1 to 2 percent is where unconnected reach starts, 3 percent plus is viral; recipient-framed and situational send prompts work, "tag 3 friends" and "send to 5 people" trip the bait filter (https://creatorlanehq.com/blog/instagram-sends-per-reach-2026). Brock Johnson's type-matched share asks are already in research/2026-09-15-brock-hooks-formats-digest.md.

**Automotive hooks that worked.** "I just doubled the reliability of my old Ford truck with one single part," said while holding up a second key, then the $2,000 reason, then teardown time-lapse, then the clean coolant a week later; 1.4M views (https://www.dailydot.com/news/how-2k-and-60-hours-of-work-can-fix-a-ford-powerstroke/). TheStradman's grandpa truck: sold for $500, three-year rebuild, mom drives it (https://supercarblondie.com/utah-man-restores-family-gmc-truck-for-mom/). The twisted-frame Silverado that was a meme until people learned it was a man's only ride; the reveal turned mockery into $26,500 of donations (https://www.carscoops.com/2026/01/viral-silverado-road-legal-replaced-indiana/). The repo's diesel scan already logged the Westen number hook ("29 year old engine. 67 year old truck. 0 regrets."), the rate-the-swap face format, and the Nov 7 "359 days" reel that got shares at a 52 percent skip, which means the subject works and the hook did not.

## 2. Hook taxonomy, written for this account

Each hook is three layers: the frame (visual), the line on screen (text, by frame 4), and the first sound (audio). Fill the blanks.

1. Quoted dialogue (Harley Trade pattern). Text: "[real question a stranger asked]?" over a push-in on the truck. Audio: the actual answer. "how much you pay for that truck?" / "what's it gonna be when it's done?" / "why not an LS?"
2. Price and number. Text: "$[number] [thing] in a $[number] truck." / "[year] engine. [year] truck. [number] regrets." Audio: the loudest mechanical sound in the clip.
3. Trade and sacrifice. "I gave up my [Harley / weekends / garage] for a [1959 Ford]." Payoff is the shot that justifies it.
4. Nobody tells you. "Nobody tells you a 7.3 swap starts with [a frame, not an engine]." Must pay off with the specific thing in under 8 s.
5. Belief challenge (Kallaway contrast). "Everyone says [LS swap it]. I'm putting a [27-year-old Ford diesel] in it." Stated A vs B.
6. Callout. "If your project has sat more than [a year], this is for you." / "Send this to the guy who said it'd be running by spring." Also works as the end card.
7. Result first, then how. Open on the finished part or the one clean shot, text "[part] done. Here's what it took." Rewind to the mess.
8. Expert confession. "[X] years at a diesel parts counter. [Number] things I'd never do to a 7.3." Credential, then list.
9. Stakes and clock. "SEMA is in [N] days. The truck is [in this many pieces]." Big number top-left, motion under it (the Nunley countdown).
10. Story cold-open mid-motion. Start on the hand slapping the fender, the hood dropping, the engine hoist taking weight. Text: "So the [frame swap] just became real."
11. Before/after flash. First 0.4 s is the payoff frame (rendered part, running engine), hard cut to the "before" with text "[time] earlier."
12. Correction. "Stop [buying a tune first]. Do [filters and fuel] first." Short, shop-voice, no hedge.
13. Put a finger down. "Put a finger down if a customer has ever described his truck by its color." Long, specific, true, twist at the end.
14. Rate it with my face. "Rating [engine swaps / exhaust tips / lift pumps] for a 1959 Ford." One-second cuts, the last one cuts to the real build.
15. The quiet flex. Text only, no music: "[Number] shares last week were about a truck that doesn't run yet." Honest, specific, meme-cadence.
16. Timely day-three angle. "Everyone's talking about [SEMA truck / recall]. Here's [the part number and what it costs]."
17. I need your help. "Help me settle this. [Question with two real camps]." Comment and send both.
18. The sound-first hook. Zero text for 10 frames, only the [starter crank / air chisel / turbo spool], then the text lands on the first silence.

## 3. Frame-by-frame spec, first 3 seconds (30 fps)

- Frame 0 (0.00 s): subject already in motion or the camera already moving. Never a static wide, never a logo. Punch-in starts here (zoom 1.00 to 1.08 across the slot). Optional: saturation 0.6 blooming to 1.0 by frame 6.
- Frames 0 to 3 (0.00 to 0.10 s): first sound is the loudest or most specific sound in the clip, not music. If the clip has no good sound, a single low click on frame 0 and nothing else.
- Frame 4 (0.13 s): the text hook is fully on screen. One line, 3 to 7 words, top third, inside the safe zone, never over the subject's face or the comment column. If the line is two beats, beat one at frame 4, beat two at frame 20 (0.67 s). Pop in (scale 0.92 to 1.0 over 3 frames), no slide longer than 4 frames.
- Frames 4 to 30 (0.13 to 1.0 s): the viewer decides. The frame must answer "what is this" (topic clarity) and the text must open one question (curiosity). If the visible frame already answers the question, blur, crop or punch past the answer.
- Frame 30 to 45 (1.0 to 1.5 s): first pattern change. Either a hard cut to a second angle, a punch to a detail, or the first spoken word with karaoke captions starting. The 2026 benchmark the repo already logged: passing reels held 58 percent at 1.5 s versus 35 percent for failing ones, and the whole gap opens here.
- Frames 45 to 90 (1.5 to 3.0 s): the foreshadow. Spoken or on-screen: what is coming and what the conflict is ("but" or "and it's already apart"). The hook text leaves by frame 78 (2.6 s) at the latest; the first payoff beat (the answer, the number, the reveal) must land by 4.5 to 5.5 s. Harley Trade lands "I traded my Harley for it" at 4.8 s, which is right on the line; the next reel should land its first payoff by 4 s.
- Rule from Kallaway: read only the first line in isolation and ask whether it can be misread. If yes, rewrite before rendering.

## 4. Re-hook schedule

A re-hook is a scheduled new reason to keep watching, not a random effect. Devices available in post: cut to a new angle, punch-in, speed ramp (slow or 2x), a Cap line that opens a new question, a Cap line that counts progress ("part 2 of 3"), an audio drop to silence before a reveal, a before/after flash, a label on the thing that matters (circle, arrow, name tag), a freeze frame with a label.

- 20 s reel: hook 0 to 1.5, foreshadow by 3, first payoff by 5, one scheduled re-hook at 8 to 10 (new question or the "but"), escalation 10 to 16, biggest beat at 16 to 18, end card 18 to 20 holding 1.5 to 2 s. Three interrupts total.
- 30 s reel: hook, foreshadow, payoff one by 5, re-hooks at 9 to 10 and 18 to 20, biggest beat at 24 to 27, end card 27 to 30. Four interrupts. Watch the 30 to 60 percent zone (9 to 18 s) for the crater; that is where B-roll not matching audio kills it.
- 45 s reel: only when the story earns it. Hook, foreshadow, payoff one by 5, re-hooks at 10, 20 and 32 (vary the device: a question, a number, a reveal), biggest beat at 38 to 42, end card 42 to 45. Any static shot over 4 s needs a punch or a Cap line.
- In every length: no visual stays unchanged longer than 3 to 4 s, no two re-hooks use the same device back to back, one sound effect per reel at most on the transition that matters, and the loudest sound sits at a payoff, not at a cut.

## 5. Endings

- Loop ending: last slot is the same framing as slot one (same clip or a matched angle), motion continuing in the same direction, xfade 0.3 into it, no end card text in the final 0.5 s so the replay lands clean on the hook. Best under 10 s. Use when the hook is a question whose answer is only in the first second of the replay.
- Send ending: a recipient-framed line, specific person, specific reason, on screen for the final 3 s and held: "send this to the guy who made a worse trade" / "send this to whoever is still waiting on parts." Pair with a small series tag (PROJECT RIVAL · PART 1) so the sent reel is self-explaining. Never "tag 3 friends" or a number of sends.
- Comment ending: only when two real camps exist. "LS or 7.3, settle it below." Mosseri ranks comments low, so this is a bonus, never the main ask.
- Rewatch trick: put one fast detail in the last second (a part number, a date, a face reaction at 2x) that the eye misses the first time; the replay catches it.
- Payoff must beat the hook. If the end emotion is smaller than the opening promise, cut the reel shorter or kill it.

## 6. IMPLEMENT IN OUR STACK

The EDL generator (build_reel2.py input) should be built in three blocks, not as one list of slots.

OPEN block (slots 0 to 2, 0 to 3 s). Slot 0: in-point chosen on motion or on the loudest audio transient (scan the clip with ffmpeg ebur128 or astats and pick the peak), zoom [1.0, 1.08 to 1.12], dur 1.0 to 2.6. A Hook caption at start 0.0 with pop true, end no later than 2.6, pos in the top third (y around 700 to 800 of 1920). An audio bed entry at "at": 0.0 carrying the real sound, loudnorm on. Slot 1 at 1.0 to 1.5 s is the first pattern change (new clip or detail). Karaoke Sub captions start on the first spoken word. Add a two-beat option: Hook line A at 0.0 and Hook line B at 0.67. Add an optional 6-frame desaturate-to-color grade on slot 0.

RE-HOOK block (middle). The generator takes the target length and emits re-hook markers at the schedule above (20 s: 9; 30 s: 10 and 19; 45 s: 10, 20, 32) and requires each marker to carry one of: a new clip, a zoom, a speed change, or a Cap line that opens a question. Lint rules: no slot over 4 s without zoom or a caption event, no two adjacent slots from the same clip and same framing, audio transitions only where a caption or reveal lands.

END block (final 2 to 3 s). Three templates: loop (final slot clip equals slot 0 clip, same framing, xfade 0.3, no caption in the last 0.5 s), send (Cap line with fade_in 0.12 held 3 s plus the Small series tag), comment (Cap question held 2.5 s). Default is send for build reels, loop for meme reels under 10 s. Hold the last frame 1 to 2 s before the file ends.

Also add: a freeze-and-label slot type (speed 0 for 0.6 to 0.8 s with a Cap pointing at the part), a flash-forward slot (0.4 s of the payoff clip at the top, then a Cap "[time] earlier"), and a hook-text check that fails the build if the Hook string exceeds 7 words or is not on screen by frame 4.

## 7. Top five

1. Text hook on frame 4, inside the safe zone, 3 to 7 words, one question, and a frame that cannot answer it by itself. This is the single lever behind the 48 vs 32 percent skip gap.
2. First payoff by 4 to 5 s, and the biggest beat last. Harley Trade does this; keep it as the house rule and measure skip at 48 h.
3. Schedule the re-hooks (20 s: one at 9; 30 s: two) and lint for static shots over 4 s. Build reels already hold 10 to 22 s; this is what carries them to the end card.
4. Send ending with a named recipient on every build reel, plus the series tag, so the build footage inherits the meme reels' 2 to 4 percent share rate.
5. Start on the loudest sound and the moving frame, never on a wide or a logo; a desaturate-to-color bloom and a punch-in cost nothing in ffmpeg and read as effort.
