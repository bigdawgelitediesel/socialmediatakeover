# How 2026 build series are structured on short-form, and the Project Rival series bible

Written 2026-11-21 for @elcoyotediesel (577 followers). Sources fetched the same day; anything that is a vendor claim with no data behind it is marked "unsourced" so nobody mistakes it for a measurement.

Context that shapes everything below:
- Instagram removed Reels linking on 2026-10-28 (launched Aug 2025, lasted about 14 months). Series, the replacement, is a limited test since 2026-06-02 (profile tab, episodes in order, resume, save a series, a Watch Next button per creator reports). Meta has given no rollout date; the 11-21 algorithm memo expects a US test early 2027. Until then the series is held together by format, captions, pins and highlights, not by a platform feature.
- The account's own numbers (live pull 11-21): build and partner reels 195 to 554 views at 59 to 75 percent skip and 0 shares; Harley Trade (a build story told as one joke) 37.8 percent skip, 10.9 s watch on 18.5 s, 2,161 views in 17 h; memes send at 1.5 to 2 percent of reach. Every reel under 40 percent skip passed 1,000 views; every reel over 60 percent stayed under 600.
- House rules (reel-editor skill, FONT-LIBRARY, coordinator 11-21): one poster lockup on the hook and nothing else burned in on the fast tier; no on-screen CTA, no handle card, no outro, no fade; end within 1.5 s of the payoff; share line in the caption's first line; song added inside Instagram; El Coyote only, never the shop; SEMA is a target; partners get a thank-you and a tag; one man, phone plus Pocket 3.

## 2. Retention mechanics across episodes

**Series branding without a platform feature.** With linking gone and Series not here, the series is recognized by: a part label on the cover and in the first frame, the same cover design with a visible number so the grid reads in order, Part 1 pinned (Instagram allows three pins), a Highlight named after the series holding every part in order, and a caption first line that states the part number and where Part 1 is ("Part 2 of 3. Part 1 is pinned on our profile," nine words). Haba's test for every episode: would "a stranger who lands here" know where the story started. Awarexone adds: write part numbers in full in scheduled captions, keep an offline sheet of part number, URL and next-part line, and rewrite any brand contract that listed linked Reels as a deliverable. (haba.social/insights/your-part-2-is-about-to-get-lost, awarexone.com/research/instagram-reels-linking-removed, selfemployed.com/news/instagram-removes-reels-series-linking-october-28-2026/)

**"Previously on" in one line, not a replay.** dev.to's method: keep a continuity ledger (established facts, open loops, needs confirmation); recap in one or two spoken sentences drawn only from established facts ("We tore out the kitchen, found water damage, and lost three days"); show the part number so search traffic knows where it landed; add ONE callback per episode (a detail from an earlier episode that matters again now, about two seconds); end on a "true" cliffhanger, meaning the outcome is genuinely unknown, never a dramatized event that has not happened. Returning viewers leave if you replay the series. CapCut agrees: reference the previous cliffhanger in the first line, label the part, orient immediately after a time or setting change.

**The "Part 2 in my profile" trap vs "every episode stands alone."** The 80K to 21K case is the trap. Tokime's guide says viewers go to the profile to find Part 2 and offers only pins, playlists and a pinned comment ("Part 2 is up now!") as aids. YouTube's own stat (40 percent of microdrama views after day 30) and Metricool's (83 percent of interactions in 10 days) point the same way: an episode is watched out of order and late, so each one must pay off alone and the series link is a bonus. Rule: every episode is a complete reel with its own hook, payoff and share reason; the series is the second layer. Instagram itself already serves Part 2 to Part 1 completers (the CHIEF mechanism in playbook/00-content-plan.md), so the job is making each part complete, not pushing people to the profile.

**Posting gap between parts.** Tokime: immediate posting captures interest but can cannibalize Part 1's reach; 1 to 24 hours builds comments; start by posting Part 2 after Part 1 "has gained significant traction, often within a few hours"; a predictable schedule trains return visits. 1kreach (unsourced): publish tightly, eight clips over two weeks rather than four months; adding clips weeks later rarely gets the autoplay lift. Lilach Bullock's own test: going from 3 a week to daily lost watch-through and followers.

**Pinned episodes, highlights, grid.** Pin Part 1 (or the current season's Part 1) plus the best-ER personal reel; not the 260k gooseneck reel (it recruits the wrong viewer, per the content plan). One Highlight per season, episodes in order, cover carrying the series mark. Covers: same lockup composition every episode with the number visible at grid size. 1kreach's claim that TikTok playlists show an "Episode 3 of 8" chip and that saved playlists act like an owned channel is unverified; its practical advice (five to twelve clips, 35 to 70 s, two or three a week, first caption line names the series and count) is a reasonable default, not data.

**Do TikTok Playlists, Shorts series, Instagram Series matter?** TikTok: Playlists exist (SocialBee says gated at 10k followers plus Creator Next, 1kreach says no gate in 2026; conflicting), and "Series" was renamed to (Mini) Drama and is a paywall product. YouTube: Shorts series needs YPP; a playlist of only Shorts becomes a series; the related-video link is the one in-player link and may have been pulled from the feed UI in mid-2026 (conflicting reports). Instagram: Series is the one that will matter for this account because it creates a profile tab and a Save Series action, which is a follow substitute; prepare for it by numbering everything now so the back catalog can be bundled the day it arrives (Meta's test lets creators bundle old Reels). None of these change what the episode itself must do.

**Retention shape to grade against (house rules plus benchmarks).** Skip under 45 percent, average watch over 10 s on a 30-s cut, end retention above 50 percent, sends over 1 percent of views; healthy curves hold 70 to 80 percent at second three; the last 10 percent is a drop-off zone, so the payoff lands before it and the file ends within 1.5 s.

## 3. Sponsor integration that does not kill the reel

**The 2026 data.**
- Virev, 175,104 sponsored posts out of 20.2M (0.87 percent): sponsored posts get 0.69x the creator's usual likes and comments; label only 0.67x engagement and 0.90x Reel views; label plus #ad 0.61x and 0.81x; #ad with no label 0.73x and 0.80x; "gifted" with no ad tag 0.94x and 0.86x. - SociaVault, 3,702 posts from 170 accounts (Oct 19, 2026): labeled posts median 0.46x the account's usual (n=31), 13 percent of labeled posts reach 2x usual vs 23 percent of others; the authors attribute the gap to the post being an ad, not to the label. (sociavault.com/blog/instagram-paid-partnership-label-study)
- Modern Retail 2026 Creator Marketing Index (beauty, Jan to Sep 2026): Reels 11 percent ER vs stills 7 and carousels 5; sponsored posts average 334,055 views vs gifted 48,008 (paid amplification included); demos beat other formats for gifted product. (modernretail.co/marketing/modern-retail-research-2026-creator-marketing-index-...)
- Influencers-time claims that labeled posts now get 15 to 20 percent more reach cite unnamed "internal tests"; Meta has not confirmed any label effect either way. Treat as unverified.
- This account: SPONSOR 1.36 percent ER vs PERSONAL 14.80 percent on near-identical reach (1,203 vs 1,127 views); sponsor skip 72.5 percent vs account 48.2; the four partner shout-out reels this month did 195 to 347 views at 66 to 75 percent skip with 0 shares. The format is the problem, the reach is not.

**How the series creators show partner parts.**
- Install day, not announcement day. Westen's Airlift: unboxing and install in one video, with the custom work (mount plate, chrome lines) as the content. Brands-for-creators: a creator who installs a part and explains each step draws viewers "often ready to buy"; one who "clearly doesn't know how to use a socket wrench" breaks trust. Glossy staged lighting triggers ad-blindness on Reels; the authentic look wins.
- The "why this part" explainer beats the "thank you" reel. Houck is praised for "telling the story"; the shout-out reels on this account are the floor. A thank-you is a Story or a card inside a real episode, never a standalone reel.
- The booth is the finale. Hard Up's series ends at the Aces Fuel Injection booth; Houck's ends with the Prismatic Powders space and an IG Live. Rival's SEMA target gives every partner the same finale to point at.
- Follow as deliverable. Houck's entry mechanic (follow on IG or YouTube) turned the giveaway into a follower engine the partners paid for.
- Tagging: tag the partner account in the reel (Tag people) and invite as Collaborator when they will accept, because Collab is the only lever in the data that recovers reach (0.79x vs 0.64x; 1.52x views for sponsored Collab Reels). Keep the Paid partnership label whenever parts were provided (it is the exchange of value Instagram's policy names and the FTC's material connection); Virev's advice is "leaving it off is not a fix." Disclosure also goes in the caption. Put the partner tag pill (Remotion `tooltag`) on screen the moment the name is spoken, not a logo bumper.

**What to pitch partners, in their language.** Install-day episode with their part as the payoff, a one-line "why this part" from the counter guy, a tag and a Collab invite, a Story thank-you with their handle, and a place in the finale at the show. Not a dedicated reel about them: the data says that reel does one third of the numbers.

## 4. Cadence and mix for a one-man show

**What the field says.** 2026 guides converge on 3 to 5 Reels a week (Kontentino, Komet, SocialRails) with 7 as the ceiling and "10 plus a week leads to diminishing returns and burnout"; Lilach Bullock's test lost watch-through going daily; Instagram's secondhand guidance is 5 to 7. 1kreach: two or three series episodes a week. Hard Up and Tom's Refurb: weekly long-form. Dealership Reels are 43.8 percent of posts (Emplifi Q3 2026) and get 25 median interactions vs 9 for images; Reels and carousels both do over twice an image. Metricool 2026 (24M posts): Reels get 4x the interactions of single images. Mix guidance (brands, not solo creators): 60 to 70 percent Reels, 20 to 30 percent carousels. The 11-21 algorithm memo already set the house ceiling at 3 to 4 Reels a week with Trial Reels as extra reps; Jacob currently posts about 1.5 a day. (kontentino.com/blog/how-often-to-post-on-instagram/, emplifi.io/resources/q3-2026-auto-dealership-worldwide-benchmarks/, metricool.com/press-release-instagram-study-2026/)

**Build fatigue.** No measured study exists. The pattern in the record: "Why YouTubers Abandon Their Big Project Cars" names burnout and budget and says the algorithm does not reward slow meticulous work; Westen wraps projects fast and leaves the failure in the title; Graveyard Carz refocused on the cars to win back tired viewers; Finger's 24-part series is held up as the exception because it is "down to earth." Paddy Galloway's rule from the pacing lane: transitions, ends of storylines and heavy context are retention danger zones, keep them short. Mat Armstrong's cure is the milestone episode: each one has its own news. Donut's cure is the repeatable question.

**The mix for @elcoyotediesel (what the account's own data supports).**

| Slot | Share of posts | Why | Cost to Jacob |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rival episodes | 2 a week, 3 when something real happened (about 30 percent) | the sponsor product; the build is the long game | moderate: film every time the truck is touched, bank it |
| Memes and relatable | 3 to 4 a week (about 45 percent) | 1.5 to 2 percent sends, the only thing that shares; feeds reach into the episodes | low |
| Talking head (counter credential, "why this part," Rival decisions) | 1 to 2 a week (about 20 percent) | Why companies hate your truck: 21.6 s watch, 30.1 skip; this is the register sponsors belong in | low: one take |
| Partner thank-yous | Stories plus a card inside an episode, never a standalone reel | 195 to 347 views, 0 shares as reels | near zero |
| Feed posts | zero as reach plays | 78 to 283 views | |

Anti-fatigue rules: never two episodes in a row without a meme between them; every episode carries its own news (a number, a part, a mistake, a decision) or it is a Story; one "state of the truck" wide-shot episode every four weeks so new viewers can catch up without a recap; and when the truck sits (machine shop weeks), the series goes to decision and explainer episodes, not footage of waiting.

## 5. PROJECT RIVAL series bible

### 5.1 Name treatment on screen

- One lockup, Combo B MEGANTE (Archivo Black 260 px, deep red #8E1010, caps, tracking +4; labels Poppins Italic 34 px), the preset the font library assigns to build updates and partner reveals. Same preset every episode; the preset IS the series brand. Combo A LOUDER is reserved for joke episodes (a Harley Trade style story); Combo C TROPI for the one golden-hour "state of the truck" episode a month.
- Block word = the episode's payoff noun, two to three words max ("BLOCK OUT", "FORGED RODS", "DONOR FRAME", "FUEL SYSTEM"). Sub line = `PROJECT RIVAL · EP 07`. Labels (optional, top-left and bottom-right of the lockup): `EL COYOTE` and the date `07.12 / 2026`. The episode number lives in the sub line, so the numeral is on screen without a second graphic.
- Placement: empty part of the frame (sky, white shop wall, the tan hood), Jacob or the truck low and whole, on by frame 4, off by 2.5 to 3.0 s. Nothing else burned in on the fast tier. On the Remotion tier a `numeral` card may replace the sub line and a `tooltag` carries the partner name when spoken.
- Renderer line: `{"type":"lockup","preset":"megante","block":"BLOCK OUT","sub":"PROJECT RIVAL · EP 07","start":0,"end":2.8,"pos":[540,520]}`
- Cover frame: export the lockup frame as the Reel cover so the grid reads PROJECT RIVAL · EP N in order; same crop every time.
- Sound: added inside Instagram. Recognition comes from the first-frame composition and the red lockup, not from a jingle, so the audio stays free to ride trending sounds on music episodes and original dialogue on voice episodes. (Note for the renderer: because the song is added in-app, the "music dip before the payoff" from beat sheet B cannot be done on music episodes; the slow-down before the payoff is done with picture only, one 2.5 to 3 s half-speed shot.)
- Caption line 1 (first 125 characters): the share line or the question. Caption line 2: `Project Rival, EP 07. 1959 F600, 7.3 Powerstroke, Road to SEMA 2027 (the target). EP 06 is pinned.` Then partner tags and the disclosure when parts were provided. No hashtag wall.

### 5.2 Episode template, second by second (30 s version; the 20 s version is marked)

Built on beat sheet B (cinematic build progress) plus the CHIEF rule (cold-open on the finished change, rewind, end on a problem), the dev.to one-line recap and one callback, the CapCut 3 to 6 s ending, and the house end rule (no CTA, no card, no fade, end within 1.5 s of the payoff). The 11-20 hooks lane's "share line as text plus a small series tag" is superseded by the 11-21 house rule: share line in the caption, series tag inside the lockup.

| Time | Beat | Picture | Sound (voice episodes) | Rules |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.00 to 0.13 (frames 1 to 4) | first frame | the finished change, wide, truck or Jacob low, room for the lockup | audio starts 0.2 s before picture (J-cut) | lockup on by frame 4 |
| 0 to 2.5 | cold open on the result | hold the result shot with a small eased push (1.0 to 1.06), anchor on the subject | first sentence = the stake with a number ("Eight new rods in a 29-year-old block") | no greeting, no logo |
| 2.5 to 4.0 | rewind cue and one-line recap | one reversed shot or a 0.3 s dissolve into the "before" | one or two sentences, established facts only ("Block's been on the bench two weeks. Shop wants it Monday.") | lockup leaves by 2.8 s |
| 4.0 to 5.0 | first payoff beat | the part out of the box, the number on the gauge, the bare deck | the answer to the hook lands here | by 4.5 s at the latest |
| 5 to 14 | progress montage | 5 to 7 shots, 1.2 to 2.2 s, every cut on the beat, every third shot a macro, one half-speed moment on real motion, `mblur` on fast hands | spoken or none | include ONE callback (2 s) to an earlier episode ("the cam we picked in EP 3") |
| 14 to 17 | midpoint re-hook | cut to the new thing ("then the bore gauge said this") | the line that opens loop two | 20 s version: skip 14 to 23 |
| 17 to 23 | second montage | faster, 0.8 to 1.6 s shots | | max 3 s without a change, max 3 slots under 1.5 s in a row |
| 23 to 26 | pre-payoff beat | one 2.5 to 3 s half-speed shot | voice stops; breath kept | picture-only slow-down (song is in-app) |
| 26 to 29 | payoff | the thing the cold open promised, now in context, 2.5 to 3.5 s on a downbeat | the line that pays the claim, on Jacob, no cut during it | |
| 29 to 30.5 | the problem | 1 to 1.5 s of the next open loop, shown not told (the pistons that cannot be ordered yet, the empty frame rail) | last line names it ("and that is why the pistons wait") then cut 0.6 to 1.2 s after | hard end; no card, no fade; last frame rhymes with frame 1 when it can |

Lengths: 20 to 24 s when there is one payoff; 28 to 35 s when there are two loops; nothing over 45 s as an episode; the 90 s story version is a Trial Reel experiment, not an episode.

Shot list per episode (Pocket 3, ActiveTrack, HDR off, lav or the Pocket mic): the five-shot sequence from the shooting brief (wide establishing, medium of the work, hands close, macro of the part, a reverse or reaction), plus the "finished change" wide shot filmed LAST so it can open the episode, plus one continuous move. Film the problem shot before leaving.

Pre-flight checklist (seven items): hook noun on the lockup; first payoff by 4.5 s; one callback; one half-speed moment; the problem shot exists; share line written for caption line 1; partner tag and disclosure decided.

### 5.3 Recurring elements

1. The red MEGANTE lockup with `PROJECT RIVAL · EP N` as the sub line, same position family, on frames 4 to 84.
2. The cold open on the finished change, then the rewind.
3. One spoken recap line from the continuity ledger (keep the ledger in F600-BUILD/notes: established facts, open loops, needs confirmation).
4. One callback per episode to an earlier episode.
5. One number per episode (bore size, dollars, days to the machine shop, horsepower target, miles to the donor truck).
6. The problem ending: the next open loop shown in the last 1.5 s.
7. The cover frame = the lockup frame, so the grid is the episode guide.
8. Caption line 1 = share line or question; line 2 = series line with the previous episode pinned.
9. Pins: current season's EP 1 plus the best personal reel plus the latest partner install episode (three pins max). Highlight `RIVAL S1`, episodes in order.
10. Partner tag via Tag people plus Collab invite; the tooltag pill when the name is spoken; thank-you as a Story the same day.
11. Monthly "state of the truck" episode (TROPI lockup, golden hour, the whole truck or the whole pile, no recap needed).
12. Season = a phase of the build with a hard end: S1 "Long block" ends when the engine is assembled; S2 "Chassis" ends when it rolls; S3 "Road to SEMA" ends on the floor or the drive to Las Vegas. Number episodes within the season (S1 EP 07) so the count never passes about 12, the range where the playlist guides say completion holds.
13. Voice: Jake, aka El Coyote, plain blue-collar wording, no AI tells, no em-dashes. El Coyote only. SEMA is "the target."

### 5.4 Four-week episode plan (Nov 23 to Dec 20, 2026), tied to what is actually happening

### 5.5 Sponsor-episode template (install day or box day, 25 to 32 s)

Goal: the partner's part is the payoff of a real episode, the reel behaves like a build episode in Insights (skip under 45, sends over 1 percent), and the partner gets a tag, a Collab, a thank-you Story and a line in the finale. Never a thank-you reel.

| Time | Beat | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 2.5 | cold open on the part INSTALLED or in hand at the truck, lockup block = the part's job, not the brand ("FUEL SYSTEM", "GAUGES"), sub = `PROJECT RIVAL · EP 05` | the brand is spoken, not burned in; no product-first static shot (the 72.5 percent skip pattern) |
| 2.5 to 4 | one-line recap of why this part exists on this truck, from the ledger | "550 horse on a 7.3 needs fuel the stock system cannot give" |
| 4 to 5 | first payoff: the box open or the first fitting on | partner name spoken here; Remotion `tooltag` pill appears on the name for 1.5 s (motion tier only) |
| 5 to 16 | install montage, hands on it, one macro of the fitting or the dial, one half-speed moment, one callback to the episode that set up the need | authentic light, no glossy staging; Jacob's hands or the counter guy's explanation |
| 16 to 19 | the "why this part" line from the counter credential | the only sales line, said once: what fails on the cheap version, what this one fixes |
| 19 to 22 | re-hook: the catch (a fitment surprise, a line that needs re-routing, a part that is back-ordered) | the honest beat is what separates it from an ad; Westen keeps the failure in |
| 22 to 25 | pre-payoff half-speed shot | picture only |
| 25 to 28 | payoff: the part on the truck, or the gauge lit, or the regulated return plumbed | spoken thank-you lands here, one sentence, first names if Jacob has them |
| 28 to 29.5 | the problem: the next thing this part needs (the pump that is not here, the harness, the tune) | hard end |

Caption: line 1 = share or question ("Send this to whoever is still running a stock lift pump" or "What would you run for fuel at 600 hp?"); line 2 = series line; line 3 = "Parts provided by @drivendiesel for Project Rival" (the disclosure, plus the Paid partnership label when parts or money changed hands); tag the partner with Tag people and send a Collab invite. Same day: a Story with the reel, a line of text, the partner handle, and the thank-you. Partner deliverable sheet (what they get, in their words): an install-day episode with their part as the payoff, a spoken "why this part" from a parts manager, a tag and Collab on a reel that is built to be shared, a Story thank-you, a place in the season finale, and the SEMA target as the long tail.

What not to do on a sponsor episode: no logo bumper, no "huge thanks to" opening, no product on a clean table as frame one, no two partners in one reel, no sales line longer than one sentence, no on-screen CTA, no hashtag wall, no shop name.
