# The best pitches in diesel and truck social: how creators land partners, how brands pitch back, and the Rival pitch kit

Research agent report, 2026-11-21, for @elcoyotediesel / Project Rival (1959 Ford F600 on a 2005-2007 Super Duty frame, built 7.3 Powerstroke, 550 to 600 crank hp on compounds, Road to SEMA 2027). Builds on `research/2026-11-21-partner-repostability.md` (brand table, Collab mechanics, the per-episode partner kit) and the live partner page at elcoyotediesel.com/partners.html (the 10x case, the five big items, the "What I won't promise" list, the 2028 road plan). Nothing here restates those; the templates point at them.

Method: four research lanes run 2026-11-21, roughly 11:30 to 14:30 MT. Web search (extended) plus page fetches of brand sites, SEMA PDFs, trade press, Instagram profile and post pages read logged out, YouTube and TikTok metadata via yt-dlp. Vendor claims are marked as such. Anything not confirmed is labeled unverified. Account state at write time: 582 followers, 103 posts (logged-out read, 11-21).

The five things that change the plan:

1. **There is no independent-builder door at SEMA.** Every vehicle on the floor or in the lots must be "owned or sponsored by an exhibiting company." Feature-vehicle space is $499 (member) or $599, applied for by the exhibitor by roughly July 30, 2027, paid by about August 6. So the partner ranking below weights brands that exhibit at SEMA. The 2027 display rules are already published: non-exhibitor brands get at most two marks of 50 square inches on the truck, and tires from non-exhibiting makers get no signage at all.
2. **Small accounts got real parts in 2025 to 2026 when the brand could use the footage, never because of a follower count.** A 274-follower account got a Derale kit and a Collab credit. A 3,987-follower shop became Power Hungry's product-launch venue. An 804-follower account got reshared by Power Hungry off a race result. The currency is the Collab post and the milestone clip, which is exactly the kit in the repostability doc.
3. **Show spot first, sponsors second.** Killer Creations, Project 15 Motorsports (a 1968 F-250 on a 2007 Super Duty chassis, the closest analog to Rival) and Dillon Houck all had a SEMA placement before the sponsor list filled in. Baja Designs paid Project 15's entry outright. Rival's version: lock one exhibiting partner's booth or feature space by July 2027, then shop that spot.
4. **The pitch data is consistent across 1.3 million emails:** a specific first line about the brand, a link to your work, one concrete content idea, no rates, no call request, a yes-or-no closing question, under 120 words, to the partnerships person by name, follow up once at day 3 or 4. "Collab" in the subject line out-replies "Sponsorship" five to one.
5. **Sixteen diesel and truck programs have no follower floor and an open form** (BD, FASS, Edge, MBRP, Mishimoto, Wehrli, PPEI Collabs, Air Lift, Sinister, Fuel, Dakota Digital, Restomod Air, POR-15, Cometic, Harbor Freight, AirDog). Two have floors (KC HiLiTES 2,000; KC Turbos 100,000, "subject to negotiation"). Three are invite-only (Hot Shot's Secret Secret Society, Nitto Pit Crew, Toyo Treadpass). Industrial Injection's sponsorship page is dead as of today and nothing replaced it, so the text to Scott Dana is the application.

### 1b. The on-camera and caption lines that actually moved a brand

The meme formats ("tag a brand that should be on this," "if you work at X my DMs are open," "I'm not sponsored but...") returned zero verifiable diesel instances with counts. What verifiably traveled:

| Line | Who, count | Why it worked |
|---|---|---|
| "Sponsors will never give you free parts if you don't add value." And: "notice how I never said the word free... I always just did 'in exchange for product.'" | Powerstroke Central, 59.9K views | Names the deliverable, not the gift |
| "Offer to pay for what you're asking for. Then you either succeed in marketing, or they get a sale." | Will Collette, 3,878 views | Removes the brand's downside |
| "One burnout. That's all it took." (brand-written hook over the creator's wreck) | BD on @boostedsixx | The brand wrote the line; the creator supplied the footage |
| "First 20-25 trucks automatically entered for give aways!" | Holder's Diesel, 36 likes, reshared by PHP | An event hook the brand could reuse |
| Race-result caption crediting the part; brand reply "We need the full pull!" | @73dipstick, 22 likes | The brand asked for more footage in public |
| "Big thank you to Baja Designs for sponsoring our entry into SEMA and Battle of the Builders." | Project 15, 10,959 views | The thank-you is specific and attached to a show, not a product |
| "Big thanks to my guys @deraleperformance for the oil filter relocation kit" | @cockrencustomz, 274 followers | Derale accepted the Collab anyway; the brand name is the first clause |

Older but still the template brands link to: T.J. Hunt emailed Subaru parts distributors to introduce himself and offered install videos for store credit ("I got lucky because the person who saw the email was 18 and actually a viewer of my channel"); Kyle Kuhnhausen landed Radium at a local Oregon show ("Give me a call and we'll get you whatever you need"); Gabby Downing: "I shifted to promoting myself as a business and not a person" (SEMA News, Jun 2023).

**One Rival format worth testing (untested, no evidence yet):** at the payoff of a milestone episode, one spoken sentence naming the next open slot without asking anyone for anything. "Block's at the machine shop. Rods are next. Haven't picked whose." Caption line 1 stays the share line. It gives brands and their reps a reason to comment the way MagnaFlow and OPTIMA commented on Houck's posts and PHP commented on @73dipstick, and it costs nothing. Measure brand-account comments and DMs over the following 48 hours; drop it after two tries if nothing moves.

### 3b. The rules, in one list

1. Send it to the person who runs partnerships or posts the Instagram, by name. For the small diesel shops (ISSPRO, CNC Fab, Strictly, US Shift) that is the owner; for the rest it is the name in section 2.
2. Subject line has "Collab" in it. Never "Sponsorship."
3. Line 1 is about them and specific. Line 2 is the truck in one sentence. Line 3 is one concrete audience fact. Line 4 is the content idea with their part in it. Line 5 is the link. Close with a question they can answer yes or no.
4. No rates, no "hop on a call," no "I love your brand," no critique, no deck attached, no competitor named.
5. Under 120 words by email, under 60 by DM.
6. One follow-up at day 3 or 4 with something new (a clip, a log entry, a milestone). A second at day 10. Then stop, and send the first-fire clip when it happens regardless.
7. Five pitches a week, Tuesday morning Mountain time, each one written for that brand.
8. Say the follower count out loud before they look it up, then give the output numbers: the Collab tiles already live on @issproofficial and @powerhungryperformance, the dated public log, the 30-day views and shares. Refresh the numbers from Insights the morning you send (the partner page figures are from Nov 19: 651K views, 480K reach, 124K interactions, 21K shares in 30 days; 511K on the best reel).

### 3f. The counter-pitch when a brand offers a code only

This is the default reply in diesel. BD's and PPEI's programs are built on it, @boostedsixx's bio is a row of 5 percent codes, and 5 of 13 replies in Powerstroke Central's test pushed a code-and-form path. A code is the brand testing whether you sell anything. The answer is to take the code as the measuring stick and ask for the smallest version that puts the part on the truck.

> "Appreciate it, and keep the code live. That's how you'll see what the truck sold, and it goes in every post. A code doesn't put the part on the truck, though. Here's the smallest version that does: one [part], one install episode, Collab on your grid, part number and your link on the public build list. If it moves ten of them, we talk about the next part. If it doesn't, you're out one [part] and you still got the episode. Which way do you want to run it?"

If the answer is still no product: offer the dealer-cost buy with the code on top (the Collette model: "offer to pay for what you're asking for"), or a buy-back tied to placements (the Dakota Digital model: credit back when the install reel is live on their grid, more when the truck is on a show floor). Say it as: "Then sell me one at dealer cost and I'll earn the rest of it back on your grid." That keeps the part on the truck and the brand in the series without anyone pretending it was a gift.

Never in the counter: "I don't work for free," another brand's offer, a view guarantee, or anything that reads as a threat to go next door. If the final answer is no, the reply is one line: "Noted. I'll send you the first-fire clip when it happens." Then do it.

Disclosure holds either way: code, gifted part or dealer-cost part are all material connections. "Parts provided by @brand for Project Rival" in caption line 3 plus the Paid partnership label, as in the repostability doc.
