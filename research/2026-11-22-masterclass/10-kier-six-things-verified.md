# 10. Kier Porter's six things, checked against everything else that was measured (2026-11-22)

Researched 2026-11-22. Method: WebSearch only, about 45 queries (extended for 2025 to 2026 material, standard for older papers). WebFetch failed with `getaddrinfo ENOTFOUND` on every host tried (motionapp.com, creatorscience.com, opus.pro, substack.com, thecontentlabs.app, getreelyze.com, merra.ai, videodatabase.io, klipkanvas.com), so every number below comes from the search snippet of the source page or from secondary coverage, plus the library's own earlier reads where files 01, 03, 04 and 06 in this folder already covered the ground. Kier Porter's reel is not indexed by any search engine reachable from here (reel pages are login-walled and the handle did not surface under any spelling), so his figures (112 reels past one million views, 6,097 likes, September 2026) are taken from the brief and not re-verified.

Grades: **measured** (a dataset or an experiment with a stated sample), **practitioner** (a working creator, coach or vendor describing what they do or count), **folklore** (repeated with no origin).

One thing to hold in mind about Kier's method. It is a count of what 112 winners had in common, with no losers in the sample. That is prevalence, not lift: a feature shared by every winner can be shared by every loser too. The same limit applies to the other "we looked at N viral videos" counts below (100 creators, 79 reels). Where a source compared winners against the rest of an account's output (Emplifi, Content Labs, Robertson, Motion), that is said.

## a. The large-sample studies, 2024 to 2026, and what each says

Eight questions, coded: movement in frame 1 (M), talking immediately (T), angle change at 2 to 3 s (A), on-screen text (X), "you" versus "guys" (Y), topic breadth (B), legibility (L), UI safe zones (U).

- **Emplifi, Facebook Reels, 10,110 Reels from 704 brand pages.** Speech in the first 3 s: plus 11.7 percent retention at 3 s, plus 24.7 at 10 s, plus 6.6 at 30 s against music-only. A person visible for at least 1 s in the first 3 s: about plus 3.6 at 3 s and plus 10 at 10 s. Text overlays pooled: plus 5.1 at 3 s, plus 7.6 at 10 s, about zero at 30 s; on speech-driven video minus 6 at 10 s and minus 4.5 at 30 s; on music-led video plus 9.8 at 30 s. Covers T, M, X. Silent on A, Y, B, L, U. https://emplifi.io/resources/facebook-reels-data/ . **Measured**, correlational, Facebook not Instagram.
- **TikTok and MetrixLab "How to Hook", 150 ads coded on 163 variables, 7,163 viewers in four countries (2023, still the only coded hook experiment).** Everyday people 1.7x more likely to hook than celebrity talent; a creator who is a natural fit for the category 1.5x; direct-to-camera talent plus 14 percent 2 s view-through (library figure via Vidmob); 90 percent of recall and 80 percent of awareness captured in the first 6 s. Covers T, M. Silent on the rest. https://vidmob.com/resource/tiktok-hook-analysis and https://tolunacorporate.com/lp/building-brand-success-through-effective-hooks/ . **Measured**, ads, gated report.
- **The Content Labs, several self-published studies, TikTok and Instagram, each video normalised to its own account's typical views.** 14,424 videos from 355 accounts: "you/your" hooks 9.62x, "I/my/me" 6.18x, hooks with neither pronoun had the highest breakout rate (32.8 percent doubled the account's norm); question hooks 10.08x against statements 7.04x; a number in the hook 7.77x against 7.42x; 9 to 12 word hooks 37 percent above long hooks. 5,012 videos from 76 creators: heavy on-screen text 1.22x, none 0.78x. 3,997 videos from 109 creators, June 2025 to April 2026: Hot Take hooks led at 141,133 average views over 758 videos, story hooks about 20x lower. 7,431 videos: face-on-camera median higher on every platform, 73.2 percent of the 235 million-view videos were face-on, though faceless had the higher share over one million (4.4 against 2.9 percent). 165 million-view scripts: "you" and "I" more balanced than in long-tail scripts, so the author calls "use second person" overstated at script level. 2,485 graded hooks: A-tier 10.14x, B 1.66x, D and F 0.77x on a proprietary rubric. Covers Y, X, B, T, partly A (hook length). Silent on M, L, U. https://thecontentlabs.app/blog/you-vs-i-hooks-data-study , https://thecontentlabs.app/blog/on-screen-text-density-data-study , https://thecontentlabs.app/blog/what-goes-viral-in-2026-data-study , https://thecontentlabs.app/blog/faceless-vs-face-on-camera-data-study , https://thecontentlabs.app/blog/question-hooks-data-study , https://thecontentlabs.app/blog/numbers-in-hooks-myth-data-study , https://thecontentlabs.app/blog/what-viral-scripts-actually-say . **Measured**, vendor, correlational, hooks taken from the spoken line by transcript, not from the on-screen text.
- **Motion Creative Benchmarks 2026, 578,750 Meta ad creatives, 1.29 billion dollars of spend, 6,015 accounts.** Hook hit rates run 6 to 11 percent. Top tactics by hit rate: Newness, Sale Announcement, Price Anchor, Urgency (concrete) and Confession, Contrarian, Shocking Statement, Warning (pattern interrupt). Identity Call-Out and Pain Agitation are named as the triggers under relatability hooks and as the dominant triggers for specific audiences (crafters, busy professionals), not as top performers overall. Covers B (pain and identity as tactics), partly Y. Silent on M, T, A, L, U. https://motionapp.com/library/research/creative-benchmarks-2026/top-hook-tactics , https://content.motionapp.com/hubfs/Benchmarks-Report-2026.pdf , https://motionapp.com/library/hooks/tactics/relatability . **Measured**, paid ads, not organic.
- **Buffer, 3,780,620 Reels published 1 January to 15 August 2026.** Length only: 90 to 179 s reels reached 7.4 percent more unique accounts than the account's own baseline, 180 s and over reached 49.3 percent fewer but engaged 2.65 points higher. No hook variables. Buffer's hook material ("Good Hooks", and Tamara Oladipo's "anatomy of a hook": visual, text and audio hooks run at once; format, platform, psychology, algorithm) is practitioner guidance with no sample. Silent on all eight. https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-reels-length/ , https://buffer.com/resources/good-hooks/ , https://x.com/buffer/status/2064507547436954048 . **Measured** for length, **practitioner** for hooks.
- **Metricool 2026 Social Media Study, 24.3 million posts from 375,000 accounts; Metricool and HypeAuditor 2025 Instagram Content Playbook, about 700 million posts from 28 million accounts.** Average Reel watch time 8.5 s; Reels lead reach at every follower tier. No hook variables. Silent on all eight. https://metricool.com/social-media-study-2025/ and https://metricool.com/instagram-content-playbook-2025/ . **Measured**.
- **Socialinsider 2026 Instagram benchmarks, 67.5 million posts from 579,000 brand accounts (2025 data); skip-rate table, June 2026.** Reels engagement 0.52 percent; skip rate (share swiping away inside 3 s) 65.5 percent at 1 to 5K followers, 65.3 at 5 to 10K, 63.4 at 10 to 50K, 62.6 at 50 to 100K, 60.5 at 100K to 1M. Socialinsider's reading: people stop for creators they already know. Covers B indirectly (trust lowers skip). Silent on content variables. https://socialinsider.io/blog/instagram-reels-statistics/ and https://www.socialinsider.io/social-media-benchmarks/instagram . **Measured**. For scale: this account's average skip rate is 48.2 percent and its best posts sit near 32, already better than the brand average at its size.
- **OpusClip research pages, 13.5 million clips, 3.19 million classified by hook type.** Direct Address, Intrigue or Question is the most common hook on Instagram at 29.9 percent (952,227 clips), Shock or Surprise second (618,469). Text overlays detected in only 4.5 percent of clips. A 34,635-clip performance cut had product or project showcase hooks at about twice the views of the lowest type. Instagram lift data "being collected". Covers T, X, B as frequency, not lift. https://www.opus.pro/research/how-to-go-viral-instagram-reels and https://www.opus.pro/research/best-video-hooks-instagram . **Measured** counts, vendor.
- **Viral.app, about 150,000 posts cross-posted to TikTok and Reels.** Numbered-list captions broke out on 7.4 percent of Reels against 4.9 percent overall; "This app..." openers worst at 2.4 percent. Covers X and B at caption level. https://viral.app/blog/insights/tiktok-hooks-vs-instagram-hooks . **Measured**, vendor.
- **Clipspeed, 2,421 TikTok hooks.** "POV:" scenario openers beat question hooks in all four follower bands; questions underperformed above 1,000 followers. Conflicts with Content Labs on questions. Covers X, Y. https://www.clipspeed.ai/blog/instagram-reels-hooks.html . **Measured**, vendor, TikTok.
- **Smartphone Freedom Lifestyle, 100 viral creators across 10 niches.** On-screen text was the hook delivery in 56 percent of viral videos, the most common method. Prevalence only. Covers X. https://smartphonefreedomlifestyle.com/creator-trends/ . **Practitioner** count. A separate blogger's count found text in the first 3 s on 75 of 79 Reels (https://getreelyze.com/guides/on-screen-text-reels). Same limit.
- **Academic eye tracking, all small.** University of Twente MSc thesis 2025, 20 Gen Z participants watching TikTok ads: a three-second engagement threshold, and text elements were the first fixation more often than faces (https://essay.utwente.nl/essays/109005). Communication Today 2026, 16 participants: three attention modes, stable, segmented, fragmented (https://communicationtoday.sk/watching-tiktok-an-eye-tracking-study-of-visual-attention-paid-to-short-form-videos/). iMotions exploratory study: subtitles and split screens change where people look, immediate recall unchanged (https://imotions.com/blog/publications/short-form-videos-an-exploratory-study-on-the-impact-of-subtitles-and-asmr-split-screen-format-options-using-eyegaze-and-facial-expression-data/). Covers X, L. **Measured**, tiny samples. No peer-reviewed study of the first three seconds of Reels exists as of today.
- **Not found despite the brief naming them.** Hootsuite: 2025 guidance says "a strong 3-second hook", no experiment (https://blog.hootsuite.com/instagram-best-practices/). Later: Reels guide says "strong hooks in the first 3 seconds", no data (https://later.com/blog/instagram-reels/). Dash Hudson: only one secondhand blog claim (authority hooks 1.8x comments), no primary page found; treat as **folklore** until located. HubSpot: the 2024 Video Marketing Report is a survey of 500-plus marketers (42 percent name 21 to 30 s as the best length), not a measurement of videos (https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/video-marketing-report). A "June 2026 Instagram Reels Virality Analysis" of 100,000 reels circulating as a PDF appears to be built on a synthetic Kaggle dataset and found no correlation above 0.006 with anything; ignore it.
- **Older measured mechanisms that carry the six.** Abrams and Christ 2003, motion onset captures attention, Psychological Science (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12930472/). Reddit's 2026 creative study: overlay text plus 8.2 percent conversion (https://ppc.land/reddits-2026-ad-creative-study-what-actually-moves-conversion-rates/).

## b. Claim by claim

**1. Visual hook.** Agrees for movement and talking: Emplifi (speech plus 24.7 percent at 10 s, person plus 10), MetrixLab (direct to camera, everyday people 1.7x), Abrams and Christ (onset of motion, not motion itself, captures attention, which is why a frame that is already moving beats one that starts moving later), Twente (three-second threshold). All **measured**, none on Instagram. The angle change at 2 to 3 s: **silent**. Lang 2000 says cuts within a scene raise arousal and recall cheaply, which supports one change; the library's file 01, question 3, found every specific interval (1.5, 2 to 3, 3 to 4, 4 to 5 s) to be folklore, and Kier's own wording is "tends to", an observation of winners. The unusual location: Itti and Baldi give the mechanism (surprise draws gaze), MetrixLab adds the limit (a creator who fits the setting hooks 1.5x better, so odd is good only when it is still his), no reel dataset tests it. **Practitioner** on location.

**2. Yap, green screen, polished yap.** Agrees for the talking head: Content Labs 7,431 videos (face-on median higher everywhere, 73 percent of million-view videos), OpusClip industry report (talking head 57 percent of creator output, so it is dominant by volume before any lift), Emplifi (person visible lifts retention), Social Media Examiner's Shannon McKinstrie ("talking head Reels, like FaceTiming a friend, are particularly effective for driving new followers", https://www.socialmediaexaminer.com/whats-working-with-instagram-marketing-right-now/). Green screen: **silent**; the only numbers (31 percent reach boost, 67.2 percent completion) sit on a vendor page with no method (https://insights.ttsvibes.com/tiktok-green-screen-effect-performance/). Practitioner guides agree on why it works: it borrows attention from whatever is behind the speaker (https://www.usevyra.com/formats/green-screen-commentary). "Polished yap": no indexed use outside Kier; see section c.

**3. Specific, pain or identity text hooks.** Pain: Robertson, Feuerriegel and others, Nature Human Behaviour 2023, 105,000 Upworthy headline variants, 370 million impressions, negative words raised clicks and positive words lowered them, with sad words beating angry or scary ones (https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-023-01538-4 , https://www.niemanlab.org/2023/03/negative-words-in-news-headlines-generate-more-clicks-but-sad-words-are-more-effective-than-angry-or-scary-ones/). **Measured**, headlines not video. Identity: about 35 percent of people hear their own name in a channel they are ignoring (Wood and Cowan 1995, Conway, Cowan and Bunting 2001, https://memory.psych.missouri.edu/assets/doc/articles/2001/conway-et-al-pbr-2001.pdf); "you" raises involvement for low-collectivism consumers (Cruz, Leonhardt and Pezzuti 2017, https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/joinma/v39y2017icp104-116.html). **Measured**. Specific: this is where the evidence parts from Kier. Content Labs found a number in the hook worth 5 percent; Science Advances 2024 found simpler headlines won clicks regardless of content (https://news.osu.edu/simple-headlines-attract-more-online-news-readers/); Clipspeed found scenario openers ("POV:") beat questions; the 36 percent Conductor figure for number headlines has no locatable primary. Kier's own example ("what type of person is most likely to become a CEO" against "how to get better at business") is not more detailed, it is more personal: it names a person type. Read "specific" as "names the one person and the one feeling", not "adds a number". Motion's benchmark is the caution: in paid ads the identity and pain tactics are not the top hit-rate cluster; concrete offers and pattern interrupts are. Organic has no equivalent table.

**4. Broad TAM.** For views: Berger and Milkman 2012 (awe, anger, anxiety and practical value raise sharing; sadness lowers it; https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2011/11/Virality.pdf), Tellis and others 2019 (information-focused content shared less except for risky purchases; positive emotion and drama shared more; prominent branding hurts; https://www.ama.org/2020/06/11/what-drives-virality-sharing-of-online-digital-content-the-critical-role-of-information-emotion-and-brand-prominence), the repo watch list (file `2026-11-21-MILLION-VIEW-RULE.md`: the million-view posts are the universal work joke and the regret story, never the tribe update). **Measured**. Against, for conversion: Kallaway's trust zone (section e) and the Socialinsider skip gradient. Verdict: agrees that breadth buys reach, and the brief's own number (45,641 reach to 17 follows) is the reminder that reach was never this account's problem.

**5. "You", not "hi guys".** Agrees. Content Labs 14,424 videos (9.62x against 6.18x), Berger and Packard 2020 (https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0956797620902380), Cruz 2017, plus the parasocial work in file 03, question 20 (direct address and direct gaze raise felt contact). Two caveats from the same Content Labs data: hooks with no pronoun at all had the highest breakout rate, and at whole-script level the million-view videos used "you" and "I" in balance, so "you" belongs in the first line and "I" carries the proof after it. No controlled test of "hey guys" exists; the 23 percent and 30 to 40 percent penalties quoted on vendor blogs have no method (**folklore**). The mechanism (a greeting is seconds without information, on a platform where the viewer owes you nothing) is enough.

**6. Readable, high contrast, clear of the UI.** Agrees. Meta's safe box for Reels ads, 14 percent top, 35 percent bottom, 6 percent sides, is 269, 672 and 65 px on 1080 by 1920 (https://www.outfy.com/blog/instagram-safe-zone/ , https://vidova.ai/blog/instagram-reels-tiktok-youtube-shorts-safe-zones); nothing official exists for organic; the right-hand action stack is the one zone every checker agrees on; the 3:4 grid tile hides 240 px top and bottom. The repo's own measurement of 55 winners (file 04, question 26): titles at y 220 to 541, nothing in the right 130 px, cap height 44 to 58 px. Reading speed: Netflix allows 17 characters per second for adults, the old six-second rule is about 10.5 to 12, and eye-tracking shows comprehension dropping at 20 and 28 (https://backlothelp.netflix.com/hc/en-us/articles/216787938-Portuguese-Timed-Text-Style-Guide , https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/id/eprint/100082/1/6_Szarkowska_et_al.pdf). So a lockup shown for 3 s should carry at most 35 to 50 characters, which is Jacob's 3 to 7 words. The one measured note against: Emplifi's text penalty on speech-led video, which is why one lockup, not a text layer.

## c. Yap and polished yap

- **The word.** "Yap" and "yapping" come from 1990s and 2000s rap and AAVE; the first viral meme use was a July 2022 TikTok by @blexxia (a cat "talking"), with Sean Odigie's April 2023 prank videos spreading it; mainstream by early 2024 (https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/yap-yapping-yapper , https://meme.com/memes/yap-yapping-yapper). Merriam-Webster's 2025 citation already uses it for "TikTok and Instagram clips of people having a yap".
- **The format and who made it a format.** Nobody is credited with coining "yap video". The person who turned it into a named method is Jessi Jean (@jessijeanhome, about 547K Instagram followers in October 2026), whose "Yap On Camera" challenge asks for one face-to-camera reel a day for 40 days (some pages say 42, six weeks), priced 277 to 297 dollars, taught as "the art of a damn good yap", with a module on "the first 3 seconds of a hook" and "what makes a 60-second yap actually land"; her launch did 1.2 million dollars in 13 days by her own account (https://jessijean.com/challenge , https://thekarareport.com/jessi-jean-viral-yap-challenge/ , https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/articles/viral-yap-challenge-teaches-people-173109850.html , https://creatordb.app/creatorstats/jessijeanhome/). **Practitioner**. Her rule that the library already uses ("flat claim, never a question" for the title) is from this challenge.
- **What the 2026 guides say it is.** CreatorFlow: "talking straight into your phone camera in a short, unscripted Reel, with captions and a text hook on screen", a term that "became widespread in 2026 as talking-head Reels replaced highly produced content as the default advice for personal brands" (https://creatorflow.so/blog/yapping-on-instagram/). Zawa and Manychat: "selfie cam, off the cuff, subtitles at the bottom, one hook line on screen at the start" (https://zawa.ai/resources/instagram-reel-ideas , https://manychat.com/blog/instagram-trends-for-creators-2026/). Merra: hook 0 to 3 s, body 10 s to 2 min (https://www.merra.ai/blog/art-of-yapping-yap-videos). Quasa: "a subgenre of talking-head video, loose, unscripted, low-pressure" (https://quasa.io/media/the-yapping-phenomenon-why-relaxed-unscripted-talking-head-videos-are-dominating-american-marketing-in-2025-2026). Videodatabase's format list draws the line Kier draws: "Yap: raw, your phone, a real room or car, just cuts and captions"; "Speaker address: the polished version of a raw yap"; "Talking Head Edit: shot professionally, cuts away to B-roll"; and "a static talking head with no background motion is a talking head video, not a yap" (https://videodatabase.io/formats). All **practitioner**.
- **"Polished yap".** No indexed source uses the phrase; it reads as Kier's own label for what Videodatabase calls Speaker address and what the repo calls the motion tier with notebook inserts: same delivery, better light and lens, one or two graphics. Nobody has measured raw against polished. The practitioner claim that raw wins (Social Media Examiner, 24 September 2026, "Why unpolished Reels win", https://www.socialmediaexaminer.com/why-unpolished-reels-win-an-instagram-growth-strategy-for-business/) is advice, not a test.
- **Typical specs, as far as the guides agree.** Length: 30 to 90 s, with 60 s the number Jessi Jean teaches to and Merra's ceiling at 2 min; Instagram's own 2026 guide says 15 to 60 s for most formats and tells beginners to start at 15 (https://www.buzzincontent.com/news/instagram-shares-step-by-step-guide-to-making-reels-in-2026-12592736). Camera: phone at eye level, window light, a car or a real room, no set. Text: burned subtitles bottom-centre plus one hook line at the start, nothing else. Caption: the guides say only that it carries the hook; no source describes a yap caption style, so the repo's line-1 rule stands unchallenged. Cadence: daily for the challenge; the general benchmark is about 8 Reels a month for brands (Socialinsider). Editing: jump cuts only, no B-roll, no music bed or music under 10 percent. Delivery: unscripted, mid-thought, one idea per breath.
- **Numbers.** None exist for yaps as a category. Proxies: Emplifi's speech lift; Content Labs' face-on medians; Jessi Jean's and Nate Gaffney's growth (Gaffney 10K to 500K in 40 days when he packaged his talking heads as a series, library file `2026-11-21-PLAYBOOK-ADDENDUM.md`); Mariah's 300K in two months with notebook inserts and no transitions; and this account's own PERSONAL pillar at 14.8 percent engagement against 1.4 for sponsor reels on the same reach. The yap is the format this account already wins in; what it has never had is the daily cadence.

## d. Pain hooks against identity hooks

- **No measured head-to-head on organic video.** The nearest: Copy Posse's five-hook Facebook ad test, where the pain hook had the highest opt-in rate (24.4 percent) and zero immediate buyers, and the identity-flavoured FOMO hook had fewer opt-ins and the buyers (https://copyposse.com/blog/marketing-hooks/, **practitioner** test, one offer). Alici's skincare test: curiosity against ultra-specific hooks, 68 against 71 percent 3 s retention over 17 variant sets (https://alici.ai/blog/ugc-hook-strategy-types-testing-2026, **practitioner**). Content Labs' 3,997-video study put Hot Take and Investigator hooks top and Story hooks about 20x lower, and in a 331-video finance set "outrage" (debunking bad advice) averaged 178K views and never stalled at 300; those archetypes are not defined as pain or identity, but Hot Take and outrage are pain-shaped (https://thecontentlabs.app/blog/what-goes-viral-in-2026-data-study). The Robertson headline result is the strongest measured case for pain wording; the cocktail-party and Cruz results are the measured case for identity wording. Neither literature compares them.
- **Motion's framework (paid social, the most complete).** Tactics are the frame of the hook, triggers are the emotional mechanism. Eight trigger categories, including Identity Call-Out ("names the viewer's role, identity or circumstance": "If you wake up at 3am every night, this is for you"; "This is for the girl who cancels plans because of her skin") and Pain Agitation ("reflects the viewer's own experience back to them": "You're spending 5K a month on ads and you have no idea what's actually working"). - **The useful synthesis.** Identity opens the door (it is the viewer's name being called), pain holds it (it is the viewer's problem being named), and the two are one sentence in the best examples: "For the guy who..." plus the thing that is wrong. ## e. Broad TAM against the trust zone

- **Kallaway's argument, in his words (Creator Science podcast):** "An entertainment video that has a million views is not getting the trust accumulation the same way an education video gets 40,000 views. And so if you expand the TAM beyond the trust zone, those views are actually empty calories." His topic model: idea timing first (a common topic early, or an uncommon angle on it); a common idea with a large audience and a unique point of view is the target; an uncommon idea with a unique point of view is usually too niche. For education creators the goal is "on-target views", with awareness content carrying a shareable frame and a large TAM. https://creatorscience.com/podcast/kallaway/ , https://modedigitalmedia.com/videos/how-kallaway-uses-data-to-pick-better-video-topics-without-becoming-a-copycat . **Practitioner**, with a billion views behind it.
- **The measured side of both halves.** Breadth earns reach: Berger and Milkman (arousal and practical value, not topic size as such), Tellis (emotion shared, information not, except where the purchase is risky, and a diesel truck is a risky purchase, which is the loophole for this account's trust content). Trust earns hold: Socialinsider's skip gradient (five points lower skip at 100K to 1M than at 1 to 5K, attributed to recognition), MetrixLab's natural-fit multiplier (a creator who belongs in the setting hooks 1.5x better), Content Labs' faceless result (faceless gets the bigger lottery tickets, face-on gets the better median, which is the same trade stated as a distribution).
- **How working creators resolve it.** Hoyos: broad audience in the topic (a one-dollar meal), narrow and repeatable in the bucket, Shorts as the wide door into the long videos. Kallaway: common topic, uncommon angle, early. Kindra Hall's four components (an identifiable character, authentic emotion, a significant moment, specific details) are the storytelling version of the same rule: the emotion is broad, the detail is narrow (library file 02). The social-psychology result that supports it is in file 06: a mild shared complaint about a third thing bonds strangers faster than shared enthusiasm, which is why "the 10 mm" works on people who have never held one.
- **For this account the resolution is already written down** in `2026-11-21-MILLION-VIEW-RULE.md`: the stranger test, one universal emotion named out loud (money lost, pride, regret, the boss, the wife), the diesel detail as proof not premise, and two broad posts to one tribe post. Kier's item 4 confirms the first half; Kallaway's trust zone is why the second half (the named tribe inside the broad story, the counter as credential) stays. The repo's own numbers settle the direction: reach is 3.8x followers per post and conversion is 0.037 percent, so the next 1,000 followers come from trust inside broad stories, not from more breadth.

## What it means for a one-man diesel creator filming a daily talking head in his truck cab

What he already does and the evidence endorses: move on frame 1, first noun by 0.9 s, one title lockup top centre for 3 s, no greeting, caption line 1 names a person type. Nothing found says to drop any of it. The additions:

1. **Voice before everything.** The spoken line starts by 0.5 s, mid-thought, and the first clause contains "you" or the person type. The measured lifts are for speech and a face, not for text, so the lockup is the backup for muted viewers, never the hook itself.
2. **Write the lockup as a name-call, not a topic.** "For the guy still running the stock pump" beats "CP4 failure explained". Identity first, the pain in the same breath. Numbers only when the number is the story (the watch list's million-view titles had them; Content Labs says they add 5 percent otherwise).
3. **Sad or rueful beats angry.** Robertson's data had sad words out-clicking angry and scary ones, and the library's relatability evidence says a mild shared grievance bonds and a rant does not. The tone of the open is "I did this too", not "you people".
4. **One change at 2 to 3 s, cut on motion, never a montage.** A reach for the part, a punch-in on the first noun, a turn from the windshield. Lang says same-scene cuts add attention cheaply; nothing says the interval matters, so cut on the gesture and let the timing fall where it falls.
5. **The cab is the yap set; polish it with sound and a lockup, not with graphics.** Kier's format 1 is what Jacob films. "Polished yap" is the notebook-insert tier for trust episodes. No third tier exists.
6. **Rotate the location once a week.** The cab is a known set after ten episodes. The F600 cab in the bay, the parts room, the counter after close, a Class 8 cab are pattern interrupts that are still his (MetrixLab: fit matters). Say "I work here" when the shop is in frame.
7. **Broad emotion, diesel proof.** The first sentence must survive a viewer with no truck: money, time, the boss, the wife, pride, a thing that should not run but does. The 6.7, the 7.3 and the counter arrive between 5 and 15 s as the reason to believe him. Two broad to one tribe stays the ratio.
8. **Put the text where Meta says and test it on the phone.** Lockup inside the 269 to 1,248 px band, nothing in the right 130 px, cap height 44 to 58 px, 3 to 7 words, 35 to 50 characters, high contrast with no stroke, checked at arm's length with brightness down before posting. It also has to survive the 3:4 grid crop (y 240 to 1,680).
9. **Keep it to one piece of burned text in the first 3 s.** Emplifi's text penalty is on speech-led video with layered text. One lockup, off by 3 s, then captions in the chest band. Not a second line, not a sticker.
10. **Judge by skip rate, not views.** The brand average at his size is 65 percent, the account is at 48, the best posts at 32; the target stays under 40. When Trial Reels are open, test two lockups on the same take, one identity-led and one pain-led, and keep a tally; nobody has published that comparison and in ten episodes he will have the only diesel one.

### The first three seconds, rewritten

- **0.0 s.** The frame is already moving (door shutting, head turning from the windshield, hand bringing the part up). Real sound first (the door, the key). The lockup is already up: top centre, inside the safe band, 3 to 7 words, a name-call ("For the guy who...", "You were told...") with the pain in it, flat claim, never a question.
- **0.0 to 0.5 s.** First word spoken, mid-thought. No greeting, no name, no "so today".
- **By 0.9 s.** First noun, and it is a noun a stranger owns: the payment, the boss, the truck that will not start, the thing he was told. Not a part number.
- **By 1.5 s.** "You" or the person type has been said out loud, once, to one person.
- **2 to 3 s.** One visual change on a gesture: punch-in, prop reach, or a cut on the turn. Lockup off at 3 s. Captions begin in the chest band.
- **At 3 s the viewer knows** who it is for, what is wrong, and that an answer is coming later, and has not needed to know what a CP4 is to get there.
- **Before posting:** lockup read in under a second at arm's length; nothing under the right rail or the bottom 35 percent; caption line 1 repeats the name-call and names the person type; the stranger test passed; skip rate read at 48 h.
