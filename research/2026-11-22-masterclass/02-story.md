## 7. Matthew Dicks compressed into 60 to 120 seconds

**Answer.** Dicks' whole method reduces to three moves that fit a reel better than they fit a stage: find the five-second moment (the one instant where something changed in you), open as close as possible to the opposite of that moment, and connect every beat with "but" or "therefore", never "and then". In a 90 second reel the opposite-opening is the hook (state the belief you held before the change), the five-second moment is the last ten seconds, and the but/therefore chain is the middle, which forces every sentence to be a reversal or a consequence. Homework for Life is the supply chain: one moment a day, written down, which for Jake is the daily cab talking head's raw material. None of this is measured; it is a professional storyteller's craft doctrine, corroborated by South Park's independent discovery of the same but/therefore rule and by Jenny Hoyos reporting she uses it in Shorts with retention numbers she tracks.

**Sources.**
- Lenny's Podcast with Matthew Dicks, chapters "five-second moment" (04:27) and "knowing the ending" (10:29); Dicks: every story is about a singular moment, which he calls five seconds, and knowing that moment tells you how the story ends, so storytellers start at the end. https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-to-tell-better-stories-matthew . Practitioner.
- Shortform and Obsidian reader notes on Storyworthy: "the beginning of the story should be the opposite of the end"; "we all tend to connect our sentences with the word 'and'. This is a mistake. The ideal connective tissue in any story are the words 'but' and 'therefore'." https://www.shortform.com/summary/storyworthy-summary-matthew-dicks and https://publish.obsidian.md/aidanhelfant/Literature+Notes/Readwise/%F0%9F%93%9ABooks/Storyworthy . Practitioner, secondhand.
- Art of Manliness interview with Dicks on Homework for Life and the five-second moment. https://www.artofmanliness.com/people/social-skills/how-to-tell-better-stories/ . Practitioner.
- Parker and Stone, NYU "Storytelling Challenges" class (2011) and Tisch 2017: beats must connect with "but" or "therefore", not "and then"; "that gives the causation between each beat, and that's the story." https://perell.com/note/but-therefore-rule/ and https://thehustle.co/storytelling-secrets-netted-matt-stone-trey-parker-600-million . Practitioner.
- Jenny Hoyos (10M average views per Short) describes "but, so" storytelling: "every 'but' stops someone from scrolling"; she writes the hook and the last line first. https://vidiq.com/blog/post/how-jenny-hoyos-gets-10m-views-per-youtube-short/ and https://podcast.creatorscience.com/jenny-hoyos/ . Practitioner with self-reported retention targets (about 90 percent), not independently verified.

**Note.** Three independent practitioners (a Moth champion, two TV writers, a Shorts creator) converge on the same rule, which is as strong as practitioner evidence gets, but nobody has published a retention curve split by connective word.
**Evidence grade:** practitioner. **Confidence:** high that the rule is sound, medium that it is the dominant factor in a 90 second reel.

## 8. Which big structure survives 68 seconds

**Answer.** Kindra Hall's Normal, Explosion, New Normal is the only one of the four that fits a reel without amputation, because it is three beats and its four components (an identifiable character, authentic emotion, a significant moment, specific details) are things one person can supply in a cab. Pixar rule 4 ("Once upon a time. Every day. One day. Because of that. Because of that. Until finally.") is the same shape with two "because of that" beats and is the best spoken template for a 60 to 90 second piece. Harmon's eight-step circle and Duarte's sparkline are built for 22 minutes and 18 minutes respectively; in a reel they collapse to a single "what is, what could be" contrast (Duarte) or to steps 1, 2, 5 and 8 (Harmon: you, need, find, change), which is just Hall's arc again. No source applies any of these to reels with data; the maccelerator and CapCut time allocations are invented templates.

**Sources.**
- Hall, Stories That Stick: Normal, Explosion, New Normal, and the four components (identifiable characters, authentic emotion, a significant moment, specific details). https://www.nicknathan.com/books/stories-that-stick-by-kindra-hall and https://www.shortform.com/blog/kindra-hall-stories-that-stick/ . Practitioner, secondhand.
- Pixar's 22 rules (Emma Coats, 2011), rule 4 confirmed as the "once upon a time" structural template. https://www.creativebloq.com/art/animation/why-the-pixar-rules-of-storytelling-are-as-relevant-in-2026-as-they-were-15-years-ago and https://www.fastcompany.com/3018559/pixars-22-rules-of-storytelling-visualized . Practitioner.
- Harmon story circle, eight steps You, Need, Go, Search, Find, Take, Return, Change. https://studiobinder.com/blog/dan-harmon-story-circle and https://reedsy.com/blog/dan-harmon-story-circle/ . Practitioner. The one guide applying it to short form argues "the stakes function as your hook" with no data. https://www.socialrevver.com/blog/dan-harmon-story-circle .
- Duarte sparkline: alternate "what is" and "what could be" to a "new bliss". https://www.duarte.com/resources/secret-structure-great-talks/ . Practitioner.
- Short-form time templates (2 to 3 s hook, 15 to 20 s conflict, 20 to 30 s solution, 10 to 15 s CTA) are marketing inventions, cited only to flag them. https://maccelerator.la/en/?p=41547 . Folklore.

**Note.** The test for a structure at 68 seconds is whether it still works with one beat removed; Hall and Pixar 4 do, Harmon and Duarte do not.
**Evidence grade:** practitioner. **Confidence:** medium.

## 9. Open loops: how many, and how soon the first must close

**Answer.** The theory says curiosity is a feeling of deprivation about a specific gap in knowledge the viewer already partly holds (Loewenstein 1994), it peaks at moderate uncertainty and drops when the viewer either knows nothing or knows too much (Kang 2009, inverted U), and the memory half of the Zeigarnik story does not replicate, though the urge to resume an unfinished thing does (Ghibellini and Meier 2025 meta-analysis). The lab evidence on cliffhangers says withholding raises arousal but does not raise enjoyment or intent to continue (Wirz 2023), and that resolving suspense makes people more willing to come back (Hahn 2023). Nobody has measured a loop count for 90 second video; practitioner guidance converges on one loop per video that must close inside the video, with payoff rather than count as the thing that separates curiosity from clickbait, and the biggest documented loop user (MrBeast) spaces re-engagement points about three minutes apart in long video, which scales to at most one re-hook in a 90 second reel. The defensible rule is one main loop opened in the first sentence and closed in the last ten seconds, plus at most one small loop closed within 20 to 30 seconds of opening.

**Sources.**
- Loewenstein, The Psychology of Curiosity (1994): gap produces deprivation, curiosity needs prior knowledge, gap size predicts intensity. https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/sds/docs/golman/Information%20Gaps--Golman%20and%20Loewenstein.pdf and https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/nova/article/how-to-stimulate-curiosity/ . Measured (theory paper with reviewed studies).
- Kang et al. 2009, Psychological Science, n=19 fMRI plus behavioral: curiosity tracks reward circuitry, people pay tokens or wait to get answers, curiosity "increases with uncertainty (up to a point)". https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19619181/ and https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/sds/docs/loewenstein/WickCandleLearning.pdf . Measured, small n.
- Ghibellini and Meier 2025 meta-analysis: "no memory advantage for unfinished tasks but a general tendency to resume tasks"; Zeigarnik "lacks universal validity", Ovsiankina resumption holds. https://ideas.repec.org/a/pal/palcom/v12y2025i1d10.1057_s41599-025-05000-w.html . Measured.
- Wirz et al. 2023, Psychology of Popular Media, n=133: cliffhangers raised arousal, did not raise enjoyment or intention to keep watching. https://sonar.ch/documents/319990/files/2022_Wirz_Role.pdf . Measured.
- Hahn 2023, Media Psychology: unresolved suspense is a "noxious" state; resolution makes moving to the next installment more likely. https://www.buffalo.edu/news/releases/2023/06/hahn-cliffhangers.html . Measured, written narrative not video.
- Practitioner: "open it too wide and the video reads as bait; too narrow and nobody bothers; the gap has to close inside the video or retention collapses"; "'wait until the end' in every video trains your audience to ignore it". https://www.descript.com/blog/article/the-youtubers-guide-to-the-curiosity-gap-how-to-keep-your-audiences-interest and https://www.socialync.io/blog/open-loops-in-content-2026 . Practitioner.
- MrBeast production guide (leaked Sept 2024): re-engagement roughly every three minutes, Hook, Story, Payoff. https://www.tubefilter.com/2024/09/17/mrbeast-internal-production-guide-leaked-key-points/ and https://omr.com/de/daily/mrbeast-doc-pdf-leak . Practitioner.
- The "68 percent first-3-second retention for curiosity hooks" figure is one creator's dataset with no method. https://dev.to/bulkdl/hook-engineering-the-science-of-the-first-3-seconds-in-short-form-video-2026-2jp9 . Folklore.

**Note.** The science supports gaps that the viewer can almost fill themselves and that get filled; it does not support stacking loops, and the cliffhanger studies cut against ending on one.
**Evidence grade:** measured for the mechanism, folklore for any count. **Confidence:** medium.

## 10. Stakes a stranger feels: what transportation research says

**Answer.** Green and Brock's transportation model says a viewer is persuaded and held to the degree they are absorbed, and absorption rises with story quality, vivid imagery, and emotional engagement, with emotional engagement the dimension that best predicts effects in Busselle and Bilandzic's narrative engagement scale. Identification is driven more by whose perspective the story is told from than by surface similarity (Hoeken 2016), which is why first-person "I" stories in a cab beat "a guy came in" stories even for an audience that is not a parts manager. Berger and Milkman found sharing tracks high-arousal emotion (awe, anger, anxiety, amusement) and practical value independently, which explains the library's winners: money, pride, the boss, the wife and time are all high-arousal and all concrete. What else works, per the same literature: a specific loss already in motion rather than a hypothetical, a character who wants something simple, and a detail the viewer can picture (Hall's "specific details", Dicks' five-second moment). Nothing here was tested on 90 second vertical video.

**Sources.**
- Green and Brock 2000, JPSP; later work: "stories made with high craftsmanship that follow the narrative format encourage transportation"; literary texts transported more than purpose-written prose. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5954649 and https://gwern.net/docs/www/pdfs.semanticscholar.org/39691cee32e52fbfc33f1a0316aa0d008cb699a4.pdf . Measured.
- Green and Appel 2024, Advances in Experimental Social Psychology: antecedents sorted into story factors, individual differences, situational variables; vivid mental imagery central. https://mcm.uni-wuerzburg.de/fileadmin/06110300/2024/Pdfs/Green___Appel__2024__Advances_Preprint.pdf . Measured review.
- Busselle and Bilandzic narrative engagement scale: narrative understanding, attentional focus, emotional engagement, narrative presence; emotional engagement most predictive. https://opus.bibliothek.uni-augsburg.de/opus4/frontdoor/index/index/docId/39512 . Measured.
- Hoeken, Kolthoff and Sanders 2016, Human Communication Research 42(2): perspective beat similarity as a driver of identification across two studies. https://research-portal.uu.nl/en/publications/story-perspective-and-character-similarity-as-drivers-of-identifi/ . Measured.
- Berger and Milkman 2012, JMR, about 7,000 NYT articles plus experiments: high-arousal emotion and practical value each raise sharing; sadness lowers it. https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2011/11/Virality.pdf . Measured, text not video.
- Wirz 2023 (above) is the caution: arousal alone without resolution did not raise intent to continue.

**Note.** The research names the ingredients (first person, emotion, imagery, a concrete want) but the "money, pride, boss, wife, time" list is the library's own measured finding and is consistent with it.
**Evidence grade:** measured. **Confidence:** medium-high for the mechanism, medium for the transfer to reels.

## 11. The pilot line: "if you're new here, I'm..."

**Answer.** There is no published test of the phrase itself, and the search found none. What exists: Mosseri's own statement that the first three seconds are the hook and creators should not spend them introducing themselves or thanking viewers; YouTube's retention report that lets a creator split new versus returning viewers to see what an intro costs; and practitioner consensus that a self-introduction belongs after the hook has landed, if at all, and that short-form viewers who want to know who you are go to the bio. The strongest argument against any pilot line in a talking head is Hoeken: identification comes from perspective, which the story supplies, not from credentials. If a credential is needed for the story to work ("I order these parts"), say it as a clause inside the story's first or second sentence, not as a separate introduction; if it is not needed, leave it to the caption and the name field.

**Sources.**
- Mosseri, quoted March 2025: the first three seconds are "the hook"; do not spend them "introducing yourself or thanking them for watching the video. Get right to the point." https://www.inman.com/2025/03/07/head-of-instagram-reveals-secrets-to-social-media-success/ . Practitioner (platform head), no numbers.
- YouTube Studio key moments report: "Intro" figure is share still watching at 30 s; split by new vs returning viewers. https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415 . Measured tool, no published average.
- Social Media Examiner: "you don't need to introduce yourself in every single short-form video. Viewers will go to your bio." https://www.socialmediaexaminer.com/shareable-short-form-video-content-get-more-views-and-engagement/ . Practitioner.
- Captions.ai: avoid "an intro, your name, or context the viewer didn't ask for"; identity "can come after you've earned the watch". https://captions.ai/blog/how-to-make-instagram-reels . Practitioner, vendor.
- Backstage talking-head guide: introduce yourself after the hook to answer "why should I be listening to you?" https://www.backstage.com/magazine/article/how-to-make-a-talking-head-video-78858/ . Practitioner.
- The "65 percent drop in the first three seconds" figure has no traceable source. https://medium.com/@techwithlandon/want-over-70-retention-on-your-short-form-videos-heres-how-to-do-it-5ce7bae94a43 . Folklore.

**Note.** This is testable on the account: the account's `skip_rate_pct` per reel is the exact measure; run five reels with the credential folded into sentence one against five with none.
**Evidence grade:** practitioner. **Confidence:** medium that a standalone pilot line costs hold, low on exact size.

## 12. The ending: hard cut on payoff, question, cliffhanger or call to action

**Answer.** The retention-chart guidance from several analytics vendors reads a steep fall in the last seconds as the ending outliving its value, and the fix they all give is the same: cut the moment the payoff lands, delete the sign-off, make the last frame rhyme with the first so the replay is seamless, and if there is a call to action put it before the usual drop point and make it one ask. The lab evidence on cliffhangers (Wirz, Hahn) says ending unresolved raises arousal but not the wish to continue, and the CSR and TikTok studies say stacked calls to action lower engagement and that "share this" videos did not out-perform on plays. Replays count as views on Instagram and on YouTube Shorts (average percentage viewed can exceed 100), which is the only measured reason a loop ending beats a hard stop. No controlled comparison of ending types exists; the question ending for comments is folklore and the one anecdote on payoff-to-opening loops (rewatches 4 to 21 percent) is uncontrolled.

**Sources.**
- Instagram retention chart and skip rate (rolled out Nov 2025): scrub per second; replays are counted. https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-adds-retention-insights-reels/758464/ and https://metricool.com/instagram-reel-analytics/ . Measured tool.
- MeetEdgar reading of an end-of-reel drop: repeated conclusion, ending that ran long, disconnected CTA, several asks, branded outro; fix is "end as soon as the message is complete". https://meetedgar.com/blog/instagram-reels-analytics-guide . Practitioner.
- Async: cut immediately once the final point lands, remove trailing pauses and sign-offs for a cleaner loop. https://async.com/blog/how-to-edit-instagram-reels/ . Practitioner.
- "An abrupt outro or a clear 'okay, bye!' ending reduces replay probability. Reels with endings that connect back to their beginning earn replays." https://creatorflow.so/blog/instagram-reels-retention-graph/ . Practitioner.
- YouTube Shorts: average percentage viewed above 100 means rewatching; a half-second fade breaks the loop. https://vidiq.com/blog/post/double-youtube-watch-time-looping-shorts and https://contentstudio.io/blog/youtube-shorts-analytics-guide . Measured metric, practitioner advice.
- Multiple CTAs in one message reduce engagement (2,000+ branded posts); brand-program invites cut likes and shares. https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/13/7/3812 . Measured, not video.
- #TulsaFlop TikTok study: call-to-action videos did not get uniquely higher plays. https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.07716 . Measured.
- Wirz 2023 and Hahn 2023 (question 9) on cliffhangers. Measured.
- Jenny Hoyos writes the last line first and ends on it. https://vidiq.com/blog/post/how-jenny-hoyos-gets-10m-views-per-youtube-short/ . Practitioner.

**Note.** This agrees with Jacob's standing rules (no on-screen CTA, no outro, no fade) and with the account's own share data; the only addition from the research is that the last line should be the five-second moment, and should rhyme with the opening so the loop lands.
**Evidence grade:** practitioner, with measured support against stacked CTAs and unresolved cliffhangers. **Confidence:** medium-high.

## What it means for a one-man diesel creator filming a daily talking head in his truck cab

Rules, each with the thing to measure:

1. **Write the last line first, then the first line as its opposite.** The last line is the five-second moment, said plainly. The first line states what he believed before it. Test: skip rate on reels built this way against the account's 48 percent average.
2. **Every sentence after the first must earn a "but" or a "therefore".** Read the script aloud and strike any sentence that joins with "and then". Test: average watch time per reel against the 6.8 second baseline.
3. **Three beats only: normal, explosion, new normal.** If a beat cannot be cut without the story breaking, it stays; if it can, it goes. Target 60 to 90 seconds with the explosion before the halfway mark.
4. **One main loop, opened in sentence one, closed in the last ten seconds.** At most one small loop, closed within 25 seconds of opening. Never end on an unresolved one.
5. **Tell it from inside.** "I" did, "I" paid, "I" was wrong. A customer story becomes his story only at the point it cost him something; otherwise it is not a reel.
6. **Name the concrete loss in the first ten seconds.** Dollars, hours, a Saturday, a word from the boss or the wife. Hypothetical loss does not transport anyone.
7. **No pilot line.** Any credential rides inside the story as a clause ("I order these for a living, and I still bought the wrong one"). Test: five reels with the folded credential against five with none, compare skip rate.
8. **Cut on the last word of the payoff.** No sign-off, no breath after, no fade. Make the last frame match the first frame (same seat, same angle) so the replay is invisible.
9. **One ask, and only in the caption.** Share line in caption line 1, nothing spoken, nothing burned in.
10. **Homework for Life, cab edition.** One moment a day written in the phone before the drive home; the day's talking head is tomorrow's best moment, not today's topic.

### Worked 90-second beat sheet: the part that failed three times

Premise: a part on his own 6.7 (keep it his truck, not a customer's) that he replaced three times before finding out why.

| Time | Beat | Line (spoken, one take) | Structure |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 to 4 s | Opposite opening, loop opens | "I order parts for a living and I bought the same wrong part three times for my own truck." | Hall: normal. Dicks: opposite of the ending. Main loop: why three times. |
| 4 to 14 s | Concrete loss | "Three hundred and some dollars, three Saturdays, and my wife asking why the truck's on jack stands again." | Stakes: money, time, the wife. First person. |
| 14 to 30 s | First failure, "but" | "First one went in, fine for two weeks, but then the same code, same symptom. So I figured bad part." | But / therefore chain starts. |
| 30 to 45 s | Second failure, "therefore" | "So I ordered the better brand. Lasted a month. Then dead again. Now I'm mad at the brand, not at myself." | Small loop opens: what is he missing. |
| 45 to 60 s | Turn, "but" | "But here's the thing I see at the counter every week on semis and ignored on my own truck: when the same part dies twice, the part isn't the problem. Something upstream is killing it." | Explosion. Small loop closes with the principle. |
| 60 to 78 s | Third time, the fix | "Third time I checked what feeds it. Found the real fault in ten minutes. Twenty-dollar fix. The part was fine all three times." | Payoff of the main loop. |
| 78 to 88 s | Five-second moment | "I knew that rule. I tell truckers that rule. I just didn't think it applied to me." | New normal. Hall's authentic emotion, Dicks' change. |
| 88 to 90 s | Hard cut | Cut on "me." Same framing as frame one. | No outro, loop lands. |

What to check after posting: skip rate (target under 40), average watch time against 6.8 s, shares per reach, and whether comments repeat the rule back ("if it dies twice, look upstream"), which is the sign the five-second moment landed.
