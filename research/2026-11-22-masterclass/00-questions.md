# The inspired questions (2026-11-22, start of the two-hour run)

Jacob: "Go through every study, everything you know about social media and create inspired questions. Then research what you need to get those answered by multiple sources. Become a master of the research and use that to create the ultimate editor of reels and social media master. The goal is to become as human as possible and edit in a way that tells a story but also hooks people in for the whole time without blowing them up with tons of cuts. Dive into what you have done and learn from it."

What the library already says, in one paragraph: 58 studied reels and profiles, 109 numbered rules. The account's own data says personal talking heads engage at 14.8 percent against 1.4 for sponsor reels on the same reach, skip rate under 40 is the gate for shares, average watch is 6.8 s on 30 s reels, memes share and build reels do not. The studies say: move on frame 1, first noun by 0.9 s, one person named in sentence one, say the thing they already think, the answer held to the last ten seconds, cut on motion every 2 to 4 s, no outro, series packaging, one day ahead, double down. Today Mariah (13-year editor, 50K likes) argued the opposite of the slot-machine edit and proved it with eight notebook inserts and no transitions. Caleb Ralston held one locked shot. Reesa-style one-take car stories exist at the scale of millions. So the open tension in the library is this: the rules say cut every 2 to 4 s, and the best human work this week barely cuts. These questions are about resolving that.

## A. Holding attention without cutting
1. What actually holds attention in a long single take: change in the frame (gesture, prop, light, distance), change in the voice (pitch, pace, pause), or change in the information (open loops)? Which has evidence, not folklore?
2. Walter Murch's rule of six ranks emotion over story over rhythm over eye-trace over screen plane over space. Does short vertical video change that order, and what does "cut for emotion" mean in a talking head?
3. Is "a visual change every 3 to 5 seconds" a measured retention finding or a repeated claim? Where did it come from?
4. What do the one-take masters of 2024 to 2026 do (Reesa Teesa's 50-part car series, Keith Lee's one-shot reviews, Nara Smith's slow cooking, Drew Afualo's rants, Hank Green, Corporate Natalie)? Shot length, camera distance, movement, where the change comes from.
5. When is a jump cut invisible and when does it read as robotic? Cut on breath, cut on gesture, punch-in percentage, eye-line continuity. What do documentary interview editors do to hide a cut?
6. How much B-roll is enough: cover every noun, or one insert per idea? What does the retention curve say when a face holds versus when an insert lands?

## B. Story
7. Matthew Dicks (Storyworthy): the five-second moment, start with the opposite, "but" and "therefore", no "and then". How do these compress into 60 to 120 s?
8. Dan Harmon's story circle, Pixar's rules, Duarte's sparkline, Kindra Hall: which structure survives a 68 s reel and which is too big for it?
9. Curiosity gap (Loewenstein) and Zeigarnik: how many open loops can a 90 s video carry before it feels like a trick, and how early must the first one close?
10. Stakes a stranger feels: in the studies the winners named money, pride, the boss, the wife, time. What does the research on narrative transportation say about why those work and what else does?
11. Where should the pilot line ("if you're new here, I'm...") sit, if anywhere? Evidence on self-introduction and drop-off.
12. Ending: hard cut on the payoff versus a question versus a cliffhanger. What do retention and share data say about the last 3 seconds?

## C. The hook as a human
13. First 1.5 s: spoken line, visual change, text, or all three? Data on talking-head hooks specifically, not montage hooks.
14. "Don't start with hi": why does a greeting cost views, and does a mid-sentence open read as a trick to a 35-year-old man in Wyoming?
15. The "re-hook": is the one-third, two-thirds re-hook measured anywhere, or inherited from YouTube?
16. The title sentence burned top for 3 s: does on-screen text in the first second raise or lower hold for a face-to-camera video? Any A/B data?

## D. Delivery
17. Words per minute, pause length, pitch range: what do the most-watched talking heads measure at, and what does speech research say about perceived warmth and competence?
18. Filler words, stumbles, restarts: authenticity signal or retention cost? Where is the line?
19. Teleprompter tells and how to beat them: bullet scripting, memorised first and last lines, eye position relative to the lens.
20. Talking to one person: evidence that second-person singular raises completion or shares.
21. Lowering status (Mariah: "I look like what I look like", Jake: "I don't own a shop, I work at one"): research on self-deprecation, vulnerability and trust.

## E. Sound
22. Voice loudness, music under voice (what dB below the voice), room tone, silence as a device: what do mixers for short vertical actually do and what does Instagram do to the file after?
23. Is sound design (whooshes, risers, SFX on text) a retention lift or a tell? Any evidence either way; what did the no-SFX winners this week do instead?

## F. Text on screen
24. Burned captions: the "80 percent watch muted" claim, where it came from, and what the current Instagram data says. Do captions raise completion for a face-to-camera reel?
25. Word-by-word karaoke versus phrase captions versus none: any retention evidence? What do the 2026 slow-edit creators use?
26. Caption placement and size relative to the Reels UI (the right rail, the caption overlay at the bottom). Safe zones as measured now.

## G. Instagram in late 2026
27. What ranks a Reel now: watch time, sends per reach, likes per reach, 3-second views, completion? Mosseri's statements 2025 to 2026, in order, and what changed.
28. Trial Reels: current eligibility (follower floor), how winners graduate, and what creators report about using them for hook tests.
29. Length: Reels up to 3 minutes since January 2025, "Views" as the headline metric since April 2025. Does longer help or hurt a 500-follower creator? What do the retention curves in Insights show by length?
30. Original content and "aggregator" penalties, the "repost" label, and what counts as original when the same master goes to TikTok and Shorts.
31. Posting cadence versus quality at under 1,000 followers: any data from creators who documented it?

## H. Humour and relatability
32. Comedy structure that survives one shot: setup and turn, the rule of three, the callback, the straight face. What do solo skit creators do with no second actor?
33. Relatability: what makes a stranger see themselves? Research on self-disclosure, shared frustration, specificity ("the 10 mm") versus generality.
34. Trust: competence and warmth, the "pratfall effect", parasocial trust research. What does a parts manager say or show that builds it fastest?

## I. Camera and light for a human face
35. Eye level, distance, lens width, 24 versus 30 fps, side window light, background depth: what makes a cab talking head feel like a person and not a vlog?
36. Face position and the Reels UI: where the eyes should sit in the frame; headroom; what breaks when Instagram crops to 4:5 in the grid.

## J. Learning from our own work
37. Across the account's 84 posts: what do the ten lowest skip rates share (open, length, subject, first word), and what do the ten highest?
38. The "Find the gratitude" cut (64.6 s, four cuts, cold open at the strongest line): judged against questions 1 to 36, what did it get right and what would the better cut be?
39. The Harley Trade reel (18.5 s, original dialogue, "how much you pay for that truck?") against the same test.
40. Where do the 109 rules contradict each other, and which side does the evidence take?

## K. The masters
41. Who holds the longest watch time with the fewest cuts in 2025 to 2026, across niches, and what do they share? Is there a diesel or trades creator doing it?
42. What did the 2026 "anti-slot-machine" movement (Mariah, others) actually change in the numbers of the creators who adopted it?
