# 09. What the studied library says about cut rate versus results

Written 2026-11-22 for the masterclass run, question 40 of `00-questions.md` and the open tension named in its first paragraph: the rules say cut every 2 to 4 s, the best human work this week barely cuts. Sources: every BREAKDOWN.md and DOSSIER.md under `research/reel-references/`, the scene lists (`scenes.csv`, PySceneDetect) in those folders, the per-video cut counts in the omgadrian, tenfoldmarc and nxtlvl dossiers, the montage-craft and hook-atlas tables, and `research/2026-11-21-DIESEL-HOUR.md` (rules 48 to 109). No web.

How to read the table. One row per studied reel; pure profile dossiers with no single reel (ultraskills, edita, tudormorari, junyuh, jessicacarter, nxtlvl's own shorts at 0 to 15 views) are left out, and their per-video reads are used in the analysis where they carry a number. "Cuts" is hard cuts: the breakdown's stated count where it gives one, else the scene count from `scenes.csv` minus one, else a count from the second-by-second table. Card reels where nothing cuts and elements enter are 0 cuts, and the element rate is in the device column. Likes are Instagram unless the reel was pulled from a TikTok or YouTube mirror; where likes are hidden the cell carries views. Comments on the AI-edit lane are comment-gate counts, not conversation. Cuts per minute is cuts divided by length times 60. "Camera" is how the frame was held: locked, handheld, mount, walking or moving, crew for chase cars, n/a for no camera. The device column is the one thing the breakdown credits for the hold, in its own words shortened.

## 3. Where the breakdowns contradict the DIESEL-HOUR rule numbers

## 4. What the library proves about cuts

1. **Cut rate does not sort winners from losers.** Top and bottom terciles sit at 16.6 and 14.5 cpm across 107 reels, 10.9 and 7.5 in diesel, 17.0 and 16.7 in talking heads; the spread inside a tercile runs 0 to 49 cpm (Mariah, Rourke, c10talk, trent_cody, omgadrian flowers).
2. **What sorts them is whether something is changing that the viewer cares about, and a cut is only one way to change it.** Rourke relit the room 34 times on one shot; Lana added an element every 2 to 4 s and never cut; nxtlvl and post2all cut zero times in 50 s with a new element every 0.9 to 1.3 s; Mariah changed the frame every 8 to 10 s with a notebook and held 50K likes.
3. **In diesel, the share leaders are one to three shots and the view leaders cut on every clause.** Tom's hard worker 1 shot 6.36 percent, DDC nature doc 18 shots 6.89, DPP 1 shot, Lifted Rippers 3, Freedom Syndicate 3, the 10 mil 1; Oversteer 49 and DriveHub 77 for the views at under 0.1 percent sends. Cuts buy watch, not sends.
4. **When a cut is motivated by information it is free; when it is motivated by rhythm it costs trust.** Oversteer and DriveHub cut to the noun of each clause and win; the beat montages share less than the same creators' one-take talk; Mariah's thesis ("the edits should serve the story") is the house thesis with a 50K receipt.
5. **A hold is alive if the sound or the subject is moving.** Westen's 13.5 s crank, Tom's 5.9 s idle, rust_ridge's 66 s with the IDI rattle, Tatum's 40.8 s walk. A hold is dead only when nothing happens in it, which is what killed Harley Trade's last 3.5 s and ICON's 25 noon-sun holds, not the cut count.
6. **The one-take talking heads that won carry exactly one physical device.** Notebook (Mariah), legal pad and hands (Mariah), countdown ring and headline (Caleb), pyramid and pills (Lana), fill-in list (Rourke), two running cards (justyn duel), costume changes (dealerplateguy), the part in hand (c10talk). None of them carries two.
7. **The fast-cut talking heads that won are one creator's system, and even there the subject did the work.** omgadrian 19 to 43 cpm; same cadence on camera settings 0.79 percent, on "your girlfriend" 3.65 to 4.33. His slowest episode (3.1 s per cut, the cuts were the subject) has his highest comment rate.
8. **Cards that enter and leave do the job of cuts on a face plate.** tenfoldmarc's 19 "cuts" are card swaps while the face never cuts; justyn's three framings read as 5 scenes because whips hide the joins; chandler's 21 cuts carry a PiP that slides instead of cutting. The plate stays, the furniture moves.
9. **Camera movement replaces cutting in every truck winner a single person can shoot.** Kindig CF1 half orbit 9 s 39K; mister_zachary 13 s 7.8K; joey's walk to the pipe 17.3M; Karissa's walk; abigaildaniellla's five hooks, all moves, not edits. The addendum already says whip only when the camera moved.
10. **Two to four seconds is the cadence of one lane: the sold AI-edit talking head (Kallaway, justyn, viashaan, Edita's prompt), and it is the bottom third of the table on likes.** The library has no winner whose breakdown credits the 2 to 4 s cadence itself for the hold.
11. **The hook rule is about frame 1, not about cuts.** Every reel above 1 percent sends moved inside frame 1 or had an element entering by 0.33 s (hook atlas, rule 43); the two best diesel share rates (FASS 2.95, Dirty Diesel 2.25) have no cut in the first 3 s. What moves is the subject.
12. **Our own account agrees.** The 10 mil (1 shot, 17.6 s) is 4.05 percent sends and the record; the gooseneck (long hold, 27 s watch) is 0.003; Harley Trade (4 cuts, 18.5 s) skipped at 37.8 percent, which is good, and sent at 0.05, which is the dead hold and the missing line, not the cuts; "Yep" (1 shot, 17 s, steam) 0.6 percent, failing on the joke being about Jacob, not on the edit.

## 5. The cut rule, rewritten

Cut when the information changes, never on a clock. The viewer stays for a change they care about, and a cut is the most expensive way to make one; the cheaper ways, in the order the library's winners use them, are a hand and a prop in the frame, a written word landing on the noun (notebook, list, headline, counter), the subject or the camera moving, the sound changing, and the voice turning a corner. A talking head gets one physical device per episode and cuts only to it, in on the noun and out when the point lands, which in Mariah's hands is eight times in 68 s and in Caleb's is never; a story told over footage cuts to whatever the sentence just named and holds there until the next sentence names something else, which is every one to three seconds in Oversteer and DriveHub and is fine because the voice is carrying it; a montage cuts once per bar on the beat and is the pride slot, not the growth slot. A hold is alive for as long as something in it is moving or sounding, and dead the moment nothing is, whatever its length: Westen holds a crank for 13 s, Harley Trade died in 3.5 s of quiet truck. Frame 1 must move inside a third of a second, the last frame is the payoff, and nothing else in the reel needs a number. If a cut cannot be named for what it shows, it is a transition, and the house does not use those.
