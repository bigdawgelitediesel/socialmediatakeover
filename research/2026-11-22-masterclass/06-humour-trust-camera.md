## 32. Comedy structure that survives one shot

**Answer.** The only comedy structure with a measured result behind it is the rule of three: a reported experiment found jokes with three repetitions before the punchline were rated funnier than jokes with two, which supports the practitioner account that two beats set a pattern and the third breaks it. Everything else solo creators use (setup and turn, heightening, callback, straight face) is practitioner craft from improv and sketch teaching, consistent with but not tested by the benign violation theory of humour (something is wrong and okay at the same time). The improv version of escalation ("do the comedy again but make it more", levers are specifics, emotion and stakes) is the cleanest description of "and then it gets worse" for a one-shot video. Solo skit creators do not need a second actor: the biggest ones write from one punchline backwards, play every part themselves with left-right framing and eyeline, or make the viewer the second character by talking to them as if they were the customer, the boss or the wife. Deadpan works because the humour sits in the gap between flat delivery and absurd content; no study was found that compares straight face with laughing at your own joke.

**Sources.**
- Psychological Science / APS, "Explaining the funny, then not funny, then funny again joke" (https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/explaining-the-funny-then-not-funny-then-funny-again-joke.html). Reports an experiment where three repetitions beat two. **Measured**, one study, secondhand.
- Writer's Digest, "Comedy writing secrets: triple the funny" (https://writersdigest.com/there-are-no-rules/comedy-writing-secrets-triples). Two set the pattern, the third breaks it; the third must be specific and familiar. **Practitioner.**
- Warren and McGraw 2016, "Differentiating what is humorous from what is not", JPSP, six studies, 1,200+ participants (https://www.ovid.com/journals/jpspy/pdf/10.1037/pspi0000041~differentiating-what-is-humorous-from-what-is-not) and the 2010 paper (https://leeds-faculty.colorado.edu/mcgrawp/pdf/mcgraw.warren.2010.pdf). Humour needs a violation that also reads as benign. **Measured**, authors' own work, one methodological critique exists (Westwood, UCT, https://humanities.uct.ac.za/media/251707).
- The Free Association, "Easy ways to heighten game" (https://www.thefreeassociation.co.uk/blog/easy-ways-to-heighten-game) and Will Hines, "Name the game seven ways" (https://willhines.substack.com/p/name-the-game-seven-ways). Find the unusual thing, heighten it by specifics, emotion, stakes; "if this is true what else is true". **Practitioner.**
- Digiday on Kris Collins (Kallmekris), 37M followers, who plays every character herself: she bases the script on one punchline and writes backwards from it, and scripts rather than chasing trends (https://digiday.com/media/why-tiktok-creator-kris-collins-takes-a-scripted-approach-to-content-and-doesnt-rely-on-popular-trends-to-gain-followers). **Practitioner.**
- Kapwing, "How to make a talk-to-yourself video" (https://www.kapwing.com/resources/how-to-talk-to-yourself-in-a-video-in-4-easy-steps/). Character A left of frame looking right, character B right looking left, camera stays on one side of the line. **Practitioner.**
- Comedy Crowd, "How to write a comedy sketch" (https://thecomedycrowd.com/how-to-write-a-comedy-sketch/). Setup, escalation, punchline; a "false dawn" mid-sketch gives a breather before the last climb. **Practitioner.**
- StudioBinder, "What is deadpan" (https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/what-is-a-deadpan-definition/). Deadpan is the contrast of flat delivery with absurd content; the audience has to find the joke. **Practitioner.**
- Vice, "The science of being funny" (https://www.vice.com/en/article/the-science-of-being-funny) and NBC on humour research methods (https://www.nbcnews.com/healthmain/people-who-dont-laugh-easily-are-only-fooling-themselves-784122). Most humour research uses joke-book jokes, so transfer to performed comedy is uncertain. **Measured** caveat.
- "Three is the smallest number that makes a pattern" (Copyblogger, https://copyblogger.com/rule-of-three/). **Folklore**: plausible, never tested.

**Confidence:** medium on the rule of three and the benign violation frame; low on everything about delivery, because the deadpan and self-laugh question has no study and the solo creator material is interview-level.

## 33. Relatability: what makes a stranger see themselves

**Answer.** The measured findings point one way: people like those who disclose to them (Collins and Miller 1994, three meta-analyses), disclosure deepens parasocial attraction and relationship on Instagram (Kashian, New Media and Society), and sharing a negative attitude about a third thing bonds strangers faster than sharing a positive one, as long as the feeling is mild rather than a rant (Bosson et al. 2006; Weaver and Bosson 2011, "I feel like I know you"). That last result is the mechanism under the 10 mm socket: a small shared grievance about an outside object, held lightly. On specificity the evidence is weaker than the folklore says: Hansen and Wänke 2010 found concrete wording reads as more true, but a registered replication found the effect near zero, and a 2024 Auckland thesis argues it is detail, not abstractness, that does the work in social ads. Two cautions from 2024 and 2025 work: a disclosure that the audience can blame on your choices invites suspicion of manipulation, while one blamed on circumstances draws sympathy, and sensitive disclosure helps only where the audience expects a friend-like relationship rather than an information service. The "I thought it was only me" recognition effect has no rigorous study behind it that I could find; it survives as a clinician's explanation (Hendriksen on Humans of New York) and as creator folklore.

**Sources.**
- Collins and Miller 1994, "Self-disclosure and liking: a meta-analytic review", Psychological Bulletin 116(3) (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7809308/, PDF https://labs.psych.ucsb.edu/collins/nancy/UCSB_Close_Relationships_Lab/Publications_files/Collins%20and%20Miller,%201994.pdf). Disclosers are liked more; people disclose to those they like; being disclosed to raises liking. **Measured.**
- Bosson, Johnson, Niederhoffer and Swann 2006, "Interpersonal chemistry through negativity", Personal Relationships 13 (https://digitalcommons.usf.edu/psy_facpub/1188). Shared dislike of a third party predicted liking for a stranger more than shared liking, only when attitudes were weak. **Measured.**
- Weaver and Bosson 2011, "I feel like I know you", PSPB (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21296970/). Sharing a mild negative attitude made strangers feel they knew each other. **Measured.**
- Kashian, "Can you persuade 100,000 strangers?", New Media and Society (https://open.bu.edu/items/dbcf88f8-8ee0-4514-9b7a-bce760a7f399). Disclosure depth works through social attraction and parasocial relationship; follower count works through perceived expertise. **Measured**, experiment.
- Kim et al. 2024, Current Psychology, sensitive disclosure and relationship norms (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12144-024-06116-z). Helps in communal relationships, hurts in exchange ones. **Measured.**
- Achar et al., Kellogg working paper 2025, "Should I talk about cancer?" (https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/academics-research/research/detail/2025/should-i-talk-about-cancer-onset-controllability-and-relatedness). Disclosures blamed on controllable causes raise suspicion of manipulation. **Measured**, working paper.
- Jiang et al., Harvard, revealing weaknesses signals authenticity because observers infer no strategic self-presentation (https://dash.harvard.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/c4dc4408-2b76-4927-9364-6e9fcec80307/content). **Measured.**
- Hansen and Wänke 2010 summary (https://spring.org.uk/?p=14144); Kingston registered replication, effects dz 0.08 and 0.11 (https://eprints.kingston.ac.uk/id/eprint/43028/1/Henderson-E-L-43028-VoR.pdf); Auckland "Detailedness effect" thesis (https://researchspace.auckland.ac.nz/handle/2292/34080?show=full); van Horen, Wänke, Mussweiler, concreteness helps under uncertainty (https://www.london.edu/faculty-and-research/academic-research/w/when-it-pays-to-be-clear-the-appeal-of-concrete-communication-unde). **Measured**, mixed.
- Reese and Orrach 2023, Personal Relationships, people want reciprocal disclosure but dislike one that out-discloses them (https://phys.org/news/2023-05-situation-equally-validating.pdf). **Measured.**
- Hendriksen, "The psychology of why you love Humans of New York" (https://quickanddirtytips.com/?p=53299). A stranger doing the thing you thought only you did breaks "I'm the only one". **Practitioner.**
- Jalopnik, "Remembering the 10 mm socket" (https://jalopnik.com/remembering-the-10mm-socket-you-will-be-missed-1848762910) and Hagerty "7 jokes just for car people" (https://hagerty.com/media/maintenance-and-tech/7-jokes-just-for-car-people/comment-page-5). Why the joke bonds: common, constantly set down, true for everyone. One forum voice hates it as lazy (https://grassrootsmotorsports.com/forum/off-topic-discussion/lets-discuss-the-10mm-socket/161871/page2). **Folklore**, with a dissent worth remembering.
- Barasch and Berger 2014, JMR, broadcasting makes people avoid sharing what makes them look bad; narrowcasting favours useful content (https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/05/Barasch-Berger-2014,-JMR_1.pdf). **Measured.** Relevant because a share is a broadcast: the viewer shares what makes them look good for having noticed it.

**Confidence:** high that disclosure and a shared mild grievance build liking; medium that specific detail beats general; low on "recognition" as a distinct, measured effect.

## 34. Trust: competence, warmth, the pratfall, parasocial trust, and what a parts manager shows

**Answer.** Warmth is judged first and weighs more; competence is judged second and is what warmth is checked against (Fiske, Cuddy, Glick 2002 and later reviews; Wojciszke found the two dimensions carry about 82 percent of the variance in everyday social judgment). The pratfall effect is real but narrower than the blogs say: in Aronson, Willerman and Floyd 1966 the blunder helped only the already competent person and hurt the average one, the main effect of the blunder alone was not significant, and no large modern replication was found. So the order matters: establish competence, then show the mistake. Two-sided messages (saying the downside) raise source and message credibility (Eisend 2006 meta-analysis), and a voluntary disclosure is more credible than a forced one. For creators, parasocial relationship predicts trust and purchase intention across a 2024 Psychology and Marketing meta-analysis and a 251-paper review, and self-disclosure plus interactivity are the drivers; the same literature finds that once viewers sense persuasion, attitude and intent drop, which is why sponsor reels on this account sit at 1.4 percent engagement. Edelman's data says "a person like me" is among the most trusted voices (78 percent in the US flash poll on AI, about twice a CEO; the main barometer puts scientists at 77 and teachers at 75 against CEOs at 53 and journalists at 52). For the auto trade specifically, AAA found two in three US drivers distrust repair shops in general, mostly for unnecessary services (76 percent) and overcharging (73 percent), while 64 percent have one shop they trust. That tells a parts manager exactly which trust to supply: name the part you would not buy, say what it cost you on your own truck, and show the failure, because the viewer's prior is that the trade hides exactly those three things.

**Sources.**
- Fiske, Cuddy, Glick and Xu 2002, JPSP 82(6), stereotype content model (https://leeds-faculty.colorado.edu/dahe7472/Fiske-et-al-2002.pdf, PubMed https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12051578/); Fiske 2018 review on why warmth comes first (https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/0963721417738825); Wojciszke 82 percent variance figure via the Wikipedia SCM entry (https://Www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/Stereotype_content_model). **Measured.** Caveat from Kervyn, Fiske and Yzerbyt: most SCM studies measured warmth with only "friendly" and "warm" (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3792573).
- Aronson, Willerman and Floyd 1966, Psychonomic Science (https://www.doi.org/10.3758/BF03342263); Wikipedia summary with the 1970 self-esteem moderator and the self-comparison explanation (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pratfall_effect); TCU follow-up finding the blunderer bias only for competent and incompetent stimuli (https://repository.tcu.edu/handle/116099117/34775). **Measured**, old, small, interaction only.
- Eisend 2006, "Two-sided advertising: a meta-analysis", IJRM (https://data.zhenzhi.com/details/1/862192765/1/5); Allen 1991 and O'Keefe 1999 critique via MDPI review (https://www.mdpi.com/2076-0760/10/6/229). Two-sided messages raise credibility; voluntary disclosure beats forced. **Measured**, debate still open.
- Han and Balabanis 2024, Psychology and Marketing meta-analysis of influencer antecedents (https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/31583/1/); 1,531 effect sizes from 251 papers, persuasion knowledge reduces attitude and intent (https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s11747-024-01052-7); PLS-SEM study, interactivity and self-disclosure build PSR and intent (https://www.doi.org/10.1108/itp-07-2021-0548). **Measured.**
- Hartmann and Goldhoorn 2011, "Horton and Wohl revisited", Journal of Communication: a performer who addresses the camera (eye contact, "you") raises the viewer's sense of parasocial interaction. Cited from memory; **not verified this session**.
- Edelman AI flash poll, Nov 2025, 78 percent US trust "someone like me" (https://www.edelman.com/sites/g/files/aatuss191/files/2025-11/AI%20Flash%20Poll_Top%2010%20Findings_White.pdf; Fortune summary https://fortune.com/2025/11/18/trust-missing-ingredient-in-ai-boom-edelman-barometer-poll/); 2025 main barometer, scientists 77, teachers 75, CEOs 53, journalists 52 (Axios, https://www.axios.com/2025/01/19/edelman-2025-trust-barometer; Edelman Top 10, https://www.edelman.com/sites/g/files/aatuss191/files/2025-01/Global%20Top%2010%202025%20Trust%20Barometer.pdf). **Measured**, survey. A global 2025 "person like me" figure was not found.
- AAA 2016 auto repair survey (https://newsroom.aaa.com/2016/12/u-s-drivers-leery-auto-repair-shops/, PDF https://newsroom.aaa.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/12/Auto-Repair-Survey-FINAL.pdf); ConsumerAffairs 2024, 78 percent skeptical (https://www.consumeraffairs.com/automotive/auto-mechanics-trust-issues.html); AutoServiceWorld, 65 percent frustrated by cost opacity (https://www.autoserviceworld.com/how-many-think-theyve-been-overcharged-for-repairs/). **Measured**, surveys, the AAA one is old.
- AutoServe1 ("they distrust what they can't see", https://autoserve1.com/why-todays-customers-dont-trust-repair-shops-and-how-to-fix-that) and AutoNetTV (https://autonettv.com/why-customers-mistrust-service-recommendations/). Showing the part builds approval. **Practitioner** (vendors), no published data.
- FTC Endorsement Guides FAQ: an employee endorsing an employer's product must disclose the employment, and the profile bio is not enough (https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/documents/plain-language/pdf-0205-endorsement-guides-faqs_0.pdf; Disclosures 101, https://ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers/). **Measured** in the sense of regulation text. Supports the standing "I work there" rule.
- "Show weakness and people like you more" as a general rule (Upworthy, https://www.upworthy.com/tiny-mistakes-make-you-more-likable/). **Folklore**: overstates the 1966 interaction.

**Confidence:** high on warmth-first and two-sided credibility; medium on pratfall (real, conditional); high that the auto trade's trust deficit is about unnecessary work and cost; medium on the exact Edelman figures because the "person like me" number came from a flash poll, not the main report.

## 35. Eye level, distance, lens, frame rate, window light, background: person, not vlog

**Answer.** Three measured results decide most of this. Eye level is the most trusted camera height (Baranowski and Hecht 2018; Olsen 2021 found students rated eye-level lecture video more professional than low or high angles). Camera distance is a trust cue on its own: Bryan, Perona and Adolphs 2012 found faces shot at about 2 feet were rated less trustworthy, competent and attractive than the same faces at 7 feet, with viewers unaware of the warp, and Ward et al. 2018 modelled a 12 inch selfie as showing a nasal base about 30 percent wider than at 5 feet. The Pocket 3 is a 20 mm equivalent f/2.0 lens with a 20 cm minimum focus, which is why Engadget called it unflattering for close faces; the fix is distance, not a different camera. On frame rate there is no study of naturalness for a seated talking head; measured HFR work shows people can just tell 24 from about 27 fps and rate 48 and 60 as smoother and sharper, while practitioners say 24 softens fidgeting and 30 is the web default, so this is a taste call and 30 is the safer default for Reels. Window light is the one free soft key: expose for the face, put the window to one side at roughly 45 degrees, accept the window behind you only if you can live with it blowing out, and pull the subject off any flat surface so the background has depth and falls away.

**Sources.**
- Baranowski and Hecht 2018, camera angle and trust (https://experimental.psychologie.uni-mainz.de/files/2024/06/2018BaranowskiHecht_Camera_Angle.pdf). Eye level most trustworthy; slight elevation gives subtle changes, strong deviation dominates; personality ratings unchanged. **Measured.**
- Olsen 2021, camera angles in learning videos (https://repository.uwl.ac.uk/id/eprint/7749/1/Olsen%20%282021%29%20The%20impact%20of%20camera%20angles%20in%20learning%20videos%20on%20the%20perception%20of%20teaching%20excellence%20and%20emotional%20connectedness%20of%20students%20in%20the%20creative%20industries.pdf). Low angle reads patronising and informal; eye level professional; high angle received worse. **Measured**, small.
- Bryan, Perona and Adolphs 2012, PLOS ONE 7(9) e45301 (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3448657). Close (about 2 ft) versus far (about 7 ft) portraits; lower trust-game investment and lower ratings at close range; controlled for image size, expression, lighting. **Measured.**
- Ward et al. 2018, JAMA Facial Plastic Surgery, "The selfie effect" (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5876805; Rutgers release https://www.rutgers.edu/news/selfies-drive-self-image-and-may-lead-many-seek-plastic-surgery). 30 percent wider nasal base at 12 inches versus 5 feet; no difference at 1.5 m. Cooper's reply letter argues it is viewing habit rather than photographic distortion (https://escholarship.org/content/qt0g40g8j8/qt0g40g8j8.pdf). **Measured**, modelled, contested.
- Gothenburg and Stanford webcam study: looking into the lens read as more sociable and sympathetic; distance did not change impressions in video meetings (https://www.gu.se/en/news/look-into-the-camera-during-video-meetings). **Measured.** Note the distances tested were ordinary webcam distances, not 2 ft.
- DJI Osmo Pocket 3 specs: 1 inch sensor, 20 mm equivalent, f/2.0, wide-angle accessory to 108 degrees (https://www.dji.com/osmo-pocket-3/specs; FAQ, minimum focus 20 cm, https://www.dji.com/osmo-pocket-3/faq; CineD, same FOV as Pocket 2, https://www.cined.com/dji-osmo-pocket-3-released-rotatable-oled-touchscreen-1-inch-type-sensor-4k-120fps-10-bit-d-log-m-and-more/). **Measured**, spec sheet. Engadget: too wide to flatter close faces (https://www.engadget.com/dji-osmo-pocket-3-review-maybe-the-only-vlogging-camera-you-need-163028145.html). **Practitioner.**
- Frame rate: DoVale RIT thesis, 77 observers, JND from 24 fps at about 26 to 28 fps (https://s3.cad.rit.edu/cadgallery_production/storage/media/uploads/faculty-s-projects/1918/documents/177/framerate-visibility-thesis.pdf); Allison, Fujii and Wilcox 2021, sharpness and motion quality rise most between 24 and 48 (https://static1.squarespace.com/static/565e05cee4b01c87068e7984/t/6000a853306bd72acf1ddb50/1610655828987/Allison_Fuji_Wilcox_2021IEEE.pdf); Helliker et al., viewers favoured higher rates on realism and smoothness (https://core.ac.uk/download/286608399.pdf). **Measured**, none on talking heads. Vimeo: 24 softens nervous movement (https://vimeo.com/blog/post/camera-frame-rate); Descript: 24 reads cinematic, 30 reads broadcast and web (https://descript.com/blog/article/frame-rate); Wistia on matching timeline to delivery (https://wistia.com/learn/production/what-is-frame-rate). **Practitioner.** "Reels must be 30 fps" appears only in third-party preset tables, not from Meta (https://evolveamz.com/meta-video-ad-specs-2026/). **Folklore.**
- Window and car light: Videomaker, "Lighting car interiors", expose for the face, the side window blows out, bounce windshield light with a reflector, ND gel on the window if you care (https://www.videomaker.com/article/13489-lighting-car-interiors/); Artlist, window as soft key, daylight shifts between takes (https://artlist.io/blog/interview-shooting/); UC ANR, subject between window and white bounce, shade on sunny days (https://class.ucanr.edu/node/139700); ASC "The talking head", cooler background and warmer subject for separation, watch background lines (https://theasc.com/article/shot-craft-the-talking-head-shooting-interviews/); RedShark on separation as the start of the cinematic look (https://www.redsharknews.com/the-secrets-of-the-cinematic-look-separation); Bucknell on backlight for separation (https://etlife.blogs.bucknell.edu/?p=480). **Practitioner.**
- "35 to 100 mm is the flattering range for faces" (Engadget, above). **Practitioner**, widely repeated, consistent with the distance studies but not tested as a lens claim.

**Confidence:** high on eye level and on distance as a trust cue; high on the Pocket 3 numbers; low on 24 versus 30 (no evidence either way for this use); medium on the light recipe (practitioner consensus, no measurement).

## 36. Face position and the Reels UI, headroom, and what breaks at 4:5 and 3:4

**Answer.** Meta publishes safe margins only for Reels ads: leave the top 14 percent, the bottom 35 percent and 6 percent each side clear (about 269, 672 and 65 px on 1080 by 1920). Organic Reels have no official figure; community checkers converge on 220 to 270 px at the top, 320 px at the bottom for the caption and audio line, and a 200 px right rail from about 1,040 px down for the like, comment and share stack. The profile grid moved from square to 3:4 in January 2025 (Mosseri confirmed a taller grid; the ratio is community-measured and Buffer says 4:5, so verify in the cover editor), which trims about 240 px off the top and bottom of a 9:16 cover, and the home feed preview is commonly quoted as 4:5 (1080 by 1350), which trims about 285 px top and bottom. The practical consequence: anything that must survive all three views lives in the middle band from roughly 285 to 1,250 px from the top. For a talking head that means eyes on or just above the upper third line of the full frame (around 600 to 700 px from the top), head and shoulders in the upper two-thirds, a palm of headroom not a hand, the face left of centre so the right rail never touches it, and the poster lockup in the band between the eyes and the caption line rather than at the top or the bottom. What breaks at 4:5 and 3:4 is headroom and chin: a face framed high for the 9:16 UI loses the top of the head in the grid, and a lockup burned near the bottom disappears entirely.

**Sources.**
- Meta Reels ads margins 14 / 35 / 6 percent (Lilach Bullock, https://www.lilachbullock.com/instagram-reels-safe-zone/; Sirency, https://www.sirency.com/blog/instagram-reels-safe-zone; Coinis, https://coinis.com/how-to/design-facebook-reels-ad). **Measured**, Meta ad spec quoted secondhand; Meta's own page did not load for one of them. 40 percent bottom for ads with disclaimers (same sources).
- Krumzi safe zone checker: 220 px top, 320 px bottom, 65 px sides, 200 px right from 1,040 px down (https://www.krumzi.com/tools/safe-zone-checker/instagram-reels). **Practitioner**, screen-measured.
- Revid: grid 3:4 since January 2025, 240 px trimmed top and bottom; safe middle band 285 to 1,250 px (https://www.revid.ai/instagram-reels-safe-zone). Brandshift says 4:5 (https://brandshift.squarespace.com/blog/instagram-sizing-guide). inro notes Mosseri confirmed the taller grid without a ratio (https://www.inro.social/tools/instagram-reels-safe-zone-checker). **Practitioner**, conflicting.
- Grid change coverage: Engadget on the test (https://www.engadget.com/social-media/instagrams-experimental-profile-grid-has-rectangular-images-instead-of-squares-160007086.html); Kapwing, 3:4 and centre-crop (https://www.kapwing.com/resources/instagrams-new-grid-layout-size-and-dimensions-2025/); Buffer, 1080 by 1440 preview for Reels (https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-grid/); Inquirer on the thumbnail adjust tool and Mosseri's "visible as intended" rationale (https://usa.inquirer.net/165044/bye-squares-why-instagram-switched-to-a-taller-grid). **Practitioner** reporting a platform change.

**Confidence:** high on the ad margins and the direction of the grid change; medium on the exact organic pixel numbers (community-measured, drift with each UI update); low on the exact grid ratio until checked in the cover editor.

## Claim ledger for clusters H and I

Measured (safe to build a rule on):
- Three beats before the turn rate funnier than two (one experiment, secondhand).
- Humour needs a violation that also reads as benign (Warren and McGraw, six studies).
- Being disclosed to raises liking (Collins and Miller 1994, meta-analysis).
- Disclosure depth builds parasocial relationship and persuasion (Kashian; PLS-SEM studies; two 2024 meta-analyses).
- A mild shared grievance about an outside target bonds strangers more than shared praise (Bosson 2006; Weaver and Bosson 2011).
- Disclosures blamed on your own choices invite suspicion; those blamed on circumstance draw sympathy (Achar 2025).
- Warmth is judged first and carries more weight than competence (Fiske and colleagues).
- A blunder raises liking only for someone already seen as competent (Aronson 1966).
- Two-sided messages raise credibility (Eisend 2006).
- Sensing persuasion lowers attitude and intent (251-paper review).
- Two in three US drivers distrust repair shops in general; unnecessary work and overcharging are the reasons (AAA).

## What it means for a one-man diesel creator filming a daily talking head in his truck cab

Each rule names the evidence it rests on and how to test it in Insights (skip rate, average watch, shares per reach).

1. **Camera setup, exact.** Pocket 3 on a suction or magnetic mount on the dash or the inside of the windshield at the passenger A-pillar side, lens at Jake's eye height when he sits normally (raise it on the mount, do not tilt down). Horizontal distance from lens to face 70 to 90 cm (arm's length plus a hand). At 20 mm equivalent that gives a frame about 65 to 80 cm wide in portrait, so his head is about a fifth of the frame width and the shot is a loose medium close-up, chest up, both shoulders in. Below 50 cm the lens widens his nose and the trust studies say viewers mark that down without knowing why. Never closer than 50 cm; if the cab forces it, use ActiveTrack from the passenger seat headrest instead of the dash. Evidence: Baranowski and Hecht, Bryan et al., Ward et al., DJI specs.
2. **Light from the side window, not the windshield.** Park with the sun on the passenger side so the driver's window is in shade and the passenger window is the key, about 45 degrees off his face. Expose for the face and let the passenger window go bright. Avoid the driver window behind his head: it blows out and takes the edge of his face with it. Overcast is better than sun; between 16:00 and 19:00 (his posting window) winter light in Wyoming is already low and soft. Test: shoot the same line both ways once and compare skin and eye detail on the phone. Evidence: Videomaker, Artlist, UC ANR.
3. **Depth behind him.** Sit so the camera sees past his shoulder through the rear cab window or down the side glass to the yard, not a flat headliner or B-pillar. Depth is what separates a person from a vlog. Evidence: ASC, RedShark, practitioner consensus.
4. **Frame for the three crops at once.** Eyes on the upper third line of the 9:16 frame, roughly 600 to 700 px from the top on a 1080 by 1920 canvas; a palm of headroom; face left of centre by about a tenth of the frame width so the right rail never crosses it. Poster lockup sits between the collarbone and the caption line, inside the 285 to 1,250 px band. Check the Profile Grid tab in the cover editor before every post. Evidence: Meta ad margins, Krumzi, Revid, Hootsuite.
5. **30 fps, 4K, no grade.** No evidence favours 24 for a seated face; 30 is the web default and avoids judder on the hand gestures he makes. Shoot one week at 24 and one at 30 only if curiosity demands it; judge by skip rate, nothing else. Evidence: frame-rate studies plus practitioner guides; Jacob's no-grade rule.
6. **Look into the lens, speak to one person.** Direct address is what the parasocial literature keeps measuring. Put a small sticker beside the lens if the screen pulls his eyes. Evidence: Gothenburg webcam study, Hartmann and Goldhoorn (unverified this session).
7. **Competence first, then the mistake.** In every reel that admits a failure, the credential comes before the failure: "I order these for a living, and I still bought the wrong one." The pratfall helps only the already competent. Test: pair two reels that admit the same mistake, one with the credential up front, one without, and compare shares. Evidence: Aronson 1966, Eisend 2006.
8. **Say the downside out loud.** Every "what I put on my truck" reel names one thing it does not do or one thing that annoyed him about it. Two-sided messages measure higher on source credibility, and the whole auto audience's prior is that the trade hides cost and unnecessary work. Evidence: Eisend, AAA.
9. **Shared grievance, held lightly.** Relatable talking heads complain about a thing, not a person, mildly: the 10 mm, the parts catalogue that lists four part numbers for one hose, the customer who says "it's making a noise". Measured bonding comes from mild shared negativity about an outside target; strong negativity does not bond. Evidence: Bosson et al. 2006, Weaver and Bosson 2011.
10. **Write the joke backwards from one turn, land it on beat three.** One premise, two true beats, the third one breaks the pattern, then stop; no fourth beat, no explaining. If the bit needs a second person, the viewer is the second person: talk to them as the customer or the boss. Keep the face straight through the turn and let the comment section laugh. Test: tag humour reels by beat count and compare skip rate. Evidence: rule of three experiment, UCB heightening, Kallmekris's method.
11. **"I work there" in the first ten seconds whenever the shop is in frame.** FTC employee disclosure, and it doubles as the credential line from rule 7. Evidence: FTC Endorsement Guides FAQ.
