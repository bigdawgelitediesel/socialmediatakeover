# Share engineering for El Coyote build reels (2026-11-21)

Question: what makes a truck build reel get SENT to one friend, and how do the top automotive and shop creators engineer it, given Mosseri's Oct 2026 line that sends per reach is the metric that decides unconnected reach.

House rule this document obeys (Jacob, 2026-11-21): no on-screen call to action, no burned-in "send this to" line, no handle card, no outro, the reel ends within 1.5 s of the payoff. The send reason lives in the moment itself and in the first line of the Instagram caption, which Jacob writes. "Comment WORD" reels only with a real deliverable, at most one post in four.

## 1. Why a person DMs a video to one friend (the psychology and the data)

### What Instagram says it is measuring

- Jan 2025, Mosseri's ranking explainer: "The top three signals that matter most for ranking are watch time, likes and sends." And: "Likes are slightly more important for connected content, and sends are slightly more important for unconnected content." (ZoomSphere AMA log; Buffer algorithm guide, Oct 23 2026)
- Oct 23 2026 AMA: "We actually focus a lot more on finding reels and photos that you might send to a friend." Comments: "It's not the most important part of value by a long shot." (ZoomSphere)
- Oct 26 2026, Colin and Samir interview: the three metrics to follow are "Sends per reach, likes per reach, and then watch time or average watch time," and "Sends per reach is particularly important for that unconnected reach." Also: "We focus way less on watch time than all the competition does, but we do care about watch time." (Buffer; buzzincontent; our 2026-11-21 algorithm file)
- Buffer's reading of the same interview: DM shares from people who do not follow you count for more when Instagram decides whether to show a post to more non-followers.

Working target for a Rival reel: 1% of reach is the floor, 2% is the goal. Below 1% the format failed, not the footage.

### Why people send (the literature, then what it means for a build reel)

1. **Sharing is about the sender, and in a DM it is about the recipient.** Berger's 2025 Annual Review of Psychology piece ("What Gets Shared, and Why?") lists the motives: impression management (looking good), emotion regulation (venting, awe), information acquisition, social bonding, persuasion. His summary line in the companion interview: "sharing is driven a lot by the self, by the sender and not just a recipient." The one paper that splits audience size, Barasch and Berger 2014 ("Broadcasting and Narrowcasting," Journal of Marketing Research), found that when people share with one person they shift from self-presentation to the other person: narrowcasters pick content that is useful or fitting FOR THE RECIPIENT, broadcasters pick content that makes THEMSELVES look good. A DM is narrowcasting. So the question a build reel has to answer is not "does this make Jacob look good" but "whose face pops into the viewer's head in the first five seconds."
2. **High arousal, not just positive.** Berger and Milkman (Virality, 2012): awe, anger, anxiety and amusement raise sharing; sadness and other low-arousal states lower it; practical usefulness raises it independently. A clean truck at golden hour is awe. A driveshaft on the freeway is disbelief. A quiet reflective trade story is low arousal.
3. **Asynchronous channels raise the bar to "interesting."** Berger and Iyengar (Knowledge at Wharton write-up): in face-to-face talk people mention whatever is cued, but online and in messages "you're only going to share things when they cross a certain threshold of interesting," because "the option of not saying anything is fine." Surprise and expectation violation are what clear the threshold. A build update that is merely pleasant does not.
4. **Impulse and habit, 2026 short-video evidence.** A 2026 Current Psychology study of short-video platforms (dual-systems model, sample mostly 31 plus) found social imitation and impulsiveness drive an impulse that produces sharing, and that past frequency and satisfaction build a sharing habit. Translation: the send happens in the first second after the payoff, or not at all. Anything that sits on screen after the payoff (a hold, an outro, a card) is time for the impulse to cool. Jacob's 1.5-second rule is correct.
5. **Relaxation and entertainment are the strongest satisfaction paths to word of mouth** in a 973-viewer survey of short-video users (ScienceDirect 2025). Not education. The reel can carry information, but it has to feel like a break, not a lesson.
6. **The five triggers everyone in the 2026 creator-marketing trade lists** (Influencer Marketing Hub playbook, 1kreach, Socialync; vendor sources, but they match the account's own data): "this is so you" recognition, "my friend needs this" utility, disagreement that wants a second opinion, a visual payoff worth showing, and local relevance. Influencer Marketing Hub's one-line version: "DMs are recommendations."

### Which triggers a build reel can actually pull

| trigger | fires for a build reel when | does not fire when |
|---|---|---|
| Identity ("this is you") | the truck, the sound, the era, or the dumb decision belongs to a named tribe: 7.3 guys, OBS guys, guys who traded a bike, guys whose wife counts boxes | the reel is about Jacob's journey |
| Utility ("you need this") | one trick, one part number, one failure, one price, in under 20 s, that a specific buddy is currently suffering | the information is general or needs the whole reel to land |
| Disbelief / awe | something that should not work works, or a number that should not be true is true | a product is being thanked |
| Inside joke | the reel finishes a sentence the group chat already says ("just LS swap it", "it's not done yet") | the joke needs context |
| Settle the argument | two options, a result, and no hedging | the result is "it depends" (that makes comments, not sends) |
| Outrage | a quote, a rule, a cost that is unfair to the viewer's people | it is outrage about Jacob's problem |

Trade stories, the format Harley Trade used, only fire on identity, and only if the ending gives the recipient a role. Right now the ending gives Jacob the role.

### What the thirty say

1. **The top tier (1.4 to 2.5%) has no ask in it.** Rows 1 to 12 are a car, a truck, a sound, a trick, or a couple moment, plus a caption that names a tribe or a person ("7.3 brother", "Built not bought", "Your boyfriends dream truck", "Lady approved"). Eight of the twelve are under 20 seconds and five have no speech at all.
2. **Explicit asks sit at the bottom.** The spoken "tag him" clip (row 30) is the worst ratio on the list at 0.03% on 850k plays. "Comment below if your truck could do this" (row 25) got 0.47%. Our own "Send this to someone in need" got 0.4%. The ask converts the impulse into a comment or into nothing.
3. **Debates make comments, not sends.** "What would you do?" (row 26) got 6,482 comments at 0.32% sends. IDI vs Powerstroke (row 27) 1,725 comments at 0.25%. Mosseri just said comments are "not the most important part of value by a long shot." Use debates when you want comments; do not confuse them with share formats.
4. **Useful beats pleasant, and short useful beats long useful.** The ratchet-strap unlock (52 s, reaction format) hit 2.13% because the viewer has a specific buddy who locks his keys in the truck. Whiteface's explainers run 0.7 to 0.9%; the one that hit 1.47% had the grease gun line, a tool every ranch guy has rolling around loose.
5. **Feel-good gets reach, not sends.** The 12-year-old farm kid did 4.1M plays and 376k likes at 0.10%. Harley Trade is a small version of the same curve: best like rate on the account, one send. Warm stories are liked for the person in them.
6. **Naming the recipient in the caption is the whole trick for the identity shots.** "Your boyfriends dream truck" (727k plays, 1.13%) is a 31 s static reveal. The caption did the targeting. Nothing was asked.

## 3. The craft of the send line in 2026

### What the field tests say (all small and vendor-reported; weigh accordingly)

- **mycomps 200-post test (6 months, one creator, pop lists and meme-adjacent):** 15 to 18 s reels with an explicit share line at the 13 to 17 s mark and a replay loop did +102% shares vs a no-CTA baseline; the same length with a tag line ("tag someone who needs to see this") did +65% but shifted the action to comments and tags; under 10 s with no CTA did -18%; 20 to 30 s story arcs did +30% and got saves more than shares. Takeaway for us: length and the moment of the payoff matter; the words themselves moved comments more than sends.
- **Influencer Marketing Hub "Sends per Reach" playbook:** recipient-based lines beat generic ones ("Know someone with dry skin? Send this." vs "Share this with 5 friends"); rotate explicit asks with implicit ones ("Your editor will thank you for this"); the proof of a working line is comments like "sending this to my sister." No numbers.
- **1kreach and Socialync:** specific beats generic; end on an opinion, not a summary; "save this" overlays read as bait; a healthy send rate is over 2% of plays (uncited). One claim worth keeping even though it is secondhand: shared Reels under 30 s get opened by the recipient far more often than Reels over 60 s. The recipient has to actually watch it for the send to count as a quality send.
- **Meta policy:** Instagram's Recommendations guidelines list "clickbait or engagement bait" among content people broadly dislike, which can keep a post out of recommendations. Meta's Spam policy prohibits requiring likes, shares or follows to access exclusive content. No official text names "tag a friend" or "send this to" as banned. The risk is a classifier reading the caption and on-screen text, not a rule. This is one more reason Jacob's "no on-screen CTA" rule is right: the content never looks like bait, because it is not.

### Where the send reason lives, in priority order, for El Coyote

1. **In the moment.** The last shot is the thing the recipient needs to see: the sound, the result, the face, the number. Hard cut inside 1.5 s. Per the 2026 impulse study, the send happens on the cut or not at all.
2. **In the caption's first line (the ~125 characters before "more").** This is Jacob's send line. Rules for it: name the recipient as a type, not an action ("your 7.3 brother", "the LS swap guy in your group chat", "whoever still talks about grandpa's F600"), one sentence, no "please", no "if you", no emoji string. Robert Benjamin's advice from the Nov 4 video applies in reverse: the caption's first line should feel like the reel's natural last line, so a viewer who already thought of someone sees their thought written down.
3. **In Jacob's own pinned comment.** Not on screen, so it does not break the rule. It can carry the longer version, a part number, or a second recipient type. Brock Johnson's posting routine puts a pinned comment on every reel; it is also where "sending this to my brother" replies will stack, which is social proof for the next viewer.
4. **In the Story share with one line of text.** Mosseri (via Hootsuite): Stories reduce unfollows and are where DM conversations start. Post every reel to Stories with a single line that names the recipient type.

Never: spoken "tag your buddy," on-screen "send this to," a send sheet mock-up, pointing at camera, a hold after the payoff. The data in section 2 says those lines live in the 0.03 to 0.5% band.

### Native tools, as of November 2026

- **Add Yours:** a Stories sticker first; it exists on Reels since 2022 but no 2026 source shows a reach or send lift from it, and Reels with the sticker cannot be boosted. Useful for one thing: a partner or UGC chain ("Add yours: your 7.3 at idle") where the goal is replies, not sends. Not a send tool.
- **Polls:** a Stories feature. They make taps and replies among followers, not sends to non-followers. Use for deciding things with the 577 (wheel finish, color), not for reach.
- **Reposts:** public reposts (Aug 2025) put a reel in front of the reposter's followers with credit. This is the quiet lever for Rival: a repost by Driven Diesel, ISSPRO, Power Hungry or Move Bumpers of a REAL reel is worth more than a shout-out reel that nobody sends (our shout-out reels: 160 to 347 views, 0 shares). Ask partners to repost, stop making standalone thank-you reels. Accounts that mostly repost others are not recommended (Mosseri, 2024 originality update; Buffer), so Jacob reposts partners sparingly.
- **Trial Reels:** Mosseri Oct 16 2026: fine to test several variants, not fine to repost the same cut repeatedly. Test two caption first lines on the same cut as Trials, pick the one with the higher sends per reach, then post to followers.

## 5. Ten rules for engineering sends in El Coyote reels

1. **Write the caption's first line before the shot list, and write it as the name of a person type.** "Your 7.3 brother." "The LS swap guy in your group chat." "Whoever still talks about grandpa's F600." If no person type exists for the reel, it is a Story, not a Reel.
2. **The last shot is the thing the recipient needs to see, and the cut comes inside 1.5 s of it.** No hold, no card, no outro. The send impulse is one second long.
3. **No ask on screen or in the audio, ever.** The 0.03% clip had a spoken "tag him." The 2.4% clips had a truck and a caption. Reels with an ask on screen also look like bait to the classifier.
4. **Make the reel about the viewer's people, not about Jacob.** A narrowcast (DM) is picked for the recipient. Jacob's journey earns likes; the recipient's truck, sound, mistake or argument earns sends. Harley Trade is the proof.
5. **One reel, one trigger.** Identity, utility, disbelief, inside joke, or a settled argument. Mixing a meme beat with a build update splits the topic classifier and the send reason.
6. **Shorter than you think for sends.** Eight of the twelve top clips are under 20 s and five have no speech. Cut the sound reel at 10 s. Save 30 to 60 s for utility reels where the trick needs the time.
7. **If it is a debate, decide it.** A result ("he beat me twice") sends at 1.5%; a question ("what would you do?") comments at 0.3% sends. Comments are the weakest signal Mosseri named.
8. **Put the identity marker on frame one.** Visible 7.3, OBS cab, 1959 grille, Wyoming plate. The classifier and the viewer both need to know whose reel this is in the first half second.
9. **Pin a comment that repeats the person type, and share to Story with the same line.** Two more places for the send reason that stay off the footage. Reply to "sending this to my brother" comments by hand within 20 minutes.
10. **Grade every reel on sends per reach, not views.** Floor 1%, goal 2%. Under 1% on a build reel means change the format (which trigger, who it is for), not the footage. Test two caption first lines as Trial Reels when unsure.

## 6. Harley Trade: three alternative endings that earn the send without on-screen text

Current cut: 18.5 s. Quoted dialogue line over the truck on frame 1, cut to the two men at 2.5 s, ends on a 3.5 s static rear-quarter hold carrying the "send this to the guy who made a worse trade" caption in Montserrat. Skip 37.8%, average watch 10.9 s, 103 likes, 4 saves, 1 share on 1,610 reach. The hook and hold are solved. The ending gives Jacob the role; every ending below gives the recipient the role, and the reel ends on the cut.

### Ending A: "He thinks he won"

- **On-screen text:** none.
- **Visual:** cut from the two men to the Harley leaving (riding off, or being loaded or rolled away), shot from behind Jacob's shoulder so his back and the truck's grille are both in frame. One shot, 2.5 s.
- **Spoken line (Jacob, off camera or half-turned, dry):** "He thinks he won."
- **Timing:** line lands at the moment the bike's engine note peaks or the trailer gate closes. Hard cut on the last syllable. Total reel 15 to 16 s.
- **Why it sends:** every group of friends has the guy who thinks he won the trade. The line is the inside joke, not an instruction. Both parties to any trade can be sent this.
- **Caption first line:** "He thinks he won. Your buddy who traded his bike for a truck knows exactly how this ends."

### Ending B: "Show your wife this one"

- **On-screen text:** none (a real text message on a phone screen is footage, not a caption).
- **Visual:** cut from the men to a phone-screen recording or a shot of Jacob reading a text out loud: his wife's actual reply when he told her (whatever she really said; if it was a single word, better). Then one 1 s cut to the F600's grille, cut.
- **Spoken line:** Jacob reads the text flat, no reaction: "[her exact words]." Nothing after.
- **Timing:** read takes 1.5 to 2 s, grille 1 s, cut. Total reel about 16 s.
- **Why it sends:** the couple format sent at 1.39% (row 12) and the "cab lights for Valentine's Day" story got the most comments per view on that page. Men send this to their wives as a pre-emptive apology; wives send it to husbands as a warning.
- **Caption first line:** "Show your wife this one before you trade the bike."

### Ending C: the callback

- **On-screen text:** none.
- **Visual:** the quoted dialogue line that opened the reel on frame 1 is said again at the end, by the OTHER man, to camera, with a straight face, then both break. Cut on the first laugh, not after it. Two men, truck behind them, one shot, 2 to 3 s.
- **Spoken line:** the other man repeats the frame-1 quote word for word.
- **Timing:** quote 1.5 s, break 0.5 s, cut. Total reel 15 to 16 s.
- **Why it sends:** the reel closes its own loop (replay bait without a replay card), and the repeated line becomes a quote the viewer can send to the one friend it describes. Loop structures were the one variable in the 200-post test that moved shares in every niche.
- **Caption first line:** "Every one of us has a buddy who made this exact trade. He knows who he is."

Shoot all three if the footage can be grabbed in one session; post A as the main reel, run B and C as Trial Reels with their own first lines and keep whichever clears 1% sends per reach.
