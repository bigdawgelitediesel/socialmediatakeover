### The 17 effects in CapCut desktop, solo creator, exact steps

Shared setup for anything that needs his cutout: put the NOCAP plate on the main track, press Ctrl+D (or right-click > Copy, Paste onto the track above) so both copies start on the same frame, select the top copy, Video > Remove BG > Auto removal, wait for the mask, scrub three places to check the edge. If the edge chatters, use Claude's `-cutout.webm` or `-cutout-green.mp4` instead (section 3), which is the same RVM matte Remotion uses.

Keyframes everywhere below mean: select the layer, park the playhead, click the diamond beside Scale / Position / Rotation / Opacity in Video > Basic, move the playhead, change the value (a second diamond appears). Right-click a diamond > Keyframe curve > Ease out (or draw the curve) so nothing is linear. CapCut scales a layer around its own centre, so a zoom that must stay anchored on a face needs a matching Position keyframe pair.

1. **Orbit (2.5D parallax).** Feasible, 10 minutes. Plate on track 1 with Scale keyframes 100 to 104 over the shot. Import four card PNGs (Claude supplies them full-frame, alpha, already placed). Each card gets a Position keyframe pair over the same 3 s: near cards travel 60 to 90 px, far cards 15 to 25 px, all in the same direction, Ease in-out. Optional Opacity 0 to 100 over the first 6 frames. The depth illusion is purely the travel difference.
2. **Couch wall.** Feasible, 15 minutes, tedious. Cutout copy on the top track. Ten to twelve card PNGs on tracks under it, each starting 3 frames after the previous (0.1 s). Per card: Video > Animation > In > "Zoom in" or "Slide" at 0.2 s, Rotation set to a value between -6 and +6 in Basic. Everything stays behind him because the cutout track is on top. Claude can hand the wall as one alpha `-graphics.webm` so this becomes a single drag.
3. **UI world.** Feasible only as a flat composite. Claude renders the UI card and tumbling thumbnails as `-graphics.webm` (alpha) or a cream card PNG with a "Drop files here" zone; Jacob places it over the plate, scales the plate to 45 percent and parks it under the card with Position. Building the tumbling thumbnails by hand in CapCut (four clips, each with Rotation + Position + Scale keyframe pairs) is 20 minutes and never looks as good. Recommendation: Remotion.
4. **Frame break.** Feasible and worth learning, 5 minutes. Three layers: (a) bottom: dark background (Media > Library > black, or a 1080x1920 PNG), (b) middle: plate scaled to 68 percent with Video > Mask > Rectangle, rounded corners up, feather 0, so it reads as a post frame; or skip the mask and use Claude's IG-chrome PNG which already has the frame cut out, (c) top: cutout copy at Scale 100. Because the cutout is full size and the framed plate is 68 percent, his head and hands cross the frame edge. Add the IG chrome PNG (heart, comment, share, handle, Follow) above everything with Opacity 0 to 100 over 8 frames. Keep the cutout and the framed plate starting on the same frame or the two versions of him drift.
5. **Digital zoom (snap punch).** Feasible, 1 minute. Scale keyframes 100 to 160 over 4 frames, Keyframe curve Ease out, then hold. If Scale tops out, right-click > Create compound clip first and keyframe the compound (Aeko, section 4). For the directional smear, Effects > Video effects > search "Zoom blur" (or "Radial blur") trimmed to those 4 frames; Intensity around 40. Video > Basic also has a Motion blur toggle, but it only reads motion already baked into the frames, so it needs an export-and-reimport pass (Gravenberg, section 4); the Zoom blur effect is the one-step route. The blur-pop word: Text layer with Animation > In > "Blur" at 0.2 s. All free.
6. **Phone mockup.** Feasible as a flat mockup, 5 minutes. Claude provides a phone PNG with a transparent screen cut-out and the screen UI as a 9:19.5 clip. Order: plate, screen clip (Scale and Position to fit the screen hole, Video > Mask > Rectangle with rounded corners to clip it), phone PNG on top. Float: Position keyframes drifting 10 px over 2 s, Rotation 0 to 1.5 degrees. The typing message and Approve button are either baked into the screen clip by Claude or built as Text > Animation > In > "Typewriter" plus a button PNG. No 3D perspective in CapCut; it is a flat mockup and that is fine at reel size.
7. **Checklist.** Feasible, 8 minutes, fiddly. Card PNG from Claude (dark card, three empty rows, counter "0 of 3"). Three Text layers, Montserrat or Poppins SemiBold, each with Animation > In > "Typewriter" 0.4 s, staggered. Checkmarks: Stickers > search "check" (pick a plain one without a crown) with Animation > In > "Zoom in" 0.15 s. Counter: three stacked Text layers "0 of 3", "1 of 3" with cuts, or a Text layer per state. Progress bar: a yellow rectangle PNG with Video > Mask > Linear keyframed left to right. This one is cheaper in Remotion; CapCut is fine for a one-off.
8. **Headline wall.** Same as couch wall without the cutout; cards can sit beside him so no matte needed. Eight PNG notes, staggered 0.1 s, In animation "Zoom in" 0.2 s, individual Rotation. 10 minutes.
9. **Zoom-through cut.** Feasible, 2 minutes. Outgoing shot: Scale keyframes 100 to 300 over its last 8 frames, Ease in. Incoming shot: Scale 140 to 100 over its first 6 frames, Ease out. Effects > "Zoom blur" spanning the last 5 frames of the outgoing and first 3 of the incoming. Shortcut: Transitions > search "zoom" > "Zoom swipe" (free, confirmed 2026-10-29) or "Pull in" at 0.3 s, which does a softer version with no setup. Free either way.
10. **Chart grow.** Feasible as a reveal, 5 minutes. Cutout copy on top. Claude provides the finished chart as a PNG (line, points, label pill, glow baked in) on a transparent canvas. Chart PNG under the cutout, Video > Mask > Linear, rotate the mask 90 degrees so the edge is vertical, keyframe its Position left to right over 1 s with Ease out. Points popping one by one need separate PNGs or stickers with In "Zoom in" 0.12 s at the moment the mask passes them. A true path-draw with glow is Remotion.
11. **Pull-back reveal.** Feasible, 5 minutes. Claude provides the desktop-window PNG (cream frame, side panel, timeline strip) sized so the video slot is a known rectangle. Plate on top track: Scale keyframes 100 to 55 and Position keyframes from centre to the slot centre over 20 frames, Ease in-out. Window PNG under it with Opacity 0 to 100 over the first 6 frames of the move. The moving playhead and checklist ticks inside the panel are either a short alpha WebM from Claude or skipped.
12. **Green screen (background replace).** Easiest of the 17. Plate copy with Remove BG > Auto removal on top; the new background (the purple app screenshot, a portal screen recording, a partner's site) on the track below, scaled to fill. If he films against a real green wall: Remove BG > Chroma key, pick the green, Intensity until the fringe disappears, Shadow up a little, Clean up edge 1 to 2. Free.
13. **Paper cut (sticker edge collage).** Partly feasible. The collage background is a PNG from Claude. The white sticker edge around his body: on a still, current builds show a **Stroke** row (colour, thickness) in the Remove BG panel right after Auto removal (Fix Pilot, 2026-11-09, section 4); check whether it appears for a video cutout. If it does not, Effects > Body effects > "Outline" is crowned, and the free workaround that looks right at reel size is: duplicate the cutout, select the lower duplicate, Adjust > Basic: Brightness +100, Exposure +100, Saturation -100 (it goes to solid white), Scale 104, and because CapCut scales from the centre the white edge is uneven in a way that reads as hand-cut. Ransom-note letters: separate Text layers in different fonts, each with In "Zoom in" 0.1 s, staggered, small Rotation. 15 minutes. Jacob's rule already says skip this one for El Coyote.
14. **Doodle boil.** Not feasible natively. Body effects > "Scribble" or "Outline" gives a static or shimmering outline (Pro), no frame-to-frame redraw, and the doodles are stickers with no boil. Remotion or the ffmpeg edge-jitter pass in BREAKDOWN.md. If a sketchy outline is wanted for one beat, the white-duplicate trick from 13 at Scale 103, then Effects > "Sketch" on that duplicate, is the closest free look.
15. **Vertigo (dolly zoom).** Feasible and good, 3 minutes. Cutout copy on top at Scale 100, untouched. Plate copy below: Scale keyframes 100 to 135 over 1 s (30 frames), Ease in-out; add a Position keyframe pair so the point behind his head stays put (drag the plate in the preview at the second keyframe until his shoulders line up with the cutout). Optional Effects > Blur at 10 percent on the plate copy only, ramping in. The Puletech two-layer guide and the French TikTok single-layer version both describe this; the two-layer one is the real effect, the single-layer version is just a zoom.
16. **Cover story (masthead behind head).** Feasible, 3 minutes, the socialcontentking "text behind your head, one click" trick. Three layers: plate, Text (serif or Anton, 300 to 360 px, white, no stroke) positioned so the baseline sits at his chin, cutout copy on top. Reveal: Text > Animation > In > "Wipe right" (free) at 0.5 s; or put the Text in a compound clip (right-click > Create compound clip) and use Video > Mask > Linear keyframed across it. If the timeline refuses to let the Text sit between two video layers, compound the plate + text first, then add the cutout above (capcutguide fallback).
17. **Glass type.** Not feasible as letters, feasible as a panel. CapCut cannot blur the footage behind a text shape (masks take rectangles, circles and filmstrips, never text), but a frosted glass PANEL with text inside is a 5-minute build: duplicate the plate, Mask > Rectangle with corner radius 20 to 30 on the copy, Effects > Blur on it, a white rectangle sticker at 20 to 30 percent fill over it, a second sticker copy with no fill and a 1 to 3 px white stroke for the rim (Cortex and Edit Hacks, section 4). For the letter-shaped version the approximations are: Text with white fill at 35 percent opacity, Stroke white 2 px at 60 percent, Shadow soft, plus a duplicate of the same Text in black at 10 percent opacity offset 2 px for depth; slide with Position keyframes. Text > Effects has "3D"/"glass" presets but they are crowned and the glass ones are template bait. Remotion does the real thing (backdrop-filter blur plus a rim) in a few lines. Jacob already skipped glass type for the account.

Summary: 12 of 17 are a solo-creator job in free CapCut (1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 11, 12, 15, 16), 3 are partial (3, 10, 13), 2 are no (14, and 17 as letter-shaped glass; a glass panel is fine). Everything that needs cards or UI is dramatically cheaper when Claude hands over the artwork as full-frame alpha PNGs or an alpha WebM graphics pass and Jacob only drops it on the track above the plate.

## 3. The handoff: Claude's render to Jacob's last touch

**Generations.** Every lossy encode costs detail and Instagram's own transcode (4 to 6 Mbps, measured yesterday) is the last one. Claude's render is generation 1, a CapCut or Edits export is generation 2, Instagram is generation 3. The job is to make generation 2 nearly free or skip it entirely.

**Decision first.** If the only thing Jacob wants to add is a song, he should not open an editor at all: post the `-POST.mp4` (or `-NOCAP.mp4`) straight from Photos and add the track in Instagram's composer, where the sliders for original audio and song live and the audio page stays attached. Open CapCut or Edits only when he is adding something the plate does not have (a card, a re-cut, a different caption pass).

**What Claude hands over, per reel** (the Drive folder `<Name> - completed YYYY-MM-DD`; keep the three-file rule from the skill, the extras go in a `handoff/` subfolder only when a finish is planned):

|---|---|---|---|
| `<name>-POST.mp4` | finished reel, captions burned, dialogue bed, no music | 1080x1920, 30 fps, H.264 High 4.2, crf 17, maxrate 16M, closed 1 s GOP, yuv420p, bt709/bt709/bt709/tv tags, AAC 48 kHz 192k at -14 LUFS | Jacob posts it as is |
| `<name>-NOCAP.mp4` | same cut, no captions | same | Jacob, if he prefers Instagram's or Edits' captions |
| `<name>-EDIT.mp4` | the NOCAP plate as an editing master | same chain but crf 14, maxrate 30M, bufsize 60M (about 25 to 30 Mbps on shop footage) | CapCut or Edits timeline source; its export becomes generation 2 at 16 Mbps with almost nothing lost |

Why the EDIT master exists: CapCut and Edits both decode to raw frames and re-encode on export; what they re-encode is the plate. A crf 14 plate at 25 to 30 Mbps re-encoded by CapCut at 16 Mbps comes out visually identical to Claude's crf 17 direct render (tested yesterday: a 4.5 Mbps simulated Instagram pass of the recipe scored VMAF 94, so one extra generation at 16 Mbps is below the noise). Do not hand over ProRes: CapCut Windows decodes it but Edits on iPhone may refuse it, the files are 10x larger on the Drive mirror, and the gain over crf 14 H.264 at this resolution is invisible after Instagram.

**Export settings that preserve a bt709 1080x1920 H.264 master.**

CapCut desktop, Export dialog: Resolution 1080P (shows as 1080x1920 when the project is 9:16), Quality: leave on Recommended but open Bitrate and set Custom 14000 to 16000 kbps (Recommended alone lands near 8 to 10 Mbps on some builds), Codec H.264, Format MP4, Frame rate 30 (must equal the project frame rate set at New project; if the project says 60 it resamples), Colour space / HDR export OFF (SDR Rec.709), audio AAC. Before export, check nothing carries a crown. Nothing in CapCut writes a colour tag other than bt709 for an SDR project, so the tags survive; what breaks colour is importing an HLG iPhone clip raw into the same timeline, which is why every clip should already have gone through Claude's tone map, or be shot on the Osmo (bt709).

Edits (iPhone): tap the resolution label top right of the project before export and pick HD (1080p), 30 fps, SDR. Do not pick 2K or 4K for a 1080p plate (upscaling buys nothing and doubles the upload), and do not pick HDR for SDR material (Edits would tone-map SDR into HLG and the greys go milky again, the exact failure fixed yesterday). Then Next > Share to Instagram, not Download then upload from Photos; Bender's "minimize re-encodes" applies only to the direct share. If a draft is needed, Next > Instagram > Save draft keeps the same path.

Instagram: Upload at highest quality ON, Wi-Fi, no in-app trim, crop, filter or Enhance (each forces a client-side re-encode before upload).

**The double re-encode trap, the five ways it happens here.** (1) Exporting from CapCut, then importing that export into Edits "for the Edits boost," then posting: three lossy passes before Instagram, and Mosseri says pass-through is detected. Pick one app. (2) Adding a song in CapCut, then adding another track or trimming inside Instagram: the in-app edit re-encodes the whole file; add the song in Instagram only, from a clean export. (3) Exporting 4K from a 1080p plate: Instagram downscales with its own filter and the file uploads slower, which the app answers by lowering the encode on a weak link. (4) Sending the file to the phone through Messages, WhatsApp or email (all re-encode); Drive > Send a copy > Save video, or AirDrop, keep the bytes. (5) Letting CapCut export at "Recommended" bitrate on a busy shot (dust, grain, LED flicker): the 8 Mbps export is already at Instagram's output budget, so Instagram's pass compounds visibly; set the custom 14000 to 16000.

**Round trip rule.** If Jacob changes the cut in CapCut (not just overlays), he sends the CapCut export back and Claude treats it as a plate for captions only (`--captions-only` on an EDL pointing at it), encoding once more at crf 17. That is still three generations before Instagram, so the better path for a re-cut is a note ("cut 4 s off the middle, hold the dyno shot longer") and a fresh render from the slot cache, which is minutes.

## Handoff checklist (one page, Jacob's side)

Before anything: decide whether an editor is needed. Song only means no editor: post `-POST.mp4` from Photos, add the song in Instagram, done.

CapCut desktop finish
1. New project, 9:16, 30 fps, SDR. Import `<name>-EDIT.mp4` (not POST, not NOCAP) and whatever `ov-*.png`, `-graphics.webm`, `-cutout.webm` the folder holds.
2. EDIT plate on track 1 from frame 0. Overlays on the tracks above at Position 0,0 and Scale 100; trim each to the in/out in its filename. `-graphics.webm` goes on top of all of them.
3. If a card must sit behind him: `-cutout.webm` on the top track, same start frame as the plate. Black instead of transparent means this machine does not read VP9 alpha: use `-cutout-green.mp4` with Video > Remove BG > Chroma key, or Remove BG > Auto removal on a duplicate of the plate.
4. Captions: Captions > Import captions > `<name>.srt`, restyle one cue (Montserrat SemiBold or Poppins SemiBold, white, thin stroke, 64 to 72 px, y about 1230), Apply to all. Or skip and keep the burned POST version.
5. No song in CapCut. No LUT (the plate is already graded). No Auto Adjust. No clip with a crown.
6. Export: 1080P, 30 fps, H.264, MP4, Bitrate Custom 16000 kbps, HDR off, AAC. Name it `<name>-FINAL.mp4`.
7. Get it to the phone by Drive (Send a copy > Save video) or AirDrop. Never Messages or email.

Edits (iPhone) finish
1. Save `<name>-EDIT.mp4` to Photos via Drive > Send a copy > Save video. New project in Edits, import it.
2. Overlays: Overlay > choose the `ov-*.png` files from Photos, pinch to full frame, trim to the timecodes in the README. Behind-the-head effects are not possible here; those stay in CapCut or in Claude's render.
