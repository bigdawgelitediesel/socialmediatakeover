## 0. The finding this file serves

The 6.7 tribe is the biggest Ford diesel tribe by a wide margin and it is organized around one fear and one argument. The fear is the CP4 (a certified class action as of Sept 30, 2026, no settlement, Ford appealing). The argument is what to do about it: live with it, Disaster Prevention Kit, DCR, CP3, or lift pump plus filtration. Every one of the top twenty 6.7 videos on YouTube by views is either a CP4 video, a "what it really costs / what to buy" video, or a 600 hp video, and the comment sections on all three are the same 400 people arguing the same four positions.

Jacob's position is the one nobody on that list holds: he kept the factory high-pressure side, disabled the CP4, and put a FASS on it, and he holds it for a reason that is specific and quotable (clean consistent fuel now, volume for 600 later, one part). The thread that proves the lane is open: Dave's Auto Center's "Replacing CP4 with CP3 Pump & Fass Fuel System" has 201K views and 2.94 percent likes, the highest like ratio of any CP4 video measured, and the top comment asking "What did this job cost?" has no answer. "Disable instead of swap, here is what it cost me" is the reel that answers it.

Second finding: the tribe's reaction to the "600 hp, emissions on" claim is suspicion first ("is there a dyno run for proof," 23 likes, top comment on TopCorey's 650 whp tune video; "I wonder how the factory components will hold up," 42 likes, top comment on his emissions-on 200 hp video). The clean-power lane is real (TopCorey, Banks, ATS, AMDP, EZ Lynk shops) but it is small, under-served, and its audience wants receipts, not claims. Chief's over-spec parts list is the receipt.

## 4. The running arguments, and Jacob's honest position on each

Positions below use only the facts in the brief and CLAUDE.md. Where the tribe will ask something the facts do not answer, it is flagged **ASK JACOB**.

### 4.1 The CP4 and the class action (2026 status)
**Facts.** *Droesser v. Ford Motor Company* (E.D. Mich., filed 2019, Judge Laurie Michelson). On Sept 30, 2026 the court certified classes under implied-warranty law in five states: California, Ohio, Louisiana, Pennsylvania, South Carolina (Indiana and New Jersey dropped out). Who qualifies: bought a 2011-or-newer 6.7 Super Duty in one of those states, paid out of pocket for a CP4 repair, did not sign a dealer arbitration agreement, was not already reimbursed. Owners whose pumps have not failed are excluded. Plaintiffs cite about $9,000 per repair; owner reports run $8,000 to $20,000 for a full fuel-system overhaul. Ford's defense: owner neglect (ignored water-in-fuel light, skipped filters, DEF in the tank) and four pump revisions (RPx, RP6, RP7, RP8); the judge treated it as one alleged defect. Oct 14, 2026: Ford petitioned the Sixth Circuit to appeal. No settlement, no fund, no claim form, no deadline; be wary of "settlement" sign-up sites. Separately, Ford recall 24S78 (late 2024 into 2025) covered 295,449 2020 to 2022 6.7 trucks with RP7 pumps and the remedy was a PCM software update to reduce "aged biodiesel" deposits, not a pump; a follow-up campaign 26V158 / 26S17 exists; 2011 to 2019 trucks are in no recall. The mechanism in the court's words: when lubrication runs thin the roller and tappet "grind instead of roll," shaving metal that travels to the injectors and the rest of the system; U.S. diesel is allowed a 520 micron HFRR wear scar versus 460 in Europe.
**Jacob's position.** He is a 2011 owner, outside every recall, and he did not wait for a court. He disabled the CP4 and put a FASS on the truck. He does not do outrage content about Ford; he tells what he did and what it cost. **ASK JACOB:** the one-sentence mechanical explanation of "disabled the CP4" that he is comfortable saying on camera, because ARod's viewers will ask in the first ten comments.

### 4.2 CP3 or DCR conversion vs disable-and-FASS
**Facts.** The tribe's default advice is a swap: S&S DCR (the 805K video; S&S says it chose the DCR for the 6.7 on purpose), SPE or II CP3 conversion (about $2,500 to $3,500 parts, plus labor), or the S&S Disaster Prevention Kit ($300 to $400, called "temporary" by swap sellers, "holy grail" by owners). Commenters on the DCR video: "I just spent 4K to DIY my fuel replacement system," "Thought the price was kind of high," "The challenge is finding a sharp diesel tech to install the DCR." Fleet voices push back on the whole panic: "We have 300 6.7 Fords and the only CP4s we've lost got DEF or gasoline in the fuel. Motorcraft filters every 10,000" (13 likes under the 900K pump video).
**Jacob's position (the angle nobody covers).** He did not swap. He disabled the CP4 and installed a FASS, and the FASS is doing both jobs at once: it feeds the CP4 clean, consistent fuel today, and it is already the volume the truck needs at 600. That is the cheaper middle path between "do nothing and pray" and "thousands for a conversion," and he can show the receipt. He does not run down the DCR or CP3 crowd; he says what he chose and why it fits a truck that is being built to 600 anyway.

### 4.3 "Zero reason to upgrade a lift pump unless you're going over 750 hp"
**Facts.** This line (from a commenter on Jacob's own post) is the pure power-only view of a lift pump. The tribe's counter-view already exists in its own words: "the fuel filters are the problem, this guy gets that" (85 likes), "I think the #1 killer of the CP4 is fuel temps, the tank is the cooler" (106 likes), "Clean fuel is a game changer for CP4 longevity" (12 likes).
**Jacob's rebuttal (owned).** It is not either/or. The FASS protects the CP4 now, with the fuel it is fed, and it is already there when the power comes. One part, two jobs, bought once.

### 4.4 6R140 limits
**Facts.** The 6R140 TorqShift is the 2011 to 2019 six-speed. Tribe content on it is almost all shift complaints and fixes (ARod's two videos, 94K and 46K; Transmission Digest 64K; HR Repair 38K) and cooler installs (Mishimoto 23K). The power-limit threads say "stock holds a tune, a built one holds a build," and the HO vs SO comments note that chassis, brakes and driveline set the tow number, not the engine.
**Jacob's position.** The MPD 6R140 build is a locked part of the plan, not an afterthought, because the torque is what the trans feels at 600. He is not asking the stock converter and clutches to carry it.

### 4.5 "600 hp reliable"
**Facts.** TopCorey's "600hp with only 2 mods" (629K) owns the phrase and its top comments are "skip to 4:37" (357) and "it's easy to tune a Powerstroke to 600, building the engine to run at 600 is harder" (9 likes, and the truest line in the thread). The tribe's reflex to any 600 claim: "is there a dyno run for proof" and "how will the factory parts hold up."
**Jacob's position.** His 600 is a parts list, not a tune number: Industrial Injection 30 percent over injectors, XR2 turbo, ARP studs, MPD 6R140 build, and a FASS already sized for it. Every piece is rated above the target. He says the number last and the parts first.

### 4.6 Emissions-intact power
**Facts.** See section 5. The tribe's loud half assumes power means delete; the quiet half (fleet owners, 2020+ owners under warranty, anyone in a test state) wants power that keeps the truck legal.
**Jacob's position.** House rule: no delete or defeat-device content, ever, and no answering the "just delete it" comments. Chief's content is about parts, fuel, the trans and the cost, and it never goes near that topic in either direction.

### 4.7 The 2026 Super Duty
**Facts (ford.com, 2026 page).** 6.7 standard output 475 hp at 2,600 and 1,050 lb-ft at 1,600; 6.7 High Output 500 hp at 2,600 and 1,200 lb-ft at 1,600; 7.3 gas 430 hp / 485 lb-ft; TorqShift 10-speed; 40,000 lb gooseneck, 30,000 conventional, 6,952 lb diesel payload. The first-gen (2011) launched at 390 / 735 and a software update made it 400 / 800. The tribe's 2026 arguments: HO vs SO (same tow rating, "the HO is for having fun when not towing," "swap the red badges"), 15,000 mile oil intervals ("that truck won't last," 45 likes), DEF consumption, and the 10-speed vs the 6R140.
**Jacob's position.** He drives the first one. A 2011 making 400 / 800 from the factory is being built, carefully, to more than a 2026 HO makes, and he gets to show every part that makes that honest. That is a named-tribe hook in itself: "2011 to 2016 owners, this one's for you."

## 7. 6.7 glossary (25 terms the tribe uses without explaining)

1. **CP4 / CP4.2**: the Bosch high-pressure fuel pump on every 6.7; the thing the lawsuit is about.
2. **RP7**: the CP4 revision in 2020 to 2022 trucks named in Ford's recall; Ford's four versions are RPx, RP6, RP7, RP8.
3. **DPK / Disaster Prevention Kit**: the S&S bypass that routes CP4 return fuel through a filter so a failing pump cannot seed the injectors with metal.
4. **DCR**: the S&S replacement high-pressure pump sold as a CP4 conversion; the 805K video.
5. **CP3 conversion**: swapping the CP4 for a Cummins-style CP3 (SPE, II); about $2,500 to $3,500 in parts.
6. **Lift pump**: the low-pressure pump that feeds the high-pressure pump; FASS, AirDog, Fleece PowerFlo are the names.
7. **FASS**: Fuel Air Separation System; frame-mounted lift pump plus two filters plus air separation; "Protect Your Truck."
8. **Fuel pump screaming**: the 11 to 16 in-tank pump whine; ARod's two videos; part numbers differ 11 to 14 vs 15 to 16.
9. **Blue tab / blue cap**: the fuel line quick-connect tabs that break during filter changes.
10. **WIF light**: water-in-fuel light; ignoring it is Ford's first neglect defense.
11. **Lubricity / HFRR wear scar**: how slippery diesel is; U.S. allows 520 microns, Europe 460; the lawsuit's core.
12. **Typewriter**: the 6.7's cold idle tick ("tragic typewriter death"); owners are told it is normal and reply "hard to believe."
13. **Regen / static regen / manual regen**: the DPF burn-off; "I start panicking at long red lights."
14. **DEF**: diesel exhaust fluid; DEF in the fuel tank is the tribe's cardinal sin and Ford's favorite story.
15. **CCV filter**: crankcase vent filter; skipping it is blamed for the upper oil pan leak.
16. **Upper oil pan leak**: the first-gen seep everyone has and nobody fixes.
17. **Cold side pipe**: the plastic intercooler pipe that splits; "upgrade it before she splits."
18. **6R140 / TorqShift**: the 2011 to 2019 six-speed; shift flares, harsh 2-3, range sensor.
19. **10R140**: the 2020+ ten-speed.
20. **SO / HO**: standard output (475 / 1,050) vs High Output (500 / 1,200), 2023+; "swap the red badges."
21. **Alumiduty**: 2017+ aluminum-body Super Duty.
22. **First gen / second gen / third gen**: 2011 to 2016 (11 to 14 and 15 to 16 split on turbo and pump), 2017 to 2022, 2023+.
23. **GT32 SST**: the first-gen single sequential turbo (dual compressor wheels on one shaft), the one the XR2 replaces on Chief.
24. **30 percent over**: injectors flowing 30 percent more than stock; Chief's size from Industrial Injection.
25. **ARP studs**: head studs replacing the factory bolts; "mandatory at 600" in every thread, and locked on Chief.
Bonus: **Godzilla** (the 7.3 gas), **death wobble**, **Platinum** (the trim the 600 hp videos are shot in), **D3L3ted** (the word the tribe spells to dodge filters; we do not use it in any spelling).
