## 1. What she teaches (the curriculum, assembled from the free videos)

### 1a. Show up: the yap

Talk to the camera every day about one idea, as yourself, unpolished, with a hook on screen and captions burned in. The deadline is the mechanism (40 posts in 6 weeks); the coaching is not. Views go down at first and likes per view go up, because the content is about you and only travels to people who already care. That is the floor you build on, not a failure. Her line: "my views are technically lower right now than they used to be and yet my account is growing more than it literally ever has."

Her delivery, from the transcripts: 245 words a minute, about 415 words, about 1 minute 45. Teleprompter, and she says so. Scripts drafted with an AI tool, then rewritten in her words ("Yes, I use AI. No I do not use AI to replace my own thoughts", 63,000 views). "I" 3.7 per 100 words, "you" 2.2: a diary that turns to the viewer at the end ("here's what I want you to hear").

### 1b. Six first lines she uses (with the title on screen as the first line written down)

1. Result plus "you" (17 of 71 openers carry a number): "I just grew 7,000 followers overnight from one video and here's why that matters for you."
2. The process promise: "Here's exactly how I edit my talking head reels, aka my yaps."
3. Contrarian: "Niching down is the worst advice on the internet and I am done following it."

## 4. Her teacher's rules that she follows (Jessi Jean, from 93 of her TikToks, free analysis)

No intro ("this isn't foreplay"). Talk to one person, never "hey guys" (0 of 93). Open with a flat claim, no question, no hedge, no "I" up front (claims 205 views a day vs questions 114). Explain 80 percent and leave a gap for the comments. Name what the viewer is too embarrassed to say. Perspective over facts. Five or six rotating topics, pick one. Share the misses next to the wins. Five takes per post. Re-film your best video every 90 days. Speak faster than real life and lower the pitch; end sentences on a period. Save "you" for the end (1.6 per 100 words in the first two sentences, 4.3 in the last third). Small number next to the big one. Lower your status in one line ("I'm not an expert"). Open a loop to the next video. Ask the comments what to make next.

## 5. The El Coyote translation (what Jacob does with this)

Jacob already is the non-expert one step ahead: a parts manager with no shop, building a 1959 F600 on a 7.3 in a home garage, and he already has the thing her viewers begged for (a real editing system, a real build, real numbers). Her method maps onto the million-view rule and the house rules like this.

1. **The yap tier exists now.** One talking video a day, 60 to 120 s, same corner of the garage, same light, Pocket 3 on the fender, teleprompter allowed, five takes, captions burned in (the plain caption, not per-word pills), the hook sentence on screen as the title. It is Shape A of the million-view rule with his face instead of b-roll. It will lose views for two weeks and gain likes per view; that is the floor.
2. **The receipts series.** A monthly "what the frame swap has cost, to the dollar" report with the same five lines in the same order, the small numbers first (the $234 rule). Our idea 32 becomes a recurring series, not one post.
3. **The scoreboard.** Every post for the next 30 days opens by updating a number: followers, the frame swap total, days to SEMA (November 2 to 5, 2027), parts on the truck. His numbers never wrong.
4. **Let the comments pick the next product.** Log every question asked twice (coyote.py idea queue). The first one that gets three asks in a night gets a "Comment INTERESTED" line the next day, under the free lesson, not a product page. Candidates the account already has: how he edits (the "How I edit my reels" series; her part 1 did 12x median), the 7.3 acronym glossary PDF, the Rival parts list, the SEMA cost sheet.
5. **The "How I edit" series is the growth engine, not the build.** Her editing lessons did 3.6x her median; her life did 1x. Jacob's version is six parts, one technique each, taught in his words on his footage, with the lesson shown while he explains it (her lessons show the lesson). This does not violate the real-footage rule; it is him teaching on his own reels.
6. **Sell quietly.** House rules already ban the on-screen ask; her method agrees ("buy" 3 of 113). One word in caption line 1 when there is a real deliverable, at most one post in four, the free lesson is the video.
7. **Pilot line for strangers.** Every yap carries one sentence that places him: "If you're new here, I'm Jake, parts manager at a semi shop, building a 1959 Ford on a 7.3 in my garage for SEMA 2027." Said, not burned in.
8. **The soft post after the hard one.** After a numbers post, a quiet one (the truck at dusk, a line about the old man who drove it for the lumber company). Her faith posts did the least views and the most trust; his equivalent is the family and the reason.
9. **What not to copy:** the messy-bun set and mom framing (his set is the garage and the counter), the Stan Store funnel for now (no product until the comments ask three times), the follow line at the end (her teacher's own numbers say it does nothing for reach), per-word pop captions (Jacob's caption audit retired them; the plain style stays).
