## Why it matters for Jacob

Every one of these is a shooting move, not an editing move. They solve the problem the playbook named as root cause 2 ("the footage has no camera moves, so the edit has nothing to ride"). All five work with a phone on a tripod or the Pocket 3 on ActiveTrack and one person:

- **Top down**: phone held high in one hand, looking down at the engine or the bench, Jacob looks up and starts talking as he lowers it to the mount.
- **Side sit**: camera on the fender, Jacob walks in from the side and sits on the bumper or the stool facing it.
- **Close turn out**: start with the lens a foot from his face (or the part in his hand), then he turns to the truck and the Pocket 3 widens via a step back.
- **Swipe down**: his gloved hand wipes across the lens, next shot is the truck or the part. This is the one to use between shop locations instead of a rendered whip pan, because it is a real camera move and the house rule says whip only when the camera moved.
- **Sit back**: start tight on the part, lean back on the toolbox so the bay opens up behind him.

Add these five to the shooting brief as the "talking-head openers" row. They cost nothing and each gives the edit a real move to ride for the hook.
