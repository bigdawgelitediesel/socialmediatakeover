An African king once defeated the greatest army on earth, using nothing but spears in perfect geometry. It was 1879 in South Africa. Britain had conquered half the world. No enemy had stood against them for decades. But deep in Zulu land, a king had spent years building something no European general had ever seen. He trained an army that ran 50 miles a day barefoot. No supply lines. No wagons. Just speed and discipline. And he gave them a formation called the Bulhory, a chest to pin the enemy, two horns to wrap around them, total encirclement, simple, deadly, perfect. On the morning of January 22nd, 20,000 Zulu warriors poured over the hills at Isandaluna by noon. Over 1,300 British soldiers were dead. His name was Ketchwaya. They eventually captured him, brought him to London, and introduced him to Queen Victoria. She called him dignified.
