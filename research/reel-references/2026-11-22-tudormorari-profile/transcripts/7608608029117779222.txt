As a German man crossed the border into East Berlin, he had no idea the government would raise a 3.6 meters concrete wall overnight, and it would be the last time he would ever see his family. You see, he lived in the East but had family in the West. After the war, Berlin was split in two. One side was taken by the Soviets and the other by the West. The East was poor, so by 1961, over three million people had left the East. Factories couldn't run. Hospitals were understaffed. The system was collapsing, so the government decided to build a wall and stop the people leaving. Back in his apartment, the radio announces the news. The border is closed. He doesn't know it yet, but from now on, every morning for the next 28 years, he will walk past the wall on his way to work. Food lines will grow longer. Meat will become rare. Shifts will get longer and heavier. Neighbors will stop trusting each other. Until one night in 1989, the wall is opening. But for many, it's too late.
