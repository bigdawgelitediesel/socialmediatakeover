This country built the most expensive and corrupt sporting event in history. And over 6,000 workers lost their lives. It's 2010, the International Football Association votes on the next World Cup host. 32 countries are in the running and out of nowhere. A tiny desert nation with no stadiums, no football legacy, wins. Investigations later reveal millions in bribes. FIFA officials are arrested. Whistleblowers say the vote was bought. But by then, it's too late. Qatar has the World Cup. And they build it with the cheapest labor on earth. Thousands of workers are brought in. They live in slums, work in 50 degrees Celsius heat, passports taken, 12 hour shifts, no rights, no escape. By the time the first match begins, over 6,500 workers already lost their lives. Billions are spent. Stadiums rise from the sand. A desert transformed into a global stage. But beneath the lights, the price was buried.
