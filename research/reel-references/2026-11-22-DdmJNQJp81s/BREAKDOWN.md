## The five rules, transcribed from the captions

1. **Stop overcomplicating it.** "All personal branding is an intentional pairing of relevant things done consistently. What that does is it creates the byproduct, which is brand: when your audience inherently associates you with the thing that you are consistently pairing yourself with."
2. **Optimize for trust, not views.** "We need to stop optimizing our content for views and virality and start leaning into building trust, because we want to attract customers, not just viewers."
3. **Find your contrarian take.** "What do you believe that is different than all the other individuals in your space?"
4. **Find your pond.** "You have your ocean, your lake, your pond, your puddle. Which one of these bodies of water does your credibility let you actually compete in? If you're a minnow, do not go in the ocean right now. A puddle or a pond. I don't talk about brand or personal brand, I talk about personal brand for entrepreneurs. That is my pond."
5. **Figure out what your goal is.** Four questions: What is your desired outcome? What would you need to be known for to get that outcome? What would you need to do to build that reputation? What would you need to learn to actually do it?

## What to take for El Coyote

The rules are a brand filter, not an editing style. Applied to Jacob with the house facts:

- **Pairing (rule 1).** The pairing is already decided and must not drift: Jake plus a 1959 Ford F600 on a 7.3 Powerstroke, built in a garage by a parts manager, headed to SEMA 2027. Every post pairs him with that. The counter is the credential inside it, not a second brand.
- **Trust, not views (rule 2).** This is the honest tension with the million-view rule. The data in this repo says both: the 10 mil meme reached 418,000 people and the account gained about 110 followers in six weeks. Views from strangers with no trust convert at 0.28 percent. The resolution is the shape, not the goal: the stories (60 to 120 s, receipts, the problem shown) build trust while chasing views; the loops buy reach. Keep the million as the target and let the story slot carry the trust.
- **Contrarian take (rule 3).** Jacob has four nobody else holds, all already in the repo: a parts guy knows what fails better than the mechanic, because he orders the replacements; you do not need a shop to put a truck on the SEMA floor; disable the CP4 and run a FASS instead of a CP3 or DCR swap; "built", never "bulletproofed", and 2WD stays 2WD. Pick one per post and say it as a flat claim, not a question.
- **The pond (rule 4).** Diesel is the ocean. 7.3 Powerstroke is the lake. The pond is a 7.3 in a 1957 to 1960 Ford body headed to SEMA, which no one has put on the floor in three years (tribe report). That is the pond to say out loud: "I build 7.3s into old Fords."
- **The goal (rule 5), answered.** Outcome: the F600 on the SEMA floor November 2 to 5, 2027, paid for in parts by partners who exhibit, and an account big enough that brands write first. Known for: the parts manager who built a '59 Ford on a 7.3 in his garage and tells you what parts actually fail and what they cost. Do: a post a day from the build, one named tribe per post, real receipts, the counter in every third post, every post also on TikTok and Shorts. Learn: shoot with moves, talk to camera for 60 to 120 s (the yap tier from the Jessica Carter study), the story shape.
