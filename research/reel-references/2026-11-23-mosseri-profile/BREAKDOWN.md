## 3. What he says, by topic

### 3.1 What ranking is

- 2026-09-03, said: "there isn't one algorithm on Instagram... There are two general types of algorithms, one to rank content from accounts you follow and one to rank content from accounts that you do not follow. We call those recommendations."
- 2026-09-03, said: "what we're trying to do is make an educated guess about what you're interested in by looking at what you've historically done on the platform, and then predicting how likely you are to like, comment, send to a friend, watch a video, watch it to the end... We use those predictions to come up with relevancy scores for every post you could see, then we order posts by those scores."
- 2026-09-03, said, on why they rank at all: "If we do not rank content, we know that the feed for the consumer gets overrun with content from the accounts that post the most often."
- 2026-09-24, said: "we can't know for sure what they're interested in so we use proxies. How much of the [reel] did they watch? How likely they are to send it to a friend? How likely they are to like that piece of content."
- 2021-06-23, said, on Reels: "The most important signals are, first and foremost, your activity... Next is your history of interacting with the person. So if you happen to actually watch a lot of Reels from that person in the past or like a lot of their content, that's super valuable and super important. Then there's information about the Reel itself... And then last, information about the person who actually posted."

### 3.2 The three signals, and which comes first

- 2025-01-21, said: "for both, the most important signals are the same three. They are how much of a video we think someone is going to watch and whether or not we think they're going to like that video or they're going to actually send that video to a friend. And so what you need to look at is average watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach."
- 2025-01-21, said, same breath (checked against his burned-in caption, which reads "But time is the most important for both."): "likes are actually slightly more important than sends for connected content and sends are actually a little bit more important than like for unconnected content, but time is the most important for both." **The post caption leaves that last clause out, which is why every guide that quotes the caption lists three equal signals.**
- 2026-05-26, said: "like rates matter more for your followers, and send rates matter more for those who don't follow you."
- 2026-09-24, said: "what percent of them like that reel, what percent of them sent it to a friend, what is the average watch time of the overall reel."
- 2024-08-07, said: "sends, reach and views, probably the most important metrics for anybody trying to understand how their content is doing."
- Secondhand (Colin and Samir interview, recorded 2026-10-23, quoted by [ZoomSphere](https://www.zoomsphere.com/blog/instagram-algorithm-2026-what-actually-gets-reach) and already in the repo from the YouTube transcript): "Sends per reach is particularly important for that unconnected reach. So if you're a new creator, that's probably one of the most important metrics you could follow," and "We focus way less on watch time than all the competition does, but we do care about watch time." That second line compares Instagram with other apps. It does not rank watch time below sends inside Instagram, and nothing first-hand here does either.

### 3.3 Sends

- 2024-07-02, said: "One of the most important signals we use in ranking is sends per reach. So out of all the people who saw your reel or your photo, how many of them sent it to a friend in a DM?"
- 2024-07-02, said: "So don't force it as a creator, but if you can, when you're making content, think about making content that people would want to send to a friend, to someone that they care about."
- 2024-08-15, said, sends against saves: "we actually do value both in ranking. But we focus more on sends primarily because our whole hope is to inspire creativity that brings people together... [a save is] usually something you do on your own... But saves matter too, so don't ignore those either."
- 2025-12-31, memo slide 8: "The primary way people share now is in DMs."
- 2025-09-24, said: "almost all our growth has been driven by DMs, reels and recommendations."

### 3.4 Watch time, hooks, length

- 2024-10-07, said: "The first three seconds of a reel are super important. Now we call this the hook. Now that doesn't necessarily mean you need to bait the user or have a cliffhanger, but you do need to get the user's attention."
- 2024-10-07, said: "One, use text when possible because a lot of times people actually have [the] sound off to start. And two, set expectations. Let people know what you're gonna talk about so they know if they are interested in viewing a bit further."
- 2025-11-05, said: "One, you need a hook. Give me a reason to keep watching in those first three seconds so I don't keep scrolling on."
- 2025-08-20, said: "there is no perfect length for a reel, but generally we recommend reels under three minutes because Instagram is generally focused on shorter video."
- 2025-08-20, said: "if you go to insights you can actually see what your drop-off rate is and whether or not you're managing to capture people's attention in those first three seconds. You can also, and this is new, look at where in your video you seem to be getting your [likes]."
- 2025-01-22, said: "keep your reels less than three minutes long. It will also help with your reach."

### 3.5 Captions on screen, sound, music

- 2025-11-05, said: "Two, turn on captions. About half the time that people are in feed their audio is off. So they're not going to know that you're talking unless you turn on captions."
- 2024-09-03, said: "about half the time people have audio turned on when they use Instagram, you're more likely to get someone's attention and that'll help in terms of getting more likes, comments, and therefore more reach. The second is for reels specifically you get to be on the audio page and a lot of people go to audio pages to look for inspiration."
- 2025-01-22, said: "use music or dynamic audio. Trending is great, doesn't have to be trending, but if the audio is engaging, it will help the reel overall."
- 2025-11-05, said: "use music particularly if you're using photos or carousels. But even in reels if it works with your content strategy."
- Nothing in any reel studied is about the written caption under the post as a ranking input. See limits.

### 3.6 Posting frequency

- 2024-12-06, said: "the more you post in general, the more reach you're gonna get. The other is if you commit to too much content creation, you're gonna burn out... I have seen creators that are very successful that create only one thing a month and I've seen creators that are very successful that create five reels a day."
- 2025-01-20, said: "you should prioritize quality over quantity, but in general, posting more will help. The more you post, the more people you're going to reach, and the more your follower count is going to grow over time."
- 2025-01-20, said, the one risk: "if you post really infrequently right now, and you're thinking about posting way more, so say going from once [or] twice a week to five or six times a day, you do run the risk in that edge case of alienating people who followed you with a different set of expectations. So if you are in that edge case... I do highly recommend you use trial reels."
- 2026-09-24, said: "posting more often is going to increase your reach over time because more at bats means you've got a higher chance of getting a hit... try and pick a frequency that you can sustain."
- 2025-01-22, said: "post regularly, which [means] at least once or so a week, maybe more, but also consistently in theme. If people know what they're gonna get by following you, they're more likely to follow you in the first place."
- 2024-10-30, said: "If you have a video that does really well... it would be good to post relatively soon, in the next day or two, if you want to capitalize on that momentum. Cause you've got a lot of people who are now expressing interest in your content and they're more likely to see whatever you post next."

### 3.7 Trial Reels

- 2024-12-10, said: "you're gonna be able to share a reel and mark it as a trial, and then it won't show up on your profile, or on your reels tab, or even for anybody who follows you. Instead, we will show it just to... some people who do not follow you... I'd recommend you look at how many likes and sends it's getting per reach. Look at that ratio. And then you can decide after a day or two if you actually wanna commit."
- 2025-01-21, said: trials allow "you to bypass the entire connected ranking system and just go straight to the unconnected recommendations."
- 2025-02-20, said: "because it's not going to your followers, you can't compare it to a normal reel. It's going to get less reach almost always at the beginning. And so what you need to do is compare trial reels to other trials and look at sends, look at likes, look at watch time. Don't look at it in the first 10 minutes, wait a couple of hours and compare it to how other trials were doing around [a] similar amount of time."
- 2025-11-05, said: "Use trials if you're not comfortable posting a lot to your main grid [and] to all of your followers on a regular basis."
- 2026-04-02, said: "Back in November someone asked why can't you schedule a trial reel and so we built it and now you can."
- 2026-09-24, said: trial reels are "a less pressurized... way to experiment because you can post reels not to your profile or to your followers but just to non followers."
- Not said in any reel studied: a follower minimum, a daily cap, anything about duplicates. Those live in his Stories Q&As. Secondhand, [ZoomSphere](https://www.zoomsphere.com/blog/instagram-algorithm-2026-what-actually-gets-reach): 2026-10-16 "If you're just trying to experiment with lots of different reels and you want to use your trial reels a lot, that's totally fine. It's not going to affect your account in a negative way"; posting the same reel as a trial again and again "is not okay. We're going to crack down on that"; 2026-10-30, several different trials in one day "totally fine."

### 3.8 Originality, reposts, watermarks

- 2026-04-30, said: "we try to take a look at everything you post over a month and if most of what you posted is content from other accounts, we make it so that your account's content is no longer recommended to people who do not follow you. We don't get between you and your followers, though."
- 2026-04-30, said: "until now, we've only looked at the reels you've posted over the last month... over the next month, we're gonna start looking at your photos and your carousels as well."
- 2026-04-30, said, the ways out: "remix the content in some way, shape, or form. Make it your own... your own commentary," or "repost someone else's content using the repost button," or "do a collab post."
- 2026-09-09, said: "we try to estimate how likely a piece of content is to be original, to be from the account [that] actually made that piece of content by looking at what they posted in the past and other signals."
- 2025-01-22, said: "don't post reels with watermarks or logos. They will get less reach."
- 2024-10-23, said: "if you watermark a reel with your own logo will that affect its reach and the answer is no... What we try to do is not recommend reels with logos from other apps."
- 2025-12-31, memo slide 19: "Continue to improve ranking for originality." Slide 6: "The bar is shifting from 'can you create?' to 'can you make something that only you could create?'"

### 3.9 Small accounts, breakouts, recency

- 2026-09-09, said: "We don't just want to be a place where large creators can get a lot of [reach]. We want to be a place where a new creator with a great idea can actually reach a massive audience, and we do this by auditioning content. So, we will show content from a creator or a public account to some accounts that don't follow it just to see if it might actually be more interesting... And if it does really well, we will do so again and again and again."
- 2026-09-09, said: "you can see a post from a small public creator reach hundreds of thousands, even millions of people, because it's just out-performing expectations."
- 2026-09-09, said, on recency: "we look mostly at what percentage of impressions in a given day are of posts that are less than 24 hours old, and that number's also gone way up."
- 2026-09-03, said: "Recency matters a lot."
- 2024-04-24, said: "the engagement rate on your content is more important than your follower count. Now it's not that your follower count [isn't] important, it is important, it will definitely correlate... the more followers you have, the more reach you're going to get on average."
- 2024-04-24, said: "If your followers are going down but your engagement is going up, that's actually good... Whereas if your follower count is going up and your engagement is going down, that's much more concerning."
- 2025-09-24, said: "that world, where following still matters, but not as much as it used to."

### 3.10 Stories, carousels, collabs

- 2024-10-28, said: "The best way to maximize reach is actually to post reels or carousels to feed, but stories are an important way of engaging accounts that already follow you. They're not going to help you get new followers. They're not going to help you reach accounts that don't follow you yet... Creators that post to stories often see less unfollows than creators who don't."

## 5. How he films (format reference)

He is a one-man talking head, the same job as Jacob's afternoon episode. Sheets in `sheets/`.

- **Length.** Advice and Q&A reels run 15 to 90 s; the middle of the pile is 45 to 65 s. Answers to one question are 15 to 35 s. Only the 2026 ranking series goes long (95 to 177 s). Announcements with screen demos run 50 to 120 s.
- **One take.** 36 of his 50 reels are a single shot with no cut at all. The cuts that exist are screen recordings dropped into product announcements, and since late 2026 those sit over a blurred copy of the talking shot rather than replacing it.
- **Opener.** No greeting, no name, no warm-up. The first sentence is the subject, said flat, done inside three seconds: "The first three seconds of a reel are super important." "Five simple things you can do to increase your reach on Instagram." "Why should you use audio when you post reels." Q&A reels open by reading the question. Lists are announced as lists and counted out loud ("One... Two...").
- **Framing.** Phone held at arm's length or propped low on a table, a little below eye level, so he leans into it. Head and chest fill the frame; his hands are in the bottom third and move the whole time. Office couch, desk or meeting table, daylight from a window behind or beside him, nothing dressed. Different shirt and room every time, same distance.
- **Burned-in captions.** Always. Small white sans with a soft shadow, centred, at about two thirds of the way down the frame (chest height, under the chin, never on the face). One to three lines, a phrase at a time, each card up one to three seconds. No box, no colour, no emoji. The auto-caption mistakes are left in ("engagement ring" for "engagement rate" on 2026-09-24). In 2024 a few reels add a pink pill over a key word (LIKES, VIEWS, RESHARES). On 2026-11-05 the captions are karaoke style: the line shows whole and the words not yet spoken are grey.
- **No title card.** Frame one is his face mid-word. There is no hook text beyond the caption line itself, no lockup, no logo, no end card.
- **Ending.** A spoken sign-off, nearly every time: "More to come soon. Peace." Often "let me know down below in the comments" before it. The asks are spoken and in the post caption, never on screen.
- **Post caption.** One to three plain sentences that restate the point, so the post makes sense with the sound off and the video unwatched. Q&A reels are captioned "AMA:" plus the question. No hashtags.
- **Where the house rules differ.** He uses an outro line and a spoken comment ask on almost every reel; Jacob's rules are no outro and no ask. He puts captions on everything; the kit ships rollers with none. Noted, not recommended either way.
