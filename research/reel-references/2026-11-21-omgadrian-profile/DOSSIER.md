## 5. Per-video breakdowns (from the sheets and transcripts in `watch-*/`)

Shared vocabulary used below: **pop word** = his signature title treatment, a yellow (about #F3D21C) condensed heavy sans (Anton/Bebas class) in all caps at 150 to 180 px cap height, placed in the upper third, with the connective words of the sentence set small in a white handwriting script above it ("to get the best" / POSSIBLE QUALITY; "think good" / EDITING; "cutting to" / MOTION). Pop words land on the spoken noun, hold 0.6 to 1.2 s, and are gone. **Serif caption** = one line, 2 to 6 words, a bold old-style serif (Times class), about 40 px cap height (56 px font), white or cream with a thin dark edge, centred at y about 1485 (not 1185 like the software accounts), present on every spoken line, sentence case with punctuation, never on the face because he keeps his face in the upper two thirds. **Stage** = a phone camera UI mock or a white-bordered print on a cream vignette plate, used to show the photos he is talking about.

## 6. His editing system, as measured values

| Element | Measured |
|---|---|
| Frame 1 | A pop word or its script lead-in is already on screen at 0.0 in 13 of 13, and the next word lands by 1.3 s, so something enters the frame inside the first 0.33 s every time (addendum rule 43). His body is in motion toward or across the locked camera (walk-in, turn-in, lean-in) in 5 of 13; in the other 8 he is standing or seated and gesturing while the type does the moving. A prop in hand (phone, camera, flowers, notebook) in 10 of 13. Never a title-only frame, never a face with nothing happening. |
| First words | "Today I'm going to show/teach y'all how to..." (6 of 13), a question ("Do you want to...", "Have you ever...", "So is it a waste to...") (4 of 13), or a contrarian claim ("Most people think...", "People say...", "If you're not getting views, it's not because...") (3 of 13). The lesson or the tension is named inside 3.4 s every time. Transcribed hook length 11 to 17 words. |
| Pop words | 4 to 8 per reel, 70 percent of them in the first 13 s, the rest on chapter breaks and the one plug. Yellow condensed caps, 150 to 180 px, upper third, each lands on its spoken word, holds 0.6 to 1.2 s. Connective words in a white hand script about 90 px. Never a box, never a stroke, no drop shadow visible at delivery size. |
| Captions | Every spoken line, one line, 2 to 6 words, serif bold about 40 px cap height, white/cream with a thin dark edge, centred at y about 1485, sentence case with commas and periods. No per-word color, no karaoke, no emoji. |
| Chapter cards | Giant yellow numeral (about 500 px) with a script label ("1 Stationary", "2 In Motion") over a texture plate, 1.2 s. Or a chalkboard list where future items are blurred until reached. |
| Series card | Cream paper with a soft vignette, series name in a two-font lockup (script over block), "ep. 02" in a monospaced small line, 1.2 s, music continues under it. |
| Stage objects | Phone camera UI mock (iPhone frame, grid, shutter button) for photos, white-border prints with a soft shadow on a cream plate, a paper shot list with a yellow highlighter line. The stage lets him show 3 to 6 photos in 4 s without a montage. |
| Cut rate | 1.4 to 3.1 s per cut (median about 2.0). Series episodes cut fastest (1.4 to 1.5), method demos slowest (3.1). B-roll runs at 0.8 to 1.3 s per shot. |
| Camera | Locked tripod wides and mediums, the talent moves. Top-down overhead for asides (always black and white). Macro for menus and hands. Over-shoulder phone POV. No gimbal orbits, no whips. |
| Color | A warm, slightly lifted filmic grade (his own LUT) on everything; black wardrobe and olive or khaki on the subject; yellow as the single accent; cream for plates. Black and white is a register switch (jokes and sign-offs), not a mood. |
| Sound | His own voice, close-miked, about 150 to 185 wpm. A music bed 7 to 8 dB under the voice that rises in b-roll gaps. No SFX kit: the only "hit" in 13 reels is a laptop slamming shut on camera. |
| Length | 60 to 100 s for tutorials and episodes, 21 s for the list reel, 100 to 180 s for mindset talks. |
| Ending | A fixed sign-off, seated, slower: "Hope this helps, and as always, take care, happy creating, and I'll catch you all next time. Peace." ("Peace." closes 9 of 13.) Series episodes swap in "follow along for part three, we saved the best for last, see y'all in a few days." The list reel and the reintroduction have no sign-off at all. |
| The ask | Spoken once, late, inside the body ("link in my bio", "comment COLLEGE", "comment APCC"), with a matching pop word (BIO). Never a title card, never on frame 1, never a sticker. 4 of 13 carry any product or keyword ask; 5 of 13 carry a series cliffhanger; 4 carry neither. |
| Other recurring devices | A wall or 3D carousel of his own reel covers as proof (4 of 13); a comment wall of real comment bubbles as the episode's justification; a chalkboard list where future items are blurred; a shot-list page with the current line highlighted; a thin centre line drawn over faces; a small sponsor label ("#ADOBEPARTNER") top-centre for the first 5 s of brand reels. |

His own stated rules, from the tutorials watched (5.9, 5.12, 5.13): the first three seconds need "relatability and tension... state the problem your audience already feels and make it sting a little"; on-screen titles must be "an amplifier", never "a one-to-one transcript of your words"; captions always, because sound is off and "styled right they add rhythm, almost like a second hook"; leave the upper third empty on set because "the text is gonna end up going here"; pop words are hand-timed per word, text behind the cutout, no preset; put the lead eye on the vertical centre line, not the face; "I don't think the method is posting every day, but maximizing your efforts" (Aug 2025).

## 7. His growth engine

1. **The subject of the reel is the viewer's relationship, the craft is the vehicle.** The Boyfriend Bootcamp run (May to Aug 2026) is 6 of his 8 biggest organic posts of the year and 3 to 4 percent sends; the same editing system on camera settings does 0.5 to 0.9. He found it by accident (the Sandcastles writeup says the 6x5 topic/format grid "runs without him naming it"), then turned it into a numbered series with a title card.
2. **Series with a cliffhanger instead of a follow ask.** "Make sure you follow along because EP. 3 is the most important." The follow buys the next episode.
3. **Daily posting built the base, every-third-day keeps it.** 365 days in 2023 to 200K; now one post per three days, 2,276 lifetime posts.
4. **Everything free on the feed, products one layer down.** "All the content I've made here and everything I've ever taught you, I don't put behind a paywall." The LUT is the one product that appears in-reel, and only inside a reel that teaches the camera the LUT is for. Content College is a Manychat keyword at the very end of one editing reel.
5. **Platform endorsement as distribution.** Rings winner, the official Edits tutorials, Mosseri spotlights, Cannes with Adobe. Nothing Jacob can copy, but it explains the 1M more than the editing does.
6. **Saves from settings, sends from people.** Settings and gear reels (Pocket 3, Sony, iPhone) are his save engine and sell the LUT; relationship reels are his send engine and grow the account; mindset talks are the comment engine and hold the community. He rotates all three.
7. **Cross-posting as a mirror.** TikTok (528K) and YouTube (75K) get the same files; the captions are written for Instagram and sometimes left broken on TikTok ("the CTA here won't work"). YouTube long-form (15 to 16 min "How to be a full time creator" 202K, "This is your new content strategy" 307K) is where the mindset lane lives.

## 9. What Jacob can take (fits the house rules: sends over everything, no on-screen CTA, one lockup, no grade, caption line 1 names a person type, real footage only)

1. **Open on a walk-in with the part in hand and the first noun on screen at 0.0.** Pocket 3 on the fender, walk toward it carrying the turbo or the head, say "Today I'm going to show you..." Adrian's frame 1 is always him moving plus one word. (Rule 20, 43.)
2. **Point the craft at the viewer's person, not at the viewer.** His 4 percent reels are "photos of your girlfriend"; ours should be "your 7.3 brother's lift pump", "the guy who told you to keep it stock", "your dad's old F600". Caption line 1 names them. (Rule 1, 3.)
3. **Run a numbered mini-series with a cliffhanger instead of any follow ask.** "Part three is the one that matters, few days." Three to five episodes on one job (frame swap in five parts). The MEGANTE card with EP N is our version of his cream title card. (Rule 30.)
4. **Pop words on the spoken nouns, upper third, yellow only inside the LOUDER lockup, tan elsewhere.** Four to eight per reel, 70 percent in the first 13 s, 0.6 to 1.2 s hold, no box, no stroke. The connective words in a small hand script is a cheap, distinctive look we do not have yet; test it in Kaushan Script (already in the LOUDER lockup) to keep one family. (Rule 12, 46.)
5. **Chapter numerals.** A giant numeral with a script label for 1.2 s between sections ("1 Pull the cab", "2 Cut the frame"). It is the HUD counter of a talking-head reel. (Rule 9.)
6. **Use a stage object for photos and parts lists:** a white-border print on a cream plate, or the portal page in a phone frame. He shows six photos in four seconds this way without a montage. (Rule 14.)
7. **The 21 s list reel with labels held exactly as long as each shot** ("five shots" reel): "The 5 shots I film every Rival update with" is a literal copy that teaches the audience to recognise the series. One label per shot, label in the top band, voice only, no ending.
8. **Black and white as the aside register.** Top-down, B&W, one joke line ("Said no girlfriend ever"), back to color. It separates the meme beat from the build beat inside the same reel without breaking the one-topic rule, as long as the joke is about the topic. No grade on the color footage still holds; B&W is a switch, not a grade.
9. **Show the camera in the shot, then show the result.** His invisible-cut demo (phone on a stand in frame, perform, cut, show) is the cheapest "how I filmed this" reel there is, and it is a second topic Jacob can own: how a one-man shop films a build.
10. **Cut on motion, not on the beat, for talking segments.** Head turn, hand reach, tool pick-up, written into the shot list before filming. 1.5 to 3 s per angle, two angles per statement. This is the one craft rule of his that fixes "static face until 2.7 s" on Harley Trade.
11. **Captions small and serif, bottom band, every line.** His 40 px cap-height serif at y 1485 reads clean at 1M followers; it contradicts the "68 px sans" default. Keep PlainCaption as the spec for card reels, but test the small serif on a walk-and-talk where the face owns the frame. (Rule 46 says 44 to 58 px; he is at the floor.)
12. **A fixed spoken sign-off, seated, slower, no product.** "Catch you on the next one" in Jacob's words, same line every time. It is a brand asset and it is not an on-screen CTA. Series episodes swap it for the cliffhanger.
13. **Settings reels are the save lane, not the send lane.** "Pocket 3 settings for a diesel shop" will save and sell nothing; budget it as one in six, like his brand slot.
14. **One music bed, 7 to 8 dB under the voice, rising in b-roll gaps, no SFX.** His reels prove the SFX kit is optional on talking-head reels; spend the SFX budget on the card reels only. (Rule 27 stays for menu reels.)
15. **Posting rhythm: one every three days, with a daily burst around a milestone.** He did daily for a year to get to 200K; Jacob's version is daily raw for the frame-swap week (rule 24), then back to the three-day rhythm.
