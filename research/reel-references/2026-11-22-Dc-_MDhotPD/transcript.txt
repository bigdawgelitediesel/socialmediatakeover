 I must not have a life because I spent the bank call on a weekend breaking down the first three seconds of over a hundred reels that broke a million views and what I found was boringly consistent. I purposely didn't include any reels from famous people or entertainment style memes or people without their clothes on but I did focus purely on the first three seconds of the video in the audio. Almost all of them had these six things in common but what's really interesting is that I've had thousands of videos go through me before being posted and the biggest outliers had at least four, five and six. Number one is a visual hook. Sorry business pages who like to put your logo or weird little intro. There's always movement as soon as the video starts or at least someone's talking immediately as soon as the clip starts or they're in a location that is unique enough to act as a pattern interrupt. Something else that I noticed as well after second two or three the clip or angle tends to change. Two, the dominant formats were YAP, green screen and what I like to call a polished YAP which is working quite well right now which is essentially yes a stream of consciousness raw style video but filmed nicer in better location maybe with better camera but also reinforced with graphics. Number three the on-screen text hooks were specific but they usually focused on either pain-orientated hooks or identity-orientated hooks. A good identity orientated hook could be instead of how to get there a business it could be what type of person is most likely to become a CEO. Number four was that the videos were about broad-tamed topics. Think about relationships, family, philosophy, health, sleep habits, anything that's broadly relatable. Number five they didn't say hi guys they said you they spoke directly to the person behind the camera. And number six the on-screen text hook was clear and readable at a glance high contrast usually and always in view not blocked by any of the UI. The takeaway here is yes hooks and formats are important but if you're not speaking about a topic that has a broad enough tam you're not talking directly to the other person on the camera and you're not giving them immediate context about what the video is about you are unreasonable to expect a one million view video.
