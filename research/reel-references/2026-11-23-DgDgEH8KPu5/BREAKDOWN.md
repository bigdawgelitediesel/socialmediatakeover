## What is on screen

A two-hander skit in a pool hall. Five shots in 28 s: her at the table (0 to 2 s), her standing (2 to 6), him in close-up (6 to 11), the two of them at the table (11 to 22), him alone at the end (22 to 28), plus a two-frame insert. So the cut rate is low; the motion is the type. Every beat gets one big word that pops in with scale: a heavy rounded sans in white, yellow or orange ("but", "how do y'all do these", "this!", "huh", "those", "you wanna know", "Alright", "I'll send it over", "comment captions") and an italic serif in orange or cream for the flavour words ("cowgirl", "y'all", "Wild West"). Two type families, three colours, and the big word always sits in clean space, above the heads or beside a face, never on one. Under the big words run small plain white captions, one or two words, at chest height. Two gags in the type: the caption styles he is "selling" are shown blurred ("it's a secret, they're blurry"), and a red crosshair lands on his face on "before they take me out". Ends on a "Wild West" lockup and a hard cut.

## Why it works, and what the numbers are

1. The comments are the gate, and the deliverable is real (a 30 minute tutorial). 1,783 comments on 827 likes is the keyword-funnel signature; the reach is bought by the comment count. The house ceiling stands: one post in four, caption only, a real free deliverable.
2. The product is demonstrated by the reel itself. He sells captions, so every beat is a caption; the thing being sold is the thing you are watching. That is the honest version of a lead magnet.
3. Five shots, twenty-odd words: the change comes from type and from two actors reacting, not from cuts. Change, not cut, in a skit.
4. The withheld secret is a gag, not a tease: the samples are shown blurred and the characters say so. The viewer is in on it.
5. A straight man and a question. She asks, he bluffs, the ask lands as a joke with a crosshair. The turn is on the last line and the cut is on it (playbook skit shape).
6. Picture first: lit practicals, real contrast, skin natural. It looks expensive because the light is right, not because of the graphics.
