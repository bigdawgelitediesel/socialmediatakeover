# Edita Visuals (@edita.visuals) dossier

Compiled 2026-11-21 for the El Coyote editing lane. Trigger: Jacob sent the 5.5 s 2x2 reel https://www.instagram.com/reel/Df3tIAzhB9h/ (frame breakdown in `../2026-11-21-Df3tIAzhB9h/BREAKDOWN.md`). ## Bottom line

Edita Visuals is not a motion designer. It is a four-week-old funnel selling a $29 Skool course ("Edita Club") that teaches people to have Claude Code copy reference reels onto their own footage. The page, the TikTok mirror, the YouTube channel and the website all came online between 2026-10-22 and 2026-10-31. The "full guide" they gate behind "comment CLAUDE" is public on their own site: three long prompts that have Claude Code render every shot as an HTML/CSS/JS page through headless Chrome (Playwright), with ffmpeg for audio and encode and Whisper for word timestamps. No Remotion, no GSAP, no After Effects. The "I didn't touch a single keyframe" line is literally true of that pipeline (there are no keyframes in a code render) and still misleading: their own tutorials describe v1 failures, notes rounds and 20 to 45 minutes of watching and checking per edit. The craft worth taking is the measurement discipline and the numeric motion and sound rules, not the AI portraits, mascots or comment gates.

## The gated "full guide": what is actually public

The comment gate leads to the same place the YouTube descriptions link: **https://editaclub.com/g/57-tulum/** ("Podcast edit. Built in code."), a free page with the raw clip (16:9, 10 MB), the reference edit (1080x1920, 16 MB) and three prompts. Saved copies: `sources/editaclub-free-guide-57-tulum-2026-11-21.html` and `.txt`.

**Setup (their six steps):** 1) Claude with Opus 5.5 plus Claude Code (desktop app or VS Code extension), "what lets it run code, Chrome and ffmpeg on your computer"; 2) put `raw.mp4` and `reference.mp4` in one empty folder; 3) open the folder in Claude Code; 4) paste Prompt B to copy the reference 1:1; 5) Prompt C for the before/after post; 6) notes one round at a time, specific ("the pill comes in late", "the caption covers her mouth").

**Renderer:** "Build it in code: every shot is an HTML/CSS/JS page that headless Chrome renders frame by frame (Playwright), ffmpeg for the audio and the final encode. No After Effects." Not Remotion, not GSAP by name, not HyperFrames. Their YouTube explanation of the same thing: "it writes the edit down like a recipe... your computer reads that recipe and cooks the video frame by frame." The 3D pieces (mascots, monitor rooms, laptop rooms) come from a separate "Blender Scenes With Claude" module; the public page does not say how Blender is driven.

**How Claude is prompted (Prompt B, copy 1:1):**
1. Split the reference into shots. Per shot write `REF.md` "with every number measured from the frames, never guessed": positions and sizes in px, fonts matched by x-height and letter shapes, colours sampled from the frame, when each word appears, how it moves and on what curve.
2. Transcribe the raw clip with word timestamps; line sentences up with the reference shots; cut only in real pauses; never stretch the voice.
3. One still 9:16 crop per speaker shot, no fake camera moves; the only move is the zoom-out on shot one.
4. B-roll from free stills (Pexels, Unsplash), never cut out of the reference.
5. Build one shot at a time, render it next to the reference frame for frame, write `DIFF.md` worst-first, fix, compare again, move on only when it matches.
6. Rebuild graphics clean at full resolution.
7. Sound: the reference's whooshes, clicks and glitch on the same frames, a quiet bed, master around -14 LUFS.
8. Finish with a side-by-side and a per-frame motion check "so nothing jitters. Don't ask me questions, show me the result."

**Prompt A (same style on your own clip) is a numeric style sheet, which is the interesting part:**
- Whisper word timestamps first, then a beat plan shown before any render: a b-roll or a split every 2 to 4 s, never more than 4 s on a talking head.
- Colours by hex (rust #A24A24, terracotta #C77136, peach #ECA572, cream #E1D1BB, warm white #FBF8F3, red #DD3C37 only for "!" icons). Type: Inter captions, Bebas Neue accent words.
- Text zone: captions only between y 950 and 1410, at least 51 px from the left and 58 px from the right; nothing in the top half on full-screen shots.
- Shot one zooms out 115% to 100% and "flashes a white silhouette of me on frames 1, 3 and 5 with a glitch click" (the negative flash we clocked in the quad reel).
- Every caption word slides in from 29 px on the right with a circ.out ease; 1 to 3 lines per page; one accent per page (a pill, a bar that wipes open, or one big word), never two.
- Full-screen b-roll: stills slide right to left from the cut, gradient at the bottom, fine scanline, the whole run stepped at about 14 fps; one caption grows word by word across the run.
- Split screen: white canvas, two rounded panels, radius 22. Lists: one chip per item popping in from below as it is said.
- Motion rules: hard cuts only, no dissolves, nothing animates out; pills slide 54 px with a small spring; bars wipe with an expo ease.
- Audio: a whoosh on every speaker/b-roll cut, a double click on cuts inside a b-roll run, bed 15 dB under the voice, master about -14 LUFS. (Our measurement of the IG quad reel: -15.2 LUFS, consistent.)
- QC: export a still of every shot, check no text covers a mouth or leaves the zone, check every caption against the word timestamps, then render `final.mp4`.

**Prompt C (before/after post):** 1440x2560 at 60 fps, raw clip in black and white on a small left card labelled "Raw footage", the final edit on a larger right card with an "Opus 5.5" pill, 'Follow & comment "OPUS" for the full guide' centred under the cards, everything on screen from frame one, stills every second before export.

**Sound source:** not named publicly; a "sound effects" pack is a paid bonus. The free page says only what plays where and at what level.

## The "I didn't touch a single keyframe" claim

- Consistent with the pipeline: HTML pages rendered per frame have no keyframes to touch. The human work is choosing the reference, the prompt, the notes and the rules file.
- Their own words undercut the zero-effort reading: v1 of the Tulum edit had "melted" text and a b-roll still with another company's logo; fixes came through notes; "most reels, 20 to 45 minutes... most of those minutes isn't me working, it's me watching and checking"; "the first version will not be perfect."
- No repo, no screen recording of Claude Code at work and no live render was found anywhere public. The YouTube tutorials show finished results with voiceover. The only hard artifact is the prompt text on the free page.
- The woman in the podcast reference "doesn't exist either, we made her with AI for free" (their video of 2026-11-14). The quad reel's portraits are AI as well.
- Model naming is marketing: the headers say "Opus 5.5 cooked", while two November Shorts are titled "Claude Fable 5.2 Edited This".
- The 1,448 comments on 832 likes is a comment-gate artifact, not conversation.

## What El Coyote can take

House constraints apply to every item: real footage only, no AI imagery, one lockup on the hook, no on-screen CTA, no em-dashes in copy.

1. **Measure, don't describe.** Their only real edge is `REF.md` plus `DIFF.md`: every number read off the reference frames (px, font by x-height, sampled hex, per-word timing, curve), one shot built at a time, rendered next to the reference, the worst difference fixed first. Our BREAKDOWN.md already reads at 10 fps; add a DIFF pass whenever we rebuild a borrowed look in Remotion or build_reel3.
2. **A rules file that grows from Jacob's corrections.** EDITA BRAIN is just a CLAUDE.md of editing rules. We have the MASTER PLAYBOOK and the reel look rules; the missing habit is turning each correction (the "crap" outlines, 2026-11-20) into a one-line rule the cut tool reads before every render, so no step back between sessions.
3. **Adopt the numeric motion grammar, drop the look.** Caption words slide in 29 px from the right on circ.out; pills 54 px with a small spring; bars wipe on expo; hard cuts only; nothing animates out; one accent per page. For Rival, the "accent" is one lockup or one spec number, never two.
4. **The silhouette hit, exactly specified.** White cutout on frames 1, 3 and 5 with a glitch click (three alternating frames, not a two-frame hold). Pairs with the RVM matte idea in BREAKDOWN.md for engine-start or first-fire moments.
5. **Sound as numbers.** A whoosh on scene changes, a double click inside a still run, bed 15 dB under the voice, master at -14 LUFS, each hit landing on the cut frame. Set build_reel's bed ducking to -15 dB and add a LUFS check to the export step.
6. **Text zone constants.** Captions only between y 950 and 1410 on 1080x1920, 51 px left and 58 px right margins, nothing in the top half on full-frame shots. Hard-code these in build_reel3 so captions never sit under IG's buttons.
7. **Stepped b-roll runs.** Stills (parts, the frame, Fullbay screens) sliding right to left at about 14 fps with a faint scanline read as documentary rather than slideshow. Cheap in ffmpeg, no AI needed.
8. **The before/after card, with real footage.** Raw Osmo clip in black and white beside the finished cut, in sync, is their best stopper and it is honest for us: raw bay footage vs the cut, or a raw casting vs the machined part. The lockup goes where their CTA pill sits; nothing else burned in.

Do not take: comment gates, AI portraits or AI "clients", mascots, "Opus cooked" headers, fear copy ("editors are taking your clients"), unverifiable testimonials, borrowed Apple or Netflix trade dress.
