## Why it got 3,000 comments on 3,500 followers

1. **The comments are a gate, not a conversation.** Caption: "DM 'Ai' and I'll send over the prompt and resources." The count (3,029 comments to 3,044 likes, almost one to one) is the signature of a keyword funnel: people type the word, an automation replies. Reach came from the comment velocity. This is the growth mechanic the house already declines (no follow gates; "Comment INTERESTED" allowed at most one post in four, in the caption, for a genuinely free deliverable). It works, it is allowed by Instagram, and it buys comments, not community.
2. **The title is a tool fight.** "0% Higgsfield, 100% Claude" is for the AI-video crowd, who argue about exactly this, and the Claude logo is the brand hook. The viewer who stops is a maker, not a plumber; the plumber is the pretext.
3. **The screen is filmed, not screen-recorded.** A phone pointed at a laptop in a dark room, keyboard glow in the bottom third, slight handheld drift. It says "this is real, I just made it, look at my screen." A clean export of the same animation would read as an ad. The imperfection is the proof.
4. **The story shape is the house shape.** Trouble in sentence one that a stranger understands (missed call up a ladder), the system revealed in order, one node per breath, the payoff and the title line held to the last four seconds, hard cut, no outro, no spoken ask.
5. **Specific, small, trade-real details.** "Plumber near me", 07:00 to 21:00, 4 quotes to write, a review reply. The HUD looks sci-fi; the words are a Tuesday.
6. **One voice, no music.** The VO carries it; the visuals amplify (Jun Yuh's rule from earlier today).

## What transfers to El Coyote

- **Film the screen when the kit renders something.** When a RivalReel or CardReel render exists (the receipts, the price slate, the parts-counter menu), the fast way to post it without it reading as a graphic is Jacob's phone pointed at his own laptop or the shop PC after hours, dark room, one lamp, keyboard glow in frame, the render playing. Real footage of a real screen keeps the real-footage rule honest while letting the motion tier out of the artboard. This is also how Tudor's parked animation idea would ship if it ever does: never as a clean export, always as a thing on a screen in the shop.
- **Your night laptop is a set.** The after-hours parts room with the monitor on is the same frame he used. Kitchen table, laptop, a 7.3 part on the desk beside it.
- **Nine nodes, one hub is a reusable diagram for Rival.** "Everything the truck needs, one build" with the hub as the F600 and the nodes as drivetrain, fuel, cooling, brakes, wiring, body, interior, tires, paint. It is honest while barely any parts exist: every node starts dark and lights up as a part arrives. That is the monthly check-in graphic, filmed off the screen, and it only ever shows what is true.
- **Trouble a stranger has, in sentence one, with the trade detail.** "Every call you miss up a ladder" is the parts-counter equivalent of "the truck that came back three times". The counter has these lines; the talking-head page already asks for them.
- **Title as a claim about the making.** "100% my own footage", "edited on the kitchen table", "zero AI" is a hook for Jacob's crowd the way "100% Claude" is for Tom's. Say how it was made when the making is the differentiator.

## What does not transfer

- The comment gate at that ratio. One in four posts, caption only, real deliverable, is the ceiling. A 1:1 comment-to-like count is a funnel, and the account is building a community, not a list.
- Sci-fi HUD styling on everything. The house look is paper, serif caps, tan accent, plain captions. Borrow the structure (hub, nodes, one lit at a time), not the glow.
- Targeting a different audience than the one you address. He speaks to plumbers to be seen by AI people. Jacob speaks to truck people to be seen by truck people.
