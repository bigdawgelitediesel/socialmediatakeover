## Why it worked (36K likes on a 73 s tutorial)

1. **The title is a number a creator cannot ignore.** "20 times PER DAY" on frame 0, held four seconds, before her first sentence lands. It is a flat claim, not a question.
2. **Credential in sentence one, spoken.** "Six years." Then the method is named in two coined words so it can be repeated and commented on. A name makes a method shareable.
3. **The lesson is demonstrated in the place it happens.** She teaches framing by pointing at the sky she is about to use as a background. The whip pans are not sloppiness; they are her looking for the shot, which is the subject.
4. **Low status on purpose.** "I'm not dressed up, I look like what I look like." It makes the method look available to anyone.
5. **A real tally.** "15 clips in five minutes" and "40 reels" are the receipts. Two small numbers next to the big one (20 a day, 2M).
6. **Part 2 withheld.** The method has two halves and she only teaches the first. The cliffhanger is the follow ask without saying follow.
7. **Caption does the long version.** The caption repeats the method in prose, credits the person who put her onto it, and asks whether it was helpful. Comments were 567, which is the part 2 demand.

## The method itself, stated plainly

- **Situationmaxxing:** wherever she is, she looks for a background that will hold text: a solid dark sky, a lit wall, colored light. She films 10 to 15 short B-roll clips of herself standing, walking, turning, reacting, in five minutes, with the text she will add later already in mind. They go into one album called "trial reels".
- **Content roulette (part 2, not yet posted):** pair those clips with different text hooks and music, make 30 to 50 trial reels in a day, post them as Trial Reels (non-followers only), keep what the numbers pick.
- The framing rule is the whole craft: subject low or to one side, a quiet field above or beside her for the words. The clip itself is not the content; the text is the content and the clip is the plate.

## What transfers to El Coyote, and what does not

**Transfers.**
- **A standing B-roll bank, shot in the shop playground.** Jacob already has the sets: the bays after hours, the counter, the parts room shelving, the lot at dusk, Chief's cab, the F600 in its bay. The shop at night under the bay lights is her car wash: dark field, a few hard lights, a vehicle. Ten clips of him walking the bay, leaning on the counter, pulling a part off the shelf, turning to the lens, each 6 to 10 s, framed with a quiet upper third. Shot once a week in five minutes, before leaving. The album name on his phone: "trial reels".
- **Frame for the text that comes later.** Every B-roll clip is shot with the words already in mind: the title sentence in the upper third, chest band captions below. Face in the lower two thirds, nothing busy where the type goes. This is the same rule the talking-head page gives for the dash mount, now extended to walking shots.
- **The loop and question slots get cheaper.** The AM loop (6 to 12 s) and the question clip (9 to 25 s) are exactly a plate plus a line. A bank of plates means the morning post is a text decision, not a shoot. One hook, three plates, or one plate, three hooks.
- **Trial Reels as the testing bench, at a volume he can sustain.** She posts 20 a day. Jacob is a one-man show with a full day; 20 is worthless advice for him. The honest version: three trial reels on a night he has plates, the same line three ways or three lines on one plate, winner to the feed. That matches what Jun Yuh's and Nate Gaffney's reels said (rules 102 and 106). Confirm the Trial toggle in the composer first; it may already be open at this follower count.
- **Teach it from inside the moment.** When he explains anything on camera, do it where it happens: framing explained while standing in the bay he is about to film in, parts knowledge explained with the part in hand. This is also why the F600 check-in should be filmed in front of it, not described from Chief's seat.
- **Name the method.** Two words, his own, that a commenter can repeat. He has candidates already ("find the gratitude", "parts guy rules"); the naming habit is the point.
- **The two-part withhold.** When a lesson has two halves, post the first and name the second. Series packaging again, from the other side.

**Does not transfer.**
- Volume: 20 a day is her job; his ceiling is two posts a day plus a handful of trials on bank nights.
- Fit checks and walking outfit clips: his version is the walk through the bay, the part on the counter, the key turning.
- Caption style: her small centred white sans is the trial-reel template look. House captions stay Poppins SemiBold 68, chest band, no stroke, one lockup. The upper third stays clear for the title sentence.
- Her B-roll is wordless and gets its meaning from text later. His talking heads stay voice first. The bank serves the morning slots and inserts; it does not replace the daily seat.
