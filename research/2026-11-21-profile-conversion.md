# Profile conversion: turning reel viewers into followers (Nov 2026 update)

Research run 2026-11-21 for @elcoyotediesel (583 followers at the time of the pull, Creator account, Cheyenne WY). This updates the "Profile rebuild" section of `playbook/00-content-plan.md` (written Sept 2026) with November 2026 evidence. Where the Sept plan still holds, the line below says SAME. Where it changes, it says NEW and gives the reason.

The gate, restated: in the October viral wave, 38 percent of profile visitors followed. The profile already converts once someone arrives. The job is to get more people from the reel to the name, and to make sure the profile they land on tells them what tomorrow's post will be.

**Access note that changes how this repo does research.** `WebFetch` on `instagram.com/<handle>/` works again as of today (CLAUDE.md says it died 2026-10-17). A logged-out fetch returns the name field, follower and following counts, the first lines of the bio, the external link, highlight titles, and the date plus auto-generated alt text of the first 12 grid tiles. It does not return captions, cover images, post counts, or pinned markers. Private accounts and a few profiles come back as images only. Everything in the account table below came from that method plus Google snippets; nothing was viewed logged in.

### What the table says

1. **The accounts that grew post video with a person in it. The ones that stalled post photos of a truck.** Four 7.3 or OBS single-truck pages under 5K (@retrocoal, @obsworktruck, @c10united, @sicario67c10) all share the same profile: truck-named, photo or build-clip only, no link, no face, no premise beyond "my build". @truck_transformation_ has the identical premise to @retrocoal (grandpa's truck) and 96K followers; the bio says "Watch the journey unfold" and the highlights are "Just Me", "Polls", "My Followers".
2. **Nobody in the growing set uses a "Name | keyword" name field.** They use a human name (Dustin Bishop, Dillon Houck, Dylan McCool, Dalton Summitt) or the truck's or brand's name (Low Buck C10, Miranda the OBS Ford F350 7.3 Powerstroke, Doc's Diesel). The human-name accounts are all YouTube-fed, where the name already carries search weight. For an account with no YouTube, the truck-or-brand name with the engine in it (Miranda's) is the closer analog. @elcoyotediesel already runs "Jake Dalton | Diesel Trucks", which is more keyworded than any account in the table.
3. **Line 1 of the bio is one sentence that states the premise.** "Restoring my grandpa's truck after he passed." "I work on cars." "Sharing funny Memes!!! 1972 C10 Build!!!" "Diesel truck maintenance made easy." The shop pages that read like a Yellow Pages listing (Raven, 805) sit under 6K.
4. **Every growing reel account's grid tiles carry text.** Instagram's alt text flags text on 100 percent of the top six tiles for @dusty.build, @polebarngarage, @squarebodyoffroad, @docsdiesel, @dylanmccool, @c10talk and @lowbuckc10. On @elcoyotediesel's current top six the alt text reads "meme of car", "image of generator", "image of blower", "selfie of one or more people, beard, hat", none flagged as text. The stalled shop pages are also text-free. This is the single clearest visual difference between the growing set and this account.
5. **Pins are moments, not best-ER posts.** Dillon Houck pins his two SEMA weeks. Truck Transformation pins an origin clip. Doc's pins one evergreen. Nobody pins a thank-you.
6. **Highlights are chapters or trucks, never "about me" labels.** The OBS, The C10, Ole Red; The Start, Early Days, SEMA 24'; Truck, Just Me. Product-navigation highlights (Brakes, Filters, Turbos) belong to shops selling parts.
7. **Link: linktr.ee in 5 of 10, YouTube in 3, own domain in 2.** The two with their own domain are the shop (805diesel.com) and the build page (sicario67.com). A build portal is a legitimate, rare choice.
8. **The meme-plus-build mix has a working precedent at 20K.** @lowbuckc10's bio says it in one line and the grid is almost entirely memes with the build threaded in. That is the account's lane, written down by someone who got there first.

## 2. Covers, grid, bio and name field: 2026 evidence

**Grid mechanics (settled).**
- Profile grid tiles have been 3:4 since January 2025; a 9:16 reel cover is center-cropped, losing roughly 240 px top and bottom at 1080 wide (Planoly puts the displayed cover at 1013x1350; Hootsuite says keep 480 px top and bottom clear of text and logos). https://www.planoly.com/blog/guide-to-instagrams-new-vertical-grid , https://blog.hootsuite.com/instagram-reels-cover/ , https://nealschaffer.com/instagram-post-size/
- "Adjust Preview" lets you pan or Fit/Fill a post's crop inside the tile after posting; sources disagree on whether it launched March 2025 or February 2026 and on whether it works on reel covers (one guide says you can scrub to a frame, Instagram's reels help says the cover cannot be edited after upload). Choose the cover before posting. https://metricool.com/edit-grid-instagram/ , https://almcorp.com/blog/instagram-thumbnail-editing-profile-grid/
- "Reorder Grid" (long-press a tile, drag) rolled out June 2026, visible to everyone immediately, pins stay on top. https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/technology/news/story/instagram-finally-gives-you-control-over-your-profile-grid-heres-how-535728-2026-06-09 , https://www.hopperhq.com/blog/how-to-rearrange-instagram-grid/
- Pins: three per profile across posts and reels. A "six for Plus subscribers" claim appears in one secondary source only. https://brandid.app/blog/how-to-pin-a-post-on-instagram/ , https://www.reeldrop.io/learn/meta-one-subscription-explained
- Highlights are being moved from the row of circles into a dedicated tab (heart icon), with some accounts showing both; the rollout is not universal as of November 2026. Do not over-invest in highlight cover art until this settles. https://www.planoly.com/blog/instagram-is-moving-story-highlights-to-the-grid , https://www.brandvm.com/post/instagram-dedicated-tab-for-story-highlights
- Links: five native bio links free; Meta One subscribers get 20 plus images and titles. Clickable links inside posts and reels are paywalled (Meta One Advanced and up). Bio links and Story link stickers remain free. https://www.meta.com/help/subscriptions/29142271265407659/ , https://www.manylinks.io/blog/instagram-paid-links-meta-one

**Cover style: face vs truck vs text.** No head-to-head 2026 data exists. One vendor claims text covers get 23 to 37 percent more taps, another claims posts without text overlays get 60 percent more interactions per view; neither names a source. The only face data is the 2014 Georgia Tech / Yahoo study on photos, not reels. What is defensible: Metricool and Hootsuite both say a short title (3 to 4 words) in the middle band gives a grid scroller a reason to tap and gives search something to read; and the account table above shows 7 of 7 growing reel accounts carry text on every tile. Treat it as the niche norm, not a proven lift. https://skysnail.io/blog/instagram-reel-cover-design-2026 , https://www.fanpagekarma.com/insights/instagram-text-overlays/ , https://metricool.com/instagram-reels-guide/

**Bio formulas (Later, Buffer, Hootsuite, Sprout, Aug to Nov 2026).**
- Buffer (Aug 21 2026, built on 52M posts across 200K accounts): "treat the name field as a search field", add one or two keywords, do not stuff; the bio should give a category, one line on what you do, personality, and "a reason to stay"; pin "your best work or a reel that answers your most common questions"; make the profile photo recognizable at thumbnail size. No profile-specific numbers. https://buffer.com/resources/grow-on-instagram/
- Hootsuite: bio 150 characters including emoji, name field 30 characters and searchable, "in 2026, Instagram search leans heavily on the words in your name field and bio". Checklist: who you are, what you do, where you are, contact, timely update, a CTA, no typos. https://blog.hootsuite.com/instagram-bio-ideas-business/
- Later: name field is name plus one keyword (their example: a chef with "vegan recipes"); stylized Unicode fonts break screen readers, keep them off the bio. https://later.com/blog/how-to-make-a-good-impression-with-your-instagram-bio/
- Sprout: creator bios follow "[Niche] Creator" plus a contact line; a Charleston photographer ranks with "Charleston Wedding Photographer" as the profile name. https://sproutsocial.com/insights/instagram-bios-for-businesses/
- Brock Johnson (repo digest, 2026-09-15): name field 56 characters, searchable on Instagram and Google, "the optimized Instagram profile ... is actually going to convert people from just a viewer into an actual follower"; the bio's searchability contradicted across his own videos. Note the 30 vs 56 character discrepancy between Hootsuite and Brock; the current name field is 27 characters and works either way, test the limit in the app before committing to a longer one.

**Is the name field still indexed?** Yes by every 2026 source, none of them official. The last official statement is Mosseri's August 2021 keyword-search post (usernames, bios, captions indexed; comments not). No 2026 announcement changes it, and Hootsuite, Buffer and the SEO vendors all still treat name field first, bio second. https://blog.hootsuite.com/social-media-updates/instagram/instagram-search-seo/ , https://www.toptal.com/creator/post/instagram-seo

## 3. In-reel mechanics: what makes a viewer tap the name

**No controlled 2026 study exists.** Every number below is vendor-reported and unverified; the useful part is the direction, which matches this account's own data.

- **Follower counts still matter, by Instagram's own account.** Mosseri, Colin and Samir interview recorded Oct 23 2026: "We want to make sure that having a follower count on Instagram means something even in a recommendations first world." Geekout, week of Nov 13 2026: Mosseri said "Following counts still matter on Instagram more than they do on platforms like TikTok." The Insights redesign spotted in November sorts content by follows. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sxizdQ2hRfk , https://geekout.mattnavarra.com/p/eu-addiction-rules-tiktok-shop-subscription-meta-muse-uk-launch-report
- **The series promise is the follow reason, and Instagram is building a product around it.** Linked Reels were removed Oct 28 2026; the separate Series format (grouped episodes in a profile tab, viewers can save a series to "stay up to date") is in international test with a US test expected early 2027. Tessa Lyons (VP Product) called the series question "a goldmine" on Rachel Karten's Nov 3 call. A "Road to SEMA 2027" series is exactly what the product is for. https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/02/meta-tests-series-for-episodic-reels-on-instagram-and-facebook/ , https://www.linkinbio.news/p/my-call-with-instagrams-vp-of-product
- **Vendor benchmarks for the funnel.** Reel to profile tap: 1.5 to 3.5 percent "healthy". Profile visit to follow: 13.5 percent average across 50K accounts (one vendor), 20 to 40 percent "with strong proof assets" (another). Series CTA 0.5 to 1.2 percent view-to-follow vs about 0.1 percent for "follow for more" (unsourced). Against these, this account's 38 percent visit-to-follow is at the top of the claimed range, and its reach-to-follow (0.037 percent in Sept) is far below. The hole is the tap, not the profile. https://www.wavevision.io/blog/instagram-reel-gets-likes-but-no-followers-fix-the-profile-visit-gap , https://creatide.ai/blog/views-but-no-followers-fix-the-social-conversion-gap , https://growcreator.pro/instagram-views-not-converting-to-followers
- **Tease the next part without saying "follow".** The consistent advice across the vendor set and Brock Johnson: name the series and the next episode in the reel and again in a pinned comment; a "part 2" reference gives a specific reason to open the profile now. Brock's one-man-shop version from the repo digest still holds: do not pre-announce a long series, retroactively call the winner "part one". https://creatorflow.so/blog/instagram-content-series-strategy/ , research/2026-09-15-brock-hooks-formats-digest.md section 8.1
- **Pinned comment converts, does not reach.** Lilach Bullock: pinning does not boost distribution; a pinned comment that carries context or social proof does more for conversion than a caption line. Metricool: reels pull most comments in the first hour, so pin early; up to three pins per post. Brock's Oct 26 posting-day routine includes "pinned comment, reply to everything". Buffer's dataset: replying to comments lifts engagement 21 percent. https://www.lilachbullock.com/how-to-pin-a-comment-on-instagram/ , https://metricool.com/pinned-comments-instagram/ , https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BsVFpdNCZhA
- **Face plus truck in frame.** No platform data on face vs no face in reels (one 2026 guide says so outright). This account's own numbers do the work: Harley Trade (a person talking, truck behind) ran 37.8 percent skip; every Rival reel that opened on a part or a thank-you ran 59 to 75 percent. The live pull's line stands: "Rival content can win when the first 2 seconds are a person saying something."
- **What the account's own data adds.** Memes share at 1.5 to 2.2 percent sends per reach and bring the younger, broader viewer; build reels do not share. Pins and bio therefore have to do two jobs at once: tell the meme arrival that this is also a build worth following, and tell the build arrival that the next part is coming.
