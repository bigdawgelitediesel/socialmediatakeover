# Truck cinematography and stills for Rival: how the best classic-truck accounts shoot, and what one man copies with an iPhone and a Pocket 3 in Wyoming light

Date 2026-11-21. Adds to section 6 of `2026-11-21-camera-hooks-and-moves.md` (the shop moves, 34 named) and section 3 of `2026-11-20-EDITING-MASTER-PLAYBOOK.md` (the five-shot sequence and the settings block). This file is the exterior half: the truck as the subject, outdoors, in Wyoming, with no second person. Nothing here needs a drone, a chase car or an operator.

Subject: 1959 Ford F600, Coyote Tan body, gloss black on everything else, no chrome, black 22.5 singles, twin 5-inch stacks, saddle leather. Two finishes that want opposite light: tan is shaped by direct light and shadow, gloss black is shaped by what it reflects. Most of this file is about serving both in one frame.

Research method and limits: 24 Instagram profiles read logged out (counts below are from 2026-11-21), 15 clips downloaded (11 Instagram, 4 YouTube Shorts) into `reel-references/2026-11-21-truck-cinematography/` and run through watch.py; the sheets are read in section 1. The web-search budget ran out mid-session, so a few items are marked unverified and left for a phone call. No Hoonigan shooter, Westen Champlin shooter or "Tom's Refurb" shooter could be identified; those three are noted as gaps, not filled with guesses.

## 0. The twelve findings

1. The top restomod shops do not shoot "car cinematography" on Instagram any more. ICON, Ringbrothers, Kindig and Velocity post mostly stills carousels, episode teasers and shop content; the pure beauty reel is rarer than expected and when it runs it is a product listing (ICON's 48-second Defender walkaround: 205 likes at 220K followers). The account that actually shoots trucks for the feed is Fat Fender Garage (377K): five reels in one week, all trucks, all 9:16, all shot by their own people. Copy Fat Fender, not Ringbrothers.
2. The two best numbers in the downloaded set are not beauty reels. Fat Fender's "one click at a time" (10.5K likes) is 37 seconds of a hand doing one thing to the truck with real sound: a knob, a 4WD dial, a cup holder, a tailgate latch. @classicridesonly's 39K-like clip is a nine-second handheld half-orbit of the Kindig CF1 at walking pace, no cut, no title. Hands-on detail with sound, and one short smooth move around a finished truck, beat everything else by an order of magnitude.
3. The recurring exterior shots across all of them are seven: low front three-quarter, flat profile, rolling (rig or drive-by), detail macro, interior from the door opening, the light-sweep or reflection shot on dark paint, and the reveal (door, cover, or light). The drone orbit appears only on the big-budget accounts and is not needed. The one failure mode every account shares is the parked walkaround in noon sun.
4. Every solo-shootable exterior frame in the set is either locked on a tripod or mounted on the truck. The handheld pans and the head-on rollers are crew shots. That is the whole division of labour for one man: tripod, suction cup, or the Pocket 3 tracking.
5. Height is the whole trick. For a truck, the hero wide is shot with the lens between the hub and the hood line, never at eye level. The pros move the camera up only for the profile, and then only to kill sky reflections in dark paint.
6. The Pocket 3 lens is 20 mm equivalent. Within 2 m of the truck it draws the front end like a monster-truck poster, which is right for one shot (the low front three-quarter) and wrong for every other. Stand back 3 to 5 m and use the 2x tap for proportion shots. On the iPhone the same split is 1x for the low hero, 2x for everything that has to look like the truck Jacob built.
7. Exposure rule for the two-tone: expose the tan body to sit bright but not clipped and let the black fall. Never lift the black in post. The gloss black doubles as the in-frame grey card: if it shows a colour cast, the white balance is wrong. The pros keep dark trucks against bright plain backgrounds (Fat Fender's white industrial wall) and warm paint against black with one side source (Velocity's dark studio).
8. Gloss black reflects whatever is at the mirror angle. Low camera: it mirrors the sky and goes grey-blue. Camera at hood height looking slightly down: it mirrors the ground and goes black. A CPL at 90 degrees to the sun cuts the sky out of the paint. Pick one per shot on purpose.
9. Winter in Cheyenne is the best truck-shooting light of the year. The sun never climbs above 27 degrees in December, so the whole day is side-light, and sunset sits at 4:31 to 4:40 pm with a 30-minute civil-twilight window after it. Golden hour is at 3:30 pm. One man can shoot the entire ladder of light before dinner.
10. Blue hour (the 30 minutes after sunset) is the one window where both finishes work at once: the west sky becomes a softbox the black reflects cleanly, and the tan still holds its warmth. The pros' "liquid black" shots are blue hour or a long soft light, nothing else.
11. Solo rolling shots exist and are safe: the suction or tow-hook rig shot (camera on the truck, Mimo as the remote, 15 to 25 mph on an empty road; Fat Fender's own drive video is three suction positions in rotation) and the drive-through past a tripod. Panning rollers from a chase car are out and nothing fakes them.
12. For the grid: shoot stills 4:3 native, post 4:5, keep the truck inside the centre 3:4 band; the profile grid shows a 3:4 tile (center 1080x1440 of a reel cover, the middle 1012 px of a 4:5 post). Three stills per build update: the low hero, the proof macro, the human-scale frame. Plus the one locked "progress profile" from the same taped spot, every time.

### 1b. The seven recurring exterior shots, and how the pros handle dark versus tan paint

| Shot | How it recurs | Dark truck handling | Tan truck handling | Two-tone (Rival) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low front three-quarter | Lens between hub and hood line, 45 degrees off the nose, wide lens close | Sky in the hood is accepted (it is the "liquid" look); the sides go dark because they mirror ground | Side-light from 60 to 90 degrees so the body panels and the fender crown separate; flat front light kills tan | Sun low and 60 to 90 degrees off the nose, camera on the shadow side of the sun line so the tan gets shape and the black bumper, grille shell and wheels mirror dark ground |
| Flat profile | Camera at hub-to-hood height, 2x lens, 8 to 12 m back, truck exactly side-on | Camera slightly above hood line so the doors mirror ground, not sky; CPL if sky still shows | Overcast or open shade: tan reads truest with no hot spot | The profile is the "progress" frame; shoot it in open shade or 20 minutes after sunset so neither finish fights |
| Rolling shot | Rig mount on the subject car, or chase car (not available solo) | Blur hides reflections; 1/30 to 1/60 shutter | Same | Rig shot off the bed side or door, 1/60 at 30 fps, ND |
| Detail macro | 2x, 40 cm, locked off, one light source | A single soft highlight line across the black; never a point light | Texture: side raking light across the paint, a thread, a weld, a stitch | The black-on-tan edge (fender lip, bed rail, door jamb) is Rival's signature macro |
| Interior from the door | Camera in the open door at seat height, 1x, door frame as the frame | Expose for the leather, let the window blow if it must | | Saddle leather against black: expose for the leather, drop 0.3 EV, warm WB locked |
| Light-sweep / reflection | A long soft light (tube, or the bay door edge) drawn along the body at night or blue hour | This is the shot dark paint lives for | On tan it reads as a warm wash, fine but not the point | Sweep the black first (bumpers, wheels, stacks, frame), then pan up into the tan |
| Reveal | Bay door rolling up, cover pulled, lights coming on | | | Door reveal at blue hour with the shop lights off: the sky lights the truck, the bay is a black frame |

## 3. Settings block: iPhone and Pocket 3, exterior

Keeps every rule already in the playbook (4K60 HEVC, HDR Video OFF, Lock Camera and Lock White Balance on, AE/AF lock, 1x no pinch zoom, Pocket 3 Normal 10-bit as the colour reference, manual WB, sharpness -1, ND to hold the shutter). Adds the exterior layer.

### Pocket 3 (the primary exterior video camera)

- Mode: Normal 10-bit, not D-Log M, not HLG. House rule is no grade, and the 2x lossless tap only works in Normal.
- Frame rate: 4K60 for anything that may slow down; 4K30 for rig rollers so the shutter can sit at 1/60 and the wheels blur. 4K120 only for locked inserts (spin-down, dust).
- Shutter: 180 degrees. 1/120 at 60 fps, 1/60 at 30 fps. DJI's own rollers short uses 1/30 at 4K for heavier blur; that is a choice for the rig shots only.
- ISO: 50, manual. Everything else is ND.
- ND maths at f/2 fixed, ISO 50: full winter sun on a clean lot needs about 5 stops to hold 1/120 at 60 fps (ND32) and 6 stops to hold 1/60 at 30 fps (ND64); add one more stop on snow (ND64 to ND128); overcast needs ND8; golden hour needs ND4 to ND8 or just open the shutter one notch; blue hour needs none. Carry ND8, ND16 (or 32) and ND64 as the working set; every Pocket 3 magnetic set on the market covers that range. A CPL for the Pocket 3 exists (Freewell and others) and is the one filter that fixes the gloss black sky problem; treat it as a one-stop ND too.
- White balance: locked per light, never auto. 5600 K sun, 6500 K overcast or open shade, 5000 to 5600 K at blue hour if the sky should stay blue (it should), 4200 K under shop tubes. Do not change mid-shot. Reference: the gloss black panel on screen must look neutral; if it is warm or green, the WB is wrong, not the paint.
- Exposure: expose the tan body at 60 to 70 percent of the histogram, never clipped; let the black fall; the sky may touch 90 to 95 percent but not clip. Zebra at 100 on. Drop 0.3 EV if in doubt; it protects the tan and the sky together.
- Gimbal: Follow for pushes, Tilt Lock for slides and orbits, Lock for anything where the truck moves and the camera does not (bay door, drive-throughs). FPV never on the exterior.
- ActiveTrack: double-tap the truck for orbits and drive-throughs. It is not available with 4K120 or in Low-Light, so those are locked-off shots. Lock drops on fast direction changes; start wide.
- Lens: 20 mm equivalent. Under 2 m from the truck it distorts: right for shot 1, wrong for 3, 4, 9. Use the 2x tap (tap before rolling, Normal mode only) for proportion shots and details; it costs nothing at 1080 delivery.
- Focus: 0.2 m minimum. For the macro set the camera 40 cm away and use 2x (hooks file #31).
- Mounting: SmallRig or equivalent double suction cup for rollers, tethered with a lanyard to the bed rail; test at walking pace, then 15 mph, then 25. Tow-hook or receiver mount for the low forward view. Mimo on the phone is the monitor and the record button; set up parked, then drive.
- Low light: Low-Light Video mode for the light sweep if ISO 800 is not enough, but it disables tracking and softens; prefer more light.
- Firmware before 2026-12-31 (playbook item 14).

### iPhone (stills, the second video angle, rollers from the tripod)

- Native Camera app for stills and for the quick reel angle; Blackmagic Camera (free) when the clip will cut against Pocket 3 footage outdoors, because it gives manual shutter (set 180 degrees), locked ISO at base, locked WB with a tint control, false colour and zebras, and HEVC 10-bit Rec.709 without Log. No Log, no ProRes: the house rule is no grade, and Instagram re-encodes to 4 to 6 Mbps anyway (playbook, delivery section).
- Resolution and rate: 4K60 HEVC. 4K120 (16 Pro and 17 Pro) for locked inserts if the phone is a Pro.
- Lens: 1x for the low hero and the interior from the door; 2x for the profile, the three-quarter proportion frame, the hood rake and all details. 0.5x only overhead or inside the cab. On a 48 MP main camera the 2x is a 12 MP centre crop, which is more than Instagram's 1080 width needs. Lock Camera on so it never switches lenses mid-take.
- Exposure: long-press AE/AF lock, then drag down 0.3 to 0.7 EV; expose for the tan. Turn on the grid and the level. HDR Video off stays.
- ND and CPL on the phone: a magnetic filter frame (Moment, SmallRig, Freewell, PolarPro all make one for current iPhones) with a CPL and an ND16 or a VND. The CPL is the filter that matters for the two-tone; rotate until the sky leaves the black panels and stop a quarter turn early so the body still has a highlight line. Do not use magnetic filters on the suction rig (SmallRig's own warning: vibration throws them).
- Stills: shoot 4:3 native, 48 MP when the phone offers it, ProRAW or an app RAW (Halide, Blackmagic) when the light is mixed, HEIF otherwise. Keep the exposure 0.3 under for tan in sun.
- Action mode only for walking b-roll on the wide lens (already in the hooks file). Cinematic mode never on the truck; it edges the stacks and the mirrors badly.
- Audio: the DJI Mic 2 pairs to the Pocket 3; for any iPhone line to camera outdoors use the Mic 2 receiver on the phone or re-record the line inside. Wyoming wind defeats every built-in mic; a dead cat on the Mic 2 is mandatory outside.

### Exposing a tan body against a Wyoming sky

- The sky is the problem, not the tan. At 7,200 ft in winter the sky is deep blue and bright; auto exposure sees the sky and buries the truck, then auto WB sees the warm tan and cools the frame, and the tan goes grey. Lock both.
- Order of operations: lock WB for the light, lock exposure on the tan with the truck filling a third of the frame, recompose, then check that the black bumper is neutral and the sky has texture.
- In golden hour the tan will go orange. Keep it. It is the one hour the paint is supposed to look like that, and it is the hour the pros shoot. Correct toward neutral only in overcast and blue hour.
- Snow: expose for the truck, zebras at 100 for the snow, accept snow at 95 percent. Snow lights the undercarriage and the black frame from below, which is the reason to shoot the chassis shots in snow.
- Blue hour: keep WB at 5600 K so the sky stays blue and the black mirrors blue; the tan reads slightly cool, which is correct for that window. Warmer WB here turns the black panels brown.

## 4. Rolling shots, light painting and reflections, solo

### Rolling shots without a chase car

1. **Rig shot** (the pros' solo answer, also DJI's own tutorial and Mood House Media's Pocket 3 method): camera on the subject truck. Double suction cup (SmallRig) on the bed side, door skin or cowl; or a tow-hook mount, which Mood House calls the safest but gives one position. Tether to something structural. Mimo on the phone as the live view and record button. 4K30, 1/60 (DJI's short uses 1/30 for heavier blur), ND, WB locked. Drive 15 to 25 mph on an empty straight; back roads, not highways (Mackey: 25 to 40 mph on back roads gives detail, highways give motion; the F600 does not need motion). Three passes per position. Wind noise does not matter; the audio is replaced.
2. **Drive-through past a tripod** (Spota's "stationary phone" method): camera locked at hub height on a tripod at the roadside, truck drives through frame. Multiple passes at three distances. The edit cuts the entrance and the exit; a locked frame with a truck crossing it reads better on Instagram than a shaky pan.
3. **ActiveTrack drive-through**: Pocket 3 on the tripod, double-tap the parked truck, walk to it, drive the pass; the gimbal pans with the truck. Start wide so the lock holds.
4. Not available solo and not faked: car-to-car pans, out-the-window handheld, the two-phase composite (parked truck plus motion-blurred plate). The playbook's "never fake motion" rule stands (Mackey rule 6).
5. Locations for passes: industrial frontage roads after hours, the lot behind the shop (not named on camera), county roads east of town with no traffic, the Vedauwoo access road on a weekday. Nothing with pedestrians, nothing in a school zone, nothing above 25 mph with a camera on the body.

### Light painting with a work light, one man

- Stills method (Jalopnik, The Drive, Digital Photography School, SoCal Rollers, all agree): dark lot, every other light off, camera on a tripod, 10 to 15 s exposure, low ISO, a single steady pass along the truck with the light held high and parallel to the ground, one pass per surface (two passes blotch), stand behind the light's soft edge not its hotspot, dark clothing, start on the self-timer, keep yourself moving so you do not register. Composite 3 to 5 frames only if one frame cannot cover the truck.
- With Jacob's gear: the Pocket 3's photo shutter maxes at 1 s, so it cannot do the still. The iPhone's native camera cannot either; use NightCap or Spectre (both stack to a real long exposure), or shoot the pass as video (shot 15) and pull frames. The video version is the Instagram version anyway: a locked 4K30 clip at 1/30, ISO 400 to 800, the light walked along the body, then sped up in the edit so the highlight travels the truck in 3 s. The light: the Milwaukee M18 work light he already has, or a 4 ft LED tube (an Amaran T4c or a $40 hardware-store tube) for the longer, straighter highlight line the pros use on black.
- The truck must be clean and dry. Light painting shows every dust speck on black.

### Reflections on gloss black: kill or use

- Physics: gloss black is a mirror. It reads black only where it reflects something dark. Low camera: the body mirrors sky and goes grey. Camera at hood height looking slightly down: the body mirrors the ground and goes black. The hood always mirrors the sky.
- Kill: CPL rotated at 90 degrees to the sun (Kase: little effect with the sun behind or ahead); a dark background behind the camera (a treeline, the shop wall, the granite at Vedauwoo), not an open sky; shoot an hour after sunrise or just before sunset when the sky overhead is darker than the horizon; keep the shooter out of the paint (step to the side, zoom to 2x, never wear white).
- Keep: broad continuous reflections that follow the body shape describe the curves (Skylum, Kase both say do not turn the CPL to maximum). A black bumper with one clean highlight line is the look; a black bumper that is dead matte is a rendering.
- Use: blue hour turns the whole west sky into the soft source, the single best light for the black parts of Rival; a wet lot doubles the truck; a long tube light at night draws the body line; the bay door edge at dusk does the same indoors.
- On the tan: reflections barely register, so the tan needs the opposite, direct low light for shape. That is why the sunset sequence runs tan-first (golden hour, direct sun) and black-second (blue hour, reflected sky).

## 5. Stills for the grid and the cover frames

- Formats, measured (thumbnailpeak, 2026): the profile grid tile is 3:4 everywhere (311x414 desktop, 130x173 iPhone). A 1080x1920 reel cover shows rows 240 to 1680 in the grid; rows 480 to 1640 are never covered by any icon. A 4:5 feed photo (1080x1350) shows full in the feed and loses about 34 px per side in the 3:4 grid tile. Instagram accepts 3:4 photo uploads since the Jan 2025 grid change; a 3:4 post shows whole in both.
- Rule: shoot stills 4:3 native (the sensor's own shape, maximum pixels), crop to 4:5 for feed posts and keep the truck and any text inside the centre 3:4 band; reel covers get the title inside the centre 1080x1440 (already the playbook rule). Set the cover from the camera roll with a dedicated still, never a video frame; the cover cannot be changed after posting (Instagram Help, via thumbnailpeak).
- The three stills every build update needs:
  1. The low hero (shot 1 framing, stills version): 1x, hub height, truck filling two-thirds of the 4:5 frame, sun 60 to 90 degrees off the nose, 0.3 under. This is the cover and the carousel first frame.
  2. The proof macro: 2x, the piece of work that was done this week, raking light, the black-on-tan edge whenever it exists. This is the frame partners repost.
  3. The human scale: Jacob in frame, 2x from 8 to 10 m or the Pocket 3 self-timer on the fender, leaning, working, or walking away. This is the frame that converts followers (the account's own numbers: personal content at 14.8 percent ER).
  Plus, outside the carousel: the locked progress profile (shot 3) from the taped spot, same lens, same height, same time of day, every session, for the timeline reel at SEMA.
- Grid composition the top pages share: the truck's long axis runs corner to corner in the three-quarter frames, horizon at the lower third, more road than sky, and nothing cut by the frame edge except the ground. Velocity and ICON alternate a tight three-quarter with a wide "truck in a place" frame so the grid breathes; Fat Fender runs three details for every wide.
- Colour handling of Coyote Tan (a warm, low-saturation tan, not a grey and not a gold):
  - White balance for the light, never for the paint. Locked 5600 K in sun, 6500 K in shade and overcast, 5600 K at blue hour. Auto WB on a frame that is half tan cools the whole image and the tan goes grey; warm presets and golden light push it orange.
  - The neutral references are already on the truck: the gloss black and the glass. If the black has a cast, pull tint and temperature until it is neutral and the tan will be right.
  - Make a reference frame on the first clean day: the truck in open shade at noon, a grey card in frame, RAW. Every later edit gets checked against it. One frame, kept forever.
  - Edit order (garageapp, matches Jacob's no-grade rule): exposure, contrast, white balance, highlights and shadows, and saturation last and never more than 10 percent. On tan, drop highlights before raising shadows; raising shadows lifts the black and the truck goes flat.
  - RAW (ProRAW on a Pro, Halide or Blackmagic DNG otherwise, DNG on the Pocket 3) whenever sun and shade are both in frame; HEIF is fine in even light.
- Aspect of the Pocket 3 stills: 16:9 or 1:1 only (9.4 MP). Use it for video; the iPhone is the stills camera.
