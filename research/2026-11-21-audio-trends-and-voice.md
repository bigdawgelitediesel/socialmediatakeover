## 2. Voiceover craft for faceless or part-faceless reels

Pacing
- Default 150 wpm conversational; 165 to 180 for energetic faceless explainers; under 140 reads slow; over 190 the words smear, and AI voices in particular fall apart past 190. Word budgets: 15 s = 35 to 38 words (45 energetic), 30 s = 70 to 75 (85 to 90), 60 s = 140 to 150 (170 to 180). Hook = about 15 words inside the first 5 s; a 25-word hook is too slow. Leave 5 to 8 percent of runtime for pauses (3 to 5 s in a 60). Replace "and / but / because" with periods to create breath points. (ZeroSkill, Sept 9, 2026 https://zeroskillai.com/60-second-youtube-short-script-eguide-2026/ ; AudioPod https://audiopod.ai/blog/ai-voiceover-best-practices-faceless-youtube ; Faceless.so timer https://faceless.so/tools/script-timer)
- One breath per card, derived from the numbers above: at 150 to 165 wpm a card that holds 8 to 12 words plays for 2.9 to 4.8 s, which is exactly one spoken sentence and one breath. So: one sentence per card, period at the card change, card-in SFX on the first consonant of the next sentence, never mid-sentence. Our 12 to 18 s build reel = 4 to 5 cards = 30 to 45 words. (House rule, not from a source.)
- Recording method from a Nov 15, 2026 creator transcript (nissa after hours): write the script, record the voiceover in one sitting sentence by sentence, redo a stumbled sentence immediately, cut in CapCut. Her reels with voice got about twice the views of music-only reels on her near-100K Instagram this year (n=1, self-reported). She films on a DJI Osmo Pocket 3 with a cheap DJI mic, same gear as Jacob. (transcripts/2026-11-21-audio-faceless-voiceover-only-plan.txt)

Mic chains (Post Flow, Oct 23, 2026 https://thepostflow.com/production/audio/best-usb-and-xlr-mics-for-voiceover/)
- Budget that beats the phone: Samson Q2U (about $100, dynamic, USB + XLR, handles an untreated room) or ATR2100x-USB. Step up: Shure MV7+ ($255, USB-C + XLR, onboard leveling and plosive reduction in USB mode). SM7B needs 60 dB of clean gain, skip it.
- Dynamic mic 3 to 5 inches off the mouth in a soft room (closet, truck cab with the engine off, blanket over the head) beats a condenser in the shop. "A little treatment beats a lot of mic upgrade."
- If Jacob keeps using the DJI Mic with the Pocket 3: record VO in the truck cab, not the bay; the cab is a free vocal booth.

Processing (unchanged from yesterday's 5a chain, with one correction): high-pass 90 Hz > afftdn > de-ess 5 to 8 kHz > compressor about 3:1 > limiter, voice stem to -16 LUFS, program to -14 LUFS / -1.5 dBTP after AAC. One vendor guide (AudioPod) tells faceless channels to target -9 to -11 LUFS for TikTok/Reels; ignore it, Meta normalizes at playback (yesterday's Meta xHE-AAC source) and hotter masters just get squashed.

## 3. UI / foley layering for motion-graphics reels

What goes where (Videohead guide https://www.videohead.io/resources/motion-sfx-for-overlays ; Sonniss/GFX Sounds category naming)
- Card-in / text block entrance: one soft pop or soft hit, transient on the frame the scale peaks or the position settles.
- Chip-pop (small label/sticker): tiny UI tap or glass click, quieter than the card hit.
- Stamp (approval, "DECIDED", price tag slam): low thud + short click, 1 to 2 frames before the ink lands so the attack is felt on the frame.
- Strike (strikethrough, "NOT THIS"): short marker scribble or air texture across the strike, 150 to 250 ms.
- Iris / punch-in: whoosh starting 2 to 3 frames early (yesterday's rule), ending on the settle; for an iris-close use a reverse whoosh into a soft click.
- Counter / price ticking up: ticks and clicks per digit, below dialogue, never beside it.
- Rules: one hero sound per beat (never pop + whoosh on the same frame), scale sound energy to motion energy, keep decorative, looping and under-dialogue overlays SILENT, and where a hit lands duck the music a couple of dB and bring it back.

Levels relative to voice
- Voice peaks around -6 to -3 dBFS, SFX peaks around -14 to -20 dBFS, i.e. UI hits 10 to 14 dB under the voice and bigger cinematic hits 6 to 8 dB under (bsahala https://bsahala.com/blog/video-editing-sound-effects-sfx-guide/en/ ; videohead). Yesterday's "6 dB under" stands for whoosh/impact; UI pops and ticks go another 6 dB down. Test on a phone speaker, not headphones.

- VideoEditingSFX: 100 sounds, 19 UI (Pop Sound, Click Button, Swipe, Chime, Success, Hover, Notification...), "released under a CC0 (public-domain) license," individual MP3 no signup, full pack 48 kHz / 24-bit WAV. https://videoeditingsfx.com/free-ui-sound-effects/ and https://videoeditingsfx.com/sfx/
- CreatorAssets swoosh pack: 11 motion-graphics swooshes, CC0, one zip. https://creatorassets.com/audio/swoosh/

- StoryCut: 213 CC0 sounds incl. pop/whoosh/click. https://www.storycut.com/sound-effects

- Edits app has a built-in SFX library (100+, categories incl. transitions, cinematic, mechanical, alerts) with a save button; fine for a Jacob-finished reel, but keep our ffmpeg renders on the CC0 packs so the file is clean before it reaches IG. (Edits audio guide transcript, May 2026)

Do top creators bother, given muted viewing? No measured study isolates UI SFX; the nearest thing is a proposed A/B on GitHub, not a result. Practice guidance converges on: two or three SFX per reel, on the hook text, the reveal and the last card; everything else silent. Our stance: build the SFX layer because the sound-on half of viewers (section 5) is also the half most likely to share, and keep it so light that the muted half loses nothing.

## 4. Music under voice on Instagram in 2026

- In-app vs baked in: adding the song in-app (reel editor > audio > pick track > drag to the section > two sliders, original audio 0 to 100 and song 0 to 100) is the only way to get the song page, the "Use audio" button and the trending arrow on our reel. Baked-in music gets attributed as "Original audio - elcoyotediesel" and is exposed to copyright matching with no song page. Multitrack (up to 20 tracks, each with its own volume, since July 2024) means voiceover + song + SFX can all be set in-app, and the resulting mix is saved as a reusable audio attributed to us. (Touch To Click, Nov 17, 2026 transcript; TechCrunch https://techcrunch.com/2024/07/16/instagram-now-allows-users-to-add-multiple-audio-tracks-to-reels ; Hollyland https://www.hollyland.com/blog/tips/keep-original-audio-on-reels-with-music)
- Ducking defaults: Instagram's reel editor has no auto-ducking, only the two static sliders. Edits has Volume Ducking: tap the music track > Volume ducking > "detect voiceover" or "voice in video" > it drops the music to 20 percent whenever speech is detected, adjustable (30 percent is the common nudge up); the voiceover tool also has Enhance and Reduce noise toggles and fade in/out. (Edits audio guide transcript, May 27, 2026; Genie Guide transcript, June 10, 2026; Threads post on the feature https://www.threads.com/@jonahmanzano/post/DRB50mRibtg/)
- Slider numbers for a talking reel: song 10 to 20 percent, original 100 (audiodrome, Hollyland); for a montage with occasional voice, song 25 to 30. Levels cannot be changed after posting, so Jacob previews with the phone speaker, not AirPods. (https://audiodrome.net/for-creators/how-to-add-music-to-instagram-reels/)
- "Original audio" reach effect: Mosseri's documented line is that audio is not required but grabs sound-on viewers, and that originals get more distribution than reposts; adding audio to a photo or carousel makes it Reels-eligible. There is no documented bonus for original audio over licensed, and none the other way. What IS documented is that the Search card weights "popularity of the song by country" and Reels predicts "will you tap the audio page," so a song page is a discovery surface, small but real. (Buffer, Oct 23, 2026 https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-algorithms/ ; Aubrium above)
- Song page without muting the voice: (a) in-app song at 10 to 20 percent with original at 100, voice already cleaned to -16 LUFS so it wins; (b) the "1 percent trick" (song at 1, never 0, original at 100) is widely repeated to tag the audio page without hearing the song; no official backing, and if the song is inaudible the sound-on half hears a voice-only reel, which is fine for us; (c) Edits with Volume Ducking on, then post from Edits so the song stays attached. (Chartlex https://www.chartlex.com/blog/marketing/trending-audio-instagram-reels-algorithm-2026 ; Outfame https://www.outfame.com/blog/trending-sounds-boost-engagement)
- Creator vs business: Jacob is a Creator account, so the full licensed catalog is available; several trackers flag that business accounts cannot see many of the songs above.

## 5. Captions and muted viewing

- Best-sourced current number: Mosseri told Colin and Samir that "about half of Instagram videos are watched without sound" (reported by Buffer, Oct 23, 2026 https://buffer.com/resources/instagram-algorithms/). The "80 percent of Reels are watched with sound on" line that every 2026 stats page repeats traces to a 2022 internal Meta document reported by the WSJ, never published by Meta (adamconnell https://adamconnell.me/instagram-reels-statistics/ ; loopex https://www.loopexdigital.com/blog/instagram-reels-statistics). The 69 percent and 85 percent "sound off" numbers are 2019 Verizon/Publicis and generic Facebook feed figures, not Reels (Forbes 2019 https://www.forbes.com/sites/tjmccue/2019/07/31/verizon-media-says-69-percent-of-consumers-watching-video-with-sound-off/).
- Ranking link: Meta's Feed and Feed Recommendations cards predict "will you turn sound on," and Mosseri's 2023 Reels explainer says reels that viewers mute are shown less (Aubrium). So sound-on is both a behavior and a signal.
- Implication for voice-first reels: design for the sound-off half to understand everything and for the sound-on half to feel something. Concretely: every spoken sentence is also a caption card (one breath per card), the hook claim is on screen in the first frame, numbers and part names are typed not just spoken, and the voice carries tone, jokes and asides that the cards do not need. Yesterday's caption look rules (SemiBold, light, thin outline) stand.
- A faceless voice reel that works muted is also a carousel in disguise: keep the card copy so it can be reposted as a carousel later.

## Audio rules to add to the El Coyote playbook

1. Voice is the credential. Jacob's own voice on every build reel; TTS (Kokoro am_michael) is a script timer, never a published voice. If an AI voice is ever used, toggle "Add AI label" at share.
2. One breath per card: one spoken sentence of 8 to 12 words per caption card, 3 to 4.5 s, period at the card change, card-in hit on the next first consonant. A 12 to 18 s build reel is 4 to 5 cards and 30 to 45 words.
3. Pace at 150 to 165 wpm, hook claim in 15 words inside 5 s, 5 to 8 percent of runtime left as pauses; cut "and / but / because" for periods.
4. Record VO in the truck cab or a closet with the DJI mic 3 to 5 inches off, sentence by sentence, redo stumbles on the spot. Budget upgrade if it becomes weekly: Samson Q2U.
5. Design for both halves: about half of Instagram video is watched muted. Every number, part name and claim is typed on screen; tone, jokes and asides live in the voice.
6. SFX budget per reel: two or three. Hook text hit, reveal, last card. UI pops and ticks 10 to 14 dB under the voice, cinematic hits 6 to 8 dB under, one hero sound per beat, decorative and looping elements silent. Source from SFXMint / VideoEditingSFX CC0 only.
7. Render files voice-first: voice stem -16 LUFS, program -14 LUFS / -1.5 dBTP after AAC, no melodic bed, and post the IG slider line in every kit: "original 100, song 10 to 20" (talking) or "song 25 to 30" (montage).
8. Always add the song in-app or in Edits so the reel gets a song page and the trending arrow; baked-in music gets no page and risks a mute. For voice-first reels where the song would fight, song at 1 percent (never 0) still tags the page.
9. For any reel Jacob finishes in Edits: Volume ducking on, detect voiceover, 20 to 30 percent; Enhance + Reduce noise on the VO track; post from Edits so the song stays attached.
10. Check trends in this order each Monday: Professional dashboard Trending audio, then Ignix (Instagram's own weekly list), then CreatorDB for the format each sound carries. Pick sounds whose FORMAT fits a Rival moment, not sounds that are merely big. Under about 10K reels is early, over about 100K is saturated but still fine for a meme.
11. Meme reels get the meme sound (clip audios: "oh yeah?", "Why would I deceive you?", Evil Larry); build reels get a format sound (Time, Coming Up Roses, Flower In A Hurricane) under a voice. Do not put a meme clip audio under a build reel.
12. Record two more beds this week at the shop and in the F600: 30 s cold-start idle, 30 s cab room tone. They are the only ambience that will match the picture.
