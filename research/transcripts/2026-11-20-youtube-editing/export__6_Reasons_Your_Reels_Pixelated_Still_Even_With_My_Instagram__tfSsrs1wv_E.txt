You exported video for Instagram real following the right settings, but your video still looks bad, then this is for you. Here are the six most common mistakes which can still ruin your quality even when the render settings are perfect. Mistake number one is assuming that quality is only about the file you export, but it's also about how that file gets to the server. The obvious thing you must have upload at highest quality checked in the Instagram app settings. If this is off, Instagram will crush your video even before it leaves the phone to save the traffic. Another thing is your connection. If your internet is unstable or slow during the upload, the app might dynamically lower the quality to ensure the upload finishes. Always upload on strong Wi-Fi or 3G or 5G. Mistake number two is to be inactive in social networks. This is unfair, but I assume that cold accounts, the ones that haven't posted in months, sometimes get hit harder by compression on their first posts after a break. The algorithm seems to prioritize active creators. So if your first videos look bad, don't panic. Keep posting new content. Mistake number three is the biggest myth. Thinking that 60fps gives you a smoother video. As a software engineer, let's look at the math. Instagram gives every video a bit rate budget. Let's say roughly 14 megabits per second. If you upload at 30 fps, that 14 megabits is divided across 30 frames. If you upload at 60 fps, that same 14 megabits is divided across 60 frames. You literally have half the data per frame at 60 fps. Also, Instagram doesn't play in 60 fps. It converts video in 30. So you lose your smoothness anyway. And in addition, upload less data for each frame. Mistake number four is thinking that with edits or other third party apps, you can upload at 2K or 4K resolution. Here's the reality. You cannot bypass Instagram server side processing. It doesn't matter what app you use to edit or upload. Eventually, your file hits the Instagram servers and their compression engine takes over. These apps might help with editing or promotion workflows, but they do not have a secret backdoor to give you higher quality than the Instagram app itself. Mistake number five is judging quality by file size. I saw people saying something like keep your file size under 40 megabytes. That's totally wrong. File size is just bit rate multiplied by duration. Following mentioned logic, 2 minute long video will have very low bit rate and the worst quality even before publication. Stop looking at the final file size in megabytes. Look at the bit rate in megabits per second. That is the only metric that actually defines the accuracy of your video stream. If your video looks perfect on your computer but has color shift or washed out on your phone, you probably use wrong gamma and color space.
