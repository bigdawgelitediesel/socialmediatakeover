# Script — "You can't align a solid rear axle"

**Pillar:** PERSONAL (14.80% ER) · **Format:** talking head · **Target length:** 25–30s
**Subject:** his own 6.7 · **Source:** real problem on his truck, right now

## Why this one works

Three things the last two reels were missing:

1. **A grievance everyone shares.** "What did the last owner hide from me" is the universal
   fear of every used-diesel buyer. That is the relatable wrapper the education rides inside.
2. **A rule most people don't know.** You cannot align a solid rear axle — camber and toe
   are built into the housing. There is no adjustment. A guy with uneven rear tire wear
   goes and buys a front-end alignment and the problem does not go away.
3. **A clean send trigger.** "Get under it before you buy it" is forwardable to anybody
   shopping a truck. Forwarding it makes the sender look like he saved his buddy money.

## The 3-second fix

The Oct 7 reel held **12.2s** (record) but skipped at **52.8%** (worst of the run). The body
works; the open does not. So this script **opens mid-problem** — no greeting, no setup, no
"so I found out that." First frame is already the bad news.

## Hook options — pick one, say it before the camera settles

**A (cost, hardest open):**
> "The last guy who owned my truck cost me a set of tires and he doesn't even know it."

**B (the rule up front):**
> "You can't align a rear axle. So when I tell you mine's bent, that's a parts problem."

**C (physical, best if you're pointing at it):**
> "See that dent? That's four hundred bucks in tires I haven't bought yet."

Do **not** open with "so I've got a problem with my truck." That's the 52.8% skip.

## Body — beats, not a word-for-word read

1. **What it is.** Rear axle housing on my 6.7 has a dent in it. I didn't do it. Previous
   owner did, and it didn't get disclosed.
2. **Why a dent in a tube matters.** That housing is what *holds* the angle of both rear
   tires. Bend the tube, the tires stop sitting flat. They sit at an angle, and they wear
   on one edge.
3. **The part most people get wrong — say it slowly.** There is no adjustment back there.
   On a solid axle, camber and toe are the housing. You can't dial it out. An alignment
   shop can align the front all day and it will not touch this.
4. **The real cost.** It's not one set of tires. It's a set of tires every time, forever,
   until the housing gets fixed or replaced. Plus it's working the bearings and the seals
   on that side harder than they should be.
5. **What I'm doing about it.** Pricing the housing against having it straightened.
   (← leave this honest and open. It sets up the follow-up video.)

## The rule — the last line, say it flat and stop talking

> **"Uneven wear on ONE rear tire isn't an alignment problem. It's a bent housing, and no
> alignment shop can fix it. Get under a used truck before you buy it — not after."**

Then stop. Do not add "anyway, let me know what you think." Dead air is better.

## Caption

Answerable, two options, not open-ended:

> Bent rear housing from the guy before me. Straighten it or replace it — what would you do?

(Compare to "Am I crazy?" which drew 0 comments on 130 reach. Give them a fork, not a vibe.)

## Shooting notes

- **Post 4–7 PM Mountain**, not morning. Recent reels went up ~07:24 and reach halved.
- Pocket 3, ActiveTrack on. If you point at the dent, remember **0.2 m minimum focus** —
  stay back a hand's width further than feels natural or it hunts.
- **Get a shot of the actual dent**, even 2 seconds of it. Proof beats description, and it
  gives you a cold open for hook C.

## Follow-up this sets up

- The price comparison once he has real numbers: housing vs. straightening vs. used axle.
- The tire itself as evidence — a close-up of the wear edge is its own video.
- Transfer angle: how Class 8 shops handle bent housings, since it is a known failure there.
