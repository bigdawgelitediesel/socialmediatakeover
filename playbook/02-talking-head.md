# Talking-Head Playbook

The verdict: **4 talking-head + 2 "showing" per week is right.** Talking head is the
cheapest format per unit, the only one that scales for one person, and the one this account
has already proven on — PERSONAL is 14.80% engagement, build documentation is 1.90%.

## ⚠️ The number nobody flagged until now

| Metric | @elcoyotediesel (29 reels) | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Avg watch time | 6.84 s | — |
| **Shares / reach** | **0.045%** (88 shares / 195,139 reach) | **0.3%+** |
| Saves / reach | 0.045% | — |

**Shares are a worse problem than watch time.** Mosseri named *sends per reach* as the
signal that governs reach to **non-followers** — the entire growth problem at 215 followers.

And the decoupling is stark in your own data: your two highest-share reels (0.84% and 0.33%
shares/reach) had watch times of **5.7–5.8s — below your average**. The 260,008-view reel
produced **6 shares**. That reel was *given* reach; it never earned it.

**Fixing watch time alone will not fix reach.** Every structure below is built to produce a
send.

### The test every video must pass before you post it

> **Would a guy send this to his buddy with the same truck?**

If no, don't post it — rewrite the hook until he would.

### ⚠️ The uncomfortable finding: education does NOT get shared

> "What was left off this list was educational tips… Think about your local library. There
> are thousands of textbooks that are super valuable but have been sitting on the shelf for
> a decade. No one reading them, and definitely no one recommending that textbook to a
> friend."

**Education gets SAVED. Saves are the weakest signal. Shares are the growth engine.**

This cuts directly against a parts manager's instinct, because pure teaching is the natural
content. **The resolution is not to stop teaching — it's to change the wrapper.** The tip
rides inside a relatable, controversial or timely frame instead of being served as a tip.
Cap pure education at roughly **one in five posts**.

### The five things that actually get shared

| Type | What it is | Jake's version |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Motivational** | Text on black. Zero production. Aimed at ONE specific person. | *"Your truck doesn't care that you're tired. Neither does Monday."* Cheapest weekly post he has. 90 seconds. |
| **2. Call-to-arms** | "Tag three friends" is a share instruction in costume. The cheat phrase: **"I need your help."** | A real itemized parts-counter receipt as the prop. *"Help me settle this — tell me what you actually run."* |
| **3. Relatable humor** | **"Relatability comes from specificity."** Most-repeated line in the whole corpus. | Not "diesels are expensive." The 5:40am −14° no-start. The guy who wanted a part for "the white one." The $9 clip on national backorder. |
| **4. Controversial** | Attack *ideas*, never people or businesses. Only hills you'd actually die on. | Dealership upsell, emissions as a tax on working people, "most truck guys online have never worked." |
| **5. Timely** | Breaking news is the most shareworthy content there is. **Missed the first hours? Go a layer deeper.** | *"Everyone's talking about the recall. Here's the actual part number and what it costs at my counter."* |

**The vanilla test:** almost nobody loves or hates vanilla ice cream — 95% feel nothing.
Stop making vanilla content.

### Ask for the share — and always give the reason

Never just "share this." Match the reason to the type:

| Type | CTA |
|---|---|
| Controversial | "Share this **if you agree**" |
| Relatable | "Share this **if you can relate**" |
| Timely | "Share this **if you learned something new**" |
| Call-to-arms | "Share this to **help spread the message**" |
| Motivational | "Share this **if you're feeling inspired**" |

**Put it in the reel** — spoken or on-screen. Burying a CTA at the bottom of a long caption
is one of the most common mistakes: you can't hope people find it like buried treasure.

## Targets

| Length | Hold rate (avg watch ÷ length) | Completion |
|---|---|---|
| **12–15 s** | 50%+ → 6–7.5 s | 65%+ |
| 15–30 s | 50%+ | 50%+ |
| 30–45 s | 30%+ | 40%+ |

**At 6.84s average, a 15-second talking head already passes. A 45-second one puts you at
15% and is a worse ranking signal than the short version.** Start at 15–20s. Earn longer.

## 1. Hooks — surviving the first 3 seconds with nothing but a face

The scroll decision lands at **~1.7 seconds**. Up to **50% drop inside 3 seconds.**

### Three layers, all firing on frame 1

1. **Spoken line** — starts frame 1, no dead air. At 160 wpm that's **~8 words in 3s**.
2. **On-screen text** — *present* in frame 1, not fading in. 2–4 words, bold, high contrast.
3. **Opening frame** — the most visually loaded frame you have.

> **Your unfair advantage:** most talking heads have nothing to put in frame 1. You can hold
> a broken $2,800 turbo. Face and object in the same frame, before you say a word.

### 40 hooks, in your voice — all ≤12 words, said flat and fast

**Number-first**
1. "This part is eleven dollars. Not replacing it is nine thousand."
2. "I quoted a guy $14,000 today for something a $40 filter prevents."
3. "Four hundred bucks. That's what this mistake costs, every single time."
4. "Three parts leave my counter every week for the same reason."
5. "Cheapest repair in this building is a torque wrench."

**Counter-authority** — your actual credential
6. "I sell this part twelve times a month. Here's why."
7. "I'm a parts manager. I know what's going to break on your truck."
8. "I don't fix trucks. I just know what everyone breaks."
9. "Guys come to my counter with the same three problems."
10. "Nobody buys this part until it's already too late."

**Correction / stop-doing-this** — highest-share class
11. "Stop doing this to your 6.7. I'm begging you."
12. "If your shop tells you this, walk out."
13. "You're deleting the wrong thing on that truck."
14. "Everybody says [X]. Everybody's wrong, and here's the receipt."
15. "This is the part people replace when it's not even broken."

**Identity validation** — the DNA of your 15.46% post
16. "You ever bought a part twice in the same week? Yeah."
17. "Nobody in my family had a shop. I built this one."
18. "If you've ever run hotshot, you already know where this goes."
19. "This is what a parts counter in Wyoming actually looks like."
20. "It cost you nothing. It means everything to me." ← your own proven line. Reuse the
    *shape*: [dismissive outside view] + [personal weight].

**Mid-action open**
21. "…and that's the third one of these I've sold today."
22. "…so I told him no. Here's why."
23. "So this just came off a truck with 90,000 miles."
24. "Guy just walked out of here mad. He was right to be."

**Questions**
25. "Why does every 6.7 die the same way?"
26. "Know what the most returned part in this building is?"
27. "What's actually in a $900 injector?"
28. "You know why your fuel mileage dropped in October?"

**Confession**
29. "I did this wrong for six years. Nobody told me."
30. "I bought the wrong truck. I'll say it."
31. "I was the guy selling people parts they didn't need."

**Contrarian**
32. "Deletes aren't why your truck is fast."
33. "A $200 part fixes 80% of what people pay $2,000 for."
34. "Semi parts are cheaper than pickup parts. Same part."
35. "Chief will never be a show truck. That's the point."

**Filter hooks** — hyper-specific repels the wrong audience, which the algorithm likes
36. "This is for the guy with a 2017 6.7 and 200,000 miles."
37. "If you tow over 12,000 pounds, this one's for you."
38. "Anybody who's run hotshot out of Wyoming knows this feeling."
39. "If you've got a deleted truck you're about to sell — watch this."
40. "This only matters if you live somewhere they salt the roads."

**Write the hook before the camera turns on.** Film **3–5 hook variants** of the same video
— costs 40 seconds, and it's the highest-leverage thing you can do.

## 2. Retention — pattern interrupts for a man with one camera

> A 30-second monologue with three cuts declines steadily. **The same content with 15 visual
> changes plateaus.**

**Target: a visual change every 1.5–2.0 seconds → 5–7 per 10 seconds.**
- 15s video → 8–11 changes · 20s → 10–14 · 30s → 15–21

**A visual change isn't only a cut.** It counts if it's a cut, a push/pull, a **text-overlay
swap**, a zoom, or a reveal. That's what makes this achievable on one static take.

| Interrupt | How you do it | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Jump cut** — kill every "um," breath, false start. Speech is **20–35% dead air** | the single biggest improvement available | free |
| **Zoom punch** on the key sentence | 1–2 per video, on the number and on the turn | free |
| **Text-overlay swap** every ~2s | cheapest visual change there is | free |
| **Object insert** — bring the part into frame mid-sentence | a new object is a new shot | free |
| **Position change** — turn 30°, step over, sit→stand | reads as a cut | free |
| **1–2s b-roll cutaway** | over 3s stalls pacing | needs a library |
| **Audio hit** — ratchet, impact gun, engine | a sound cue interrupts too | free |

**Jump-cut trap:** two shots too visually similar reads as a mistake. Fix by shifting
position before resuming, or inserting one second of b-roll.

**Monotone cut rate is its own killer.** Cluster fast cuts on the high-energy stretch, then
**hold 3–4 seconds on the payoff.** The contrast makes both feel intentional.

## 3. Five structures, chosen for SHARES

Universal skeleton:
```
0:00–0:03  HOOK      ≤12 words. Object in frame. Text on frame 1. No greeting.
0:03–0:06  SETUP     Who this is for + what the payoff is.
0:06–0:XX  ONE IDEA  Specifics only: names, numbers, years, mileage, prices.
last 3–5s  PAYOFF    Punchline, or a line that rhymes with the hook.
```

**One idea per video.** Three ideas is three videos. Word budgets at ~160 wpm after silence
removal: **15s ≈ 40 words · 20s ≈ 53 · 30s ≈ 80 · 45s ≈ 120.**

### 1. THE COUNTER WARNING — your default. 18–22s.
```
0:00  "This part is eleven dollars. Not replacing it is nine grand."  [part in hand]
0:03  "Third guy this month. Same truck, same failure."
0:06  What it is, where it sits, what it does.
0:10  What happens when it goes. The real number.   [zoom punch]
0:14  Who's at risk — year, mileage, use case.
0:18  "If you've got one of these, go look tonight."  [loop back to the part]
```
**Why it sends:** the viewer immediately knows a guy with that truck.

### 2. THE RECEIPT — contrarian. 20–30s.

**This already happened to you once, by accident.** On the FASS post — your *worst*-hook
video at 59% skip rate — a real argument broke out:

> "There is absolutely zero reason to upgrade the lift pump on a 6.7 unless you're going
> over 750 horsepower."
>
> "More fuel is always better, diesel cools these things man. I got a 6.7 Cummins, 100 overs
> and a fast 265, truck has 380 degree EGTs going 80."
>
> **Jake:** "My plan is to eventually go for greater horsepower. Also I have the CP4 pump,
> so disabling that creates a better insurance policy without taking the engine apart yet."

That is the single most valuable exchange in the account's comment history. Nobody was
baited — someone disagreed with a real technical decision and Jake defended it with a real
reason. **That is what this structure manufactures on purpose.**

Three videos sitting in that one thread:
- *"A guy told me there's zero reason to upgrade a lift pump under 750hp. He's not wrong.
  Here's why I did it anyway."*
- *"I didn't swap the CP4. I disabled it and put a FASS on it. Here's why."*
- *"What EGTs are you actually running? I'll go first."* ← the "I need your help" pattern

Claim → the number that disproves it → what to do instead. State the common belief *fairly*
— fairness is what stops the pile-on. Close with "Argue with me. I'll answer every one."
**Why it sends:** contrarian takes get forwarded *to be argued with*. This is the legitimate
version of correction bait — you're holding a position you can defend, not planting an error.

### 3. THE STORY WITH A BILL AT THE END — 25–40s. Your 15.46% post is this shape.
Mid-action open → the situation, first person, present tense → the turn → the number →
the line that makes it about the viewer.
**Why it sends:** stories get sent to people who were *there*.

### 4. THREE THINGS — 20–30s. The save engine.
Numbered overlays are free visual changes and tell the viewer how much is left.
"Number three is the one that gets everybody." **Saves more than it sends** — use it, don't
build on it alone.

### 5. THE COMMENT REPLY — 15–25s. Highest-leverage format you're not using.
Read a real comment out loud → **give the answer immediately** → then why → "Anybody else
want one answered, ask below."
**Why it matters:** it industrializes the thing that made your 15.46% post work. It
manufactures topics, makes commenters feel seen, and is the fastest video you'll ever shoot.

## 4. Delivery

- **Add ~20% more energy than feels natural.** On camera, "normal" reads as flat.
- **Talk at your natural 140–155 wpm. Let the EDIT produce the pace.** Don't perform fast —
  cut fast. Above 160 wpm measurably hurts comprehension, and injector failure modes are
  complex material.
- **Cut all dead air.** Target: under 4 seconds between sentence and cut.
- **Talk to one person** — the one guy at the counter. That's the register your 14.80%
  pillar already wins on.
- **Don't read a script.** The viewer can hear it. Script the hook and the one idea; bullet
  the rest.
- **Never "hey guys." Never "thanks for watching."** Both kill the loop.
- **Specifics over abstractions.** Names, years, mileage, prices. You have a price on
  everything — that's an unfair supply.

---

## 5. Framing

- Vertical 9:16 native · **chest-up, not face-only** (lets hands and a part in frame)
- **Eyes in the top third** · camera at **eye level**, arm's length
- Keep face, part and captions in the **central safe area** — the grid preview crops to 4:5

**Background: ignore the generic "clean background" advice.** The parts counter *is* the
credential. Racking, inventory, a dirty bay — all free authority. Instagram's stated 2026
direction favors raw over polished.

**The one thing to actually fix: light direction.** Face the open bay door or a window.
The two killers are **backlit** (bright door behind you) and **overhead-only** (shop
fluorescents putting your eye sockets in shadow — exactly what a parts department does to
a face).

---

## 6. Captions and audio

**Captions are non-negotiable — ~85% of short-form is watched without sound.**
- **Burn them in.** Platform auto-captions can be toggled off and don't survive re-compression.
- Bold sans, heavy outline, readable at **236px** (thumbnail size) *and* in sunlight
- **2–4 words per frame**
- **Word-by-word with a color pop** (white → yellow). Highest retention for educational
  content, and it doubles as a visual change every ~0.4s — helping you hit the pacing target
  without cutting.
- **Lead the audio by 100–200 ms.**
- **Re-time caption in/out points to the visual cuts**, not the waveform. 5–10 min per video,
  and it's the lowest-effort highest-return habit in the workflow.

**Audio in a Class 8 shop: the Pocket 3's built-in mics will lose.** Air tools, compressors,
lifts, running engines. **A DJI Mic 2/3 transmitter pairing directly over Bluetooth is the
single highest-ROI purchase on this account** — no receiver to plug in, which matters when
you're alone. Capsule **high on the sternum** (closer = more voice, less room), windscreen
on even indoors, cotton not synthetic shirts.

**2026 minimum viable quality: clear audio + captions + a hook.** Ship it. Don't color grade.

## 7. Batching — what makes 6/week survivable

**PHASE 1 — PLAN (Sunday, 30 min).** Leave with **12–15 topics**, each with a written hook
and a structure number. **Cluster them** — 2–3 themes, not 12 random ideas. Clustering keeps
energy up on take 9.

**PHASE 2 — FILM (one session, 60–90 min).** Three locations, 3–5 videos each, then move —
the location change is what stops a batch looking batched. **Record in energy order, not
topic order.** Slate every take verbally. Shoot long, don't stop for stumbles. 3–5 hook
variants per video. Change one visible thing per block (hat, jacket, part in hand). End with
**10 minutes of b-roll** — 5-second shop shots for the standing library.

**PHASE 3 — EDIT (2 sessions × 60–75 min).** Rough cut (kill silence) → captions (generate,
then re-time to cuts) → **pacing audit** → export. Budget 45 min/video for the first two
weeks, dropping to 15–25.

**The pacing audit is what makes this a system:** watch once with a stopwatch, click every
visual change, count clicks per 10 seconds. Target 5–7. If it's 3, add text overlays. Takes
3–5 minutes and it's the difference between 6.8s and a plateau.

**Weekly budget: ~6.5 hours for 6 posts** (30 plan + 75 film + 150 edit + 120 comments).
That's ~65 min all-in per published video. Decide against that number, not against a vague
"post more."

## 8. You will never run out of ideas — the five wells

1. **THE COUNTER LOG.** Note on your phone. Every day: the part somebody bought, why, and
   what it cost. **A parts manager generates 5–15 of these a day.** Infinite engine, and
   nobody else in the niche has it.
2. **THE RETURN SHELF.** What came back and why. Returns encode what customers get wrong —
   the highest-share content class there is.
3. **THE QUESTION LIST.** What you actually get asked out loud, all day, for free.
4. **THE PRICE GAP.** Class 8 vs pickup. You sit on both sides of a wall nobody else in this
   niche can see over. No competitor can copy this.
5. **CHIEF.** As the *personal* pillar, not the documentation pillar. "Here's what I got
   wrong on my own truck this week" is a story with a bill at the end.

**Your position, stated properly:** *"I'm the guy at the parts counter who sees what breaks
on your truck before you do — and I'll tell you the price."* Not "diesel truck content."

| Well | Structure | Signal it produces |
|---|---|---|
| Counter log | 1 — Counter Warning | **sends** |
| Return shelf | 2 — The Receipt | sends + comments |
| Question list | 5 — Comment Reply | comments + follows |
| Price gap | 4 — Three Things | saves |
| Chief | 3 — Story | follows + engagement |

## 9. Cadence

**4 talking-head + 2 showing, not 3+3.** The talking heads are where the proven engagement
and the sends live. The "showing" videos should be short and mostly exist to feed the b-roll
library and break up the grid — **and they should still open with your face and a spoken
hook.** A build clip with no human in it is exactly the 1.90%, 206-view pillar.

**Ramp, don't cliff:** weeks 1–2 at 3 talking heads + 1 other. Weeks 3+ at 4+2. A cadence
you abandon in week 5 is worse than one you hold for a year.

**The real argument for volume at 215 followers is data density.** 6×/week is ~300 data
points a year; 1×/week is 52. You learn which hooks work **6× faster**. You're not trying to
go viral yet — you're trying to find out what works.

## 10. Failure checklist — audit every video before posting

**Kills it in 3 seconds:** "Hey guys" · any logo or intro animation · a wide establishing
shot before the point · slow zoom / fade / black frame / a beat of silence · setup instead
of substance ("so today I want to talk about…") · text that fades in · low-contrast text.

**Kills it mid-clip:** one continuous static shot (*the* biggest reason talking heads feel
boring) · monotone cut rate · static frame held past 4 seconds · teaching four things ·
silent gaps (half a second breaks the trance) · reading off a script · a problem raised and
never resolved.

**Kills the ranking signal:** "thanks for watching" · no loop design (you lose the rewatch,
and Instagram counts replays) · length chosen by habit — **every second past 20 currently
costs you distribution.**

**Kills it structurally:** content nobody would send to a specific person · backlit or
overhead-only lighting · shop noise over the voice · **no captions.**

## 11. Risks

- **Deliberate correction bait** — planting a known error to farm "well actually" comments.
  Not a written ToS violation, but it trades away exactly what this account is short of.
  Your 260k reel is the proof: enormous reach, **6 shares**, negligible follower conversion.
  Structure 2 is the legitimate version.
- **Engagement pods, bought followers, comment rings** — platform-manipulation violations,
  and they poison the signal you need to read.
- **Reposting others' clips** — 10+ reposts in 30 days drops you from recommendations
  entirely. Reacting *with your own commentary and edits* counts as original; straight
  reposts don't.
- **⚠️ CUSTOMER PRIVACY — read this one twice.** The counter log is your best content well
  and the only one with a real-world downside. **Never a name, never a plate, never a face,
  never enough detail to identify a customer.** And **check whether your employer has a
  social media policy before the first counter video goes up.** This is the single most
  likely way the whole project blows up, and it has nothing to do with the algorithm.
- **AI-generated fill.** AI captions and AI silence-removal on your own footage are fine.
  AI voice or AI b-roll is a distribution risk.
