# Two talking heads a day: the 30-day topic plan (2026-11-23)

Jacob, 2026-11-23: "I need to do 2 talking head videos a day. Make me a 30 day topic plan and update to the portal." This page is that plan: 60 episodes, Tuesday November 24 to Wednesday December 23, 2026, two a day, both in the format of `playbook/12-talking-head-in-the-truck.md`. It replaces the morning loop, skit and question slot of the calendar in `research/2026-11-21-MILLION-VIEW-RULE.md` section 5 for scheduling; `tools/coyote.py` and the portal read this file first. The old ideas 1 to 59 stay as the bank.

The brief that governs every line here, in order: community first ("we are building a community, not just talking about 7.3 and Project Rival all the time, the more relatable the better"); the talking heads are relatable, trust-building or funny, not about the Rival build or the 7.3; the whole message under a minute; attention on the first frame from the device and the lockup, not from cuts; no on-screen ask, no outro, no fade, no grade, song in-app, caption line 1 is the share line, no em-dashes anywhere. The Rival build is barely started, so Rival is three honest check-ins in thirty days, and Chief is three episodes plus the one that answers the comments on the FASS video, because that truck exists.

## 1. The two jobs

Two talking heads a day only works if they do two different jobs, otherwise they compete for the same viewer with the same shape and the account reads as a template by day ten.

- **AM, 09:00 to 11:00 MT: the send episode.** Funny, or a list a person forwards (the ranked lines, the five stages, the five things that fail on a cold morning). 20 to 45 s. One joke or one list, the turn on the last line, cut on it. Graded like the memes were: sends per reach (floor 1 percent, goal 2), skip under 40. This is the reach engine now; the meme bank is the fallback, not the plan (section 6).
- **PM, 16:00 to 19:00 MT: the hold episode.** The story, the trust, the honest check-in, the one lesson. 40 to 60 s, the whole message, under a minute (Jacob's ceiling, rule 115). Graded on watch share (floor 50 percent) and likes per reach (floor 4), sends logged. This is the conversion engine: the one that turns the morning's stranger into a follower.

Lanes across the sixty: Funny 20, Relatable 16, Trust 15, People 3 (comments read and answered, the breakout family), Chief 3, Rival 3. The morning is mostly Funny and utility Trust; the afternoon is mostly Relatable and story Trust. From week two, about once a week the afternoon is a People episode built from the week's comments, which is how the comments become the content (Mariah's double-down rule).

## 2. One man, two episodes, one sit

- **16:30, one sit in the truck, 30 to 40 minutes: both of tomorrow's episodes.** The PM one first while the voice is fresh, the AM one second because it is shorter and lands better once he is warmed up. Three to five takes each, straight through, no stopping inside a take. Never shoot the morning of (Mariah: one day ahead).
- **The portal's Today tab lists tonight's two with the device each one needs** (the sticky note, the socket, the two parts, the phone with the comment pulled up). Grab them before getting in the truck; the device is the only thing the edit cannot add later.
- **20:00, about 20 minutes per episode:** `tools/streamline.py plan`, `challenge`, `preview --beats auto`, `render`, `lint`, then the look check in `research/2026-11-23-LOOK-GUIDE.md` section 9, then the ledger line. Open the first frame full size before anything else (rule 116).
- **Post:** AM from the phone at 09:00 to 11:00, PM at 16:00 to 19:00, same master to TikTok and Shorts the same day, pinned comment within 60 s naming tomorrow's episode, Story share with caption line 1.
- **Sunday:** the week's read, two spare episodes from section 5 filmed and banked, the five-axis check on the coming week.
- **Time, honestly:** about two hours a weekday with replies. If a day breaks, the PM goes out and the AM is skipped, never the reverse; a banked spare covers the next day. Two misses in a week means the week after drops to the fallback in section 6, not to nothing.

## 3. The constants and the rotation

Constant for thirty days: the seat in Chief as home base, his voice recorded in the room, the one lockup (3 to 7 words, a number where there is one, upper third, gone by 3 s), plain captions at the chest, beat words by the face (rule 118), no grade, no outro, no ask, caption line 1 the share line. Everything else rotates so no two episodes look the same (`research/2026-11-23-masterclass-2/01-human-not-ai.md`, the variation axes):

- **Opener type** never repeats two episodes running. In the briefs: mid-action, a number, a line read off the phone, the object held up, a flat claim, a confession, a question he gets asked, the moment, a sound.
- **Where:** the cab four days in seven; the parts room, the counter after hours, the bay beside a Class 8 or beside the F600, the driveway with Chief, the yard, main street at least twice a week. "I work there" said once whenever the shop is in frame; no customers, plates, invoices or vendor cost.
- **Device:** one per episode: nothing (hands), the notebook page, the part in hand, the phone, a numbered card, the items on the dash, the walk. Never the same device two episodes running on the same subject.
- **Length:** at least one episode under 30 s and one at the 60 s ceiling every week.
- **Tone:** funny, trust, grievance held lightly (about a thing, never a person), gratitude, low status, thinking out loud; never two grievance episodes running.
- **Payoff:** a number, a rule, the question for the comments, a callback, an open loop to a named episode (at most twice a week).

Every brief carries the first sentence, the turn, the last line, the device, the place, the lockup, caption line 1 and the question that splits the comments. Square brackets are his numbers; an episode is not filmed until the bracket has a real number in it. The series name and the fixed sign-off are still Jacob's to pick; until he does, the title line is the lockup and the episode ends on the last line.
