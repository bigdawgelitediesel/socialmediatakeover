# The daily talking head, in the truck (2026-11-22)

Jacob: "I wanna do a talking head video every day. One of the posts each day needs to be the talking head video, with me sitting in the truck. Make sure to use what you've learned." This page is that format. It is the afternoon slot of the 30-day plan (16:00 to 19:00 MT). Jacob, same day: "I don't want the talking heads to be so much about the Rival build and the 7.3. Relatable, trust-building or funny." So the rotation is Relatable, Trust, Funny; the shop and the trucks are the backdrop, not the subject; Rival is an honest check-in twice in fifteen days while the build is just starting (barely any parts bought), Chief twice because that truck exists. Personal talking heads were already the account's best pillar (14.80 percent engagement, September audit). Sources: the Jessica Carter study (daily yap, 113 posts in 155 days, lessons at 3.6x her median), the Caleb Ralston reel (five rules, countdown ring, one locked shot), the @omgadrian dossier (move at 0.0, cut on motion, fixed sign-off, cliffhanger not follow ask), the million-view rule (story shape, stranger test, answer held), the audio rules (the cab is the booth), the caption audit (68 px, no stroke, one accent) and the hook atlas (first change by 0.33 s, first noun by 0.9 s).

## The set

- **Which truck.** Chief (the 6.7) by default: it moves, it has window light, and the cab is already the vocal booth. The F600 cab for the Rival episodes that are about it; it sits in the bay, so the bay is the background.
- **Camera.** Pocket 3 on a dash or windshield mount at eye height, Face Auto-Detect on, 4K30, Normal 10-bit, WB locked. Same seat, same mount position every day so the grid reads as one series (Jessica: "same corner of the bed"). Window light from the side, never the sun straight in the lens.
- **Sound.** DJI Mic 2 clipped 3 to 5 inches off, engine off, windows up, HVAC off. The cab kills the room. Record the two beds once: 30 s of cab tone, 30 s of idle.
- **Props on the dash** when the episode has them: the receipt pile, the part, the phone with the comment.

## The open (the first second decides it)

Never a static face on frame 1. Pick one:
- The door shuts and he is already mid-sentence.
- He turns from the windshield to the lens on the first word.
- The part or the phone comes up into frame first, his face second.
- Sound first: the door, the key, the turn signal, then the voice.

First noun by 0.9 s. No "hey guys", no "so today". The title sentence sits in the upper third as the one burned-in text: 3 to 7 words with the number in it ("Frame swap, to the dollar", "One of the last '59 F600s"). Flat claim, never a question (Jessi Jean rule).

## Package it as a series, and say who it is for (added 2026-11-22 from @nategaffney, @jacob.kitton, @mariah_mpls)

- **The daily talking head is a named series.** One title, burned top centre for the whole episode in the house serif caps (the lockup, held), the same every day; Jacob picks the name. Nate Gaffney went 10K to 500K in 40 days the week he started packaging episodes as a series and saying who it was for; Jacob Kitton's 30-second car talk carries "CONTENT PSYCHOLOGY" top centre for all 30 s at 9,221 likes.
- **The first line names who it is for.** "For everyone building something after a ten-hour shift." "For the parts guy who gets asked why he's not a tech." The caption line 1 rule, spoken.
- **One person, you and I, us versus the problem (Jun Yuh, 5M).** Call out one person in sentence one, never a crowd. "You" names what they want, "I" owns the failures ("you want it to start every morning; I ran a stock CP4 for two years before I did anything about it"). Then us versus the problem, never you versus them ("you're not cheap, nobody ever showed us what that pump does"). Read every script against those three before filming.
- **Say the thing they already think.** Every episode articulates a thought the viewer has had and never said out loud (Kitton: "the most powerful content doesn't introduce a new thought, it articulates one that already exists"). Recognition, not news. If the script teaches something nobody in the audience has felt, it is a save, not a send.
- **One day ahead.** Film in the gaps during the day, edit at night, post the next day (Mariah, 300K in two months as a mom with two kids). Never shoot the morning of.
- **Double down.** When an episode works, the next three answer its comments before anything new.

## The shape (40 to 60 s now, 60 to 120 s once ten episodes in a row hold an average watch over 10 s)

1. **Trouble or claim in sentence one**, a stranger understands it. A number or a price in the title line.
2. **Credential inside a working sentence, never a standalone intro line (2026-11-22):** "I order parts for semis all day, and I still bought the wrong one for my own truck." Lower the status once, after the credential ("I don't own a shop. I work at one.").
3. **Body:** the case in order, one idea per breath (8 to 12 words), 150 to 165 wpm. Say the acronyms, never explain them. Small number next to the big one. Keep the failure in.
4. **One loop opened in sentence one, one turn in the middle, the answer last.** No scheduled re-hook; the device is inherited from YouTube long-form and measured nowhere. Read the retention chart at 24 h and put a turn where it sagged last time.
5. **Answer held to the last ten seconds.** Then one question that splits the comments ("Would you daily it?", "Is parts a trade?"), spoken.
6. **Fixed sign-off, in his words, every time.** Pick it once. Then a cliffhanger to the next episode instead of any follow ask ("Tomorrow: the part that came back three times."). End within 1.5 s. No outro, no fade.

Rotation so the page breathes: **Relatable** (the first truck, the hotshot years, why he films after a ten-hour shift, why the counter and not the wrenches), **Trust** (the returns rack, what not to buy, the three questions before a diagnosis, what he did to his own 6.7 and what it cost, "I work at a semi shop" said out loud), **Funny** (the counter lines ranked, the five stages of a backorder, worst places to drop a 10 mm). Rival only as the honest check-in ("here is exactly where it stands") until there is a build to show. Pick one contrarian take per episode and say it flat.

## The edit (motion tier on a plate)

- **Change, not cut (2026-11-22 doctrine, research/2026-11-22-REEL-EDITOR-DOCTRINE.md).** Something the viewer cares about changes every five to eight seconds: a hand, the pad, the part, a turn, a pause. Cut only to remove, on a breath or a gesture or the first consonant of a word, room tone running under the join; one or two cuts in 60 s, zero is fine; never a cut mid payoff line, never a punch-in on handheld, never both a punch and a cut on one join. The 2 to 4 s cadence is retired: across 131 studied reels it does not sort winners from losers, and the one-take talking heads with one physical device are the ones that won.
- **The default insert is the notebook (added 2026-11-22 from @mariah_mpls, 50K likes):** a legal pad on the dash or the counter, your own hand writing the point in Sharpie, shot top-down after the take with the phone on the mount or the Pocket 3 held overhead. Five to eight per trust episode, 2 to 4 s each, cut in on the noun. Ranked lists, the two questions, the price beside the OEM price, the one line a customer said. No render, no second person.
- B-roll amplifies the feeling of the line, it does not have to illustrate the exact thing (Jun Yuh: text tells the story, visuals carry the emotion). Cut on the noun within 0.3 s: the bay, the counter, the part on the shelf, the truck. Three to five inserts per episode, each 1.5 to 3 s. Without inserts the episode is still legal; it is just a yap.
- Captions: PlainCaption 68 px, 2 to 4 words, sentence case, one tan accent word, chest band, never on the face, no stroke. Title in the upper third only during the open (off by 3 s). Plus the beat words (Jacob, 2026-11-23, rule 118): one big word every 4 s, cream, coral or tan, placed by the face, never on it; `streamline.py preview --beats auto` to see them before the render.
- Pop word at 0.0 when the open is a claim (omgadrian): one word, upper third, behind the head if the matte is made.
- Chapter numerals on list episodes (the pilot's five rules, the receipts). Countdown ring on the 90-second pilot.
- No grade. bt709 tags. -14 LUFS, voice stem -16. Song in-app at 10 to 20 under the voice, or 1 percent to keep the song page.

## Five takes, then pick

Record the whole thing five times, straight through, no stopping for stumbles inside a take. Teleprompter is allowed (Jessica admits hers); write it as one sentence per line in the script file so `vo_scratch.py` can time it first. The best take is usually the fourth. Re-film the best-performing episode every 90 days as a new post.

## Posting

- Caption line 1 is the share line, a person type ("For your 6.7 brother who is still on the stock CP4"). Two to four lines after it with one real number. Five hashtags max. No follow ask.
- Pinned comment within 60 s repeats the person type and names tomorrow's episode. Story share with the same line.
- Trial Reels: if the toggle is open, every talking head goes out as a Trial first with two line-1s; winner to followers after 24 h.
- TikTok and Shorts the same day, same first line, same master.
- 48 h read for the early warning (sends per reach over 1 percent, watch over 60 percent, skip under 45), 7-day views as the judge. Expect the first two weeks to run lower views and higher likes per view than the memes; Jessica's did, then the lessons became the growth engine.

## The ones that worked for her, translated

- **The receipts episode is a series, not a post:** "what the frame swap has cost, to the dollar" monthly, same five lines in the same order (idea 32, then 43 for Chief).
- **The scoreboard:** open an episode by updating a number (days to SEMA, dollars in, parts on the truck, followers at the milestones).
- **Let the comments pick the deliverable:** a question asked three times in a night earns a free lesson with "Comment INTERESTED" in the caption, never on screen, one post in four at most.
- **"How I film this" and "how I edit my reels"** as a six-part series once the first ten episodes are out; her editing lessons were the biggest growth she ever had.
- **Sell nothing for a long time.** When a partner part is the payoff, say the brand once and keep "Gifted" in line 1.

## Do not

Open on a greeting or a promise the reel does not pay. Explain an acronym. Ask for the follow on screen. Burn a call to action. Grade it. Add a whoosh, a riser or a sound effect. Name a customer, a plate or a vendor price. Promise anything for the shop. Say SEMA is anything but "the target". Pad to a length: 40 to 60 s until the watch time earns 60 to 120; the 20 to 40 s ban is for narrated story over footage, not for a talking head that ends when the last line is said.

## Added 2026-11-22 from the doctrine (research/2026-11-22-REEL-EDITOR-DOCTRINE.md)

- **Write the last line first, then the first line as its opposite.** But and therefore, never and then. Three beats: normal, explosion, new normal.
- **Zoom into the moment (Philipp Humm):** where I was in one phrase, the verb, the raw thought, the emotion on the body, the exact words someone said. At least three of the five in the first 20 s. Never summarize what you can replay.
- **Voice before the picture, mid-sentence inside 0.5 s.** The sentence is the opener; the lockup landing is the move; a static seated face with a noun by 0.9 s is legal.
- **Delivery:** 160 to 180 words a minute for the facts, his own speed for the human line; pauses under a second at clause ends; one held beat after the turn; let the pitch move and end the key sentences falling; up to five ums a minute is free; hands in frame; eyes on the lens or two degrees under.
- **Camera, exact:** lens at seated eye height, 70 to 90 cm from the face, never under 50 cm; passenger window as the key at 45 degrees; depth through the glass behind the shoulder; 4K30, no grade.
- **Sound:** -14 LUFS integrated, -1 dBTP; one second of room tone each end and under every cut; voice only; no SFX.
- **Grade a talking head on watch share (floor 50 percent of runtime) and likes per reach (floor 4 percent).** Sends are logged, not graded, until one clears 0.7 percent; no face held past 10 s on this account ever has. Memes and loops are graded on sends.
- **The card for the dash** is section 18 of the doctrine.

## Jacob's correction on length, 2026-11-22

The 40 to 60 s band is a ceiling, not a target. On the gratitude footage he rejected the short cuts ("you cut way too much out, I want the whole message in there") and approved 57.9 s with every sentence he meant, six cuts, a checklist card and the lockup at 0.0. Streamline's `--keep` protects the sentences that must stay; `--target` is only for trimming filler toward the ceiling. When in doubt, keep the message and get attention with the device.
