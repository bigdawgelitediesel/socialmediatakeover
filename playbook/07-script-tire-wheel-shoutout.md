# Script — Tire Wheel Experts shoutout (the version that doesn't tank)

**Pillar:** nominally SPONSOR — deliberately built to perform as PERSONAL
**Format:** talking head · **Target length:** 25–30s

## ⚠️ Read the number before shooting this

| | SPONSOR posts (n=5) | PERSONAL posts (n=5) |
|---|---|---|
| Avg views | 1,203 | 1,127 |
| **Engagement rate** | **1.36%** | **14.80%** |
| **Skip rate** | **72.5%** | — |

Identical reach, **11× the engagement gap.** The prior Tire Wheel Experts post (2026-08-14,
static image + brand tag) did **117 views, 1 like, 1 share.**

**Sponsor content on this account does not fail for lack of reach. It fails in the first
three seconds, because it opens on a product instead of on something happening.** That is
the entire fix.

## The play: make it episode two, not an ad

The bent rear axle housing video (`06-script-rear-axle.md`) and this one are **the same
story in two parts.** Shoot them in that order:

1. **Episode 1 —** the housing the last owner bent is going to eat my tires.
2. **Episode 2 —** here's who took care of me on the tires.

Episode 2 lands because the audience already watched him have the problem. A shoutout that
*resolves a problem the viewer saw happen* is a story. A shoutout with no setup is an ad.
Same footage, same brand mention, completely different retention — and it costs nothing
extra, because episode 1 is already getting made.

**Do not post this one first.**

## Hook options — never open on the product

**A (resolution of episode 1, strongest):**
> "Remember the bent housing? Here's the part of this that actually went right."

**B (price-forward — his real lane):**
> "I'm a parts manager and I still got a better deal than I could get myself."

**C (the inversion):**
> "I was ready to spend [$X] on tires. I didn't, and it's not because I found a cheaper
> brand."

**Never:** "Big shoutout to my guys at ___." That's the 72.5% skip rate, verbatim.

## Body — beats

1. **What he actually got.** Name the specific wheel and tire, the size, the application.
   Specificity is the credibility.
2. **Why that combination and not something else.** This is the part only he can do — he
   orders parts for a living and knows what comes back. Load rating, sidewall, how it holds
   up on a truck that tows. This is the sentence that separates him from every other
   shoutout video on the platform.
3. **What it would have cost, and what the hookup was.** Be straight about it. (See
   disclosure below.)
4. **The honest limitation.** One thing he'd tell somebody to think about before buying the
   same setup. One. This is what makes the whole video believable instead of promotional —
   and it is the single biggest difference between 1.36% and 14.80%.

## The rule — the closing line

> **"The right tire for a truck that tows isn't the same as the right tire for a truck that
> looks good in a parking lot. Know which one you're buying."**

Then stop.

## Disclosure — do this, it is not optional

They gave him product. That is a material connection and it gets disclosed. Say **"they
hooked me up"** out loud in the video, not just in the caption, and keep **#ad** or
**"gifted"** in the first line of the caption where it isn't hidden behind "more."

Two reasons, and the second one matters more than the first:
- It is what the FTC requires.
- **Audiences reward it.** Hidden sponsorship is the thing that makes a diesel audience
  stop trusting a page. Saying it plainly in sentence one is why his version can outperform
  everyone else's.

## Caption

> Gifted setup from [tag] — but I picked the tire. [size/model] on a truck that tows.
> What are you running right now?

"What are you running" is answerable in two words, which is the point. Compare: "Am I
crazy?" drew 0 comments on 130 reach.

## What this does for the brand relationship

This is also the better deliverable *for them.* A 117-view thank-you post is worth nothing
to Tire Wheel Experts. A video where a heavy-duty parts manager explains why he chose their
setup is a testimonial they can reuse — and it is the kind of thing he can point at when he
goes back to FASS, Derale, ISSPRO, S&B or McNeil. Screenshot the insights on this one and
keep them.
