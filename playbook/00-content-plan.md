# Content Plan — @elcoyotediesel

**Built 2026-09-15** from this account's own 38-post performance data plus six research
agents. Every recommendation traces to a number in `data/instagram/` or a cited source
in `research/`. Nothing here is generic advice.

---

## The one-paragraph strategy

Reach is solved; conversion is not. The account reached 45,641 accounts in 30 days and
gained 17 followers — **0.037%**. It already gets ~3.8× its follower count in views per
post, which means Instagram's recommendation system is *already* pushing it to strangers.
The failure is that strangers who see it have no reason to follow. Fix the reason to
follow, and the existing traffic does the rest: moving conversion to just **0.3%** — still
below the "well-optimized" band — turns 17 followers/month into **~135/month with zero
additional reach**.

## The five content pillars, rewritten

### 1. PARTS COUNTER (new — the biggest untapped asset)
Jake is the **parts manager at a Class 8 / semi-truck shop** — not the mechanic. He also
owns and is building a **6.7 Powerstroke F350**. This pillar does not exist yet and should
become the backbone.

**PICKUP IS THE PRIMARY NICHE** — that's where Jake's own truck, his money, and all his
existing brand relationships are (FASS, Derale, ISSPRO, S&B, McNeil, Tire Wheel Experts are
all light-duty). The semi parts counter is the **credential and the variety**, not the
backbone. Roughly **2 pickup posts to 1 semi post.**

**Every diesel creator is a mechanic. Nobody is the parts guy. And almost nobody bridges
heavy-duty and light-duty.** Jake knows what the wrench-turners don't: what actually fails,
how often, and what it really costs, because he orders the replacements. On Class 8, where
a failure costs an owner-operator a day of downtime, that knowledge carries real weight —
and it transfers to pickup content as authority.

**The bridge is the strongest hook available:**
> *"I order parts for semis all day. Here's what that taught me about my own 6.7."*

It reaches the much larger pickup audience using much rarer heavy-duty authority, and it
turns the Chief build into a demonstration of expertise instead of just another project.

**Formats only he can make:**
- "The #1 thing I order for 6.7 Powerstrokes. Here's why it fails."
- **Real prices.** "$340 OEM, $90 aftermarket — here's which one comes back."
- **"Don't buy this."** He sees the return rate. Enormously shareable, and credible from
  nobody else in this niche.
- "What I'd actually put on my own truck."
- Unboxings — native to the job, and he already does them.
- What customers ask for vs what they actually need.

He still gets shop and vehicle access for B-roll: the part in his hand, the truck it's
going on, the mechanic installing it. **He narrates from the counter, not the bay.**

**Why:** the mechanic lane is fully occupied on YouTube — Dave's Auto Center (1M), Deboss
Garage (1M), South Main Auto (950K), Rainman Ray (663K) — and is repost aggregators and
brand accounts on Instagram. **The parts-counter lane is empty on both platforms.**
Westen Champlin won SEMA Content Creator of the Year 2025 for filming the work he was
already doing; the principle transfers, the job doesn't have to.

**Why it outperforms mechanically:**
- **Outcome uncertainty.** A broken customer truck is an unresolved question. Chief's
  ending is known on day one — which is why the origin story got 57 views.
- **Infinite supply, zero cost.** Customer trucks arrive daily; someone else buys the parts.
- **Self-feeding comments.** Every diesel guy has an opinion on what's wrong.

**Adjacent formats using shop access:** the part that came off vs the part going on ·
the expensive mistake someone else made · emissions-compliant work (DPF/EGR/SCR) — a real
sub-niche, Fox Truck & Tractor built 92.6K on it.

**Avoid:** delete/defeat-device content. Heavy D Sparks carries an $850K EPA judgment.
It is a sponsorship and legal liability.

### 2. PERSONAL (proven — 14.80%)
First-person, emotionally direct, Jake talking to camera. This is the account's best
format and matches what Instagram said it favors going into 2026 (raw and human over
polished). **Sponsor content belongs in this register, not the BUILD register.**

### 3. CHIEF (restructured)
Stop posting the build as disconnected one-offs — that's why it averages 206 views.
Number it: **"CHIEF — Episode N."** Cold-open on the finished change, then rewind.
End on a *problem*, not a reveal ("this is why the pump's coming back out").

Mechanism: when a viewer completes Part 1, completion is a strong interest signal and
Instagram becomes markedly more likely to serve them Part 2 unprompted. You need one
strong Part 1, not a fresh viral hook every week. Six to eight episodes is the minimum
arc that establishes return-visit behavior.

**Sequencing that matters: SHOP builds the audience → the audience then follows the
build.** Chief is the recurring subplot revealed at events, not the weekly episode.
Nobody subscribes to Episode 1 of 19 for a truck they've never heard of.

### 4. RELATABLE (keep — 5.79%)
Cheap, performs fine, fills cadence. Keep it as filler, not as the strategy.

### 5. STATIC — **stop entirely**
Nine static posts produced 1,046 views between them. Average 116. Reels are 99.6% of all
views. Instagram extended its anti-aggregator rules to photos and carousels in April 2026,
so the photo surface is tightening, not loosening.

## The constraint that shapes everything: this is a one-man operation

Jake runs Elite Diesel Service **and** makes every piece of content. No camera operator,
no editor, no scheduler. Any plan that assumes a second person is worthless.

**This is why PARTS-COUNTER content is the right pillar and not just a good idea.** It is
the only pillar whose filming cost is near zero — the parts are already in his hands as
part of the job. A personal build requires him to make time. Parts content requires him to
press record on time he's already spending. He is the **parts manager**, not the mechanic,
so nothing here should require him to turn wrenches on camera.

### The production model: capture always, edit once

**Capture (during the workday, ~0 extra minutes)**
- Phone on a magnetic mount or cheap tripod in the bay. Set it once in the morning.
- **Three clips per job, that's the whole discipline:**
  1. **The problem** — the truck, the symptom, the customer's words. 5 seconds.
  2. **The find** — the moment you see what failed. Hold the part up. 5 seconds.
  3. **One sentence to camera** — what it was, what it costs, what the owner did wrong.
- Never stop working to film. If the shot is missed, it's missed. There's another truck
  tomorrow. **The infinite supply is the whole point — no single clip matters.**

**Edit (one sitting per week, 60–90 minutes)**
- Sunday or whenever the shop is quiet. Cut three reels, 12–18 seconds each.
- Same template every time. Do not redesign it. A repeatable format is what makes
  3/week survivable for one person; a bespoke video every time is what kills it.
- Write all three captions in the same sitting.

**Publish (5 minutes/day, or scheduled)**
- The API has no scheduling endpoint and this container is ephemeral, so either post
  manually in the 16:00–19:00 window or connect a scheduler (Buffer and Metricool both
  have free tiers with official MCP servers — see `research/2026-09-15-tooling-reference.md`).

### Realistic cadence: 3 reels/week

Evidence says moving from 1–2 to 3–5 posts/week roughly doubles follower growth, and
10+/week shows diminishing returns and burnout. **For one person also running a shop,
3/week sustained beats 7/week for two weeks followed by three weeks dark** — which is this
account's actual history (Jun 7 → Jul 13 → Aug 16 → Sep 2, then six dark days from Sep 9
during the month's best reach stretch).

| Slot | Pillar | Solo cost |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | **PARTS COUNTER** | ~0 — filmed at work, part already in hand |
| Thu | **CHIEF Episode N** | Moderate — film whenever you touch the truck, bank it |
| Sat | **PERSONAL** or **RELATABLE** | Low — one take, talking to camera, no setup |

**Post between 16:00–19:00** — this account's own online-followers peak
(`data/instagram/online-followers-hourly.tsv`: 87–92 avg online vs 17–20 overnight).
Use it, don't over-optimize it; timing is worth a few percent.

### Bank content, never run live

Film more than you publish. Keep 2–3 finished reels in reserve at all times, so a bad week
at the shop doesn't create a dark week on the account. The dark stretches in the history
above are the single most fixable operational problem, and banking is the only fix that
survives a one-man schedule.

### What to deliberately NOT do, because you are one person

- **No elaborate build-series production.** Cold-open on the finished change, rewind, done.
- **No daily Stories obligation.** Stories retain existing followers; they don't acquire
  new ones. At 215 followers acquisition is the job. Use Stories to reshare each new reel
  (a first-hour engagement spike is worth having) and nothing more.
- **No cross-posting project yet.** YouTube long-form is genuinely the right long-term home
  for the Chief build, but it is a second production pipeline. Not now.
- **No comment-reply marathons.** Reply to everything on a post for its first hour, then
  let it go. The 15.46% post worked because replies were fast, not because they were
  exhaustive.

## Craft rules, derived from this account's numbers

**Reel length: 12–18 seconds.** Average watch time is 6.8s. At 30s that is ~23%
retention — bottom quartile. The same footage cut to 12–15s becomes ~45–57% retention
with no new content. This is the cheapest available win.

**Rebuild the first 2 seconds.** 6.8s average means a large share are gone in the first
1–3 seconds. That is a first-frame problem — no motion, no subject, slow reveal.

**Engineer for SENDS, not likes.** Mosseri named three ranking signals: *watch time,
likes per reach, sends per reach* — all ratios. He specified sends matter more for
reaching non-followers. With 215 followers, sends are the only escape route from the
follower graph. **Every reel needs an answer to "who would you send this to" before it
is shot.** The absurd part, the expensive mistake, the before/after, the thing a buddy
would tag his buddy in.

**Reply to every comment, within the hour where possible.** The 15.46% post is the one
where Jake replied to nearly everyone. Instagram's published reach tips ask for replies
within 7 days. This is the single highest-leverage unpaid action available.

**Say the keywords out loud.** Instagram transcribes reel audio and indexes it. Search
has replaced hashtags as the discovery surface.

## Profile rebuild — do this first, it takes one afternoon

The profile is where 45,641 people decided not to follow.

1. **Name field**: currently "Jake Dalton" — nobody searches that. It is an indexed search
   field. Change to `Jake | Diesel Truck Builds` or similar. Handle stays `@elcoyotediesel`.
2. **Bio must state the promise the reels imply** — what the truck is, what the series is,
   and when the next one lands. The last clause is the follow reason.
3. **Pin the three best-ER posts, not the highest-reach ones**: "It may cost you nothing…"
   (15.46%), "This should explain it" (10.7%), "Don't be afraid!" (7.7%).
   **Do not pin the 260k reel** — it recruits the wrong viewer.
4. **Three highlights**: `CHIEF` (build in order) · `THE SHOP` (Elite Diesel Service) ·
   `WHO` (Jake, first person).
5. **Ignore the 0 link taps for now.** With ~0.3% of reach reaching the profile at all,
   link taps *cannot* be non-zero. Fix upstream first.

## Hashtags — a solved, trivial problem

Capped at **5 per post since December 2025**, and Mosseri has said they don't meaningfully
increase reach. Pick five specific ones (`#powerstroke #f350 #dieseltruck` beats
`#truck #cars #diesel`), and never think about it again. Geotag every post — local
discovery is a live surface and Wyoming is uncontested.

## Do not do these — ToS violations with real ban risk

Engagement pods · follow/unfollow · bought followers · comment bots · private-API tools
(`instagrapi` and similar) · scraper services.

Beyond the ToS risk, they are now **self-defeating**: every signal Mosseri named is a
*ratio*. Pod engagement inflates numerator and denominator together, and pod members are
demographically wrong for a Wyoming diesel audience — so it actively degrades the
interest-prediction model. Buying followers would dilute the one genuine asset this
account has: a small but precisely on-target audience (87% US, 76% male, 51% aged 25–44).

**Note:** the Build Your Tribe channel has an episode promoting Instagram pods. Take the
rest of that channel's material; skip that one.

## The one outbound habit worth the time

**10 substantive comments a day on 1k–10k diesel accounts, posted within the first
10–30 minutes of them publishing.** Early comments sit at the top and ride peak
distribution. This account follows **5 accounts** and has never pulled this lever.

Ten comments is ~10 minutes with coffee. It is the highest-value-per-minute growth action
available to one person, and unlike everything else on this list it costs no filming,
no editing and no equipment.

Keep it strictly one-directional and manual. The moment it becomes a reciprocity
arrangement it is an engagement pod, which is a ToS violation.
